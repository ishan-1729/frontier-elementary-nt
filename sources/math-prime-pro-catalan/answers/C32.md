## 0. Verdict and the strongest proved statement

It uses finite ideal arithmetic, a lattice pigeonhole argument, and counting, with **no class-number formula, Artin map, reflection, Thaine, Iwasawa, Chebotarev, or Dirichlet unit theorem as an input**.

Let `H=(q-1)/2`, let `g` generate `(Z/qZ)^*/{±1}`, and define

\[
f(a)=\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}j^{-1}[(a-j)p^{-1}]_q\pmod p,
\qquad F_{p,q}(T)=\sum_{i=0}^{H-1}f(g^i)T^i.
\]

Assume `p∤H`, and put

\[
r_{p,q}=H-1-\deg\gcd\left(F_{p,q}(T),1+T+\cdots+T^{H-1}\right).
\tag{0.1}
\]

A source is impossible whenever, for some positive integer `t`,

\[
\boxed{
p^{2t r_{p,q}}H^{2(t+1)H}
>(H!)^{2(t+1)}q^{(t+1)(H-1)}(t+1)^{2tH}.}
\tag{PACK}

\[
\boxed{r_{p,q}\ge\left\lceil\frac{19}{20}(H-1)\right\rceil.}

## 2. Direct PA_phi: what the source gives, and the exact norm gap

### 2.1 PROVED: the root ideals and their one rational product

Define the integral normalized factors

\[
b_a=B_a/C_a.
\]

Their integrality can be checked locally: away from `q`, `C_a` is a unit; at the prime over `q`, numerator and denominator have the same valuation. The source factorization away from `q` is disjoint, and each valuation of `b_a` is a multiple of `p`. Therefore there are integral ideals `J_a` with

\[
(b_a)=J_a^p.
\]

Here, and in the arguments below, one may use the finite prime-power ideal arithmetic proved in §4.3, rather than import general class-field machinery.

Every real conjugate of `B_a` occurs once in `Phi_q(X)`; likewise the product of the conjugates of `C_a` is `q`. Thus

\[
N_{k/\mathbb Q}(b_a)=u^p,\qquad N(J_a)=u,
\]

and the exact scalar product gives

\[
\boxed{\prod_a J_a=(u).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Indeed the `p`th powers of these two ideals agree, and prime-exponent cancellation is valid in the finite ideal factorization. Also `prod C_a^{e_a}` is a unit, since its common ramified valuation is multiplied by `sum e_a=0`. Consequently

\[
J=\prod_a J_a^{e_a},\qquad N(J)=1.
\tag{2.2}
\]

**Stopping point of the scalar-allocation construction.** The rational element `u` supplies (2.1), the constant exponent direction. It supplies no generator for the nonconstant augmentation combination (2.2). The equation that an ideal has a principal `p`th power is precisely what is already known; extracting an element generator is not a cancellation in the group of ideals.

This explains concretely why the integer factors in the allocations do not yet provide `beta`. It does not assert that no new nonlinear identity involving those factors can do so.

### 2.2 PROVED: an integral lattice and a sharp principalization threshold

Let

\[
r_- =\max(0,-\min_a e_a),\qquad c=u^{r_-},\qquad I=(c)J.
\]

Using (2.1),

\[
I=\prod_a J_a^{e_a+r_-}
\]

is integral, and

\[
\boxed{N(I)=c^H,\qquad I^p=(\gamma),\qquad \gamma=c^p z\in\mathcal O_k.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

**Norm-one quotient criterion.** If a nonzero `b in I` satisfies

\[
|N(b)|<2c^H,
\tag{2.4}
\]

then `I=(b)` and `beta=b/c` solves `PA_phi`.

Proof: `(b)I^{-1}` is an integral ideal, so its norm is a positive integer. It equals `|N(b)|/c^H`, which by (2.4) is less than two and hence is one. An integral ideal of index one is the full order. This proves the assertion. Conversely any generator of `I` has norm of absolute value `c^H`.

A sufficient rectangular version is

\[
|\sigma(b)|<2^{1/H}c\quad\hbox{at every real embedding.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

More flexibly, for any positive weights `w_sigma` with product one, it suffices to find `b in I\{0}` in the box

\[
|\sigma(b)|<(1+\delta)c w_\sigma,
\qquad (1+\delta)^H<2.
\tag{2.6}
\]

All these are **proved sufficient criteria**, not assertions that the requisite lattice point exists.

### 2.3 PROVED: the source is extremely close to one, but this is not a lattice-point theorem

Let `E_1=sum_a |e_a|`. Since `B_a` is positive at every embedding,

\[
(Y-1)^2\le\sigma(B_a)\le(Y+1)^2.
\]

The zero sum of the exponents implies

\[
\left(\frac{Y-1}{Y+1}\right)^{E_1}
\le\sigma(z)\le
\left(\frac{Y+1}{Y-1}\right)^{E_1}.
\tag{2.7}
\]

In particular,

\[
|\log\sigma(z)|\le\frac{2E_1}{Y-1}.
\tag{2.8}
\]

If `2E_1/(Y-1)<=1/2`, the geometric majorant for the exponential gives `|sigma(z)-1|<=4E_1/(Y-1)`. The actual source size is far larger than this sufficient threshold for the standard bounded projector lifts.

The positive real numbers `w_sigma=sigma(z)^{1/p}` have product one and are very close to one. They define a particularly natural box in (2.6). **They are not known to be the embeddings of a field element.** Calling them the conjugates of `beta` before constructing `beta` would assume the missing root/principalization step.

The elementary power-basis box argument in C31 gives a nonzero `b in I` with coefficients at most `c` and house at most `c(2^H-1)`. The source's large `Y` does not improve that bound. The sharper lattice argument in §4 gives a norm factor `M_q`, not the factor less than two required in (2.4). The distinction between a large, explicit constant and a constant below two is decisive here.

Also, an arbitrary generator `beta` of `J`, if one existed, would satisfy `z=beta^p epsilon_0` for a unit `epsilon_0`. Its conjugates need not be close to those positive roots: an unbounded unit factor cannot be discarded in an archimedean estimate. The componentwise primary-unit argument handles that factor *after* principalization, not before it.

### 2.4 PROVED: crossing to a prime-to-p degree field does not create principalization

Let `E/k` be any finite extension of degree `n` prime to `p`. If `J O_E=(b)` and `J^p=(z)`, then taking the relative norm gives

\[
J^n=(N_{E/k}b).
\]

Choose integers `a,d` with `an+dp=1`. Then

\[
\boxed{J=\bigl((N_{E/k}b)^a z^d\bigr).}
\tag{2.9}
\]

Thus principalization in such an extension already yields principalization in `k`, with the displayed generator. In particular `[L:k]=2(p-1)` is prime to `p`. Adjoining further `q`th roots in fields containing `mu_q` gives towers of `q`-power degree, also prime to `p`, and the same descent applies.

This is not a proof that a generator exists in the larger field. It shows why a new cross-field formula must do actual arithmetic work, rather than obtain a root by a harmless extension. Adjoining `z^(1/p)` itself has degree divisible by `p`; the Bezout argument then has no inverse degree and cannot descend that tautological root.

The cross norms of the individual opposite-field minus ratios and their existing roots are one, as proved in C26 §5.1. A monomial in the modulus-one minus roots which is real has value `±1`, up to its rational scalar. It cannot by that operation alone acquire the required nonconstant real divisor. ## 3. PROVED integer-like model: primary, norm one, tiny house, nonprincipal divided divisor

This entire construction is explicit in `k_0=Q(sqrt(229))`. It does **not** claim a counterexample to a theorem about the full Catalan source.

### 3.1 An explicitly nonprincipal ideal of order three

Put

\[
w=(1+\sqrt{229})/2,\quad w^2=w+57,\quad
P=(3,w-1),\quad\bar P=(3,w).
\]

The full integer ring is `Z[w]`. One elementary verification is as follows. For an algebraic integer `(a+b sqrt(229))/2`, trace and norm are integers. Initially `a` is integral and `b` is rational; squarefreeness of 229 in `229b^2=a^2-4N` forces the denominator of `b` to be one. Reduction modulo four gives the same parity for `a,b`. Conversely these conditions give a monic quadratic with integer coefficients.

Reduction of `w^2-w-57` modulo three gives `w(w-1)`. Direct multiplication of the two displayed ideals yields

\[
P\bar P=(3),\quad N(P)=N(\bar P)=3.
\]

Let

\[
\theta=5+w=(11+\sqrt{229})/2,\qquad
v=7+w=(15+\sqrt{229})/2.
\]

Then

\[
N(\theta)=-27,\qquad N(v)=-1,\qquad (\theta)=P^3.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For the last identity, `theta` is zero at `w=1 mod3` and a unit at `w=0 mod3`; alternatively multiply the generators of `P` three times. The resulting lattice has index 27 and lies in the principal lattice of `theta`, also of index 27. The accompanying script checks both the index and the inclusions with integer determinants.

**There is no algebraic integer of norm `±3`.** Suppose one existed. Since `v>1` is a unit, multiply it by an integral power of `v` so that the absolute value `r` of its first real embedding satisfies

\[
\sqrt{3/v}\le r<\sqrt{3v}.
\]

The other embedding has absolute value `3/r`. Thus, writing the integer in the form `(a+b sqrt(229))/2`,

\[
|b|\sqrt{229}\le r+3/r
\le\sqrt3(\sqrt v+1/\sqrt v).
\]

The square of the last bound is

\[
3(v+v^{-1}+2)=3(\sqrt{229}+2)<54<229.
\]

Hence `|b|<1`, so `b=0`, which would make the norm an integer square rather than `±3`. This contradiction proves `P` nonprincipal. Equation (3.1) therefore proves its class has exact order three, without calculating the class number.

The divided divisor has class `[P]-[bar P]=2[P]`, which is nonzero.

The factors `27` are cubes. Every unit `1+9a` is a local cube by successively solving

\[

\tag{3.4}

## 4. Fresh mechanism I: many independent divided divisors versus finite ideal packing

This mechanism does not try to principalize one ideal. It uses the source to force *too many distinct scalar-equivalence classes of ideals*, and bounds how many can fit into a finite norm range. The decisive new open condition is a quantitative rank estimate for (0.1).

### 4.1 PROVED: an elementary unit-space upper bound

Let `U` be the unit group of a totally real field of degree `H`. Then

\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_p}(U/U^p)\le H-1\qquad(p\text{ odd}).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Only an upper bound is needed; no existence of `H-1` independent units or regulator formula is used.

Here is a proof. Every class modulo `p`th powers has a totally positive representative, since `u^(p+1)` is positive at every embedding and has the same class as `u`. A totally positive unit has norm one. Its logarithm vector lies in the hyperplane of coordinate sum zero. These vectors are uniformly separated: if each coordinate has absolute value less than `log(1+1/H)`, then every conjugate is less than `1+1/H`, while AM–GM makes its integer trace at least `H`. The trace is therefore `H`, and equality in AM–GM makes the unit one.

Take any finitely many units, and the finitely generated group they generate. Its positive part is torsion-free, so integer elimination gives a basis of `r` generators. If the real span of their logarithms had dimension `d<r`, the `(N+1)^r` distinct products with basis exponents in `[0,N]` would have separated logarithms in a `d`-dimensional box of side `O(N)`. Partitioning that box into fixed-size small boxes gives only `O(N^d)` possibilities, a contradiction for large integer `N`. Thus `r<=d<=H-1`. Finally, any set of classes independent modulo `U^p` remains independent modulo the `p`th powers of its finitely generated subgroup. This proves (4.1), without assuming finite generation of the full unit group.

The logarithm is used only as the elementary real function taking multiplication to addition. No analytic class-number or unit-rank theorem is invoked; alternatively, the same finite box argument can be phrased in multiplicative intervals.

### 4.2 PROVED: the source forces at least `p^r` inequivalent ideals

The Fermat quotient on units at `p` is

\[
\delta(a)=\frac{a^p/\sigma_p(a)-1}{p}\pmod p.
\]

It is additive under multiplication, vanishes on `p`th powers, and vanishes on local `p`th powers. The finite circular calculation of C28 §5, read there and reproduced in its essential form here, gives

\[
\delta(C_1)=\frac2q\sum_{a=0}^{q-1}f(a)\eta^a.
\]

Indeed binomial coefficients give

\[
\delta(1-\eta)=-\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\eta^j/j}{1-\eta^p},\qquad
\frac1{1-\eta^p}=-\frac1q\sum_{n=1}^{q-1}n\eta^{pn}.
\]

Multiplying and collecting exponents proves the formula. Pairing `j` and `p-j` makes `f(-a)=f(a)`. On a circular product with exponent sum zero, the constant term `f(0)` disappears, and the coefficient vector is convolution by `F_{p,q}`.

The `H` elements `eta^a+eta^-a` indexed modulo sign are independent modulo `p`: a relation is a polynomial of degree at most `q-1`, zero constant coefficient, divisible by `Phi_q`; it must be zero. The real integral order and its reduction at `p` are justified in §4.3. Therefore the rank of the Fermat quotient on the explicitly generated norm-one circular units is exactly `r=r_{p,q}`. Under `SEM`, the augmentation module is `F_p[T]/(1+...+T^(H-1))`, proving the gcd formula (0.1).

It follows from (4.1) that the subspace of primary unit classes has dimension at most

\[
H-1-r.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Now use `RAD`. Its source space `V` has dimension `H-1`. Send a source class `[z_v]` to the scalar-equivalence class of the ideal with divisor `div(z_v)/p`. This is well defined: changing a representative by a `p`th power changes the divided divisor by a principal divisor. The image is killed by `p`.

The kernel consists of source classes represented by primary units: if the divided ideal is `(beta)`, then `z_v/beta^p` is a unit and is locally a `p`th power. Conversely a unit representative has trivial divided-divisor class. By (4.2), the kernel has dimension at most `H-1-r`. Thus the image has dimension at least `r`:

\[
\boxed{\text{A source forces at least }p^r\text{ distinct ideal classes in }k.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This uses the actual `p`-torsion subgroup, not the possibly zero image of an order-`p` element in a quotient modulo `p`. The correction in §7 is therefore not a gap in this argument.

### 4.3 PROVED finite ideal arithmetic and the discriminant used in counting

For clarity, the elementary arithmetic facts needed for the upper bound are supplied here. They are not a hidden class-number or class-field input.

Let `xi=eta+eta^-1`, and let `Psi_q` be its monic minimal polynomial. The finite cyclotomic degree argument of C26 gives degree `H` and

\[
\Phi_q(T)=T^H\Psi_q(T+T^{-1}).
\]

Differentiating at a primitive root gives

\[
\Phi_q'(\eta)=\eta^H\Psi_q'(\xi)(1-\eta^{-2}).
\]

Taking products over the `q-1` conjugates gives

\[
q^{q-2}=\operatorname{disc}(\Psi_q)^2\,q,
\qquad\operatorname{disc}(\Psi_q)=q^{H-1}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The sign is positive because all the roots of `Psi_q` are real.

Also `O_k=Z[xi]`. Here is a finite denominator proof. The integral trace pairing and its adjugate show that the coefficients of any algebraic integer in this power basis have denominators supported only at `q`, by (4.4). Put `pi=2-xi`. Its conjugates are the `C_a`; their product is `q`. Its monic polynomial is Eisenstein at `q`: all its nonleading coefficients reduce to zero on setting `eta=1`, and its constant term is `±q`, not a multiple of `q^2`. In the `q`-local power basis, the orders of the terms `a_i pi^i` are

\[
H v_q(a_i)+i,\qquad0\le i<H.
\]

They are distinct modulo `H`, so a nonzero sum has the minimum order and there is no cancellation at that minimum. This follows directly from the Eisenstein relation `q=pi^H` times a unit and reduction modulo `pi`; it may be read at each finite `q`-power precision. An algebraic integer cannot have negative order, since its monic equation would then have its leading term of uniquely least order. Therefore all its power-basis coefficients are `q`-integral as well. The adjugate denominator bound now gives `O_k=Z[xi]` and (4.4) is its discriminant.

For a rational prime `ell!=q`, factor `Psi_q mod ell` into distinct irreducible factors. Coprime factor lifting to any finite precision is elementary: if `P=AB mod ell^j`, use a polynomial Bezout identity to write the error modulo `ell` as `aB+bA`, and replace `A,B` by `A+ell^j a,B+ell^j b`. Iterating gives the finite CRT decomposition of `O_k/ell^j` into rings free over `Z/ell^j`, each with a finite-field residue ring. In one such component, an element not divisible by `ell` is a unit; every ideal is a power of `ell`, by choosing the least coefficient order among its elements. At `q` the identical argument uses `pi`, the residue field `F_q`, and `q=pi^H` times a unit.

Every nonzero integral ideal contains its additive index times `O_k`. Applying these finite decompositions at the finitely many prime divisors of the index proves the required unique prime-exponent description, inverses of fractional ideals, and multiplicativity of their indices. A prime ideal above `ell` has norm `ell^f`; there are at most `H` such prime ideals. This supplies exactly the finite ideal arithmetic used in (2.1)–(2.4), (4.3), and the count below. No ideal-to-Galois correspondence occurs.

### 4.4 PROVED: a lattice representative bound, with the pigeonhole proof included

Embed `k` in `R^H` by its real embeddings. The image of an integral ideal `I` is a lattice of determinant

\[
q^{(H-1)/2}N(I),
\]

by the determinant of the embedding matrix and the additive index.

We need the following elementary lattice lemma. If a bounded open polytope `S` has volume greater than the determinant of a lattice, two of its points differ by a nonzero lattice vector. To prove it, intersect the finitely many translates of `S` meeting a fundamental parallelepiped with that parallelepiped. If none overlap, the sum of their volumes is at most the volume of the parallelepiped; but cutting and translating preserves volume and the sum is the volume of `S`, a contradiction. This is a finite partition argument. The simplex and parallelepiped volume formulas are determinant formulas; no measure-theoretic theorem is needed.

Apply it to half of the cross-polytope `sum |x_i|<R`, whose half has volume `R^H/H!`. The difference of the two points belongs to the full cross-polytope. AM–GM then supplies a nonzero `b in I` with

\[
|N(b)|\le\frac{H!}{H^H}q^{(H-1)/2}N(I).
\tag{4.5}
\]

One can first take a strictly larger `R` and then use the integer-valued norm to reach the displayed non-strict bound; only finitely many lattice points occur in a fixed bounding box.

Thus every scalar-equivalence class of fractional ideals has an integral representative with norm at most

\[
M_q=\frac{H!}{H^H}q^{(H-1)/2}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Indeed take an integral ideal in the inverse class, find `b` by (4.5), and use `(b)I^{-1}`. This proves the representative bound rather than invoking it as a named Minkowski/class-number theorem.

### 4.5 PROVED: finite counting, not an analytic class-number formula

Let `a(n)` count integral ideals of norm `n`. For each rational prime `ell`, the finite prime-exponent description in §4.3 shows that its local counting series is

\[
\prod_{i=1}^g(1-Z^{f_i})^{-1},\qquad g\le H.
\]

Its coefficients are bounded by those of `(1-Z)^(-H)`. Multiplying the coefficient inequalities gives

\[
a(n)\le d_H(n),
\]

where `d_H(n)` counts ordered `H`-tuples of positive integers with product `n`. Therefore, for every real `s>1`, the number of ideals of norm at most `M_q` is at most

\[
M_q^s\left(\sum_{1\le n\le\lfloor M_q\rfloor}n^{-s}\right)^H
\le M_q^s\left(1+\frac1{s-1}\right)^H.
\tag{4.7}
\]

The last estimate is the elementary decreasing-function integral bound on the displayed finite sum. No analytic continuation, residue of a zeta function, regulator identity, or complex analysis is used. The counting interpretation may be kept entirely as the sum over integer tuples.

Combine (4.3) and (4.7), and set `s=(t+1)/t`. A source must satisfy

\[
p^r\le (t+1)^H\left(\frac{H!}{H^H}q^{(H-1)/2}\right)^{(t+1)/t}
\tag{4.8}
\]

for every positive integer `t`. Raising to `2t` proves the conditional exclusion `(PACK)` stated in §0.

**What this changes.** The one-ideal lattice bound does not reach the norm factor below two needed by direct `PA_phi`. The same bound can nevertheless exclude a source if `RAD` forces too many inequivalent ideals. This is the reason to use all the source radicals for this alternative, even though the earlier Thaine reduction needed only one component.

\]

so `log q<(3/2)log p`.

The elementary integral bound on factorials is

\log(H!)\le H\log H-H+1+\log H.
\]

Use `t=8`, so `s=9/8`. A sufficient condition for `(PACK)` is positivity of

## 5. Fresh mechanism II: a plus parameter small at both infinity and p

This route aims to build an actual element in the lattice `I`, not to count its possible classes.

### 5.1 PROVED exact reparametrization

Put

\[
t_X=\frac{X}{X^2+1}.
\]

Then

\[
B_a=(X^2+1)(1-s_a t_X),\qquad
z=\prod_a(1-s_a t_X)^{e_a}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

At every archimedean embedding, `|t_X|<1/Y`. At `p`, its valuation is `v_p(X)>=2`. Hence **the same parameter is small at both places**, unlike the separate parameters `X` and `1/X`. It is also invariant under `X -> 1/X`.

There is an exact denominator qualification:

\[
\gcd(X^2+1,\Phi_q(X))=1.
\tag{5.2}
\]

If a prime divided both, it could not divide `X-1` unless it were 2, which is excluded by `Phi_q(1)=q` odd. Thus `X^q=1` and `X^2=-1` in its residue field, forcing an order dividing both the odd prime `q` and four, impossible. The case of the rational prime `q` also gives no exception. So the new rational denominator is coprime to `u`, and is a `p`-adic unit.

Let the formal root be

\[
W(T)=\prod_a(1-s_aT)^{e_a/p},\qquad W(0)=1.
\]

Its coefficients are computed by finite binomial multiplication, with the same explicit factorial-safe powers of `p` as in C26. One can construct a polynomial or Padé pair approximating `W` to high order at `T=0`; evaluating at `t_X` then gives a simultaneous archimedean and `p`-adic approximation.

**The new arithmetic condition is membership in `I`.** Small error at these two places does not impose the required divisibility at primes over `u`, because `t_X` is a unit at those primes and (5.2) introduces no helpful source denominator cancellation.

## 6. Fresh mechanism III: a special rational approximation forced by both allocations

This mechanism avoids ideals entirely. It uses the simultaneous **integer** allocations, not merely a cyclotomic factorization or a local congruence.

### 6.1 PROVED exact approximation

Write `x_0=|x|`, `y_0=|y|`, and `s=sgn(x)=sgn(y)`. Then

\[
x_0^p-y_0^q=s,\quad
x_0-s=p^{q-1}a^q,\quad
y_0+s=q^{p-1}b^p,
\]

where `a=|a0|`, `b=|b0|` are positive integers. They are coprime, because `a|y_0`, `b|x_0`, and `gcd(x_0,y_0)=1`.

Define the positive algebraic number

\[
\rho_{p,q}=\frac{q^{1-1/p}}{p^{1-1/q}}.
\]

The exact relation is

\[
\left(\frac{a/b}{\rho_{p,q}}\right)^{pq}
=\frac{(x_0-s)^p}{(y_0+s)^q}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For `p>=5`, elementary logarithm bounds give

\[
\boxed{0<\left|\rho_{p,q}-\frac ab\right|
<\frac{16\rho_{p,q}}{p q^{p-1} b^p}.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Here are the details for both signs. The absolute logarithm of the ratio on the left of (6.1), divided by `pq`, is at most

\[
\frac1{pq(y_0^q-1)}+\frac1{q(x_0-1)}+
\frac1{p(y_0-1)}<\frac4{p y_0}.
\]

Since `x_0>y_0` and both are integers, `x_0-1>=y_0`; also `y_0>=2`. The last bound is at most `2/5`, so `|e^t-1|<2|t|` follows from the geometric majorant. This gives error less than `8rho/(p y_0)`. Finally `y_0>=q^(p-1)b^p/2`, which proves (6.2). The ratio is not exactly one: for `s=1`, `(x_0-1)^p<(y_0+1)^q`; for `s=-1` the reverse comparison follows from `x_0^p=y_0^q-1` and the positive binomial terms. Thus the error is strictly positive.

The source size also gives the explicit denominator cutoff

\[
b^p>\frac{p^{4q-4}/8-1}{q^{p-1}}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

### 6.2 PROVED: why a generic degree bound is too weak

The degree of `rho_{p,q}` is exactly `pq`. To see this without importing a binomial irreducibility criterion, let its monic minimal polynomial have degree `d`. All its conjugates are among the roots of the binomial equation

\[
p^{p(q-1)}T^{pq}=q^{q(p-1)}
\]

and hence have the same complex absolute value `rho`. The absolute value of its rational nonzero constant term is therefore `rho^d`, a rational number. Taking rational valuations after raising to `pq` shows `p|d` from the exponent of `q`, and `q|d` from the exponent of `p`. Hence `pq|d`, and the binomial itself gives `d<=pq`.

Evaluating this integer binomial at `a/b` and using that its nonzero numerator has absolute value at least one gives an elementary root-separation lower bound proportional to `b^(-pq)`, not `b^(-p)`. Thus the generic algebraic-degree estimate does not contradict (6.2). Taking more decimal digits of the root is not a replacement for a uniform lower bound.

### 6.3 Exact sufficient statement and falsifiable test

One explicit sufficient new statement is:

> **OPEN `IA`:** for every remaining exponent pair, and every pair of positive coprime integers `a,b` satisfying the cutoff (6.3),
> \[
> \left|\rho_{p,q}-a/b\right|\ge\frac1{pq\,b^{p-1}}.
> \tag{6.4}
> \]

This is a proposed sufficient bound, not an established irrationality theorem. It would immediately contradict (6.2). Indeed `rho<q/p`, since `p^p<q^q`, and for `p>=5`, `q>=7`,

\[
\frac{16\rho}{q^{p-2}}<\frac{16}{p q^{p-3}}\le\frac{16}{245}<1\le b.
\]

Thus the right side of (6.4) is strictly larger than the upper bound in (6.2).

A constructive continued-fraction or special binomial Padé argument with this explicit threshold and constant would avoid prime selection. The missing point is the **uniform** bound beyond the cutoff, not calculation of the initial convergents. A generic irrationality result with ineffective constants or exponent `pq` would not suffice.

**TESTED first control.** Exact rational isolating intervals and continued fractions were computed for `(5,7),(5,17),(7,11),(7,13),(11,13),(13,17)`, to denominator `10^40`, with 384-bit rational isolation. There are **432** tested convergents above the source cutoff. None violates (6.4). This is finite evidence only.

The much stronger-looking but tempting exponent-two substitute `|rho-a/b|>=1/(pq b^2)` has **10** certified failures in the same controls. For example at `(5,17)`, the convergent

\[
\frac{13115962982547}{6184365758495}
\]

violates that exponent-two bound and lies above (6.3). Thus a uniform continued-fraction denominator estimate with that naive constant is already falsified. The calculation does not assert that any tested fraction is a Cassels pair or a source.

## 7. Corrections needed before reusing the previous prime-selection map

These corrections do not affect the new `(PACK)` proof, which works with the actual `p`-torsion and a dimension count.

### 7.1 Nonzero p-torsion need not survive modulo p

A nonzero element killed by `p` in a finite abelian `p`-group can have zero image in `A_p/pA_p`: in `Z/p^2`, the element `p` is the elementary example. Consequently C29's nonzero `[A]` does show that the relevant `p`-primary component is nonzero, and therefore that its quotient modulo `p` is nonzero, but **it does not show that this same `[A]` has nonzero image in that quotient**.

Likewise its support contains a prime with nonzero image in the full component, but not necessarily in the quotient used by `QP_phi`. A class-divisibility argument would be needed to obtain the latter from the given support. C31's phrase “the known nonzero `[A]_phi`” in the quotient requires that additional hypothesis and cannot be used automatically. This is recorded separately in [C31_erratum.md](C31_erratum.md), without changing the old files.

### 7.2 The phi-supported residue operator has a simpler criterion than C31 stated

For a fixed split prime, the residue-log map

\[
\epsilon\longmapsto\sum_{\tau\in G}
\operatorname{ind}_\lambda(\tau^{-1}\epsilon)\tau
\]

is `G`-equivariant. For the chosen `epsilon` whose class is supported on `phi`, its image is supported on `phi` as well. A nonzero individual residue logarithm therefore makes the `phi`-coefficient nonzero; in the simple coefficient field it is invertible. The caveat in C31 allowing such a residue to be nonzero but its only supported component to vanish does not apply to this setting.

Multiplication by a nonzero scalar is injective on a vector space over that coefficient field **in every dimension**, not only dimension one. Thus any nonzero product `t_i v_i` already contradicts its individual derivative relation. Combining several zero products cannot create a contradiction. The valid abstract counterexample in C31 still explains why class spanning and a separately nonzero residue somewhere do not ensure a simultaneous nonzero product; it is not a new way around that simultaneous requirement.

The general `QP_phi` statement remains sufficient under the derivative argument. The unsupported inference is that the source's given support automatically supplies its quotient-class condition. The band card's quotient-parenthetical should be read with this correction.

\log x=2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}+R_N,
\quad z=(x-1)/(x+1),\quad
0\le R_N\le\frac{2z^{2N+1}}{(2N+1)(1-z^2)},
\]

after power-of-two range reduction, with rational endpoints. Algebraic embedding signs in the quadratic model are decided by squaring rational numbers with their signs retained. Continued fractions are accepted only while both rational isolating endpoints have the same next partial quotient.

No script creates a Catalan source, proves an unbounded rank bound, proves a general irrationality measure, or verifies the new manuscript in Lean. Initial implementation issues (a time-heavy uncached threshold loop and a 64-bit overflow in the C++ *printed* large-modulus Wieferich control) were corrected; all retained scripts were rerun, and the stored outputs are from the corrected executions.
