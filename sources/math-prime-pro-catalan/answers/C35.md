Choose a primitive root \(g\bmod q\), hence a generator of \(G=\mathbb F_q^\times/\{\pm1\}\). Define
\[
 f(a)=\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}j^{-1}[(a-j)p^{-1}]_q\in\mathbb F_p,\quad
 F(T)=\sum_{i=0}^{H-1}f(g^i)T^i,\quad
 M_H(T)=1+T+\cdots+T^{H-1}.
\]
The brackets are least integer residues modulo \(q\); the weights \(j^{-1}\) are in \(\mathbb F_p\). Put
\[
 d=\deg\gcd(F,M_H),\qquad r=n-d.
 \tag{1.1}
\]
All new rank results assume ALG only unless an extra hypothesis is displayed.

The primitive-root choice needs only finite field algebra: let \(M\) be the lcm of all element orders in \(\mathbb F_q^\times\). By selecting the maximal prime-power parts and multiplying commuting elements of coprime orders, an element of order \(M\) exists. Every nonzero field element is a root of \(X^M-1\), so \(q-1\le M\); multiplication by each \(u\ne0\) permutes the nonzero elements, and multiplying that permutation gives \(u^{q-1}=1\), so \(M\mid q-1\). Thus \(M=q-1\).

Because \(p\nmid H\), \(T^H-1\) is squarefree. Over a finite splitting field, \(r\) counts nontrivial \(H\)-th roots where \(F\) is nonzero; \(d\) counts those where it vanishes, with individual roots counted, not merely irreducible factors.

### 2.2 PROVED: the weakest bound relative to a specified orbit inventory

For every \(m>1\) dividing \(H\), put

 d=\sum_{m\mid H,\ m>1}j_m e_m,\qquad 0\le j_m\le k_m.
 \tag{2.6}

Let \(\mathcal D\) be any explicitly justified set containing the actual deficiency, incorporating whichever proved orbit, activity, and rank constraints have actually been established. Define
\[
 b_*=\min\{u\in\mathcal D:u>A(p,q)\},
 \tag{2.7}

if this set is nonempty. Then the weakest integer upper-bound premise of this form is
\[
 d\le b_*-1.
 \tag{2.8}
\]
Indeed a source must have \(d\in\mathcal D\) and \(d>A\), hence \(d\ge b_*\). If the set in (2.7) is empty, the supplied data already exclude the source. Equivalently, for an independently proved \(d\le U\), C34's rounded bound is

 U_{\rm orb}=\max(\mathcal D\cap[0,U]);

If \(\mathcal D\cap[0,U]\) is empty, the supplied upper bound and inventory are inconsistent; otherwise the maximum in (2.9) is well defined.

## 3. PROVED: what the finite-logarithm recurrence does and does not say

### 3.1 The recurrence and the two different Fourier variables

The defining residues give
\[
 f(-a)=f(a),\qquad
 f(a)-f(a-p)=
 \begin{cases}
 -q/a,&1\le a<p,\\
 0,&\text{other residues }a\bmod q.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{3.1}
\]
For the difference, increasing a bracket by one contributes a common increment whose weighted sum is \(\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}j^{-1}=0\); the one wrap contributes \(-q/a\). For evenness replace \(j\) by \(p-j\) and use the paired least residues. Consequently
\[
 f(a)-f(p-a)=-q/a\ne0\quad(1\le a<p),
 \qquad f(p)=f(0).
 \tag{3.2}
\]
In particular \(f(1)-f(p-1)=-q\), so \(r>0\).

Write
\[
 Q(X)=\sum_{a=0}^{q-1}f(a)X^a,\qquad
 L_p(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}X^j/j,\qquad
 h(X)=\frac{L_p(X)}{1-X^p}.
\]
The recurrence gives
\[
 (1-X^p)Q(X)=-qL_p(X)\pmod{X^q-1}.
 \tag{3.3}
\]
For a primitive \(q\)-th root \(\zeta\) in a finite extension of \(\mathbb F_p\) and a nontrivial even character \(\chi\), define
\(\tau(\chi)=\sum_{a=1}^{q-1}\chi(a)\zeta^a\). Finite orthogonality gives
\[
 \tau(\chi)\tau(\chi^{-1})=q\ne0,
\]
and
\[
 \sum_{a=1}^{q-1}\chi(a)h(\zeta^a)
 =-\frac{2\tau(\chi)}q\,F(\chi(g)^{-1}).
 \tag{3.4}
\]
For the first identity, set the two additive summation variables to \(tb,b\); the inner sum is \(q-1\) at \(t=-1\), and \(-1\) otherwise. For (3.4), substitute \(h(\zeta^a)=-Q(\zeta^a)/q\), discard the constant using \(\sum\chi=0\), and replace the inner argument by \(ab\). These are precisely the finite proofs read in C33 §2.4.

A zero counted by \(d\) is cancellation of the sum in (3.4). It is not a pointwise zero of \(L_p\). The latter has degree \(p-1\), with distinct simple roots 0 and 1, hence at most \(p-3\) zeros among nontrivial \(q\)-th roots. This supplies C33's large additive-circulant rank, not a bound \(d\le p-3\). The variables have different roles: the additive root variable has order \(q\), whereas the character variable has order dividing \(H\). Formula (3.4) is a transform, not a degree-preserving substitution.

### 3.2 The exact polynomial lift and its unavoidable differentiation term

Define
\[
 R_p(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}f(p-j)X^j.
\]
**PROVED, in the polynomial ring before quotienting:**
\[
 \boxed{(1-X^p)Q(X)+qL_p(X)=(1-X^q)R_p(X).}
 \tag{3.5}
\]
At degrees \(0\le j<p\), the left coefficient is \(f(0)=f(p)\) at zero and \(f(j)+q/j=f(p-j)\) otherwise. At \(p\le j<q\) it is zero by (3.1). At \(j=q+i\), \(0\le i<p\), it is \(-f(q+i-p)=-f(p-i)\). This proves every coefficient of (3.5).

Differentiation and evaluation at \(\zeta^q=1\) now give
\[
 \boxed{(1-\zeta^p)Q'(\zeta)
  =-qL_p'(\zeta)-q\zeta^{q-1}R_p(\zeta).}
 \tag{3.6}
\]
The last term cannot be discarded. In fact the naively differentiated congruence fails as an algebra identity for every odd-prime pair \(p<q\): \(R_p\ne0\), since otherwise \(f(1)=f(p-1)=0\), contrary to (3.2). Its degree is less than \(q\), and \(-qX^{q-1}\) is a unit modulo \(X^q-1\).

For the Hasse derivative \(D^{[k]}X^a=\binom akX^{a-k}\), \(1\le k<p\), (3.5) gives
\[
 (1-\zeta^p)D^{[k]}Q(\zeta)
 =-qD^{[k]}L_p(\zeta)
 -\sum_{j=1}^k\binom qj\zeta^{q-j}
                      D^{[k-j]}R_p(\zeta).
 \tag{3.7}
\]
The derivatives of \(X^p\) in these orders vanish in characteristic \(p\); those of \(X^q\) generally do not.

There is a short algebraic reason. Any \(\mathbb F_p\)-derivation of
\(\mathbb F_p[X]/(X^q-1)\) must satisfy
\[
 0=D(x^q)=q x^{q-1}D(x),
\]
so \(D(x)=0\), and the derivation is zero. The same argument works for \(T^H-1\) under ALG. Thus the ordinary polynomial derivative does not descend to the quotient. A valid auxiliary-polynomial or Wronskian argument must retain a lift and control its error terms. This observation rules out that particular dropped-term argument; it does not rule out a correctly constructed Stepanov method.

**TESTED exact control:** for \((p,q)=(5,17)\), \(R_p\) has ascending coefficients \([4,0,0,4,3]\). The false differentiated left side has remainder
\[
 2X^2+4X^3+2X^{16}\quad\text{over }\mathbb F_5.
\]
The companion recurrence checks verify (3.5)–(3.7) independently against the defining sum.

### 3.3 The Bernoulli form and the limited use of Wieferich

For completeness, the finite proof read in solRank gives, after writing \(a=j+pu\),

 =\frac1q\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\omega(j)^{-1}
                    \sum_{u=0}^{q-1}u\chi(j+pu).
\]
The term containing \(j\) cancels before dividing by \(p\), because
\(\sum_u\chi(j+pu)=0\). Reduction therefore legitimately yields
\[
 qB_{1,\chi\omega^{-1}}\equiv2F(\chi(g)).
 \tag{3.8}
\]
This identifies the same zeros; it does not bound their number.

The finite product calculation in C33 gives

## 4. PROVED: new period restrictions and the resulting finite rank bound

### 4.1 Exact harmonic value count

Put \(h_0=0\), \(h_s=\sum_{j=1}^s j^{-1}\) in \(\mathbb F_p\). For the least representative \(0\le a<q\),
\[
 f(a)=f(0)+h_{[-aq^{-1}]_p}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]
Here \(q^{-1}\) is taken modulo \(p\). To prove this, traverse \(a=[kp]_q\). The nonzero jumps occur at
\[
 k=\lceil \ell q/p\rceil,\quad 1\le\ell<p.
\]
Their least residue is \(j=p\lceil\ell q/p\rceil-\ell q\in[1,p-1]\), and their weight is
\(-q/j=1/\ell\pmod p\). By time \(k\), exactly \(\lfloor kp/q\rfloor\) jumps have occurred. Writing \(kp=a+\ell q\) gives \(\ell=[-aq^{-1}]_p\), proving (4.1).

Every residue modulo \(p\) occurs among \(1,\ldots,q-1\). Evenness identifies sign pairs without losing values. Thus the coefficient sequence \(f(g^i)\) has exactly
\[
 V_p=\#\{h_0,\ldots,h_{p-1}\}
 \tag{4.2}
\]
distinct values. This is an exact integer computable in \(O(p)\) field operations, not an unproved lower estimate.

The relation \(h_{p-1-s}=h_s\) gives \(V_p\le(p+1)/2\). Also the differences in (3.2) run through all nonzero elements of \(\mathbb F_p\); hence \(V_p(V_p-1)\ge p-1\). This recovers C33's universal floor \(s_p\), while exact evaluation can be much stronger.

### 4.2 Least period and all recurrence jumps

Let \(\mathcal S\) be the set of exact orders of the nonzero nontrivial character components of \(F\), and put
\[
 t=\operatorname{lcm}(\mathcal S).
\]
**PROVED:** \(t\) is the least period of \(i\mapsto f(g^i)\). Fourier inversion is valid because \(H\) is invertible. A shift by \(u\) fixes the sequence exactly when \(\xi^u=1\) for each nonzero Fourier coefficient, equivalently when every order in \(\mathcal S\) divides \(u\). Consequently
\[
 t\mid H,\qquad t\ge V_p.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

Define the subgroup of argument multipliers
\[
 K_t=\{c\in\mathbb F_q^\times:c^{(q-1)/t}=1\}.
\]
It has size \((q-1)/t=2H/t\), contains \(-1\), and is generated by \(g^t\). Periodicity says \(f(ca)=f(a)\) for \(c\in K_t\) and \(a\ne0\).

Every jump in (3.2) therefore implies
\[
 \boxed{(p-a)^{(q-1)/t}\ne a^{(q-1)/t}\pmod q
             \quad(1\le a<p).}
 \tag{4.4}
\]
Otherwise \(a\) and \(p-a\) belong to the same \(K_t\)-coset, so their \(f\)-values agree. This uses all the jumps, extending C33's \(a=1\) witness.

**PROVED equivalent finite algorithm:** enumerate \(c\in K_t\setminus\{-1\}\). Exclude \(t\) exactly when
\[
 [p/(1+c)]_q\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\}.
 \tag{4.5}
\]
Indeed \((p-a)/a=c\) is equivalent to \((1+c)a=p\bmod q\); \(c=-1\) gives the impossible \(p=0\bmod q\). Since only \(t\ge V_p\) need be considered, this enumeration uses at most \((q-1)/V_p\) subgroup elements per period.

### 4.3 A stronger restriction: injective ordered jump pairs

**PROVED:** for the actual period \(t\), the map
\[
 \boxed{a\longmapsto(aK_t,(p-a)K_t),\quad 1\le a<p,}
 \tag{4.6}
\]
is injective.

If \(a,b\) give the same ordered pair, invariance of \(f\) and (3.2) give
\[
 -q/a=f(a)-f(p-a)=f(b)-f(p-b)=-q/b
 \quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p.
\]
Since \(q\ne0\) in that field and \(a,b\in[1,p-1]\), this forces \(a=b\).

**PROVED equivalent subgroup test:** enumerate distinct \(c,d\in K_t\), and set
\[
 b=[p(1-d)/(c-d)]_q,\qquad a=[cb]_q.
 \tag{4.7}
\]
Exclude \(t\) if \(1\le a,b<p\) and \(a\ne b\). Every collision in (4.6) gives
\[
 a=cb,\qquad p-a=d(p-b),
\]
whose elimination is (4.7). If \(c=d\), then \(p(1-c)=0\bmod q\), so \(c=d=1\) and \(a=b\); omitting this case is justified.

Injectivity subsumes (4.4): a same-coset pair at \(a\) would make the distinct indices \(a\) and \(p-a\) have the same ordered pair. They are distinct because \(p\) is odd. It also shows why the actual jump weights, not only nonzero differences, matter.

One can formulate a still stronger necessary condition by assigning each coset a potential and requiring its differences along every jump edge to be \(-q/a\). Every signed cycle must then have zero weight. This is a valid finite test, but **no coverage number in this answer uses an unimplemented cycle test**.

### 4.4 Rank theorem with an exact finite admissibility set

Let \(\mathcal A_I\) be the divisors \(t\mid H\) satisfying \(t\ge V_p\) and the injectivity test (4.6). For \(e_m,k_m\) as in (2.5), define
\[
 L_I(p,q)=
 \min_{\substack{\varnothing\ne\mathcal S\subseteq\{m>1:m\mid H\}\\
                 \operatorname{lcm}(\mathcal S)\in\mathcal A_I}}
           \sum_{m\in\mathcal S}e_m.
 \tag{4.8}
\]
**PROVED under ALG:**
\[
 r\ge L_I(p,q).
 \tag{4.9}
\]
The actual active-order set is feasible by §§4.1–4.3. Every active order contributes at least one full Frobenius orbit of size \(e_m\); different orders are disjoint. This proves (4.9).

For exact rounding, retain the complete inventory
\[
 \mathcal R_I(P)=
 \left\{
 \sum_m u_m e_m:
 \begin{array}{l}
 0\le u_m\le k_m,\quad \sum_m u_m e_m\ge P,\\
 \operatorname{lcm}\{m:u_m>0\}\in\mathcal A_I
 \end{array}\right\},
 \tag{4.10}
\]
where \(P\) is an independently proved pole floor. The actual rank belongs to this set. Its minimum, when combined with (2.3) or (2.4), is a proved finite source-exclusion criterion.

The finite optimizer tracks divisor periods. Begin with period 1 and cost 0; for each order \(m\), omit it or replace period \(t\) by \(\operatorname{lcm}(t,m)\) and add \(e_m\). Retain the least cost per state. The earlier C33 versions also retain a Boolean witness: some active \(m\) must satisfy
\[
 (p-1)^{H/m}\notin\{1,-1\}\pmod q.
 \tag{4.11}
\]
This is exactly C33's \(m\nmid b\) with \(g^b=\pm(p-1)\), without computing a discrete logarithm. The full period tests already imply the witness, but retaining it in all scan columns makes their comparison explicit.

Component-dependent activity data \(W_m\) from C33/C34 may be added when actually computed. They are not presumed nonzero and are not part of the complete large-row scans here.

### 4.5 New harmonic-period prime-power closures

**PROVED general order consequence:** if \(\ell^a\) is the exact power of a prime \(\ell\) dividing \(H\), and
\[
 V_p>H/\ell,
 \tag{4.12}
\]
then
\[
 r\ge\operatorname{ord}_{\ell^a}(p).
 \tag{4.13}
\]
If no active order were divisible by \(\ell^a\), their lcm would divide \(H/\ell\), contradicting (4.3). An active order \(m\) divisible by \(\ell^a\) has
\(\operatorname{ord}_{\ell^a}(p)\mid e_m\), proving (4.13). Bounds from different primes must not simply be added: one orbit can serve more than one such requirement. The divisor optimizer handles that overlap.

**PROVED prime-power corollary:** assume (VPP), namely
\[
 H=\ell^a,\quad \operatorname{ord}_H(p)=\varphi(H),
 \quad V_p>H/\ell.
\]
Then a primitive-\(H\) character must be active, and all primitive-\(H\) roots form one Frobenius orbit. Therefore
\[
 r\ge\varphi(H)=H-H/\ell,\qquad
 d\le H/\ell-1.
 \tag{4.14}
\]
For odd \(\ell\ge5\), \(d<H/4\), so COUNT34 excludes a source throughout BAND. For \(\ell=3\), \(r\ge2n/3\), and (2.4) excludes
\(q\le(13/2)p^{4/3}\). No infinitude of primes of these shapes is asserted.

## 5. PROVED: the strengthened pole floor and why it remains small

### 5.1 Retain the constant function

Put \(S(m)=\sum_{j=1}^m\varphi(j)\), \(1\le m<p\). Factor
\[
 L_p(X)=(1-X)B_p(X),\qquad B_p(1)=1,
\]
using \(L_p(1)=0\) and \(L_p'(1)=-1\). Then
\[
 h_a(X)=\frac{L_p(X^a)}{1-X^{pa}}
       =\frac{B_p(X^a)}{(1-X^a)^{p-1}}.
\]

The rational functions \(1,h_1,\ldots,h_m\) are independent. In a nonzero relation, take the largest \(a\) with nonzero \(h_a\) coefficient. At a primitive \(a\)-th root, that function has a pole of exact order \(p-1\); no smaller index and no constant term has a pole there. Eliminate all such coefficients, then the constant coefficient.

Their common denominator can be chosen as
\[
 D_m(X)=\left(\operatorname{lcm}_{1\le a\le m}(1-X^a)\right)^{p-1},
 \qquad \deg D_m=(p-1)S(m).
\]
Because \(m<p\), roots of each factor are simple, and the roots of exact order \(j\) contribute \(\varphi(j)\) to the lcm degree.

All these denominators are units modulo \(\Phi_q(X)\), since \(a<q\). A relation modulo \(\Phi_q\), after clearing denominators, has numerator degree at most \(\deg D_m\). If
\[
 (p-1)S(m)<q-1,
\]
it must be the zero polynomial. Thus \(1,h_1,\ldots,h_m\) remain independent in the whole algebra \(\mathbb F_p[X]/\Phi_q\).

The multiplicative orbit of \(h_1\), together with 1, has dimension \(r+1\): the even basis \(X^a+X^{-a}\) is independent, and (3.3) identifies its nontrivial character support with that of \(F\), up to a nonzero scalar. Adding 1 supplies the trivial line. Hence
\[
 m+1\le r+1,\quad\text{so }r\ge m.
\]
This proves (P+). Under forward DW, the trivial component vanishes; omitting 1 makes the cleared numerators proper, so \((p-1)S(m)\le q-1\) suffices for \(r\ge m\), as already proved in C33.

### 5.2 Exact scope

The constant-adjoining argument is in solRank §4.2 and the lcm denominator in C33 §4; their combination is the improvement. It is not a new fixed-fraction nonvanishing theorem. Even the elementary bound \(S(m)\ge m\) shows that the certified \(m\) is at most \((q-1)/(p-1)\), far below a fixed fraction of \(H\) for large \(p\).

For transparent comparison with C34, the coverage scan retains C33's original pole floor
\[
 P_{\rm old}=\max(1,m_*-1),\qquad
 m_*=\max\{m<p:(p-1)S(m)\le q-1\}.
 \tag{5.1}
\]
Thus its percentages do not silently include an unreported new premise or a different pole convention. The stronger floor (P+) is proved and separately tested, and is used in the obstruction example below.

## 6. PROVED: an outer-band obstruction to closing the gap with these data

### 6.1 Frobenius inventories can have no rounding gaps

Suppose
\[
 q=2u(p-1)+1,\qquad u\mid p+1,
\]
with \(p,q\) prime. Then \(H=u(p-1)\mid p^2-1\). Every nontrivial \(H\)-th root therefore has Frobenius degree one or two. Exactly
\[
 p-2
\]
of them are fixed, since \(\gcd(H,p-1)-1=p-2\). The remaining orbits have size two, giving
\[
 \frac{(u-1)(p-1)}2
\]
degree-two orbits.

Their possible deficiency sums fill every integer \(0,\ldots,H-1\): the available unit weights bridge the parity of the two-weights, and adjacent intervals overlap. Consequently raw orbit inventory gives no improvement to any integer upper bound at these pairs. This is a proved statement about inventory; which orbits the actual \(F\) kills remains arithmetic.

### 6.2 A certified actual pair and its exact required rank

Take
\[
 p=100469,\quad q=20495473,\quad
 H=10247736=102(p-1),\quad u=102.
 \tag{6.1}
\]
Trial division certifies both primalities. Since \(102\mid100470=p+1\), the preceding inventory applies. The strict integer test
\[
 12(q-2p)^2<5p^3
\]
certifies BAND; \(q>2p\) justifies squaring. ALG is immediate from \(H=102(p-1)\) and \(p>102\).

The inventory consists of
\[
 100467\text{ degree-one orbits},\qquad
 5073634\text{ degree-two orbits}.
 \tag{6.2}
\]
The original witness is already detectable in degree one:
\[
 (p-1)^{102}\equiv3958347\pmod q\ne\pm1.
\]
The strengthened pole floor is 25, because \(S(25)=200\), \(S(26)=212\), and
\[
 (p-1)S(25)<q-1<(p-1)S(26).
\]

Rational logarithm enclosures, with explicit finite-series remainder bounds, certify
\[
 \frac{35161364527332}{10^{14}}
 <\frac{A(p,q)}H<
 \frac{35161364527333}{10^{14}},
 \qquad \lfloor A(p,q)\rfloor=3603243.
 \tag{6.3}
\]
Thus (2.3) would follow from
\[
 d\le3603243,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad r\ge6644492.
 \tag{6.4}
\]

### 6.3 Even all the new period data permit rank 25

This is a precise limitation of the proved relaxation, not a guess about the actual rank.

At (6.1), \(e_H=2\). Select one primitive-\(H\) orbit, contributing rank 2, and 23 distinct nontrivial degree-one orbits. There are more than enough by (6.2). The resulting formal inventory has rank 25 and least active-order lcm exactly \(H\).

For period \(H\), \(K_H=\{1,-1\}\). Every jump restriction holds. The ordered-pair restriction also holds: here \(q>2p\), so distinct integers \(a,b\in[1,p-1]\) cannot even have \(a=\pm b\bmod q\). The harmonic floor is satisfied because
\(V_p\le(p+1)/2<H\). The order witness is satisfied by the active primitive-\(H\) orbit, and the improved pole floor is attained.

Therefore the minimum permitted rank after **these** orbit, value-period, jump, injectivity, witness, and improved-pole conditions is exactly 25. It is not merely unknown whether their minimum is large. This proves that these conditions alone do not close every BAND pair.

This formal inventory is not claimed to be the spectrum of the special \(F_{p,q}\). In particular it does not impose its actual component activity \(W_m\), its exact coefficients, or all source equations. No actual rank at (6.1) is reported. A theorem exploiting those additional constraints may still close the gap.

### 7.3 Why the scan is exhaustive without forming every \(F\)

For each \(H\), the factorization gives a proved exponent multiple \(\lambda\) of \(\operatorname{ord}_H(p)\). For odd prime powers use \(\ell^{a-1}(\ell-1)\). For \(2^a\), \(a\ge3\), use \(2^{a-2}\): an odd square is 1 modulo 8, and repeated squaring raises the guaranteed power of 2 by one. Combine prime-power multiples by lcm.

The active-order set \(\{H\}\) passes the value floor, witness, and both period filters. Its period is \(H\), so its stabilizer is \(\{\pm1\}\). To check injectivity even when \(q<2p\), use (4.7): for unequal signs \(c,d\in\{1,-1\}\), the resulting \(b\) is 0 or \(p\), outside the allowed interval; equal signs cannot give a distinct pair by the proof of §4.3. Thus no forbidden collision occurs. With
\[
 e=\operatorname{ord}_H(p),\qquad
 e\left\lceil P_{\rm old}/e\right\rceil
\]
primitive-\(H\) coordinates (that is, \(\lceil P_{\rm old}/e\rceil\) whole orbits), it also meets the pole floor. The code verifies enough primitive orbits are available. This possible rank is at most \(\lambda+P_{\rm old}-1\).

If that upper candidate cannot pass either source certificate, the pair cannot be certified by the minimum-rank relaxation and may be skipped. Otherwise the exact order and, when necessary, the divisor-state optimization are computed. The scan requires only 967 and 969 composite-\(H\) divisor optimizations on the two selected rows.

Where a minimum one-orbit cost lies below the pole floor, the implementation fills further available orbits and obtains an explicit compatible inventory above the lower bound. Adding orders enlarges the period by divisibility; its stabilizer shrinks. Both period tests are preserved under this enlargement, as is the value floor. If the lower bound and the explicit compatible inventory give different closure decisions, the program aborts instead of silently accepting an approximate rounding. There were **no such unresolved cases**.

This lower/upper certification proves the exact closure counts for the declared relaxation, including orbit availability. It does not infer that its artificial minimizing inventory is the actual spectrum.

### 7.4 Certified logarithmic comparisons

All pass/fail decisions are integer computations. To enclose \(\log x\), first write \(x=2^k y\), \(1\le y<2\), and put \(z=(y-1)/(y+1)\), \(0\le z<1/3\). For \(N\ge1\),
\[
 2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}
 \le\log y\le
 2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}
 +\frac{2z^{2N+1}}{(2N+1)(1-z^2)}.
 \tag{7.2}
\]
This follows from the finite geometric identity and its positive remainder; it can equally be proved by differentiating the finite expressions and checking the common endpoint at zero. Thus only finite sums and elementary real calculus are used.

The C++ scan uses scale \(2^{58}\), 24 terms, and outward rounding at every multiplication and division. Its rational upper bound for the tail is verified by an integer inequality. It tests the signs of
\[
 2R\log p+n\log(13/2)-n\log q
\]
and
\[
 20R\log p-10n\log q+19H.
\]
An interval straddling zero causes an abort. There were no ambiguous decisions. Floating point is used only to print percentages after the exact counts have been established.

## 9. Exact remaining statement and updated closure map

### 9.1 The still-missing bound

**CONJECTURED / OPEN — QTR35:**
\[
 \deg\gcd(F_{p,q},M_H)\le H/4
\]
for every ALG pair in BAND. C35 proves neither QTR35 nor a weaker theorem sufficient on every pair.

A weaker target sufficient for the retained C34 inequality is:

**CONJECTURED / OPEN — PAIR35:**
\[
 \deg\gcd(F_{p,q},M_H)\le\lfloor A(p,q)\rfloor
 \tag{9.1}
\]
for every remaining source-compatible pair in BAND. An independently proved upper bound that satisfies the exact inventory condition (2.8) would also suffice. These are sufficient statements, not necessary conditions for Catalan's theorem.

The still weaker full prime-free route remains C34's PACK-SPLIT inequality for some positive rational \(\delta\) and integer \(J\), with the actual rank. No proof that every remaining pair passes that optimized count is supplied here.

The present gap is explicit: period information controls which orders must occur. A nonzero order-\(m\) component supplies one orbit of size \(e_m\); it does not supply most of the \(k_m\) available orbits of that order. The outer fixture proves that even exact full-period information can coexist, at the inventory level, with a rank far below the required threshold. A successful next argument must restrict the actual missing-orbit weight or its coefficients, or use genuinely additional source arithmetic.
