## 2. Route (a): what explicit Gauss/Jacobi sums actually give

### 2.1 The ordinary operator is an exact tautology on this radical

Let distinct odd primes p<q be given, N=pq, L=Q(ζ_N), F=Q(ζ_q), H=(q−1)/2. Let α∈F^+ have real norm N_{F^+/Q}(α)=1. For 1≤c<N with (c,N)=1 define the ordinary integer operator

Θ_c = Σ_{1≤b<N,(b,N)=1} floor(cb/N) σ_b^{-1}.

**PROVED.** Its restriction to F^+ is exactly

Θ_c|_{F^+} = (p−1)(c−1) N_+,

where N_+=Σ_{a∈(Z/q)^*/±1}σ_a. In particular α^{Θ_c}=1.

Proof. Pair b with N−b. Because cb/N is not an integer,

floor(cb/N)+floor(c(N−b)/N)=c−1.

The restrictions of σ_b^{-1} and σ_{N−b}^{-1} to F^+ agree. For each fixed inverse real class modulo q there are exactly p−1 such pairs, by CRT. This proves the equality of integer operators, before any reduction modulo p. Taking the norm of α proves the last assertion. For the C26 element α=B_g/B_1, norm one follows by telescoping, not from a hypothesis about a class number.

Thus a proof giving an ordinary Θ_c-root of this particular α gives only the already explicit root 1. It has not produced a root for Xi_c. The reflected operator changes both the Galois direction and the coefficient by b^{-1} mod p. On a pair b,N−b its coefficient becomes, modulo p,

b^{-1}(2 floor(cb/N)−c+1),

which is not constant in general. The ordinary pairing of floors cannot be reused after the twist.

### 2.2 Exact Gauss/Jacobi generators at completely split primes

The following is a finite constructive version of the ordinary factorization at one split prime. Its scope is explicit; no assertion that arbitrary ideal classes contain degree-one primes is used.

#### Named hypotheses JAC

Let n>1 be odd, let ℓ be a prime with ℓ≡1 mod n, set d=(ℓ−1)/n, and choose a multiplicative character χ:F_ℓ^*→μ_n of exact order n. Put χ(0)=0, choose a primitive ℓth root ζ_ℓ, and define

G(χ^j)=Σ_{t∈F_ℓ^*} χ(t)^j ζ_ℓ^t,

J(χ^j,χ)=Σ_{t∈F_ℓ} χ(t)^j χ(1−t).

For 1≤c<n with (c,n)=1 put

J_c(χ)=∏_{j=1}^{c−1}J(χ^j,χ),

with empty product 1. These are explicit finite sums and products.

#### 2.2.1 Exact identities

**PROVED.** For 1≤j<c,

G(χ^j)G(χ)=J(χ^j,χ)G(χ^{j+1}),

and consequently

J_c(χ)=G(χ)^c/G(χ^c) ∈ Z[ζ_n].

Proof. In the product of Gauss sums group terms by s=u+v. When s=0 the sum is zero because χ^{j+1} is nontrivial. For s≠0 substitute u=st, v=s(1−t). The result factors into the displayed Jacobi and Gauss sums. Telescoping gives the ratio identity. Every Jacobi sum is visibly in Z[ζ_n]. No denominator integrality has been inferred from ideal factorization.

Also

G(χ)G(χ^{-1})=χ(−1)ℓ=ℓ.

Indeed, substitute u=tv in the product. The inner sum over v≠0 is ℓ−1 when t=−1 and −1 otherwise; Σ_tχ(t)=0. Since n is odd, χ(−1)=1. Complex conjugation gives |G(χ^b)|²=ℓ for every unit b mod n. Thus every conjugate of J_c has absolute value ℓ^{(c−1)/2}.

For an automorphism τ_s fixing μ_n and sending ζ_ℓ to ζ_ℓ^s, substitution t↦s^{-1}t gives

τ_sG(χ^j)=χ(s)^{-j}G(χ^j).

The factors cancel in G(χ)^c/G(χ^c). This gives an independent finite proof of descent from Q(ζ_n,ζ_ℓ) to Q(ζ_n). It is ordinary Galois covariance; it does not apply the reflected involution to an arbitrary radical.

v_λ(G(χ^b))=bd.

**Finite Kummer lemma — PROVED.** If a field has characteristic zero, contains `mu_p`, and `z_1,...,z_r` have independent classes modulo p-th powers, then adjoining their p-th roots has degree `p^r`, and its Galois group consists of all independent substitutions `z_i^(1/p) -> zeta_p^(t_i) z_i^(1/p)`.

Indeed, a proper monic factor has constant term `±beta^m zeta_p^j`, `0<m<p`, where `beta^p=z`; it follows that `beta^m` belongs to the field, and Bézout for `m,p` gives `beta` in the field. Thus `[z_r]` is a linear combination of the previous classes, contradiction. The next polynomial is irreducible; all independent substitutions extend and give the stated group. This proves the lemma without any reciprocity theorem.

## 4. Route (b) — PROVED: exact residue adjoint identity; two distinct missing global steps

### 6.1 Named hypotheses FORM

### 6.2 Finite root-kernel lemma

**PROVED.** If a characteristic-zero field A contains μ_p and M=A(s_1,...,s_m), s_i^p=f_i∈A^*, then an element a∈A^* becomes a pth power in M only if its class in A^*/A^{*p} belongs to the F_p-span of [f_i].

Proof. M/A is finite Galois, since it splits the defining binomials; its group embeds in μ_p^m by h↦(h(s_i)/s_i). If W^p=a, then h(W)/W is in μ_p and is a character of this group. The monomials ∏s_i^{j_i}, 0≤j_i<p, span M over A and are themselves character eigenvectors. Apply the finite character projector for the character of W to such a spanning expression for W. At least one remaining monomial m has the same character. Then W/m is fixed by every automorphism, so belongs to A. Raising to p proves a=(W/m)^p∏f_i^{j_i}. Conversely any such class clearly becomes a pth power. This proof does not assume an Artin map or a global Kummer pairing with ideals.

### 6.3 Branch valuations prove the theorem

Suppose P_e were a pth power in M. Apply the lemma and then take the discrete valuations at the q distinct linear factors t−η^a. The base-field radicands defining M have the following valuation vectors modulo p:

- r_0^p: the coordinate vector supported at a=0;
- r_N^p: the vector equal to one at all a≠0 and zero at 0;
- r_a^p: +1 at a and −1 at −a, zero elsewhere.

The target vector is zero at 0 and has equal entries e_a at a and −a. Its coefficient of the first defining vector must therefore be zero. Write its remaining hypothetical representation as a constant z on all nonzero coordinates plus an odd vector. Equality between the entries at a and −a forces every odd coefficient to vanish, since 2 is invertible modulo p. Thus all e_a=z. The augmentation condition says Hz=0. Since p∤H, z=0, contradicting e≠0.

Now suppose P_e=W^p in M'. Each root adjunction has degree 1 or q because μ_q⊂M, so n=[M':M] is a q-power. Choose integers u,v with un+vp=1. Taking the norm gives

(N_{M'/M}W)^p=P_e^n,

and hence

P_e=[(N_{M'/M}W)^u P_e^v]^p

already in M, a contradiction. This proves the theorem.

The same proof treats every finite set of algebraic constants, so no infinite algebraic-closure construction is necessary. An expression using finitely many operations uses only finitely many constants and roots.

The exact real input in that convention is the intermediate value property for real polynomials, not a class-group or analytic-class-number assumption. [TWO][TWO] [P32][P32]

Combining the filed sectors through smaller prime `251`, the finite two_place corollary, and p32 leaves, for an odd-prime counterexample,
\[
\boxed{p>100000,\qquad p<q<B(p),\qquad
B(p)=\sqrt{5/12}\,p^{3/2}+2p.}
\tag{9.1}
\]
Some of (9.1) is additionally excluded by the exact sparse certificates. Without a uniform rank/coverage theorem, an informal `c_*p` sketch must not replace this rigorously stated remainder.

**PROVED — all-BAND SR would bypass `(2L)`.** For `p>=5`,
\[
12(p-2)^2-5p=12(p-5)^2+67(p-5)+83>0.
\]
Because `p-2>0`, this gives
\[
\sqrt{5p/12}+2<p,\qquad B(p)<p^2.
\tag{9.2}
\]
Every source in (9.1) is therefore in C26's SR band. A proof of (SR), or ENTRY, throughout `p<q<p^2` would close this remainder using the filed inputs, without needing a universal `(2L)` theorem or further T4 cutoff interpolation. Even the coarser Theorem A suffices outside BAND once `p>=257`, since `(3/2)p^(3/2)<p^2` when `4p>9`.

This is a conditional coverage implication, not a proof of its missing premise. If a new bridge works only in the narrower fixed-ratio interval, the rest of (9.1) must still be covered by a proved certificate; in that strategy the rank and quantitative-coverage obligations remain.

### 9.3 The uniform `(2L)` obligation is specific and still open

**TESTED in the filed report, not rerun here.** [sol2L][TWO_LIFT] certifies every phase at all `63,649` eligible pairs in its combined manifest. The raw union has `63,676` pairs: of the `27` small cases outside the intended independent-prefix family, `11` have formal capacity certificates and `16` have explicit dependent-prefix counterexamples. These are exact stored certificates, not random-phase experiments and not counterexamples to Catalan-source `(2L)`.

**PROVED in the filed finite argument.** With `M=2^r` and the two collision energies,
\[
9M-E_1-2E_2>6q(m-1)
\]
is sufficient. But when `ord_p(q)=4`, one component's odd first slopes occupy a two-dimensional `F_q` space. Its total two-lift capacity is at most `2q^2`. A phase aligned with a nonzero first-slope functional gives at most `2q` capacity in each fiber. For the current fixed-ratio parameters, `m/q~r/(16c^2)` with unbounded `r`, so a one-component phase-free estimate cannot prove the required uniform assertion. This is the corrected single-component obstruction, not a refutation of the all-component statement. [TWO_LIFT][TWO_LIFT] [C25][C25]

A successful uniform theorem must exploit simultaneous information across components, or prove initial jet rank by another mechanism. C25's order lifting does not by itself supply either. The numerical evidence is strong finite evidence for the intended instances; it is not an asymptotic proof.

The noncircular dependency chain for the present broad SR strategy is:

1. Cassels allocations from the source.
2. The filed ordinary half-power construction from its scalar cyclotomic allocation.
3. DW and the strong divisibilities from that construction.
4. The actual ordinary root and local/denominator ledger, giving C26's size bound.
5. C26's finite truncation, giving the independent primary radicals.
6. The CRT calculation, giving the specified reflected non-power.
7. The still missing elementary SR/ENTRY or substitute contradiction.

The [half-power card][HALF] is explicitly a finite ordinary Jacobi construction; it is not supposed to assume DW, source size, or reflected annihilation. A proposed proof of `HHalf` that used the missing reflected identity would make this chain circular. No such circularity is established by this audit, but these interfaces must remain visible. The written ordinary phase argument absorbs a root of unity in its own construction; it does not silently absorb an arbitrary real-unit obstruction in a reflected construction.

Finally, an arbitrary Catalan equation with exponents greater than one reduces by prime divisors of those exponents. Equal prime exponents are handled by difference-of-powers factorization. The exponent-two/three and small odd-prime sectors cover their stated cases; the signs can then be oriented to `p<q` with difference `±1`. The [p32 corollary][P32] explicitly lists this assembly with the exceptional solution retained. This final integration is part of a complete proof, although it is not the new difficulty in C27.
