## 2. Corrected ideal-element factorization — PROVED
For a nonzero integral ideal a and α∈a\{0}, inclusion (α)⊆a gives an integral ideal b=(α)a^(−1), so (α)=ab and [b]=−[a]. Factor b=∏ λ_i^{e_i}; then Σ e_i[λ_i]=−[a]. Thus a nonzero φ-image of [a] implies at least one λ_i has nonzero φ-image **provided the projection is defined on the relevant p-primary quotient**. The class of an arbitrary prime λ_i may have prime-to-p torsion; first project to the p-primary class subgroup, then reduce modulo p. The formula holds in that quotient.

The elementary box construction bounds a *nonzero* α∈a, not its residue or the splitting of factors of b. With ξ=ζ_q+ζ_q^(−1), use the H-dimensional integer basis 1,ξ,…,ξ^(H−1). The lattice of a has index N(a). Among (⌈N(a)^(1/H)⌉+1)^H coefficient vectors in a box, two coincide modulo a. Their difference gives α∈a with coefficients |b_i|≤⌈N(a)^(1/H)⌉. Each conjugate |σ(ξ)|≤2, hence
 |N(α)|≤[⌈N(a)^(1/H)⌉(2^H−1)]^H.
Every rational prime dividing b therefore has norm at most |N(α)|/N(a). This bound has no claim of degree one or congruence 1 mod pq. A prime of degree f>1 has norm ℓ^f and can occur.

**Caution:** imposing α≡α₀ modulo an ideal M via a sublattice works only when the congruence class meets a. One can bound the first solution using a larger lattice, but congruences on α do not constrain every prime divisor of b. If α is coprime to M, the congruence merely *excludes* primes over M. It does not force new prime divisors to split in L, nor force the value of ε's p-residue at them. The same obstruction applies to polynomial values and Schur's construction: a root modulo ℓ supplies a degree-one factor for the chosen polynomial, not a prescribed class of a prime of k or a nonzero ε-residue.

## 3. The finite mixed-prime criterion — PROVED
Let Q_φ be the φ-part of the p-primary ideal-class quotient modulo p, an F_p-vector space of dimension d≥1. For a finite list of split primes λ_i over ℓ_i≡1(mod pq), put v_i=[λ_i]_φ∈Q_φ and let h_i∈F_p be the discrete-log exponent of (ε/λ_i)_p, using a compatible primitive pth root. The C29 derivative at λ_i has a group-ring coefficient Θ_i whose φ-component is h_i times a *unit* in the φ-field only if h_i≠0 (the Galois orbit of the residue may be packaged as a vector; scalar h_i is not in general sufficient to assert that Θ_i is a unit). For a fixed φ, the exact usable coefficient is t_i=e_φΘ_i∈F_p[T]/(φ), computable by residue logs at all conjugate primes.

The derivative relations are t_i v_i=0 in Q_φ (under the C29 derivative hypotheses). For coefficients a_i∈F_p, summing gives Σ a_i t_i v_i=0. A contradiction with a *known* nonzero w∈Q_φ requires a certified identity w=Σ a_i t_i v_i. Equivalently, w must lie in the column span of the vectors t_i v_i. If the derivative identities are available for every listed prime, the strongest conclusion is span{t_i v_i}=0; to refute a nonzero w, one needs w in this span. This is the exact finite linear-algebra criterion. Merely having span{v_i}=Q_φ and a nonzero vector (h_i) does not suffice: h may be supported only on indices with v_i=0, or the component coefficients t_i may vanish even when a local residue is nontrivial.

For a one-dimensional φ-component over its residue field, the criterion reduces to **some i with v_i≠0 and t_i≠0**. In higher dimension, a full-rank matrix of columns t_i v_i suffices. In particular a linear combination of primes cannot manufacture a missing nonzero product if every t_i v_i is zero. The derivative at each prime must be constructed separately; the conductor of a product is not a substitute for those separate local ramification calculations.

## 4. Explicit finite obstruction to naive selection — PROVED as a model
Over F_5 take Q=F_5², v₁=(1,0), v₂=(0,1), v₃=(0,0) and h=(0,0,1). The v_i span Q and h≠0, but every h_i v_i=0. This is a finite linear counterexample to the assertion that class spanning plus some nonzero residue is enough. It is **not** an arithmetic counterexample to QP_φ or an actual source.

A second model shows that congruence control on an ideal element does not control the residue at its prime factors: for integer n≡1(mod 5), n=6 has prime factors 2 and 3, neither ≡1(mod 5); n=11 has a prime factor ≡1(mod 5). Thus the same imposed congruence on n allows incompatible factor types. The ideal case has additional splitting possibilities, not fewer.

## 5. Prime supplies and remaining gap
Schur's polynomial values and C28's Φ_(pq)(t) construction supply infinitely many ℓ≡1(mod pq) with explicit per-stage bounds, but no guarantee that v_i or t_i is nonzero. Source primes over u give a nonzero φ-class somewhere with ℓ≤|u|, but no guarantee ℓ≡1(mod p) or nonzero circular residue. Ideal-element factorization propagates nonzero class to at least one prime factor with an explicit norm bound, but cannot force that factor to lie in the split-and-nonresidue subset.

The band's inequality q<sqrt(5/12)p^(3/2)+2p bounds H and the combinatorial dimension, but alone supplies no control of the Frobenius distribution of the nontrivial class or ε's Kummer character. No effective elementary distribution estimate is proved here. The *narrowest remaining statement* is a source-dependent finite certificate: find finitely many split λ_i with computable t_i and v_i such that the known nonzero [A]_φ lies in span{t_i v_i}; the one-prime QP_φ is a sufficient special case. Producing such a certificate uniformly, with an explicit bound, remains **CONJECTURED / OPEN**. An explicit β with div β=A (PA_φ), or ENTRY/SR, would bypass prime selection.
