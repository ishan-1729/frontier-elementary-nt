# C36: Vanishing components, primary-unit overlap, and the exact remaining PACK target

Written: 2026-10-29T09:11:30Z.  
Question: C36 — can the vanishing components be closed directly, removing a proportion bound from PACK?  
Status: **PARTIAL.** The component alternatives and their limitations are proved below. A new source-specific overlap bound or component contradiction remains open.

## 0. Verdict

**The primary-circular reflected-root theorem does not close an \(F\)-vanishing component.** On that component the reflected exponent itself is zero modulo \(p\). Its root conclusion is therefore automatic for every projected field element, before primaryness, singularity, or circular descent is assumed. The inverse used in C28 §5.3 does not exist there.

Principalizing a vanishing source component does not repair this: it can leave a nonzero primary unit class. The implication “primary + circular + principal divided divisor + explicit reflected root \(\Rightarrow\) global \(p\)-th power” is false. The actual cyclotomic example from [astraSR §§4–6][SR], independently reproduced in §5, refutes that implication at \((p,q)=(5,17)\). The finite models in §6 also retain a full independent source-shaped augmentation module and allow either principal or nonprincipal vanishing components. These are precise limitations of the specified interfaces, not an impossibility theorem for new elementary source arithmetic.

The strongest additional **PROVED** source statement is sharper than the old rank count. Write \(n=H-1\), let \(V\) be the \(n\)-dimensional source radical, let \(\overline U_0\) be the primary unit classes inside \(k^\times/k^{\times p}\), and put

\[
 J=V\cap\overline U_0,\qquad h=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}J.
\]

Then:

1. Every nontrivial unit component has multiplicity at most one. On **every \(F\)-nonzero component**, primary units are global \(p\)-th powers. This extends C29 §2 from its chosen \(D\)-nonzero support to the full \(F\)-nonzero support.
2. \(J\) is a direct sum of whole \(F\)-zero source components. Thus \(0\le h\le d\), and the source's divided-divisor image has **exactly** \(p^{n-h}\) classes. The familiar lower bound \(p^r\) replaces \(h\) by its largest permitted value \(d\).
3. C34's same finite ideal count gives the stronger necessary source inequality

   \[
   \boxed{(n-h)\log p<\frac n2\log q-\frac{19H}{20},\qquad
   h>A(p,q):=n\left(1-\frac{\log q}{2\log p}\right)
                 +\frac{19H}{20\log p}.}
   \tag{0.1}
   \]

   Consequently, **\(h\le\lfloor A(p,q)\rfloor\), in particular \(h\le H/4\), excludes a source throughout BAND without any bound on \(d\)**. These are proved implications; the needed bound on the actual source intersection is not proved.
4. A stronger, particularly simple alternative needs no proportion estimate at all: prove that every vanishing source component has a **nonprincipal** divided divisor. Together with the already proved nonprincipality on the nonvanishing components, this gives \(h=0\), \(p^n\) classes, and a contradiction throughout BAND. This source-specific statement is **OPEN**.
5. Primary circular products are killed by \(F\) itself, with an explicit root formula, not only by the reflected multiple \(D\). Thus projected circular descent on **any \(F\)-nonzero component** gives a direct contradiction. On an \(F\)-zero component it can hold without contradiction.

No uniform \(d\)-bound, source principalization, or new unconditional exclusion of a remaining band pair is established here. C35's coverage is retained. Its rank-25 example limits the particular structural information tested there; it does not prove that every possible elementary rank argument must fail.

\[
 K=\mathbb Q(\eta),\quad \eta=\zeta_q,\quad k=K^+,

\]
\[

Since \(p\nmid H\), the polynomial \(T^H-1\) is squarefree. Write

\[
 M_H(T)=\frac{T^H-1}{T-1}=\prod_{\phi\in\mathcal P}\phi(T),
 \qquad E=\bigoplus_{\phi\in\mathcal P}E_\phi,
 \qquad E_\phi\simeq\mathbb F_p[T]/(\phi).
 \tag{1.1}
\]

Here \(\mathcal P\) contains each nontrivial irreducible factor once; \(e_\phi\) is its CRT idempotent in \(R\). A component of degree \(e=\deg\phi\) represents \(e\) characters, not one character in a dimension count. Polynomial Euclidean arithmetic constructs these projectors explicitly.

Use precisely the coefficient convention of C34/C35:

\[
 f(a)=\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}j^{-1}[(a-j)p^{-1}]_q\pmod p,
 \qquad F(T)=\sum_{i=0}^{H-1}f(g^i)T^i.
 \tag{1.2}
\]
\[
 Z=\ker(F:E\to E),\qquad W=\bigoplus_{F\bmod\phi\ne0}E_\phi,
 \qquad E=Z\oplus W,
\]
\[
 d=\dim Z=\deg\gcd(F,M_H),\qquad r=\dim W=n-d.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

No inversion of character labels is made. All operators below use the same cyclic convolution as (1.2).

For the source, put \(\mathcal X=-y\),

\[
 B_a=(\mathcal X-\eta^a)(\mathcal X-\eta^{-a}),\qquad
 \alpha=B_g/B_1,
\]
\[
 \iota:E\longrightarrow k^\times/k^{\times p},\qquad
 v\longmapsto[B_1^v],\qquad V=\iota(E).
 \tag{1.4}
\]

Integral augmentation-zero lifts define these classes. RAD makes \(\iota\) injective and equivariant. Also \([\alpha]=\iota(T-1)\); since \(T-1\) is invertible on \(E\), the conjugates of \([\alpha]\) generate \(V\). ## 2. The unit components: a complete elementary argument

### 2.1 The finite primary test and the circular coefficient map — PROVED

Let \(U=\mathcal O_k^\times\) and \(\overline U=U/U^p\). This embeds in \(k^\times/k^{\times p}\): a \(p\)-th root of a unit has every finite valuation zero and is a unit. Every class has a totally positive representative \(u^{p+1}\), which represents \([u]\) because \(p+1\equiv1\pmod p\). Such a representative has norm one.

At \(p\), define

\[
 \delta(u)=\frac{u^p/\sigma_p(u)-1}{p}\pmod p,
 \qquad \sigma_p(\eta)=\eta^p.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

The ring is unramified at \(p\): the power-basis discriminant of \(\Phi_q\) is \(\pm q^{q-2}\), obtained by multiplying \(\Phi_q'(\eta)=q\eta^{q-1}/(\eta-1)\) over the conjugates. Its order index is a \(p\)-unit, and \(\Phi_q\) is squarefree modulo \(p\). Frobenius reduction makes (2.1) defined. Multiplication modulo \(p^2\) proves additivity, and \(\delta(u^p)=p\delta(u)=0\). It is \(G\)-equivariant, since the automorphisms commute.

For completeness, \(\delta(u)=0\) is also sufficient for local \(p\)-th roots. Put \(f_0=\operatorname{ord}_q(p)\) and \(M=p^{f_0}-1\). Iteration of \(u^p=\sigma_p(u)\pmod{p^2}\) gives \(u^M=1\pmod{p^2}\). Choose \(e\) with \(pe\equiv1\pmod M\); then \((u^e)^p=u\pmod{p^2}\). For the remaining quotient \(1+p^2z\), the integral polynomial

\[
 \frac{(1+pX)^p-1}{p^2}-z
\]

reduces to \(X-z\) modulo \(p\) and has derivative \(1\) there. Successively correcting one base-\(p\) digit constructs compatible roots at every finite precision. This is the finite local argument used in the project, not a global power criterion. Therefore

\[
 \overline U_0:=\ker(\delta:\overline U\to\mathcal O_k/p\mathcal O_k)
 \tag{2.2}
\]

is exactly the primary unit-class space.

Set \(C_i=(1-\eta^{g^i})(1-\eta^{-g^i})\). Ratios \(C_i/C_j\) are units: the quotients of the factors \(1-\eta^a\) are integral geometric sums, with integral inverses obtained by using \(a^{-1}\pmod q\). Thus an integer vector \(a=(a_i)\) with \(\sum a_i=0\) gives a positive norm-one circular unit

\[
 \epsilon(a)=\prod_iC_i^{a_i},\qquad
 \kappa:E\to\overline U,\quad a\mapsto[\epsilon(a)].
 \tag{2.3}
\]

Changing an augmentation-zero lift changes the product by a global \(p\)-th power. No assertion that \(\kappa\) is injective or surjective is part of its definition.

Finite binomial expansion and a finite geometric inverse give

\[
 \delta(1-\eta)=-\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\eta^j/j}{1-\eta^p},
 \qquad \frac1{1-\eta^p}=-\frac1q\sum_{b=1}^{q-1}b\eta^{pb}.
\]

Since \(\delta(-\eta^{-1})=0\), collecting coefficients yields

\[
 \delta(C_0)=\frac2q\sum_{a=0}^{q-1}f(a)\eta^a.
\]

Replacing \(j\) by \(p-j\) gives \(f(-a)=f(a)\). For augmentation-zero exponents the rational \(a=0\) contribution cancels, so

\[
 \boxed{\delta\kappa(a)=\frac2q\sum_{i=0}^{H-1}(a*F)_i
          (\eta^{g^i}+\eta^{-g^i}).}
 \tag{2.4}
\]

The \(H\) displayed real elements are independent modulo \(p\). A relation produces a polynomial of degree at most \(q-1\), zero constant coefficient, divisible by \(\Phi_q\). It must be a constant multiple of \(\Phi_q\), and its zero constant coefficient makes that multiple zero. Consequently

\[
 \epsilon(a)\text{ is primary}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad aF=0\text{ in }E.
 \tag{2.5}
\]

In particular the actual primary circular classes are

\[
 \mathcal C_0=\kappa(Z)=\kappa(E)\cap\overline U_0,
 \qquad \dim\mathcal C_0\le d.
 \tag{2.6}
\]

The equivalence concerns the circular exponent presentation. It does not make \(Z\) inject into the global unit quotient.

### 2.2 Each nontrivial unit component occurs at most once — PROVED

Here is the finite-subgroup justification of C29 §2, including its reduction modulo \(p\).

Choose finitely many positive unit representatives and all their \(G\)-translates; let \(\Lambda\) be the subgroup they generate. It is finitely generated and torsion-free. Integer elimination gives a free abelian basis, of rank \(a\). Its logarithm vectors lie in

\[
 \mathcal W=\{(x_\sigma)\in\mathbb R^H:\sum_\sigma x_\sigma=0\},
\]

and form a uniformly separated set. Indeed, if a positive unit \(u\) has every \(|\log\sigma(u)|<\log(1+1/H)\), its integer trace is at least \(H\) by AM–GM and less than \(H+1\). Equality in AM–GM forces all conjugates to be \(1\), hence \(u=1\). Apply this to quotients to obtain separation.

If the real span of the log vectors had dimension \(b<a\), the \((N+1)^a\) distinct products of a free basis with exponents \(0,\ldots,N\) would lie in a \(b\)-dimensional box of side \(O(N)\). A finite partition into boxes smaller than the fixed separation has only \(O(N^b)\) cells, a contradiction for sufficiently large integer \(N\). Thus

\[
 \Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb R\hookrightarrow\mathcal W
\]

is an equivariant embedding.

The characteristic polynomial of the cyclic generator on \(\mathcal W\) is \(M_H\). Its characteristic polynomial on \(\Lambda\otimes\mathbb R\) is monic integral, because the action on the free lattice is an integer matrix, and divides \(M_H\) over \(\mathbb R\). Monic polynomial division shows divisibility in \(\mathbb Z[T]\). Modulo \(p\), it therefore remains a divisor of the squarefree polynomial \(M_H\). Each nontrivial simple factor occurs at most once in \(\Lambda/\Lambda^p\), and the trivial factor does not occur.

The map \(\Lambda/\Lambda^p\to U/U^p\) need not be injective; its **image is a quotient**, and contains the originally chosen classes. A quotient retains the same multiplicity bound. If \(\overline U\) contained two copies of a simple component, finitely many representatives generating them would contradict this argument. We have proved, without importing Dirichlet's unit theorem,

\[
 \boxed{\overline U_\phi=0\ \text{or one copy of }E_\phi,
 \qquad \dim\overline U\le n.}
 \tag{2.7}
\]

### 2.3 The full nonvanishing support — PROVED

Fix \(\phi\in\mathcal P\). If \(F_\phi:=F\bmod\phi\ne0\), multiplication by \(F_\phi\) is invertible. Equation (2.4) makes \(\kappa_\phi\) injective and \(\delta\) nonzero on its image. By (2.7), that image fills the unit component, and a nonzero map from this simple component is injective. Hence

\[
 \boxed{F_\phi\ne0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \overline U_\phi=\kappa(E_\phi),\qquad
 (\overline U_0)_\phi=0.}
 \tag{2.8}
\]

C29 chose \(\phi\) where \(D\bmod\phi\ne0\), which implies \(F_\phi\ne0\); its unit argument needs only the latter condition. This distinction matters because \(D\) can vanish on additional components.

For \(F_\phi=0\), (2.4) says the circular **image** is primary; it does not say that image is nonzero, that all units are circular, or that a primary class is a global power. These alternatives are resolved exactly in §4.3.

## 3. What the source maps to: an exact component alternative

### 3.1 The divided-divisor sequence — PROVED under ALG and RAD

Let \(\mathcal S_0\subset k^\times/k^{\times p}\) be the strong primary singular classes. There is a well-defined equivariant map

\[
 \partial:\mathcal S_0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(k)[p],
 \qquad [a]\longmapsto[\operatorname{div}(a)/p].
 \tag{3.1}
\]

Changing \(a\) by \(\beta^p\) changes its divided divisor by \(\operatorname{div}(\beta)\). Its class is killed by \(p\). The kernel is exactly \(\overline U_0\): if the divided divisor is \(\operatorname{div}(\beta)\), then \(a/\beta^p\) is a primary unit representing the same field class; the converse is immediate. No surjectivity onto all of \(\operatorname{Cl}(k)[p]\) is asserted.

Restricting to the source gives the exact sequence

\[
 \boxed{0\longrightarrow J=V\cap\overline U_0
 \longrightarrow V\xrightarrow{\partial}\mathcal A_V
 \longrightarrow0,\qquad\mathcal A_V=\partial(V).}
 \tag{3.2}
\]

If \(h=\dim J\), then

\[
 \boxed{\dim\mathcal A_V=n-h,\qquad |\mathcal A_V|=p^{n-h}.}
 \tag{3.3}
\]

These are actual \(p\)-torsion ideal classes. They are not being identified with the quotient of a larger \(p\)-group by \(p\); the C31 erratum remains respected.

### 3.2 Whole components, not arbitrary fractions — PROVED

Each \(V_\phi\simeq E_\phi\) is simple. The restriction \(\partial\vert_{V_\phi}\) is either zero or injective. Therefore exactly one of the following happens:

| Alternative on \(\phi\) | Consequence |
|---|---|
| \(\partial\vert_{V_\phi}\ne0\) | It is injective and contributes \(\deg\phi\) dimensions to \(\mathcal A_V\). Every nonzero source class on it has nonprincipal divided divisor. |
| \(\partial\vert_{V_\phi}=0\) | \(V_\phi\subseteq\overline U_0\). Its entire \(\deg\phi\)-dimensional source component is represented by primary units. |

By (2.8), the second alternative is possible only when \(F_\phi=0\). Thus, for some subset \(\mathcal I\subseteq\{\phi:F_\phi=0\}\),

\[
 \boxed{J=\bigoplus_{\phi\in\mathcal I}V_\phi,\qquad
 h=\sum_{\phi\in\mathcal I}\deg\phi\le d.}
 \tag{3.4}
\]

This is the exact version of the PACK loss. Establishing principality for one nonzero vector in a simple source component puts the whole component in \(J\). Establishing nonprincipality for one such vector puts the whole component in the ideal-class image.

On \(F\)-nonzero components the latter is already proved. On \(F\)-zero components neither alternative has been determined from SOURCE here.

## 4. Circular roots, reflected roots, and principalization

### 4.1 On a vanishing component the reflected root is automatic — PROVED

Use C26's corrected parameter \(c=1+pk_0\), \(c\equiv p-1\pmod q\), and let \(\Xi\in\mathbb Z[G]\) be the literal restriction of its reflected exponent. The finite floor calculation in the read astraSR/C28 proofs gives

\[
 \Xi\equiv D-\frac{2k_0}{q}N\pmod p,\qquad
 D=\frac2q(1-\sigma_{p-1}^{-1})F,\qquad
 N=\sum_{i=0}^{H-1}T^i.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

On a nontrivial \(F\)-zero component, \(e_\phi F=e_\phi D=e_\phi N=0\), so \(e_\phi\Xi=0\) in \(R\). For **any** integer lift \(P\) of \(e_\phi\), coefficientwise reduction in \(\mathbb Z[T]/(T^H-1)\) gives

\[
 K_P=\frac{P\Xi}{p}\in\mathbb Z[G].
\]

Consequently, for every \(a\in k^\times\),

\[
 \boxed{(a^P)^\Xi=(a^{K_P})^p.}
 \tag{4.2}
\]

This root needs no norm-one, primary, singular, circular, or source hypothesis. It applies in particular to \(z_\phi=\alpha^P\), whose class remains nonzero by RAD. There is no conflict: the operator has killed the component, not the element before applying the operator.

C28's inversion step requires \(P\Xi U=P\pmod p\). Reducing this equation modulo an \(F\)-zero \(\phi\) would give \(0=1\). Thus that step is unavailable there. The statement “the reflected image has a root” supplies no contradiction on this support.

### 4.2 A sharper circular annihilator, and a larger useful descent target — PROVED

There is a positive refinement. Choose an integer lift \(\mathcal F\in\mathbb Z[G]\) of \(F\). If \(\epsilon=\prod_iC_i^{a_i}\) is primary and \(\sum a_i=0\), (2.5) gives coefficientwise divisibility of \(a*\mathcal F\) by \(p\). Hence

\[
 W_{F,\epsilon}=\prod_i C_i^{(a*\mathcal F)_i/p},\qquad
 \boxed{\epsilon^{\mathcal F}=W_{F,\epsilon}^{\,p}.}
 \tag{4.3}
\]

In particular \(F\mathcal C_0=0\). This is stronger than \(D\mathcal C_0=0\), and gives the componentwise disjointness

\[
 \boxed{V_W\cap\mathcal C_0=\{0\},\qquad
 V\cap\mathcal C_0\subseteq V_Z.}
 \tag{4.4}
\]

Indeed, if \(\iota(v)\) is primary circular, then \(\iota(Fv)=0\); RAD gives \(Fv=0\).

The explicit root argument also extends C28's sufficient projected descent target. Suppose \(F_\phi\ne0\), let \(P\) lift \(e_\phi\), and choose an integer lift \(U_F\) of the inverse of \(F\) on \(\phi\), zero on other components. Then

\[
 R_0=\frac{P\mathcal F U_F-P}{p}\in\mathbb Z[G].
\]

Assume the actual element identity

\[
 \textbf{CD}_\phi:\qquad z_\phi=\alpha^P=\epsilon\beta^p,
 \quad\epsilon\text{ a norm-one circular product},\quad\beta\in k^\times.
 \tag{4.5}
\]

The source makes \(\epsilon\) primary. Equation (4.3) gives

\[
 z_\phi^{\mathcal F}=T_0^p,\qquad T_0=W_{F,\epsilon}\beta^{\mathcal F}.
\]

Raising to \(U_F\) and using the displayed integer correction yields the actual root

\[
 \boxed{z_\phi=\left(T_0^{U_F}/\alpha^{R_0}\right)^p,}
 \tag{4.6}
\]

contrary to RAD. Thus **CD on any \(F\)-nonzero component suffices**, including components where \(D\) vanishes. This conclusion still needs the arithmetic identity (4.5), which has not been constructed.

Allowing \(\beta\) in \(K(\zeta_p)\) does not enlarge the descent premise: if \(a\in K\) is \(\beta^p\) there, then \(a=(a/N(\beta))^p\) in \(K\), since the extension has degree \(p-1\). The field \(K\) has no nontrivial \(p\)-th roots of unity, since \([K(\zeta_p):K]=p-1>1\). Therefore a root in \(K\) of a real \(a\) is unique and is fixed by conjugation, so it belongs to \(k\). Arbitrary circular exponents can also be normalized to sum zero: taking the real norm of (4.5) gives \(q^{\sum a_i}N(\beta)^p=1\), hence \(p\mid\sum a_i\), and a multiple of \(p\) can be removed from one exponent. These are the explicit normalizations of C28 §5.3.

### 4.3 What PA and CD mean on the zero support — PROVED classification

Write \(\mathcal C_\phi=\kappa(E_\phi)\). This means an actual global class image, not the nonzero exponent field \(E_\phi\). Define

\[
 \textbf{PA}_\phi:\quad
 \exists\beta\in k^\times\quad
 \operatorname{div}(\beta)=\operatorname{div}(z_\phi)/p.
 \tag{4.7}
\]

By (3.2), PA means \(V_\phi\subseteq\overline U_0\). CD means that the nonzero source class belongs to \(\mathcal C_\phi\); an expression initially using other components can be projected onto \(\phi\), with the discrepancy absorbed into a \(p\)-th power.

| Component information | Primary unit space on it | Consequence of PA | Consequence of CD |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(F_\phi\ne0\) | Zero, by (2.8). | Contradicts RAD. | Contradicts RAD, also by the explicit root (4.6). |
| \(F_\phi=0, \mathcal C_\phi\ne0\) | \((\overline U_0)_\phi=\overline U_\phi=\mathcal C_\phi\), one simple copy. | Equivalent to CD as a class-containment statement; can leave a nonzero primary unit. | Equivalent to PA; the reflected root is automatic and gives no contradiction. |
| \(F_\phi=0, \mathcal C_\phi=0, (\overline U_0)_\phi=0\) | Zero. | Contradicts RAD. | Contradicts RAD. |
| \(F_\phi=0, \mathcal C_\phi=0, (\overline U_0)_\phi\ne0\) | One simple primary-unit copy, absent from the circular image. | Can leave a noncircular primary unit. | Contradicts RAD because the circular class image is zero. |

For the second row, nonzero \(\mathcal C_\phi\) fills the only possible unit copy by (2.7), and (2.4) makes it primary. If PA holds, the nonzero source copy fills that same unit component; conversely circular units have trivial divided-divisor class. This proves the asserted equivalence without an index formula. For the last two rows, CD would put a nonzero source class in zero.

The zero local quotient alone does not select a row. In particular, proving that the circular subgroup surjects onto \(\overline U\) modulo \(p\)-th powers would not finish the argument: its primary part can still coincide with all of \(V_Z\). The model in §6 grants even an isomorphism from circular exponents to all unit classes and realizes this possibility.

There is also no operation that principalizes a nonprincipal divided divisor merely by multiplying by a primary circular unit. For any unit \(\epsilon\) and any \(\beta\in k^\times\),

\[
 \left[\frac1p\operatorname{div}(z\epsilon\beta^p)\right]
 =\left[\frac1p\operatorname{div}(z)\right]
 \tag{4.8}
\]

whenever the classes are singular. A new element with the required divided divisor must actually be constructed. Even after that, the residual primary unit need not be a power.

For stronger PACK, principalizing a zero component increases \(h\) and removes its contribution to the ideal-class image. It therefore cannot be counted as an additional \(p^{\deg\phi}\) factor. The alternative useful for packing is **nonprincipality** of that component, or a separate proof that a purported primary-unit representative is impossible for the actual source.

### 5.1 A primary circular class with PA and CD, but no fifth root — PROVED, with TESTED certificates

Take \(p=5,q=17,g=3,c=21\), so \(H=8,n=7\). In generator order, exact finite sums give

\[
 F=(3,0,0,4,0,4,4,3),\qquad
 \gcd(F,M_8)=T+2,
\]
\[
 D=(3,1,1,1,2,4,4,4),\qquad
 \Xi=(259,197,197,172,193,175,175,260).
 \tag{5.1}
\]

Thus \(d=1,r=6\), whereas \(\operatorname{rank}(D|_E)=4\). Explicitly, \(F(2)=3\) and \(F(4)=1\), while \(D(2)=D(4)=0\). The difference between the \(F\)-nonzero and \(D\)-nonzero supports is real.

Put

\[
 a=(2,-1,-2,1,2,-1,-2,1),\qquad
 \epsilon=\prod_{i=0}^7 C_i^{a_i}.
 \tag{5.2}
\]

The exact modular polynomial identities

\[
 a^2=a,\quad (T-3)a=0,\quad a(3)=1,\quad aF=aD=0
 \quad\text{in }\mathbb F_5[T]/(T^8-1)
 \tag{5.3}
\]

show that \(a\bmod5\) is the idempotent of the deficient factor \(\phi=T+2\), whose root is \(3\). Because \(\sum a_i=0\), \(\epsilon\) is a totally positive norm-one unit. Equation (2.5) already proves it is primary. As an independent local check, its canonical coefficient vector in \((\mathbb Z/25)[X]/\Phi_{17}(X)\) is

\[
 (15,0,6,16,0,16,22,22,6,6,22,22,16,0,16,6).
 \tag{5.4}

## 7. The strongest resulting packing theorem and the exact extra statements

### 7.1 Replace the deficiency by the actual source/unit overlap — PROVED

C32's elementary lattice argument gives an integral representative of every ideal class of norm at most

\[
 M_q=\frac{H!}{H^H}q^{n/2}.
\]

For reference, an ideal lattice has determinant \(q^{n/2}N(\mathfrak a)\). Finite-volume pigeonhole in a half cross-polytope of radius \(R\), whose volume is \(R^H/H!\), produces a nonzero \(b\in\mathfrak a\) with
\[
 |N(b)|\le(H!/H^H)q^{n/2}N(\mathfrak a).
\]
Applying this to an inverse ideal class gives the representative bound. The ideal arithmetic and discriminant steps are proved in the read C32 §4.

Let \(N_q(M_q)\) count integral ideals up to that norm. Applying the representative bound to the exact subgroup (3.3), not just the weaker lower bound \(p^r\), gives

\[
 p^{n-h}\le N_q(M_q).
 \tag{7.1}
\]

Here is the explicit upper bound retained from the read C34 §3. For any \(\delta>0\) and integer \(L\ge1\),

\[
 \log N_q(M_q)\le (1+\delta)\log M_q+\mathcal E_{\delta,L}(q),
\]
\[
 \mathcal E_{\delta,L}(q)=
 \frac{(1+1/\delta+L/4+H/(2L))(q-1)^{-\delta}}
 {1-(q-1)^{-1-\delta}}
 +\frac{q^{-1-\delta}}{1-q^{-1-\delta}}.
 \tag{7.2}
\]

The proof counts finite products of prime ideals. For an unramified prime \(\ell\ne q\) of residue degree \(f_\ell\) in \(k\), \(\ell^{f_\ell}\ge q-1\). Unramified degree-one primes lie in the two progressions \(\ell\equiv\pm1\pmod q\). For higher degree, use
\[
 \ell^{-f_\ell}\le\min\{(q-1)^{-1},\ell^{-2}\},\qquad
 \sum_{a>L}a^{-2}\le1/L.
\]
The finite geometric factors, with the elementary bound on the decreasing sums, give (7.2). This uses no class-number formula or prime distribution assertion.

For \(H\ge50000\), C34's explicit choice
\[
 \delta=1/\lceil\sqrt H\rceil,\qquad L=\lceil\sqrt{2H}\rceil
\]
proves
\[
 \log M_q<\frac n2\log q-\frac{99H}{100},\qquad
 \delta\log M_q<\frac{3H}{100},\qquad
 \mathcal E_{\delta,L}(q)<\frac H{100}.
\]
The proof uses factorial integral bounds and finite rational exponential-sum endpoint checks; it is read in C34 §3.3. Thus

\[
 \log N_q(M_q)<\frac n2\log q-\frac{19H}{20}.
 \tag{7.3}
\]

Combining (7.1) and (7.3) proves (0.1):

\[
 \boxed{h>A(p,q).}
 \tag{7.4}
\]

This says more than \(d>A\). A hypothetical source must have **whole source components of total degree greater than \(A\), all on the zero support, whose divided divisors are principal and whose classes survive as nonzero primary units**. Merely having that many Bernoulli zeros is not enough.

For the last strict inequality, \(p<q=2H+1\) gives \(\log p<2H+1<19H/5\) for \(H\ge1\), so \(19H/(20\log p)>1/4\). The exact integer cutoff is therefore

 \boxed{h\le\lfloor A(p,q)\rfloor\quad\Longrightarrow\quad

## 8. A direct attempt using the affine source and C26's size bound

The countermodels leave open additional arithmetic. I also examined the most immediate such possibility: extend C26's finite truncation proof from independence modulo global powers to independence modulo primary units. The following calculation identifies the precise failure of that extension. It does not rule out a different auxiliary polynomial.

### 8.1 A circular unit factor can have bounded height — PROVED

Suppose a nonzero source class is represented by a circular unit modulo \(p\)-th powers. Its circular exponents can be chosen with

\[
 \sum_i e_i=0,\qquad |e_i|\le p-1.
 \tag{8.1}
\]

To prove this, reduce the exponents to residues \(r_i\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\). Their sum is \(pa\). If \(a>0\), more than \(a\) entries are positive, since \(a\) positive entries would sum to at most \(a(p-1)<pa\). Subtract \(p\) from any \(a\) positive entries. The new vector has sum zero, satisfies (8.1), and differs from the original by \(p\) times an augmentation-zero vector.

The finite identity
\[
 (1-z)^{-1}=-q^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}jz^j
\]
for a primitive \(q\)-th root \(z\), together with \(|1-z|\le2\), gives
\[
 4/q^2<\sigma(C_i)\le4
\]
at every real embedding. The positive and negative exponent sums in (8.1) are both \(s\), with \(2s\le H(p-1)\). Hence

\[
 \boxed{\max_\sigma\max\{\sigma(\epsilon),\sigma(\epsilon)^{-1}\}
       \le q^{H(p-1)}.}
 \tag{8.2}
\]

Its selected positive \(p\)-th roots and their inverses have house at most \(q^H\). C26's inherited source size \(Y=|\mathcal X|>p^{8H}/8\), together with \(q<p^{3/2}\), is more than sufficient to absorb this factor in the selected-branch truncation error below. An uncontrolled circular height is therefore not the decisive problem in this attempt.

### 8.2 The exact norm after a nontrivial unit factor — PROVED

As in the read C26 §6.2, lift a nonzero even source exponent to

\[
 0\le n_c<p,\quad n_c=n_{-c},\quad
 \sum_c n_c=pm,\quad 1\le m\le H.
\]

Replacing by its inverse if necessary supplies \(m\le H\); pairing makes \(m\) positive and even. Define

\[
 \mathcal P(Z)=\prod_{c=1}^{q-1}(Z-\eta^c)^{n_c},\qquad
 P(t)=\prod_c(1-\eta^ct)^{n_c},\qquad
 P(t)^{1/p}=\sum_{j\ge0}b_jt^j,
\]
\[
 S_m(Z)=\sum_{j=0}^m b_jZ^{m-j},\qquad
 Q_j=p^{j+v_p(j!)}.
 \tag{8.3}
\]

All \(b_j\) are real algebraic numbers in \(k\). Membership of this source class in \(\overline U_0\) gives only

\[
 \mathcal P(\mathcal X)=\epsilon\beta^p,
 \tag{8.4}
\]

with \(\epsilon\) a positive primary unit; RAD makes \(\epsilon\) a non-\(p\)-th-power. In a circular intersection, \(\epsilon\) can additionally be given (8.1). Changing representatives only changes \(\beta\).

In the untwisted proof, a root \(\rho\in k\) would have the same positive branch at every real embedding. The finite binomial estimate gives
\[
 \left|\sigma(\rho-S_m(\mathcal X))\right|
 \le\binom{2m}{m+1}/(Y-2).
\]
C26's size bound makes the integral difference \(Q_m(\rho-S_m(\mathcal X))\) have house less than one, so it is zero.

The circular height does not prevent smallness on the selected branch. C26 proves \(Q_H\binom{2H}{H+1}<p^{4H}\); monotonicity in \(m\), (8.2), and the source size give
\[
 \frac{q^H Q_m\binom{2m}{m+1}}{Y-2}
 <\frac{p^{11H/2}}{p^{8H}/8-2}<1
\]
in BAND. The issue is which algebraic embeddings must enter a norm.

For (8.4), let \(u^p=\epsilon\), put \(L=K(\zeta_p)\), and \(\Omega=L(u)\). A nonpower in \(k\) remains a nonpower in \(L\): the successive degrees \(2\) and \(p-1\) are prime to \(p\), and the norm and Bezout identity descend a purported root. Since \(L\) contains the \(p\)-th roots of unity, adjoining one root \(u\) gives all of them; the nontrivial splitting-field group embeds in the group of order \(p\). Thus \([\Omega:L]=p\), with all conjugates \(\zeta_p^j u\).

Now \(\rho=u\beta\) satisfies \(\rho^p=\mathcal P(\mathcal X)\). It is integral, but only the selected branch gives the small error. The exact relative norm is

\[
 \boxed{
 N_{\Omega/L}\!\left(Q_m(\rho-S_m(\mathcal X))\right)
 =Q_m^p\left(\mathcal P(\mathcal X)-S_m(\mathcal X)^p\right).}
 \tag{8.5}
\]

This follows by multiplying \(\zeta_p^j\rho-S_m(\mathcal X)\) for \(0\le j<p\); \(p\) is odd. The right side is an algebraic-integer polynomial value.

There is an exact positive-degree obstruction, not just a rough loss in an estimate:

\[
 \boxed{\deg_Z(\mathcal P(Z)-S_m(Z)^p)=m(p-1)-1>0,
 \quad\text{leading coefficient }p\,b_{m+1}\ne0.}
 \tag{8.6}
\]

To justify nonvanishing, put \(S_1=\sum_c n_c\eta^c\). C26's factorial-safe finite coefficient calculation gives, for every \(j\ge1\),

\[
 Q_jb_j\in\mathcal O_K,\qquad
 Q_jb_j\equiv
 \left(\frac{j!}{p^{v_p(j!)}}\right)^{-1}(-S_1)^j
 \pmod{p\mathcal O_K}.
 \tag{8.7}
\]

Indeed expand the coefficient using
\[
 b_j=\sum_{a=0}^j\binom{1/p}{a}[t^j](P(t)-1)^a.
\]
The exact \(p\)-denominator of \(\binom{1/p}{a}\) is \(Q_a\), and its other denominator primes cancel in the numerator progression. After multiplying by \(Q_j\), terms \(a<j\) vanish modulo \(p\); the term \(a=j\) gives (8.7). This remains valid when \(j\ge p\).

The zero-constant-coefficient argument against \(\Phi_q\) shows \(S_1\ne0\bmod p\) for the nonzero exponent vector. The quotient ring is reduced, so every positive power of \(S_1\) is nonzero. Thus \(b_{m+1}\ne0\). The first omitted term of the formal root is \(b_{m+1}Z^{-1}\); in its \(p\)-th power the first omitted contribution is \(p\,b_{m+1}Z^{m(p-1)-1}\). All later or multiply omitted terms have smaller degree. This proves (8.6).

There is no replacement small-norm conclusion here merely from taking \(Y\) larger. The selected branch is small, but the other \(p-1\) branches produce precisely the positive degree in (8.6). Even the final modular step is consistent with this nonzero norm:

\[
 Q_m^p(\mathcal P(Z)-S_m(Z)^p)
 \equiv-(Q_mb_m)^p\ne0\pmod{p\mathcal O_K}.
 \tag{8.8}
\]

Here \(Q_mb_j\equiv0\pmod p\) for \(j<m\), so \(Q_mS_m\) reduces to its nonzero constant term; reducedness gives the final inequality. This says the value is not in \(p\mathcal O_K\), not that its rational norm is prime to \(p\) at every residue factor.

The exact coefficient tests in [C36_unit_twist_checks.py](C36_unit_twist_checks.py), with [output](C36_unit_twist_checks.out.txt), independently reconstruct the formal roots and verify (8.6)–(8.8), including a truncation index at least \(p\). The fixtures are \((p,q,m)=(3,11,2)\), with degree \(3\), and \((5,17,8)\), with degree \(31\). Both also verify (8.5) independently as a multiplication-matrix determinant.

**Scope of the attempted extension.** This proves why the same C26 truncation and norm step does not establish \(V\cap\overline U_0=0\), even after controlling a circular representative's height. It does not assert that the positive-degree polynomial cannot have a special value at an integer, or exclude a different auxiliary exploiting additional source congruences. Cassels' allocations prescribe the divided divisor in (8.4), but the unit has zero finite valuations and the allocations do not supply a generator with the missing properties. No new source-intersection bound results from this attempt.
