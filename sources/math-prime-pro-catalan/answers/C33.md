# C33: Frobenius-orbit rank bounds and explicit PACK subfamilies
written: 2026-10-29T05:00:00Z
question: C33: a uniform lower bound for the rank r_{p,q} (the PACK route)
status: partial — explicit uniform, order-sensitive lower bounds and arithmetic subfamilies are proved. A sufficient rank bound for every remaining double-Wieferich pair is not proved.

## 0. Verdict and strongest results

**PROVED, by finite algebra, for every pair satisfying ALG below.** There are explicit lower bounds

\[
 r_{p,q}\ge L(p,q):=\max\{L_{\rm pole},L_{\rm ov},L_{\rm tr}\},
 \qquad \operatorname{def}_{p,q}\le H-1-L(p,q).                 \tag{0.1}
\]

Here `L_pole` is a rational-function pole bound, `L_ov` is a finite optimization over divisors of `H` using Frobenius-orbit degrees and a value-set constraint, and `L_tr` is an inexpensive primitive-order projection bound. Their formulas and proofs are in §§3–5. The first two require no polynomial gcd or character evaluation. The third uses explicit sums of the coefficients of `F`, not a root search or a class-group computation.

These estimates do **not** prove a fixed sufficient rank fraction throughout the band. They do give the following complete, non-computational families.

* **PROVED full-rank family.** If `H=(q-1)/2` is prime and `ord_H(p)=H-1`, then
  \[
  r_{p,q}=H-1.                                                \tag{0.2}
  \]
  Thus every pair in the requested large-prime band with these additional arithmetic conditions is excluded by C32's original `(PACK)` theorem. No double-Wieferich assumption is needed for the rank assertion.
* **PROVED prime-power family.** Suppose `H=ell^a`, `p` has order `phi(H)` modulo `H`, and the class of `p-1` in `(Z/qZ)^*/{±1}` has order `ell`. Then
  \[
  r_{p,q}\ge H-H/\ell,\qquad \operatorname{def}_{p,q}\le H/\ell-1. \tag{0.3}
  \]
  For `ell>=23` this gives the 95-percent sufficient rank for the whole requested band. For `ell>=11` it gives the newer 7/8 sufficient rank, using the **different residue-degree count** in the C32 supplement. Neither statement asserts that infinitely many pairs satisfy the additional prime/order conditions.
* **PROVED half-rank near-diagonal family.** Combining C32's smaller ideal-representative bound with the supplement's residue-degree count shows that half rank suffices for `p>100000`, `p<q<=3p/2`. Hence this interval is excluded whenever `H` is prime and `ord_H(p)>=(H-1)/2`. This is a sharper **hybrid counting certificate**, not a claim that original PACK follows from half rank (§6.3).

**PROVED exact information about the Wieferich restriction.** The additive constant in the finite antiderivative is precisely the ordinary rational Fermat quotient:

\[
 \boxed{f(0)\equiv\frac{q^{p-1}-1}{p}\pmod p,
        \qquad F(1)=-f(0)/2.}                              \tag{0.4}
\]

Thus forward Wieferich makes the **trivial** Fourier coefficient zero. Adding a constant to `f` does not change any nontrivial coefficient or `r`. This identifies what this direct use of forward Wieferich does, rather than treating it as a high-rank theorem. The dual congruence and the actual source may have additional consequences; none sufficient for the general rank target is derived here.

**PROVED / TESTED boundaries.** The finite character-sum and Gauss-sum formulas are given exactly, but a nonzero Gauss factor does not force the remaining character sum to be nonzero. A large rank for the *additive* circulant does not transfer to the multiplicative circulant. A finite-characteristic Parseval sum is not positive. Explicit controls refute all three shortcuts in their stated scopes.

**TESTED:** all 933 semisimple odd-prime pairs `p<q<=199` pass the new lower-bound checks. Original `(PACK)` with `t=1,...,16` passes at 125 of those pairs using only the proved lower bounds, versus 686 using the exact rank. These are finite controls, not actual sources. The unrestricted 95-percent assertion fails at 13 pairs in this range; `(29,41)` is one such failure even after imposing **forward** Wieferich. No counterexample to the required large-prime, double-Wieferich assertion is claimed.

**CONJECTURED / OPEN:** no sufficient uniform deficiency bound for every remaining source exponent pair is obtained. The exact remaining statement is in §8. The result is not an elementary proof of all of Catalan.

## 1. Sources, hypotheses, and the precise rank

### 1.1 Named hypotheses

* **ALG:** `p<q` are odd primes, `H=(q-1)/2`, and `p` does not divide `H`.
* **BAND:** `p>100000` and `p<q<sqrt(5/12)*p^(3/2)+2p`.
* **DW:** both `q^(p-1)=1 mod p^2` and `p^(q-1)=1 mod q^2`.
* **C32-PACK:** the source-radical and finite ideal-count theorem proved in C32 §4, with its named inherited source inputs. Under those inputs, a hypothetical Catalan source is impossible if, for one integer `t>=1`,
  \[
  p^{2tr}H^{2(t+1)H}>(H!)^{2(t+1)}q^{(t+1)(H-1)}(t+1)^{2tH}.
                                                               \tag{PACK}
  \]
* **C32-RD:** the supplement §5.3–5.6 residue-degree count, with its inherited source inputs. In BAND, `r>=7(H-1)/8` suffices. C32's original proof supplies `r>=19(H-1)/20` as a sufficient condition for original `(PACK)`.

All new rank assertions below use **ALG only**, unless explicitly stated otherwise. The source inputs are used only when converting a sufficient rank into a source exclusion. No class-group, class-field, reflection, Thaine, analytic class-number, or prime-distribution theorem is an input to the rank proofs.

Let `G=(Z/qZ)^*/{±1}` and choose a generator `g`. For `a mod q`, let

\[
 n(a,j)=[(a-j)p^{-1}]_q\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\},\qquad
 f(a)=\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}j^{-1}n(a,j)\in\mathbb F_p,
\]

\[
 f_i=f(g^i),\quad F(T)=\sum_{i=0}^{H-1}f_iT^i,\quad
 Q_H(T)=1+T+\cdots+T^{H-1}.
\]

Then

\[
 r=H-1-\deg\gcd(F,Q_H),\qquad d=H-1-r.                       \tag{1.1}
\]

All inverses `j^-1` in the defining sum are in `F_p`; the least-residue brackets are integer residues modulo `q`. The coefficient field and the indexing field must not be interchanged.

Because `p∤H`, `T^H-1` is squarefree. In a finite extension containing its roots, multiplication by `F` is diagonal on the character basis. Consequently `r` is exactly the number of nontrivial `H`th roots `xi` for which `F(xi) != 0`; `d` counts zeros, **not Frobenius orbits of zeros**.

For an exact-order-`m` root, the Frobenius orbit length is

\[
 d_m=\operatorname{ord}_m(p),\qquad m\mid H.                  \tag{1.2}
\]

Proof: its `p^j`th power equals itself exactly when `p^j=1 mod m`. The product over that orbit is its irreducible polynomial: Frobenius fixes its coefficients, and a polynomial over `F_p` containing one root contains the whole orbit. Thus all irreducible factors of the reduction of `Phi_m` have degree `d_m`. This proves (1.2) without a character-field theorem.

Neither `ord_q(p)` nor `ord_p(q)` is the orbit length in (1.2).

## 2. The antiderivative, exact character formulas, and what DW actually supplies

### 2.1 PROVED finite recurrences

The defining residues give

\[
 f(-a)=f(a),\qquad
 f(a+p)-f(a)=
 \begin{cases}
  -q/b,&b=[a+p]_q\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\},\\
  0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                                \tag{2.1}
\]

For evenness, pair `j` with `p-j` and use
`n(a,j)+n(-a,p-j)=q-1`, together with `sum j^-1=0 mod p`.
For the recurrence, incrementing `a` by `p` increments every `n(a,j)` by one, except for a wrap at `q-1`. The common increment disappears after weighting and summing; a wrap occurs only for `j=[a+p]_q`, giving (2.1).

Take `a=q+1-p` and use evenness to get

\[
 \boxed{f(1)-f(p-1)=-q\ne0.}                                \tag{2.2}
\]

Thus `F` is not a constant coefficient vector and `r>=1`. Also (2.1) at `a=0` gives `f(p)=f(0)`. Summing the defining sum over all `a mod q` gives

\[
 \sum_{a=0}^{q-1}f(a)=0,
 \qquad f(0)+2\sum_{i=0}^{H-1}f_i=0.                         \tag{2.3}
\]

### 2.2 PROVED: the forward Wieferich congruence is the integration constant

For `1<=j<p`, let `t_j=[q^-1 j]_p` in `1,...,p-1`. Then

\[
 qt_j=j+p n(0,j).
\]

The `t_j` permute `1,...,p-1`. Multiplying the equations divided by `j` in `Z/p^2 Z` yields

\[
 q^{p-1}
 =\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}\left(1+p\,n(0,j)/j\right)
 \equiv1+p\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}j^{-1}n(0,j)\pmod{p^2}.
\]

This proves (0.4), including the formula for `F(1)` by (2.3).

Changing `f` to `f-c` changes `F` by `-c Q_H`, so it changes none of the nontrivial character values and none of the rank. In particular forward DW gives `f(0)=F(1)=0`, a normalization at the trivial character, not additional vanishing or nonvanishing at the other characters.

**Scope:** this does not prove that the set of exponent pairs satisfying DW is statistically or logically independent of the other coefficients. It proves that using DW only through this integration-constant identity cannot supply the desired rank estimate.

### 2.3 PROVED: a finite Bernoulli-like character expression

For a nontrivial `H`th root `xi`, define the even character `chi_xi(g^i)=xi^i`. Directly,

\[
 F(xi)=\frac12\sum_{a=1}^{q-1}\chi_{xi}(a)
                \sum_{j=1}^{p-1}j^{-1}[(a-j)p^{-1}]_q.       \tag{2.4}
\]

There is a useful division-safe conductor-`pq` formulation. In

\[
 \mathcal R=(\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z)[T]/Q_H(T)
\]

form the finite sum

\[
 S(T)=\sum_{\substack{1\le b<pq\\(b,pq)=1}}
           b\,[b\bmod p]^{-p}\,T^{\operatorname{ind}_g(b\bmod q)}.
                                                               \tag{2.5}
\]

The index is modulo sign, hence lies in `Z/H`; the inverse power is taken modulo `p^2`. Then `S` lies in `p R`, and

\[
 \boxed{q^{-1}(S/p)=(2/q)F\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p[T]/Q_H.} \tag{2.6}
\]

Proof: write `b=j+p n(a,j)` by CRT. Modulo `p^2`,

\[
 b j^{-p}=j^{1-p}+p j^{-1}n(a,j).
\]

The first term is independent of `a`; summing over `a` makes it a scalar multiple of `Q_H`, zero in `R`. The second term, pairing `a,-a`, is `2pF`. This proves both divisibility by `p` and (2.6). Division occurs **after projecting away the trivial character**, so there is no illegal division of a non-p-divisible numerator.

This is an explicit finite generalized-Bernoulli numerator, not an imported value of an L-function. Its vanishing is still the original rank problem; the reformulation does not bound the number of vanishing characters.

### 2.4 PROVED: a Gauss transform, with its precise limitation

Let `zeta` be a primitive `q`th root in a finite extension of `F_p`, and put

\[
 L_p(Z)=\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}Z^j/j,\qquad
 h(Z)=L_p(Z)/(1-Z^p),\qquad
 \tau(\chi)=\sum_{a=1}^{q-1}\chi(a)\zeta^a.
\]

For a nontrivial even character, finite additive orthogonality gives

\[
 \tau(\chi)\tau(\chi^{-1})=q\ne0.                            \tag{2.7}
\]

To prove it, set the two summation variables to `tb,b`. The inner sum over `b!=0` is `q-1` at `t=-1` and `-1` otherwise. Since `sum chi(t)=0` and `chi(-1)=1`, the result is `q`.

Writing `A(Z)=sum_(a=0)^(q-1) f(a)Z^a`, (2.1) implies

\[
 (1-Z^p)A(Z)=-qL_p(Z)\pmod{Z^q-1}.                         \tag{2.8}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{\sum_{a=1}^{q-1}\chi_{xi}(a)h(\zeta^a)
   =-\frac{2\tau(\chi_{xi})}{q}F(xi^{-1}).}                 \tag{2.9}
\]

Indeed insert `h(zeta)=-A(zeta)/q`; the constant term disappears and the substitution `a -> ab` contributes `chi(b)^-1`.

Equation (2.7) removes a potentially zero **Gauss factor**. It says nothing that prevents the sum on the left of (2.9) from vanishing. Treating that remaining sum as another automatically nonzero Gauss sum would be incorrect.

## 3. PROVED uniform order-sensitive bounds, including full-rank families

### 3.1 A witness forces an entire nonzero Frobenius orbit

Let `b in {1,...,H-1}` satisfy `g^b=±(p-1) mod q`. It is nonzero because `p-1` is neither `1` nor `-1 mod q` for odd `p<q`.

The nonconstant difference (2.2) implies that some nonzero Fourier coefficient has root order `m` for which `m` does not divide `b`. Otherwise every Fourier term would be fixed by a shift of `b`, forcing `f_0=f_b`.

As a nonzero coefficient remains nonzero on its whole Frobenius orbit,

\[
 \boxed{r\ge d_*:=\min_{\substack{m\mid H,\ m>1\\m\nmid b}}
                     \operatorname{ord}_m(p).}             \tag{3.1}
\]

The set is nonempty: `m=H` is eligible. The conclusion concerns a **nonzero** orbit; counting zero orbits by itself would give no such lower bound.

### 3.2 A value-set constraint and a stronger finite divisor optimization

Let `s` be the number of distinct values among the `H` coefficients `f_i`. All values of `f` on `Z/qZ` occur there: evenness accounts for nonzero arguments and `f(0)=f(p)` accounts for zero.

For `a=1,...,p-1`, (2.1) gives

\[
 f(a)-f(a-p)=-q/a.
\]

These differences run through all `p-1` nonzero elements of `F_p`. A set of `s` values has at most `s(s-1)` ordered nonzero differences. Hence

\[
 s(s-1)\ge p-1,\qquad
 s\ge s_p:=\min\{s\ge1:s(s-1)\ge p-1\}.                    \tag{3.2}
\]

Let `S` be the set of orders of the nonzero, nontrivial Fourier coefficients. Their inverse Fourier expansion shows that the coefficient sequence has period dividing `lcm(S)`. More precisely, Fourier independence makes its least period equal to this lcm. Therefore `lcm(S)>=s_p`, and (3.1) says that some `m in S` does not divide `b`. Each such order contributes at least `d_m` independent coordinates.

Define the completely explicit finite number

\[
 \boxed{L_{\rm ov}:=
  \min_{\mathcal S}\sum_{m\in\mathcal S}d_m,}                \tag{3.3}
\]

where `S` ranges over subsets of the nontrivial divisors of `H` satisfying

\[
 \operatorname{lcm}(\mathcal S)\ge s_p,
 \qquad\exists m\in\mathcal S:\ m\nmid b.
\]

Then `r>=L_ov>=d_*`. This is a bound in terms of integer orders and divisors, independent of the unknown source integers and requiring no computation of `F`.

A finite algorithm is included in the script. Start with state `(period=1,witness=false)` of cost zero. For each divisor `m>1`, either omit it or replace the state by `(lcm(period,m), witness OR (m∤b))`, adding `d_m` to its cost. Retain the least cost for each state. Minimize over states meeting the two displayed conditions. The possible periods are divisors of `H`, so this is a finite divisor-state computation, not an optimization over unspecified characters.

This bound may still be small. A long possible character period does not mean that all characters of that order occur: only a Frobenius orbit of size `d_m` has been forced.

### 3.3 Full rank when the nontrivial spectrum is one orbit

**Theorem 3.1 — PROVED.** Under ALG, if `H` is prime and `ord_H(p)=H-1`, then `r=H-1`.

Proof: all nontrivial `H`th roots form one Frobenius orbit. Thus `Q_H=Phi_H` is irreducible over `F_p`. A common factor of `F` and `Q_H` would be all of `Q_H`. As `deg F<=H-1`, this would make `F` a scalar multiple of `Q_H`, i.e. make every coefficient equal. Equation (2.2) excludes this. Therefore the gcd is one. This proves (0.2).

**Source consequence.** For all pairs in BAND satisfying these extra arithmetic conditions, the C32-PACK theorem excludes a source. This covers the entire stated ratio interval **within this arithmetic subfamily**, not all primes in any ratio interval.

### 3.4 A prime-power family with an explicit fractional deficiency bound

**Theorem 3.2 — PROVED.** Suppose ALG, `H=ell^a` for a prime `ell`, `ord_H(p)=phi(H)`, and the class of `p-1` in `G` has order `ell`. Then (0.3) holds.

Proof: the last condition means `b=(H/ell)u`, with `ell∤u`. Among divisors `m` of `H`, the only one not dividing `b` is `H`. By (3.1), some primitive `H`th-root coefficient is nonzero. All primitive `H`th roots form a single Frobenius orbit, since its length is `ord_H(p)=phi(H)`. All of them are therefore nonzero. This contributes `phi(H)=H-H/ell` to the rank. The other orders can only increase it.

For `ell>=23`,

\[
 H-H/\ell\ge\frac{19}{20}(H-1),
\]

so C32's original 95-percent sufficient condition closes BAND for this subfamily. For `ell>=11`, the bound is at least `7(H-1)/8`, so the C32-RD supplement closes the same band for that larger subfamily. No primality/existence assertion about numbers `2ell^a+1` is being assumed.

## 4. PROVED rational-function bound: a second, independent finite mechanism

For integers `m` with `1<=m<p`, put

\[
 S(m)=\sum_{d=1}^m\varphi(d).
\]

Let `m_*` be the largest such integer with

\[
 (p-1)S(m_*)\le q-1,
\]

and set

\[
 \boxed{L_{\rm pole}=\max(1,m_*-1).}                         \tag{4.1}
\]

Then `r>=L_pole`. If **forward Wieferich** also holds, the stronger value `L_pole=max(1,m_*)` is valid; the implementation uses this improvement when available.

**Proof.** The finite logarithm has `L_p(1)=0` and `L_p'(1)=-1` in `F_p`. Hence

\[
 B_p(Z)=L_p(Z)/(1-Z)\in\mathbb F_p[Z],\quad
 \deg B_p=p-2,\quad B_p(1)=1.
\]

For `1<=a<=m`, consider

\[
 h_a(Z)=\frac{L_p(Z^a)}{1-Z^{pa}}
       =\frac{B_p(Z^a)}{(1-Z^a)^{p-1}}.                     \tag{4.2}
\]

These rational functions are independent over `F_p`. In a nonzero proposed relation, choose the largest index `a` with nonzero coefficient. At a primitive `a`th root in a finite splitting field, its denominator has a pole of order `p-1`, its numerator is `B_p(1)=1`, and no smaller index has such a pole. This contradicts the relation. For `a=1` the same argument applies at one. The condition `m<p` makes every relevant root simple before raising the denominator to `p-1`.

A common denominator can be chosen as

\[
 D_m(Z)=\left(\operatorname{lcm}_{1\le a\le m}(1-Z^a)\right)^{p-1},
 \qquad \deg D_m=(p-1)S(m).                                \tag{4.3}
\]

To verify the degree, roots of exact order `d<=m` occur once in the lcm, with `phi(d)` such roots. Roots of different exact orders are disjoint. This proves (4.3) by finite factorization, regardless of whether the individual cyclotomic factors split over `F_p`.

After multiplying a relation among the `h_a` by `D_m`, its numerator has degree at most `deg D_m-1`: the term with index `a` has degree `deg D_m-a`. If the relation vanishes in the **whole algebra** `F_p[Z]/Phi_q(Z)`, its numerator is divisible by the degree-`q-1` polynomial `Phi_q`. The denominators are units there because `a<q`. Under the displayed degree condition, the numerator has degree at most `q-2`, and must be the zero polynomial. Rational-function independence makes every coefficient zero.

It follows that `h_a-h_1`, `a=2,...,m`, are independent in that algebra. They belong to the circular Fermat-quotient augmentation image, since that quotient is `-2h_a` for `C_a`. The coefficient identity behind this statement is already (2.8), or the finite circular calculation in C32 §4.2. That image has dimension `r`. Hence `r>=m-1`, and (2.2) supplies the additional bound one.

For the forward-Wieferich improvement, the sum of the `H` conjugates of the circular Fermat quotient is the Fermat quotient of `prod C_a=q`, namely `f(0)=0`. As `H` is invertible modulo `p`, each image vector is then the average of its differences with the other image vectors. The full image and its augmentation image agree. Independence of the `m` vectors `h_a` themselves now gives `r>=m`. This is a one-dimension improvement of the pole bound, not a density estimate for the nontrivial spectrum.

**Important scope:** vanishing at just one factor of `Phi_q mod p` would not justify the degree-`q-1` divisibility. All components are used here.

This bound is uniform but quantitatively weak for PACK. Since `S(m)>=m`,

\[
 L_{\rm pole}\le \frac{q-1}{p-1}=\frac{2H}{p-1}.             \tag{4.4}
\]

Thus in the requested large-prime band it is far below a positive fraction of `H`. Section 6.2 makes the incompatibility with a PACK-sized lower bound precise. The pole argument does not prove the 95-percent, 7/8, or even half-rank assertion.

## 5. PROVED primitive-order trace certificates without a full gcd

The following bound often improves §§3–4 and can be checked by finite coefficient sums. Unlike `L_ov`, it does use `f`.

For each divisor `d|H`, form

\[
 S_d(i)=\sum_{0\le j<H,\ j\equiv i\pmod d}f_j\in\mathbb F_p.
\]

For each `m|H`, `m>1`, define the `m`-periodic vector

\[
 W_m(i)=\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(m/d)\,d\,S_d(i),\qquad0\le i<m.  \tag{5.1}
\]

Here `mu` is the elementary squarefree-sign function. Put

\[
 \mathcal A=\{m>1:m\mid H,\ W_m\ne0\},\qquad
 \boxed{L_{\rm tr}=\sum_{m\in\mathcal A}\operatorname{ord}_m(p).}
                                                               \tag{5.2}
\]

Then `r>=L_tr`.

**Proof with all projection factors retained.** In `R=F_p[T]/(T^H-1)`, the element

\[
 P_{\le d}=\frac dH\sum_{j=0}^{H/d-1}T^{dj}
\]

is one at a root `xi` with `xi^d=1` and zero at every other `H`th root. Its product with `F` has coefficient `(d/H)S_d(i)` at `T^i`.

Therefore

\[
 P_{=m}=\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(m/d)P_{\le d}
\]

is exactly the projector onto roots of order `m`. This follows also by the elementary identity `sum_(d|n) mu(d)=0` for `n>1` and one for `n=1`, obtained by expanding `prod_(ell|n)(1-1)`. The coefficients of `H F P_(=m)` are precisely the repeated vector `W_m`.

Thus `W_m!=0` means at least one order-`m` coefficient is nonzero. Its full Frobenius orbit contributes `d_m` coordinates. Different exact orders are disjoint, so their contributions add, proving (5.2).

More exactly, if `r_m` denotes the rank contribution of order `m`, then

\[
 r_m=0\iff W_m=0,\qquad
 r_m\in\{d_m,2d_m,\ldots,\varphi(m)\}\quad(W_m\ne0),
 \qquad r=\sum_{m\mid H,\,m>1}r_m.                          \tag{5.3}
\]

In particular, when `d_m=phi(m)`, the single nonzero-vector test certifies that **every** order-`m` character is nonzero. When there are many Frobenius orbits of that order, it certifies only one orbit. Equating these two conclusions would falsely turn this partial bound into a uniform high-rank theorem.

The computation of (5.1) uses sums by residue classes of indices and divisor inversion. It does not require a primitive root in a large extension field, a class-group computation, or factoring the full polynomial `F`. The script checks (5.3) independently by exact cyclotomic gcds on its smaller audit grid.

## 6. What these bounds give for PACK

### 6.1 Exact conversion, including which subfamilies are closed

Every occurrence of `r` on the left of original PACK is monotone. Thus (0.1) gives the entirely explicit sufficient test

\[
 \boxed{p^{2tL(p,q)}H^{2(t+1)H}>
 (H!)^{2(t+1)}q^{(t+1)(H-1)}(t+1)^{2tH}.}                  \tag{6.1}
\]

It contains only primes, integer orders, divisor sums, the coefficient recurrence, and integer powers. No unknown source integer or unproved prime-existence statement enters.

Theorems 3.1–3.2 close BAND on the precise arithmetic subfamilies listed in §0. For other pairs, (6.1) is a finite certified subrange test. **No entire interval specified solely by `q/p` is proved uniformly closed here.** The small-grid success count is not a theorem about an unbounded band.

For an interpretable regional inequality, suppose a proved lower bound has the form `r>=kappa(H-1)`. The elementary factorial estimate

\[
 \log H!\le H\log H-H+1+\log H
\]

shows that original PACK follows if

\[
 \log q<\frac{2\kappa t}{t+1}\log p
   +2\left(1-\frac{\log H}{H-1}\right)
   -\frac{2tH}{(t+1)(H-1)}\log(t+1).                       \tag{6.2}
\]

This is a sufficient numerical condition, not an asymptotic replacement for strict PACK. It applies to (0.3) using its actual rank fraction and to any later stronger bound. The script uses rational log enclosures to certify large examples, not floating-point signs.

### 6.2 A proved lower floor on what original PACK needs

Let

\[
 M_q=(H!/H^H)q^{(H-1)/2}.
\]

Original PACK is equivalent to

\[
 p^r>(t+1)^H M_q^{(t+1)/t}.                                \tag{6.3}
\]

The integral lower estimate `log H!>=H log H-H+1` gives

\[
 \log M_q\ge(H-1)(\tfrac12\log q-1)>0
\]

in BAND. Hence (6.3) requires `r log p>log M_q`, and, since `q>p`,

\[
 r>(H-1)\left(\frac12-\frac1{\log p}\right)
       >\frac25(H-1).                                    \tag{6.4}
\]

For the last step, `e<3` follows from its factorial series, and `p>100000>3^10` gives `log p>10`. This is only a necessary floor; the exact best `t` generally requires appreciably more.

Equation (4.4) is much smaller than (6.4) throughout BAND. Thus the all-pairs pole bound alone closes **no** part of the requested large-prime band by original PACK. The useful closures in this answer come from the large Frobenius blocks or verified primitive-order contributions, not from concealing this quantitative deficit.

### 6.3 A sharper use of the partial rank: half rank near the diagonal

The residue-degree count in the supplement can be combined with the **smaller cross-polytope representative bound** `M_q` from original C32, rather than its coarser `q^((H-1)/2)`. This gives a further elementary certificate. It is not being mislabeled as original PACK.

For every `delta>0`, define

\[
 \mathcal E_\delta(q)=
 \frac{(H/2+1+1/\delta)(q-1)^{-\delta}}
      {1-(q-1)^{-1-\delta}}
 +\frac{q^{-1-\delta}}{1-q^{-1-\delta}}.
\]

The finite ideal count proved in [the supplement §5.5][SUPP] is valid for **every** norm bound `B`: its logarithm is at most `(1+delta)log B+E_delta(q)`. Briefly, a prime ideal of residue degree `f` has norm at least `q-1`; degree-one rational primes are `±1 mod q`. The finite geometric local factors and `-log(1-u)<=u/(1-u)` bound their contribution by the displayed expression; the remaining sums are bounded by the elementary decreasing-function integral estimate and `sum_(n>=2)1/[n(n-1)]=1`. This counts finite ideals; it uses no prime-distribution or class-number formula.

Taking `B=M_q` therefore proves the source exclusion

\[
 \boxed{r\log p>(1+\delta)\log M_q+\mathcal E_\delta(q)
        \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{no source}.}       \tag{PACK-HYB}
\]

All source inputs are the same as C32-PACK and C32-RD. Only their already proved representative bound and finite counting estimate are combined.

**Theorem 6.1 — PROVED.** If `p>100000`, `p<q<=3p/2`, ALG holds, and `r>=(H-1)/2`, then `(PACK-HYB)` holds and excludes a source with the named inputs.

Here is a uniform proof with explicit constants. Put `D=log(q-1)=log(2H)` and `delta=1/D`. Since `H>=50000`, the elementary bounds `log H<sqrt(H)` and `log 2<1` give

\[
 1+\log H<H/100,\qquad 1+D<H/100.
\]

The first bound on `log H` follows by differentiating `sqrt(H)-log H` for `H>=4` and checking the endpoint; the last comparisons follow from `sqrt(H)>200`. The factorial upper bound yields

\[
 \log M_q<\tfrac12(H-1)\log q-\tfrac{99}{100}H.
\]

Moreover, `log q/D<1+1/(2H)` by `log(1+v)<v` and `D>1`, so

\[
 \delta\log M_q< H/2+1/4<501H/1000.
\]

We have `(q-1)^(-delta)=e^-1<3/8`; the last strict bound follows from the first four terms of the exponential series, whose sum is `8/3`. The first denominator in `E_delta` is greater than `99/100`. Hence

\[
 \mathcal E_\delta(q)
 <\frac{3}{8}\frac{100}{99}\frac{51H}{100}
      +\frac1{2H}
 =\frac{17H}{88}+\frac1{2H}<H/5.
\]

Combining the three estimates gives

\[
 (1+\delta)\log M_q+\mathcal E_\delta(q)
 <\tfrac12(H-1)\log q-\frac{289}{1000}H.                  \tag{6.5}
\]

If `r>=(H-1)/2` and `q/p<=3/2`, then `log(q/p)<1/2`; the difference from `r log p` in (6.5) is greater than

\[
 \frac{289}{1000}H-\frac{H-1}{4}
 =\frac{39}{1000}H+\frac14>0.
\]

This proves the theorem.

**Rank consequence with no unproved rank-fraction premise:** if `H` is prime and `ord_H(p)>=(H-1)/2`, then §3.1 supplies the requisite half rank. Thus **all** pairs satisfying

\[
 p>100000,\quad p<q\le3p/2,\quad
 H\text{ prime},\quad\operatorname{ord}_H(p)\ge(H-1)/2
\]

are excluded by this arithmetic-family theorem with the named source inputs. The case of order `(H-1)/2` is a genuine enlargement over the single-orbit full-rank family. No general half-rank theorem or infinitude of this subfamily is asserted.

**TESTED order-only fixture:** `(p,q,H)=(100003,100103,50051)` are prime and `ord_H(p)=25025=(H-1)/2`. The script verifies `p^25025=1 mod H`, and the powers at exponents `25025/s`, for `s=5,7,11,13`, are `33912,34556,32179,46553`, none one. It certifies a lower margin `>14439` using the lower rank `25025` and (6.5), **without using the actual polynomial rank**. Both DW residues, `5123953715` and `5162411814`, differ from one: this is an implementation and theorem control, not an unresolved source candidate.

## 7. Decisions on the proposed shortcuts, with falsifiable controls

### 7.1 Controlled polynomial degree and sparsity

**PROVED distinction.** The polynomial `L_p(Z)` has degree `p-1` in an **additive qth-root variable**. The polynomial whose zeros determine `r` is `F(T)` in a **multiplicative Hth-root character variable**. The Gauss formula (2.9) is the exact transform between them; it is not a degree-preserving substitution.

The additive bound is in fact strong. `L_p` has the distinct roots zero and one, both simple, so it has at most `p-3` zeros among the nontrivial `q`th roots. Equation (2.8) shows that the additive circulant generated by `f` has rank at least

\[
 q-p+2.                                                     \tag{7.1}
\]

Its trivial additive coefficient is zero by (2.3). This proves (7.1), but not a corresponding bound for `r`. The small audit checks this additive gcd bound independently.

**TESTED actual-antiderivative control:** at `(p,q)=(3,281)`, `L_p` has degree two, while the multiplicative gcd has degree five, with ascending coefficients `(1,2,1,2,0,1)`. Thus even the assertion that the multiplicative deficiency is bounded by the degree of `L_p` is false for the actual family, not just for an arbitrary test function. This is a small-prime control, not a counterexample to a large-prime rank fraction.

**PROVED countermodel to rank transfer.** Over `F_7`, take the nontrivial quadratic character on `F_13`, extended by zero at zero. It is even. Every nontrivial additive Fourier coefficient is a nonzero multiple of its Gauss sum, so its additive circulant has rank 12. Its multiplicative circulant is an outer product and has augmentation rank one. The script verifies both ranks. This model does not satisfy the special recurrence (2.1); it refutes precisely the inference from additive rank alone.

Nor does sparsity alone count few zeros: when `H` is even, `T^(H/2)-1` has only two terms but vanishes at `H/2-1` nontrivial `H`th roots. The special coefficients of `F` would have to do the additional work.

### 7.2 Circulant and Hankel structure

The matrix `C_(i,j)=f_(i-j)` is diagonalized by the Fourier basis, and replacing `j` by `-j` turns it into the corresponding Hankel matrix. Thus their ranks agree; restricting away from the constant vector gives exactly (1.1).

This is an exact structural formula, but squarefree diagonalization does not prove that most diagonal entries are nonzero. Theorems 3.1–3.2 use it productively only when a nonzero witness controls a large irreducible block. Section 5 provides an additional finite projection test for more blocks.

### 7.3 Energy does not supply positivity in characteristic p

Let `bar f=H^-1 sum f_i`. Orthogonality gives the exact finite identity

\[
 \sum_{\xi^H=1,\ \xi\ne1}F(\xi)F(\xi^{-1})
       =H\sum_i(f_i-\bar f)^2\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p.    \tag{7.2}
\]

There is no ordering or positivity in this field. At `(p,q)=(3,11)`, with `g=2`,

`F` has coefficient vector `(2,1,2,1,1)`,

and the right side of (7.2) is zero, although the augmentation rank is the full value four. This is an exact, tested counterexample to using a positive-energy argument without retaining an integer lift and its modular cancellations.

Likewise, zeros need not be disjoint from their reciprocal partners. At `(5,157)` the exact gcd is `T^2+4T+1`, which is reciprocal. The script verifies this. A pairing argument that silently assumes at most one zero in each inverse pair is invalid.

### 7.4 The order-division shortcut and the correct orbit statement

At `(11,31)`,

\[
 H-1=14,\quad d=2,\quad\operatorname{ord}_{31}(11)=30,
\]

and the gcd is `T^2+10T+5`. Thus the proposed interpretation
`d <= (H-1)/ord_q(p)` is false. Here the zero roots are `3,9 in F_11`, both of order five; their relevant Frobenius degree is `ord_5(11)=1`, not 30.

In general, `d_m` divides the **number of zero roots of exact order m**. An upper bound of `phi(m)/d_m` counts zero **orbits** only; multiplying back by the orbit size recovers the possible `phi(m)` zero roots. Frobenius stability alone does not divide the total deficiency by an order. The positive bound (3.1) instead forces at least one **nonzero** orbit.

### 7.5 Small counterexamples to unrestricted high-rank statements

Selected exact controls are:

| p,q | H-1 | rank | deficiency | gcd, ascending coefficients |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 5,17 | 7 | 6 | 1 | 2,1 |
| 7,37 | 17 | 13 | 4 | 4,4,0,1,1 |
| 11,31 | 14 | 12 | 2 | 5,10,1 |
| 29,41 | 19 | 17 | 2 | 17,18,1 |

All fail the unrestricted 95-percent assertion. The first and third also fail 7/8 and are below their small-prime `B(p)` bound. These are counterexamples to removing the **large-p** qualifier, not to the assertion actually needed for the remaining band.

At `(29,41)`, `41^28=1 mod29^2`, but `29^40=370 mod41^2`. Thus forward Wieferich alone does not repair the unrestricted 95-percent assertion. This does not refute the version with both congruences or with `p>100000`.

### 7.6 What has been extracted from source restrictions

Forward DW is handled exactly by (0.4). Under reverse DW, if `d=ord_q(p)`, binomial lifting gives `p^d=1 mod q^2`. For `d=3,4,6`, the `q^2` divisor would have to lie in, respectively, `p^2+p+1`, `p^2+1`, `p^2-p+1`, all smaller than `q^2`. Orders one and two are excluded by `p<q`. This is the elementary restriction recorded in C26; it is not an estimate for the character orders dividing `H`.

The rank depends only on `p,q`. The source size and integrality might force further conditions on these exponents, but no additional nontrivial-character restriction strong enough for PACK is proved here. The two DW-compatible exact computations in §6.4 are evidence only at their stated pairs. They do not establish a uniform nonvanishing density.

## 8. Exact remaining statement and closure map

### 8.1 What remains after the proved bounds

For original PACK define

\[
 \mathcal P_t(p,q,r)=r\log p-
 \frac{t+1}{t}\log\left((H!/H^H)q^{(H-1)/2}\right)-H\log(t+1).
\]

The exact remaining rank target is:

> **CONJECTURED / OPEN `RANK-PACK`:** for every exponent pair in BAND satisfying all necessary source restrictions, the explicit nontrivial Fourier support of `F` has size `r` for which `P_t(p,q,r)>0` for some positive integer `t`.

The stronger uniform inequalities `d <= (H-1)/20` for the original 95-percent surrogate, or `d <= (H-1)/8` for the supplement's residue-degree count, would suffice in their respective stated frameworks. None is proved here uniformly. Finite tests do not replace that quantifier.

In the primitive-order decomposition (5.3), the gap is particularly precise: a nonzero `W_m` presently guarantees only one of the `phi(m)/d_m` Frobenius orbits. To finish this route, one must control how many of the remaining orbits can vanish, using the special antiderivative coefficients, or derive a stronger source-specific restriction. Merely changing from polynomials to character sums, circulants, or Gauss sums does not supply that estimate.
