### 3.4 How much cancellation does the equation itself identify?

There is a useful formal statement with a narrow, exact scope. It concerns separated series at infinity with coefficients independent of the local parameter.

Put `u_infty=1/X`, `w_infty=1/Y`, and introduce a formal parameter `tau`:
\[
u_\infty=\tau^q,\qquad
w_\infty=\tau^p(1-\delta\tau^{pq})^{-1/q}.
\tag{3.11}
\]
Then
\[
w_\infty^q-u_\infty^p-\delta u_\infty^pw_\infty^q=0.
\tag{3.12}
\]
**PROVED.** Below weight `pq`, all leading orders of bivariate monomials `u_infty^i w_infty^j` are distinct. Indeed `qi+pj<pq` implies `i<p`, `j<q`; equality of two orders would make `i-i'` a multiple of `p` and `j-j'` a multiple of `q`, hence both zero. The first equation-driven overlap is between `u_infty^p` and `w_infty^q`.

More exactly, over a characteristic-zero coefficient field containing the necessary roots of unity,
\[
\boxed{k[[u_\infty]]\cap k[[w_\infty]]
=k[[u_\infty^p]]=k[[w_\infty^q]].}
\tag{3.13}
\]
The substitution `tau→zeta_p tau` fixes `w_infty` and rotates `u_infty` by a primitive `p`th root. A series in `u_infty` fixed by this substitution has only powers `u_infty^(pi)`. Conversely (3.12) makes `u_infty^p` and `w_infty^q` mutually invertible formal power-series coordinates. This proves (3.13).

For cutoff `tau^W`, the dimensions of the two separated truncated series spaces are `1+floor((W-1)/q)` and `1+floor((W-1)/p)`. Their intersection has dimension
\[
\boxed{1+\left\lfloor\frac{W-1}{pq}\right\rfloor.}
\tag{3.14}
\]
The same invariance argument works coefficient by coefficient modulo `tau^W`; the powers `w_infty^(qj)` form a triangular basis for the multiples of `pq`. Thus their sum has dimension
\[
1+\left\lfloor\frac{W-1}{p}\right\rfloor
 +\left\lfloor\frac{W-1}{q}\right\rfloor
 -\left\lfloor\frac{W-1}{pq}\right\rfloor.

### 6.2 A factorial-safe finite non-power lemma

For an integer `k>=0`, put
\[
\mathcal Q_k=p^{k+v_p(k!)},\qquad

\boxed{Y>2+\mathcal Q_H\mathcal B_H.}
\tag{SIZE+}

**Theorem 6.1 — PROVED.** Under `SIZE+`, the source map
\[
\iota:E\longrightarrow F^*/F^{*p},\qquad
\sum r_a\sigma_a\longmapsto\left[\prod_a B_a^{r_a}\right]
\tag{6.3}
\]
is injective.

**Proof, including nonvanishing and the case of truncation indices at or above `p`.** Suppose a nonzero exponent class gives a `p`th-power relation. Lift the full even exponents to
\[
0\le n_c<p,\quad n_c=n_{-c},\quad
\sum_{c=1}^{q-1}n_c=p k.
\tag{6.4}
\]

\[
1\le k\le H.
\tag{6.5}
\]
Moreover `k` is even, since the exponents are paired and `p` is odd.

There would be `rho∈F` with
\[
\rho^p=\prod_{c=1}^{q-1}(\mathcal X-\eta^c)^{n_c}.
\tag{6.6}
\]
It is an algebraic integer, by its monic equation. The right side is totally positive and `F` has no nontrivial `p`th roots of unity, so complex conjugation fixes `rho`. Every conjugate is the unique positive real `p`th root of its corresponding product.

Define the formal root with constant one
\[
P(T)=\prod_c(1-\eta^cT)^{n_c},\qquad
P(T)^{1/p}=\sum_{j\ge0}b_jT^j.
\tag{6.7}
\]
The finite coefficient expression is
\[
b_j=\sum_{m=0}^j\binom{1/p}{m}[T^j](P(T)-1)^m.
\tag{6.8}
\]
In
\[
\binom{1/p}{m}
 =\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}(1-ip)}{p^m m!},
\]
every denominator prime other than `p` cancels: for `ell!=p`, the numerator progression has at least `floor(m/ell^r)` multiples of each `ell^r`. The exact remaining denominator is `p^(m+v_p(m!))`. It follows that
\[
\mathcal Q_j b_j\in\mathcal O_F.
\tag{6.9}
\]
Let `S_1=sum_c n_c eta^c`. The term `m=j` in (6.8) has coefficient `(-S_1)^j`; every term with `m<j` acquires an extra factor of `p` after multiplying by `mathcal Q_j`. Thus
\[
\mathcal Q_j b_j\equiv
\left(\frac{j!}{p^{v_p(j!)}}\right)^{-1}(-S_1)^j
\pmod{p\mathcal O_F}.
\tag{6.10}
\]
The inverse is of a `p`-adic unit. Formula (6.10) does not drop factorial valuations when `j>=p`.

For every embedding, the binomial majorant gives
\[
|b_j|\le C_j:=\binom{k+j-1}{j}.
\tag{6.11}
\]
Indeed the factor exponents are `n_c/p∈[0,1)`, the absolute binomial coefficients are bounded by the rising-factorial coefficients, and multiplication gives the majorant `(1-T)^(-sum n_c/p)=(1-T)^(-k)`. Its geometric tail proves convergence at `T=1/mathcal X`. Coefficient multiplication proves its `p`th power is `P`; realness and positivity select precisely `rho/mathcal X^k`. This uses only the explicit power-series estimate, or equivalently finite truncations with that geometric remainder, not analytic continuation.

For `j>=k+1`, `C_(j+1)/C_j=(k+j)/(j+1)<2`, and `C_(k+1)=mathcal B_k`. Therefore
\[
\left|\rho-\sum_{j=0}^k b_j\mathcal X^{k-j}\right|
 \le\frac{\mathcal B_k}{Y-2}
\tag{6.12}
\]
at every embedding. Consequently the algebraic integer
\[
\Delta=\mathcal Q_k\left(\rho-\sum_{j=0}^k b_j\mathcal X^{k-j}\right)
\]
has house less than one, by `SIZE+` and the monotonicity of `mathcal Q_k mathcal B_k`. Its integer norm forces `Delta=0`.

**This equality at one integer is not being converted into a polynomial identity.** Instead reduce it modulo `p`. The term `mathcal Q_k rho` and all terms `mathcal Q_k b_j`, `j<k`, vanish: explicitly, `mathcal Q_k/mathcal Q_j∈pZ` for `j<k`, since `j+v_p(j!)` increases strictly, and (6.9) applies. Equation (6.10) then gives
\[
S_1^k=0\quad\text{in }\mathcal O_F/p\mathcal O_F.
\tag{6.13}
\]
This ring is reduced. For an elementary justification, the discriminant of the cyclotomic power basis is, up to sign, `q^(q-2)`: use
`Phi_q'(eta)=q eta^(q-1)/(eta-1)` and take the product over conjugates. Its index in the integral order is therefore a `p`-unit. The quotient at `p` is `F_p[T]/Phi_q(T)`, and `Phi_q` is squarefree there because `p!=q`. Thus (6.13) forces `S_1=0 mod p`.

The polynomial `sum_(c=1)^(q-1)n_c T^c` has degree at most `q-1` and zero constant coefficient. If it is a multiple of `Phi_q`, the degree makes it a constant multiple, and the zero constant coefficient makes that multiple zero. Hence all `n_c=0 mod p`, contradicting the chosen nonzero exponent. This proves Theorem 6.1. ∎

On a real element the coefficients at `a,-a` combine. Hence
\[
\boxed{\Xi_c|_{F^+}\equiv
D_{s_0}-\frac{2k}{q}N_+\pmod p.}
\tag{7.10}
\]
The factor two and the negative sign of the constant are essential.

### 7.4 What has now been proved about this particular source radical

Because `alpha^(N_+)=1`, equation (7.10) gives, modulo `p`th powers,
\[
[\alpha^{\Xi_c}]=[\alpha^{D_{s_0}}]
 =[B_1^{(\sigma_g-1)D_{s_0}}].
\tag{7.11}
\]
The last exponent is nonzero in `E` by (7.7). The source-radical injection of §6 and the norm injection (6.14) prove
\[
\boxed{\alpha^{\Xi_c}\notin F^{*p},\qquad
\alpha^{\Xi_c}\notin L^{*p}.}
\tag{7.12}
\]
This conclusion is **PROVED under the hypothetical source premises** throughout `p<q<p^2`. It does not contradict any other elementary assertion proved here. It identifies exactly the element to which the missing global power assertion would have to apply.

**TESTED example of the finite operator only.** At `p=5,q=7`, choose `g=3`; then `c=11,k=2`. In generator order on `G^+`,
\[
D_{s_0}=(3,1,1),\qquad
(\sigma_g-1)D_{s_0}=(3,2,0)\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_5^3.
\]
This independently checked fixture verifies the operator orientation and nonzero exponent. It is not an example of a Catalan source or a produced radical root.

### 7.5 CONJECTURED / OPEN: the single most promising next step

The next target is the following one explicit source-specific power construction:

> **Single reflected-root target.** Under the hypothetical source premises in `p<q<p^2`, with `alpha`, `c`, and `Xi_c` exactly as in (7.1), (7.2), and (7.8), construct an algebraic number `W∈Q(zeta_(pq))` by finite elementary identities and prove
> \[
> \boxed{\alpha^{\Xi_c}=W^p.}
> \tag{SR}
> \]

**Status: CONJECTURED as an elementary construction, unproved here.** The classical reflected bridge would supply the power assertion for the corresponding primary singular radical. It is not an allowed concluding input, and it has not been used in any theorem above. An attempted proof of `(SR)` must give the element or finite product identities establishing the global root; local roots, `p`-multiple valuations, and ordinary minus carry identities do not establish it.

If `(SR)` is proved, (7.12) gives the contradiction immediately. Only this one radical and this one operator are required: no all-character vanishing theorem, all-radical annihilation statement, or all-`c` assertion is needed. The elementary construction has removed the separate need to produce a radical from an ideal-class component. It has **not** replaced reflected annihilation.

This is a narrower concrete target for the next step, rather than a claim that restating it constitutes a new elementary solution. The source-product injectivity and nonzero finite operator explain both why it would suffice and why an unjustified local-to-global power claim at this point would assume the desired contradiction.
