# C38 — Two PACK routes, cofactor-residue constraints, and a dual-period half-rank family

Written: 2026-10-29. **Status: PARTIAL.** The two PACK obstructions remain simultaneously open in general. New **PROVED** results are the opposite-cofactor residue constraint, a dual harmonic period bound, and a complete large prime-power family in which the dual PACK route excludes a source. Exact finite computations and their reproducible source code are included alongside this report. No unconditional all-band Catalan conclusion is claimed.

## 0. Verdict and main results

Let `SOURCE` mean a hypothetical nontrivial solution of `x^p-y^q=1` satisfying the filed Cassels/divisibility input, where odd primes `100000<p<q<B(p)=sqrt(5/12)p^(3/2)+2p`. Define

\[
 H=(q-1)/2,\quad n=H-1,\quad m=(p-1)/2,\quad s=m-1,
\]
\[
 h=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}(V_p\cap U_{k,0}),\qquad
 h^\vee=\dim_{\mathbb F_q}(V_q\cap U_{k^\vee,0}),
\]
where `k=Q(zeta_q)^+` and `k^vee=Q(zeta_p)^+`. C36/C37 give exactly `p^(n-h)` and `q^(s-h^vee)` divided-ideal classes. The finite ideal upper bounds force

\[
\boxed{h>A=n\left(1-\frac{\log q}{2\log p}\right)+\frac{19H}{20\log p}>H/4,}
\tag{0.1}
\]
\[
\boxed{h^\vee>A^\vee=s\left(1-\frac{\log p}{2\log q}\right)+\frac{19m}{20\log q}>s/2.}
\tag{0.2}
\]
Both inequalities are **PROVED conditional necessary consequences** of `SOURCE`; they are not assertions that sources exist. Their simultaneous failure remains the target.

**New theorem 1 (PROVED, using the actual common source).** Let `u,v` be the source cofactors in

\[
\begin{split}
 x-1&=p^{q-1}a^q,&\Phi_p(x)&=pv^q,&y&=pav,\\
 y+1&=q^{p-1}b^p,&\Phi_q(-y)&=qu^p,&x&=qbu.
\end{split}\tag{0.3}
\]

Every rational prime `ell|v` satisfies `ell≡1 (mod p)`. For **every** source class `z∈V_p` and every prime `lambda` of `k` over `ell`, `z≡1 (mod lambda)` for its canonical augmentation-zero representative. Consequently any primary-unit representative of `z` is a `p`-th-power residue at `lambda`. The symmetric statement holds for every `ell|u`: `ell≡1 (mod q)` and every source class in `V_q` reduces to `1`, so any unit representative is a `q`-th-power residue. These are genuine joint restrictions from the *other* allocation, not abstract disjoint-field identities. They yield the explicit restricted-overlap sufficient criteria of §2. No uniform lower bound for their detecting ranks is proved.

**New theorem 2 (PROVED, entirely finite).** Write `q=ap+b`, `1≤b<p`, and let `F_{q,p}` be the dual rank polynomial. If its inverse Fourier coefficient sequence on the nonzero residues of `F_p` has multiplicative period dividing `D|m`, then

\[
\boxed{D^2(a+1)\ge(p-3)/2.}\tag{0.4}
\]

In particular, in the full remaining band `p>100000`, the dual sequence is **not** of multiplicative period one or two.

**New theorem 3 (PROVED exclusion family).** Suppose additionally

\[
\boxed{p=4\ell+1\ \text{prime},\quad \ell\text{ an odd prime},\quad
 \operatorname{ord}_{\ell}(q)=\ell-1.}\tag{0.5}
\]

The dual Frobenius orbits contain two orbits of length `ell-1`, attached to primitive `ell`th and `2ell`th roots. If both vanished, `F_{q,p}` would have only the degree-one components at `1,-1`, forcing period two, contrary to (0.4). Thus

\[
\boxed{r_{q,p}\ge \ell-1,\quad
 d_{q,p}\le\ell,\quad h^\vee\le\ell<A^\vee.}\tag{0.6}
\]

This contradicts (0.2), excluding sources throughout the family. Notice that `d_{q,p}≤s/2` is **not** claimed: `s=2ell-1`, so `d≤ell` is half a dimension too weak for that convenient cutoff, but is strong enough for the sharper actual ideal count. This is an important integer-rounding distinction.

**TESTED exact coverage of family (0.5):** among eligible prime `q` in the entire band, the family contains **328,935 of 1,305,952** pairs for `p=100469, ell=25117` (25.1873729%) and **433,252 of 1,308,570** for `p=100613, ell=25153` (33.1088134%). These counts are for this *single declared theorem*, not a union with C35's earlier predicates. Its overlap with those predicates was not recomputed, and no combined-union count is asserted.

**TESTED exact dual deficiencies:** the three directly computed large-band pairs `(100003,100019)`, `(100003,100057)`, `(100469,100483)` have `d_{q,p}=0`. The previously stored C37 certificate additionally gives `d_{20495473,100469}=0`. In the complete grid of 595 odd-prime pairs `3≤p<q≤151`, `d_{q,p}=0` in 581 cases and `d_{q,p}=1` in 14; no higher deficiency occurs in that grid. Exhaustively among odd-prime pairs `3≤p<q<6000` the double-Wieferich filter returns `(83,4871)`, with exact dual deficiency zero. The double-Wieferich fixture is outside the band and is not a Catalan source.

**OPEN:** neither `h≤floor A` nor `h^vee≤floor A^vee` is proved for *every* possible source. Neither character inversion nor ordinary Frobenius pairing proves the global dual half bound. The residue ranks in theorem 1 are the new source-specific missing arithmetic condition.

## 2. The cofactor-prime test: a source-dependent joint constraint

### 2.1 Source allocation and exact residue statement — PROVED

Let `ell|v`. Because `Phi_p(x)=p v^q`, `ell∤p` by CASS and `ell|Phi_p(x)`. The identity `(x-1)Phi_p(x)=x^p-1` shows `x^p≡1 mod ell`. If `x≡1 mod ell`, then `Phi_p(x)≡p mod ell`, impossible. Hence `x` has **exact** order `p` modulo `ell`, and by finite group Lagrange `ell≡1 mod p`. Also `y=pav` makes `y≡0 mod ell`.

In `k=Q(eta)^+`, for `eta=zeta_q`, the first source has

\[
B_c=(-y-\eta^c)(-y-\eta^{-c}),\quad A_c=B_c/B_1.
\]

At every prime `lambda|ell` in `k`, `ell≠q`; reduction makes sense for each integral `B_c`, and

\[
B_c\equiv (-\eta^c)(-\eta^{-c})=1\pmod\lambda,
\qquad A_c\equiv1\pmod\lambda.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Every canonical augmentation-zero product `z` of the `B_c` therefore has residue one, including representatives with negative exponents (each factor is a unit at `lambda`). Its divided divisor has no `lambda` component. If `[z]` is a unit class, there exist `epsilon∈O_k^×` and `beta∈k^×` with `z=epsilon beta^p`. Since `z` and `epsilon` are units at `lambda`, `beta` is also a local unit. Thus

\[
\boxed{\epsilon\equiv\beta^{-p}\pmod\lambda.}\tag{2.2}
\]

The residue class of `epsilon` in `k(lambda)^×/(k(lambda)^×)^p` is trivial. The target really has `p`-torsion because `p|ell-1|ell^f-1`.

Symmetrically, if `ell|u`, then `ell≠q`, `ell|Phi_q(-y)`, and the reduction of `-y` has exact order `q` modulo `ell`: `(-y)^q≡1`, but `-y≡1` would make `Phi_q(-y)≡q`, impossible. Hence `ell≡1 mod q`. The relation `x=qbu` gives `x≡0 mod ell`. In `k^vee=Q(zeta_p)^+`, each dual factor

\[
C_c=(x-\zeta_p^c)(x-\zeta_p^{-c})\equiv1\pmod\lambda
\]

at every prime over `ell`, so the corresponding unit must be a `q`-th-power residue. This proves both assertions without assuming any auxiliary splitting prime exists: every listed prime is a divisor of the actual opposite cofactor.

### 2.2 Residue detector modules and the exact sufficient condition — PROVED

Let `U_{k,0}` be the primary unit classes modulo `p`th powers. For the finite set of rational prime divisors of `v`, define

\[
\mathcal R_v:U_{k,0}\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_{\ell\mid v}\bigoplus_{\lambda\mid\ell}
 k(\lambda)^\times/k(\lambda)^{\times p},\quad
[\epsilon]\longmapsto([\epsilon\bmod\lambda])_{\lambda},
\tag{2.3}
\]

and define `R_u` similarly on `U_{k^vee,0}` with exponent `q`. They are well-defined additive maps of `F_p`- or `F_q`-vector spaces: multiplying `epsilon` by a global relevant prime power changes each residue by the same power. The direct sums are finite and can be represented by finite multiplicative-group calculations at each actual source prime. Including all primes above a rational `ell` makes the kernels invariant under the appropriate cyclic Galois groups.

Equation (2.2) proves the new source-derived constraints

\[
\boxed{J_p\subseteq\ker\mathcal R_v,
\quad J_q\subseteq\ker\mathcal R_u.}\tag{2.4}
\]

Consequently, with

\[
K_v=\dim\ker\mathcal R_v,\qquad K_u=\dim\ker\mathcal R_u,
\]

we have the actual strengthened bounds

\[
\boxed{h\le\min(d_{p,q},K_v),\quad
h^\vee\le\min(d_{q,p},K_u).}\tag{2.5}
\]

If `K_v≤floor A(p,q)` **or** `K_u≤floor A^vee(p,q)`, the corresponding PACK inequality contradicts SOURCE. This is a **PROVED conditional closing theorem** with a new source-dependent input: a sufficiently detecting set of primes from an actual opposite cofactor. No such uniform detection-rank theorem is proved. If `u` or `v` were one, the corresponding empty residue test has the entire unit space as its kernel; the theorem does not silently assume a nonempty set.

The source gives a more operational component target: for every simple zero component of `F_{p,q}` that could intersect `V_p`, find `ell|v` and `lambda|ell` at which its nonzero unit image is not a pth-power residue, or find enough components of total degree to lower the possible intersection below `A`. The analogous target holds on the dual side. These are **OPEN**, and are strictly stronger arithmetic statements than the mere congruences `ell≡1 mod p` or `ell≡1 mod q`.

### 2.3 Split-prime congruences alone do not detect a unit — exact counterfixture

The actual primary circular unit of C36 at `(p,q)=(5,17)`, with exponent vector `(2,-1,-2,1,2,-1,-2,1)`, is a *global* non-fifth-power. At the split rational prime `1021`, evaluating `zeta_17→81` gives `epsilon=56`, and `56^204=995≠1 (mod 1021)`; this certifies global nonpower.

At the **different** completely split prime `1871≡1 mod 85`, the exact same global unit evaluates to `1548` at `zeta_17→81` and satisfies `1548^374=1 (mod 1871)`. Checking all 16 primitive 17th-root residue embeddings gives a fifth-power residue at **every prime above 1871**. Both assertions are recomputed by `C38_small_checks.py`. The example is not a hypothetical source and does not assert `1871|v` for one. It refutes only the attempted source-free inference that congruence/splitting alone forces detection of every nonpower unit. The arithmetic gap is to prove that the **specific** prime support furnished by the common Catalan source cannot all be blind.

## 3. Dual harmonic period theorem

### 3.1 The exact grouped recurrence — PROVED

Put `q=ap+b` with `1≤b<p`. Define in `F_q`

\[
A_t=\sum_{\substack{1\le j<q\\j\equiv t\pmod p}}j^{-1}.
\]

Because `sum_{j=1}^{q-1}j^{-1}=0 (mod q)`, advancing the argument of (1.1) by `q` changes each least residue by `+1`, except when it wraps from `p-1` to zero. The latter happens precisely for `j≡t+q mod p`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{f(t+q)-f(t)=-pA_{t+q}\in\mathbb F_q.}\tag{3.1}
\]

No restriction that `q<p` is used. This is the essential correction to a naively swapped single-jump formula.

For `1≤t≤p-1`, the index `j` has the form `t+kp`. Thus

\[
 A_t=\begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{a}(t+kp)^{-1},& 1\le t<b,\\
 \displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{a-1}(t+kp)^{-1},& b\le t\le p-1.
 \end{cases}\tag{3.2}
\]

All inverses are mod `q`; all displayed integer denominators lie between one and `q-1`. Each expression is the evaluation of a rational function

\[
R_e(Z)=\sum_{k=0}^{e-1}(Z+kp)^{-1}\in\mathbb F_q(Z),\qquad e=a\text{ or }a+1.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Its `e` simple poles `Z=-kp` are pairwise distinct modulo `q`, because `0<|k-l|p<q`. It is not constant: at any pole, all other summands are regular and the remaining one has a nonzero residue. If `R_e(Z)=c` for a given `c∈F_q`, multiply by the degree-`e` denominator. The resulting numerator has degree at most `e` and is a nonzero polynomial. Hence **at most `e` distinct arguments** can attain any fixed value `c` (away from its poles). This is a finite root-count argument; no estimate for distribution of prime divisors is involved.

### 3.2 Many values, hence no short multiplicative period — PROVED

Suppose the cyclic sequence `i↦f(g^i)` has period dividing `D|m`. Since `g` runs once through the nonzero residues modulo sign and `f(-t)=f(t)` (the finite identity checked in C36/C37), `f` takes at most `D` values on `F_p^*`.

Choose the longer interval among `{1,...,b-1}` and `{b,...,p-1}` in (3.2). It has at least `(p-1)/2` members. Remove, if present, the one argument `t≡q (mod p)`, so the remaining set `S` has at least `(p-3)/2` members. For each `t∈S`, both `t` and `t-q` are nonzero modulo `p`; (3.1) says

\[
A_t=-p^{-1}\bigl(f(t)-f(t-q)\bigr).
\]

It therefore takes at most `D²` values on `S`. But §3.1 shows that each of those values occurs at most `a+1` times on the chosen interval. Thus

\[
|S|\le D^2(a+1),\qquad
\boxed{D^2(a+1)\ge(p-3)/2.}\tag{3.4}
\]

This proves the period theorem, including `D=1` or `D=2`, without claiming anything about individual Bernoulli numbers.

In BAND, `q<p^(3/2)` gives `a<sqrt(p)`, and for `p>100000`,

\[
8(a+1)<8(\sqrt p+1)<p-3.
\]

The last inequality holds at `p=100000` and its difference is strictly increasing thereafter (differentiate the elementary real expression, or square the positive sides). With `D=2`, (3.4) would require `8(a+1)≥p-3`, a contradiction. So **multiplicative period at most two is impossible throughout BAND**.

### 3.3 Fourier-orbit interpretation and the new prime-power family — PROVED

Since `q>p>m`, `q∤m`; the polynomial `T^m-1` has distinct roots over an algebraic closure of `F_q`. Finite Fourier inversion on the cyclic group of order `m` gives

\[
f(g^{i+D})=f(g^i)\text{ for every }i
\iff (T^D-1)F(T)=0\pmod{T^m-1}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Indeed evaluate both sides at each `m`th root and use that the Fourier matrix is invertible, since its determinant is the nonzero Vandermonde product and `m` is invertible in `F_q`. A component of order `t|m` contributes a Frobenius orbit of degree `ord_t(q)`.

Take `m=2ell` with `ell` an odd prime and `ord_ell(q)=ell-1`. There are four types of roots of `T^m-1`:

* `1` and `-1`, each of orbit degree one;
* primitive `ell`th roots, one Frobenius orbit of degree `ell-1`;
* primitive `2ell`th roots, one orbit also of degree `ell-1` because `q` is odd.

If `F` vanished on both large orbits, its spectrum would be supported only on roots `1,-1`. Equation (3.5) would make the coefficient sequence periodic with period two, impossible by (3.4). Hence **at least one whole big orbit is nonvanishing**. Because `r_{q,p}` counts nontrivial nonvanishing roots, it follows that

\[
r_{q,p}\ge\ell-1,
\quad d_{q,p}=m-1-r_{q,p}\le(2\ell-1)-(\ell-1)=\ell.
\tag{3.6}
\]

The dual source/unit overlap obeys `h^vee≤d≤ell`. But by C37,

\[
A^\vee>\frac{s}{2}+\frac{19m}{20\log q}
=\ell-\frac12+\frac{19m}{20\log q}>\ell.
\]

The final inequality follows from `q<p²`, `m=(p-1)/2`, and the elementary `19m>10 log q` for every `p>100000` (e.g. `log q<2log p`, and `p-1>2log p` already for `p≥3`). Thus `h^vee≤ell<A^vee`, contradicting (0.2). This completes the family proof.

The same reasoning gives a smaller **PROVED** family: if `m` itself is an odd prime and `ord_m(q)=m-1`, all nontrivial roots form one orbit. If that orbit vanished, the sequence would be constant (period one), excluded by (3.4). Thus `r_{q,p}=m-1`, `d_{q,p}=0`, and the dual PACK route closes the pair. This is the exact swapped analogue of the prime-order family in C33, with the nonconstancy supplied here by the grouped recurrence even when `q>p`.

**Limitation.** The period theorem does not say that every primitive-order orbit must be nonzero. For general composite `m`, a sequence can have large multiplicative period while its nontrivial Fourier support occupies only a small part of dimension `m-1`; thus a period lower bound is not a global dual half-rank theorem. Nor is there a multiplicity argument pairing every zero eigenvalue at `zeta` with nonzero at `zeta^-1`: inverse characters are not generally in the same Frobenius orbit.

## 4. Why the two Bernoulli families and ordinary norms do not themselves force a joint contradiction

### 4.1 Different conductor-`pq` character families — PROVED structural observation

The character forms from the read finite Bernoulli calculation are

\[
\chi_q\,\omega_p^{-1},\quad \chi_q(-1)=+1;
\qquad
\psi_p\,\omega_q^{-1},\quad\psi_p(-1)=+1.
\]

Both are odd **globally**, but their **local parity at `p` differs**: in the first family the `p`-component `omega_p^-1` is odd, whereas in the second family the `p`-component `psi_p` is even. A Galois automorphism of a finite root-of-unity character field sends each character to an integer power whose exponent is coprime to its order, therefore odd wherever the local order is even. It preserves each local value at `-1`. Accordingly no Galois conjugation turns one displayed family into the other. The two divisibility conditions are additionally taken modulo *different rational primes*, `p` and `q`.

A product of all these generalized Bernoulli numbers may be formed, but its algebraic integrality/norm alone gives no inequality asserting that at least one of the two families has at most half its values divisible by the designated prime. Deriving such an inequality from a new shared-source identity is **OPEN**. We do **not** assert no such identity can exist.

### 4.2 Inversion pairing is false for the actual dual coefficients — TESTED counterfixture

At `(p,q)=(13,151)`, over `F_151` the actual dual gcd is `T+32`; its unique nontrivial zero is `zeta=119`, whereas `zeta^-1=33` and `F(33)=109≠0`. At `(43,127)` the dual gcd is `T+66`, with zero `61`, inverse `25`, and `F(25)=106≠0`. Both identities are verified from the original finite sum by `C38_small_checks.py`.

Thus the claimed rule “a zero at character `psi` forces a zero at `psi^-1`” is **false** without an additional arithmetic hypothesis. Frobenius images do preserve zero sets automatically, but C33/C34 already count whole Frobenius orbits; their sizes alone need not cover half the characters.

### 4.3 Compositum norms and independent torsion — PROVED retained obstruction

C37 §D proves that the two source divided-ideal images inject into the compositum `Q(zeta_p)^+Q(zeta_q)^+` and have disjoint prime-power orders, giving a product subgroup of size `p^(n-h) q^(s-h^vee)`. Norms recover the two groups separately. The combined necessary numerical inequality obtained by *adding* (0.1) and (0.2) is only the sum of the two existing count constraints, not a new contradiction. The shared `x,y` have not entered that formal product count.

C37 also constructs simultaneous finite integral unit/divisor modules with both actual `F`-operators in which both zero-component overlaps survive. Those models lack the common affine integer source and do not refute new source arithmetic. In C38, §2 explicitly adds information derived from that common source, but the associated residue detecting ranks are not controlled by the existing principalization, unit-index, or lattice estimates. The example in §2.3 certifies the precise limitation of congruence-only detection.

No additional cross-field identity from the factors `u,v,a,b`, the cross-field SIZE transfer, or a conductor-`pq` Bernoulli norm is proved here that forces the disjunction `h≤A` or `h^vee≤A^vee`. The cross-field size result remains useful in constructing RAD_q (C37) but does not determine the unit coset in either field.
