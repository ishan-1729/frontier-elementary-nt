## 0. Result, including what has not been obtained

**PROVED, with the inherited source inputs:** the sufficient deficiency target can be weakened substantially. It is not necessary to prove the proposed one-eighth bound. A refinement of the *finite ideal count*, combined with the smaller cross-polytope representative bound already proved in C32, gives

\[
 \boxed{r\log p<\frac{H-1}{2}\log q-\frac{19}{20}H}
 \tag{0.1}
\]

for any hypothetical source in the remaining band. Consequently:

* **PROVED:** `d <= H/4` excludes a source throughout the band. In particular `r >= 3(H-1)/4` suffices. This is an improvement of the **sufficient certificate**, not a proof that the actual deficiency satisfies the bound.
* **PROVED:** if `r >= rho(H-1)`, then the explicit regional condition
  \[
  q\le\frac{13}{2}p^{2\rho}
  \tag{0.2}
  \]
  excludes a source. For example, half rank now suffices through `q <= 13p/2`, rather than the `3p/2` range proved in C33.
* **PROVED arithmetic subfamilies, with no unproved rank-fraction premise:** in the band, a source is excluded when `H` is prime, `ord_H(p) >= (H-1)/2`, and `q <= 13p/2`. C33's prime-power family now closes the full band for its odd prime base `ell >= 5`, rather than `ell >= 11`; the base-three version closes the region `q <= (13/2)p^(4/3)`. Their exact hypotheses are in §4.4. No infinitude of these families is asserted.

The new estimate behind (0.1) is elementary and quantitative. For prime ideals of residue degree at least two, use **both** `ell^f >= q-1` and `f >= 2`, and split the rational primes at `sqrt(q-1)`. The resulting local-count cost is of order `sqrt(H)`, not of order `H`. This improves the source-counting side without importing a class-number formula or a prime-distribution theorem.

**PROVED, for the rank side:** exact Frobenius-orbit rounding converts any independently proved weak deficiency bound into a stronger integer bound. Its complete finite criterion, the special prime-`H` formulas, and the resulting subranges are in §4. This does not manufacture the needed independent weak bound.

**PROVED limitations in their specified scopes:** the two Wieferich congruences normalize trivial character values in two different coefficient characteristics; that calculation does not constrain enough nontrivial values. On a component where the circular Fermat quotient vanishes, the reflected operator vanishes too. A nonzero source radical and the finite Kummer pairing therefore give no contradiction there by themselves. A finite module model in §6 makes that last boundary exact; it is not an integer Catalan counterexample.

**TESTED:** the companion Python script checks 933 small semisimple pairs, including 186 independent defining-sum/Bernoulli-numerator controls, and the stated rank and order fixtures. A separate complete C++ scan at `p=100003` checks 1,297,409 prime `q` values in the strict band. The new order-only family covers 25,698 of the 1,297,398 semisimple pairs, 715 more than the corresponding C33 family. This is not a full rank survey or an asymptotic proportion. No pair in this particular row satisfies both Wieferich congruences, so the scan does not exhibit a newly surviving large-prime source candidate.

**CONJECTURED / OPEN:** no all-pairs positive-proportion upper bound on `d`, including `d <= H/4`, is established here. No complete elementary proof of Catalan is claimed.

## 2. PROVED: the Bernoulli count and the exact orbit data

### 2.1 The finite character sum, with division by p justified

Let `chi` be a nontrivial even character modulo `q`, and fix one residue place above `p` for its prime-to-`p` roots of unity. Let `omega` reduce to the identity character modulo `p`. Define the finite generalized Bernoulli sum

\[
 B_{1,\chi\omega^{-1}}=
 \frac1{pq}\sum_{a=1}^{pq}a\chi(a)\omega(a)^{-1},
\]

with characters zero on nonunits. Write `a=j+pt`, `1<=j<p`, `0<=t<q`. For fixed `j`, the residues `j+pt mod q` run through all residues, so

\[
 \sum_{t=0}^{q-1}\chi(j+pt)=0.
\]

The part containing `j` cancels **before division by p**. Therefore

\[
 B_{1,\chi\omega^{-1}}=
 \frac1q\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\omega(j)^{-1}
       \sum_{t=0}^{q-1}t\chi(j+pt).
 \tag{2.1}
\]

This proves integrality at the selected residue place. Reducing and collecting `a=j+pt mod q` gives

\[
 \boxed{qB_{1,\chi\omega^{-1}}=2F(\chi(g))\pmod p.}
 \tag{2.2}
\]

No complex analytic value or class-number formula is involved. Finite lifts suffice: modulo `p^2`, `omega(j)=j^p`, and a root of unity of order prime to `p` lifts by the unit-derivative digit correction. Formula (2.1) is the proof read in solRank §2, reproduced to fix the coefficient place and normalization.

The character is `chi`, not `chi^-1`, in (2.2). Using inverse characters changes the labels; their vanishing is not generally invariant under inversion at a fixed residue place.

### 2.2 Exact orbit inventory

For each divisor `m>1` of `H`, define

\[
 e_m=\operatorname{ord}_m(p),\quad
 k_m=\varphi(m)/e_m.
\]

A root `xi` of exact order `m` has Frobenius orbit length `e_m`: `xi^(p^s)=xi` iff `p^s=1 mod m`. Coefficients of `F` lie in `F_p`, hence

\[
 F(\xi^p)=F(\xi)^p.
\]

Thus there are integers `j_m` with

\[
 \boxed{d=\sum_{m\mid H,\ m>1}j_me_m,\qquad0\le j_m\le k_m.}
 \tag{2.3}
\]

This proves that the **degree** of each zero block is an orbit size. It does not give `d <= n/e_m`: the possible `k_m` blocks contain `k_me_m=phi(m)` characters in total.

There is another exact inventory identity useful for screening. The number of nontrivial `H`th roots fixed by the `p^s` Frobenius is

\[
 \gcd(H,p^s-1)-1.
 \tag{2.4}
\]

It follows simply by intersecting the roots of `T^H-1` and `T^(p^s-1)-1`. Finite divisor inversion recovers the number of orbits of each length. Again, (2.4) counts **available** orbits, not the orbits on which `F` vanishes.

### 2.3 A nonzero witness and exact-order activity

The elementary recurrence is

\[
 f(a+p)-f(a)=
 \begin{cases}-q/b,&b=[a+p]_q\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\},\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]

To prove it, each bracket increases by one except at its wrap; the common increment disappears because `sum j^-1=0`. Replacing `j` by `p-j` proves `f(-a)=f(a)`. At `a=q+1-p` this gives

\[
 f(1)-f(p-1)=-q\ne0.
 \tag{2.5}
\]

In particular `r>0`. If `H` is prime, all nontrivial orbits have the same size `e=ord_H(p)`, so (2.5) proves `r>=e`. If `e=H-1`, the rank is full.

For finer exact-order information, form the vectors from C33 §5:

\[
 S_t(i)=\sum_{0\le j<H,\ j\equiv i\pmod t}f(g^j),\qquad
 W_m(i)=\sum_{t\mid m}\mu(m/t)tS_t(i),\quad0\le i<m.
 \tag{2.6}
\]

Here `mu` is the squarefree-sign divisor function. To verify the test, the element

\[
 P_{\le t}=\frac tH\sum_{j=0}^{H/t-1}T^{tj}
\]

is the projector onto characters of order dividing `t`. Divisor inversion makes `sum_(t|m)mu(m/t)P_(<=t)` the exact-order-`m` projector. The coefficients of its product with `HF` are the repeated vector `W_m`. Hence

\[
 W_m=0\Longleftrightarrow j_m=k_m,
 \qquad W_m\ne0\Longrightarrow j_m\le k_m-1.
 \tag{2.7}
\]

This finite sum test can force a full nonzero orbit. It cannot replace `k_m-1` by zero when several orbits of that order exist.

## 3. PROVED: a stronger finite ideal count, and a quarter-deficiency certificate

This is the main additional result of C34. It addresses how much rank is needed, rather than asserting a new uniform supply of that rank.

### 3.1 The source and the representative bound

Here is the relevant content of the proofs read in C32 §4. The local Fermat quotient on units is additive modulo `p`, zero on `p`th powers, and has rank at least `r` on the unit group because its rank on circular units is exactly `r`. The elementary separated-logarithm box proof bounds the dimension of all real unit classes by `H-1`. Thus primary unit classes have dimension at most `n-r=d`.

The source space has dimension `n` by RAD. Its divided-divisor map has kernel represented by primary units, so its image contains at least `p^r` distinct ideal classes, all killed by `p`. This uses the actual torsion subgroup, not an identification with the quotient of a larger p-group by p.

The embedding lattice has determinant `q^(n/2)N(I)`. The finite-volume pigeonhole argument applied to a half cross-polytope gives a nonzero `b in I` with

\[
 |N(b)|\le M_qN(I),\qquad
 M_q=\frac{H!}{H^H}q^{n/2}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

Indeed the half cross-polytope of radius `R` has volume `R^H/H!`; two points differing by a lattice vector give a nonzero vector in the full polytope, whose coordinate product is at most `(R/H)^H`. Take `R^H` down to `H!` times the determinant. Applying this to an ideal in the inverse class makes `(b)I^-1` an integral representative of that class of norm at most `M_q`.

Write `N_q(B)` for the number of integral ideals of norm at most `B`. A source therefore forces

\[
 p^r\le N_q(M_q).
 \tag{3.2}
\]

The finite local factorization and discriminant arguments needed here are proved in C32 §4.3 and the supplement §5.3. No ideal-to-Galois correspondence is used.

### 3.2 Improve the residue-degree sum before choosing a real parameter

For `ell != q`, put

\[
 f_\ell=\min\{f\ge1:\ell^f\equiv\pm1\pmod q\}.
\]

There are `H/f_ell` prime ideals of norm `ell^f_ell`, and

\[
 \ell^{f_\ell}\ge q-1.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

The last inequality follows because `ell^f` is a positive integer greater than one, congruent to `1` or `-1 mod q`. For `s=1+delta>1`, the finite local geometric expansion and `-log(1-v)<=v/(1-v)` give

\[
 \log N_q(B)\le s\log B+
 \frac{\sum_{\ell\ne q}(H/f_\ell)\ell^{-f_\ell s}}
 {1-(q-1)^{-s}}+
 \frac{q^{-s}}{1-q^{-s}}.
 \tag{3.4}
\]

One may truncate the prime sum at `B`; enlarging the positive sums only increases the bound. It is a finite counting estimate, not an analytic continuation or a class-number formula.

For degree one, `ell=±1 mod q`, so the elementary progression sum gives

\[
 \sum_{f_\ell=1}H\ell^{-s}
 \le (1+1/\delta)(q-1)^{-\delta}.
 \tag{3.5}
\]

Explicitly, bound each of the two progressions by `(q-1)^(-s)sum_(j>=1)j^(-s)`, then use `sum j^(-s)<=1+1/delta` and `2H=q-1`.

For higher degrees, retain both pieces of information:

\[
 \ell^{-f_\ell}\le\min\{(q-1)^{-1},\ell^{-2}\}.
\]

For any integer `J>=1`, splitting at `ell=J` and bounding primes by integers yields

\[
 \sum_{f_\ell\ge2}\ell^{-f_\ell}
 \le\frac{J}{q-1}+\sum_{a>J}a^{-2}
 \le\frac{J}{q-1}+\frac1J.
 \tag{3.6}
\]

The last inequality is the elementary integral bound for the decreasing function `x^-2`. Also `ell^(-f delta) <= (q-1)^(-delta)` and `H/f<=H/2`. Combining (3.4)–(3.6) gives the new bound

\[
 \boxed{\log N_q(B)\le(1+\delta)\log B+E^*_{\delta,J}(q),}
 \tag{3.7}
\]

\[
 E^*_{\delta,J}(q)=
 \frac{\bigl(1+1/\delta+J/4+H/(2J)\bigr)(q-1)^{-\delta}}
      {1-(q-1)^{-1-\delta}}
 +\frac{q^{-1-\delta}}{1-q^{-1-\delta}}.
 \tag{3.8}
\]

The supplement's higher-degree term was `H/2`. Here it is `J/4+H/(2J)`, minimized near `sqrt(2H)=sqrt(q-1)`. The bound uses no assertion about how many rational primes actually have a specified Frobenius.

**PROVED PACK-SPLIT:** under SOURCE and ALG, any `delta>0`, `J>=1` for which

\[
 r\log p>(1+\delta)\log M_q+E^*_{\delta,J}(q)
 \tag{3.9}
\]

holds gives a contradiction to (3.2). All constants are explicit. This is a stronger sufficient test than using a coarse norm bound and then discarding residue degrees.

### 3.3 A uniform numerical bound with elementary constants

Assume `H>=50000`. Put

\[
 t=\sqrt H,\qquad s_0=\lceil\sqrt H\rceil,
 \quad\delta=1/s_0,\quad J=\lceil\sqrt{2H}\rceil.
\]

This choice of `delta` is rational. The following estimates prove (0.1).

First, the elementary factorial integral bound gives

\[
 \log M_q\le\tfrac n2\log q-H+1+\log H
 <\tfrac n2\log q-\tfrac{99}{100}H.
 \tag{3.10}
\]

For the strict comparison, `log H<=sqrt H` for `H>=4`: differentiate `sqrt H-log H` and check the endpoint using `log 4<2`. At `H>=50000`, `1+sqrt H<H/100`.

Second,

\[
 \log(2H+1)<\frac3{50}\sqrt H.
 \tag{3.11}
\]

At `H=50000`, `log 100001<12` and `sqrt(50000)>200` prove it. The first inequality is certified by the finite rational sum `sum_(j=0)^30 12^j/j! > 100001`. For increasing `t=sqrt H`, the derivative of `(3/50)t-log(2t^2+1)` is greater than `3/50-2/t>0`. Thus (3.11) holds for all subsequent `H`. As `s_0>=sqrt H`, (3.10) without its negative term gives

\[
 \delta\log M_q<\frac3{100}H.
 \tag{3.12}
\]

Third, the coefficient in (3.8) satisfies

\[
 1+s_0+J/4+H/(2J)<2\sqrt H+3<H/110.
 \tag{3.13}
\]

Indeed `s_0<=t+1`, `J<3t/2+1`, and `H/(2J)<=t/2`. For the last inequality, `t>223` and `t^2-220t-330` is positive at 223 and increasing thereafter.

Since `(q-1)^(-delta)<1`, the first denominator in (3.8) exceeds `99/100`, and its last fraction is less than `1/(q-1)=1/(2H)`. Therefore

\[
 E^*_{\delta,J}(q)<\frac{10}{1089}H+\frac1{2H}<\frac H{100}.
 \tag{3.14}
\]

The last comparison is equivalent to `108900<178H^2`, which is valid already at the stated endpoint and thereafter.

Adding (3.10), (3.12), and (3.14) proves

\[
 \boxed{(1+\delta)\log M_q+E^*_{\delta,J}(q)
       <\frac n2\log q-\frac{19}{20}H.}
 \tag{3.15}
\]

Together with (3.2), this proves the necessary source inequality (0.1). In particular, its contrapositive is a **proved source exclusion**, with no unknown prime-existence statement.

### 3.4 Consequences for the required deficiency

A source in BAND must satisfy

\[
 \boxed{d>
 n\left(1-\frac{\log q}{2\log p}\right)
       +\frac{19H}{20\log p}.}
 \tag{3.16}
\]

This is a **necessary lower bound on the deficiency under the hypothetical source**, not the upper bound sought in the question. It quantifies the contradiction that a sufficiently good upper bound would produce.

Because `q<p^(3/2)` in BAND, (3.16) implies

\[
 d>\frac n4+\frac{19H}{20\log p}.
 \tag{3.17}
\]

Therefore `r>=3n/4`, or `d<=n/4`, suffices. Even `d<=H/4` suffices: then `r>=3n/4-1/4`, and the positive margin in (3.15) is at least `19H/20-(log p)/4>0`, by `log p<log q<(3/50)sqrt H`.

For a regional version, suppose `r>=rho n` and (0.2) holds. The finite sum `sum_(j=0)^9 (19/10)^j/j! > 13/2` proves `log(13/2)<19/10`. Hence

\[
 \tfrac n2\log q-\tfrac{19}{20}H
 <\rho n\log p+\tfrac{19}{20}n-\tfrac{19}{20}H
 =\rho n\log p-\tfrac{19}{20}.
\]

This proves (0.2) with a strict margin, including its non-strict endpoint.

## 4. PROVED: combining weak bounds with orbit sizes, and what proportion this means

### 4.1 The exact finite rounding criterion

Assume an independently proved integer bound `d<=U`. Define the finite set

\[
 \mathcal S(p,H)=
 \left\{\sum_{m\mid H,m>1}j_me_m:
 \begin{array}{ll}j_m=k_m,&W_m=0,\\
                 0\le j_m\le k_m-1,&W_m\ne0
 \end{array}\right\}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

The activity tests `W_m` use only the explicit finite coefficient sums (2.6), not a polynomial gcd. By (2.7), the actual deficiency belongs to this set. Put

\[
 U_{\rm orb}=\max(\mathcal S(p,H)\cap[0,U]).
 \tag{4.2}
\]

If the intersection is empty, the alleged upper bound contradicts the exact activity data. Otherwise

\[
 d\le U_{\rm orb},\qquad r\ge n-U_{\rm orb}.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

One may compute (4.1) by a finite bounded-knapsack recurrence: start with `{0}` and add the permitted multiples of `e_m` for each order. No probability or prime distribution enters. Without activity tests, allow `0<=j_m<=k_m` and impose (2.5)'s nonzero-support witness separately; the result is weaker but still valid.

A particularly simple consequence is that an orbit with `e_m>U` cannot vanish at all. All deficiency must come from the remaining orders. If their total character count is below the certificate cutoff, the weak bound closes that pair. In general, however, many small orbits can still fill the whole allowed deficit.

For an exact source cutoff, put

\[
 A(p,q)=n\left(1-\frac{\log q}{2\log p}\right)
                  +\frac{19H}{20\log p}.
\]

Then `U_orb<=A(p,q)` contradicts (3.16). A purely simpler sufficient version is `U_orb<=H/4` throughout BAND. This is the requested precise test for when a weak counting bound plus the orbit sizes actually suffices.

**Boundary:** (4.2) consumes a proved upper bound `U`; it does not prove one. Replacing `U` by an observed maximum from a finite grid would be an unjustified extrapolation.

### 4.2 Prime H: a transparent formula and its limits

Let `H` be prime and write

\[
 e=\operatorname{ord}_H(p),\qquad k=(H-1)/e.
\]

Then `d=je`, with `0<=j<=k-1`, the last inequality following from (2.5). Thus a weak **strict** bound `d<c n` implies

\[
 j\le\lceil ck\rceil-1,\qquad
 \frac rn\ge1-\frac{\lceil ck\rceil-1}{k}.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

For a non-strict bound `d<=c n`, replace `ceil(ck)-1` by `floor(ck)`. That endpoint difference is important.

For illustration, the unproved bound `d<n/2` would give:

| Frobenius index k | Largest allowed number of zero orbits | Guaranteed r/n | Source region closed by (0.2) |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 0 | 1 | Entire BAND; already unconditional from nonconstancy |
| 2 | 0 | 1 | Entire BAND, conditional on the strict half-deficiency bound |
| 4 | 1 | 3/4 | Entire BAND, conditional on that bound |
| 5 | 2 | 3/5 | `q <= (13/2)p^(6/5)` |
| 7 | 3 | 4/7 | `q <= (13/2)p^(8/7)` |
| 8 | 3 | 5/8 | `q <= (13/2)p^(5/4)` |

For this prime-`H`, prime-`q` family with `H>3`, indices divisible by three do not occur: `q=2H+1` prime forces `H=2 mod 3`, so `3` does not divide `H-1`. Algebraic rounding formulas at indices 3 or 6 are valid for cyclic inventories generally, but do not describe members of this large safe-prime family. The scripts retain that distinction.

No theorem that `d<n/2` holds uniformly is supplied here. A non-strict half-deficiency bound would allow `j=k/2` for even `k`, and would **not** give the full-band conclusions in the index-two and index-four rows.

### 4.3 What an unrounded bound closes; interval length is not prime density

If `d<=c n`, (0.2) closes the explicit interval

\[
 p<q<\min\{B(p),(13/2)p^{2(1-c)}\}.
 \tag{4.5}
\]

For a hypothesis of the form `d<=cH`, use the exact rank fraction `rho=1-cH/n` in (0.2), or directly (3.16). In particular `c=1/4` in `d<=cH` closes **all** of BAND by §3.4.

For fixed `1/4<c<1/2`, the fraction of the *real interval length* covered by (4.5) has leading size

\[
 \min\left\{1,\frac{13/2}{\sqrt{5/12}}\,
                  p^{1/2-2c}\right\}.
 \tag{4.6}
\]

This follows by dividing the explicit powers; in this range the lower endpoint is of smaller order than the upper endpoint. At `c=1/2`, subtraction of the lower endpoint instead gives leading fraction `[(11/2)/sqrt(5/12)]p^(-1/2)`. For `c>1/2`, the interval is eventually empty. In every case `c>1/4` the covered fraction tends to zero. Thus an arbitrarily weak positive-proportion bound `c<1` does not by itself close the outer band. A bound at the one-quarter level does; orbit gaps sometimes allow a weaker bound, as in (4.4).

Equation (4.6) is **not** a density statement about prime pairs, safe primes, primitive roots, or double-Wieferich pairs. No elementary distribution theorem for those sets is assumed. The only numerical proportions claimed below refer to a declared finite scan.

### 4.4 New excluded arithmetic subfamilies with no new weak-bound assumption

The following statements require SOURCE only to turn their rank bounds into exclusions; the rank bounds themselves are finite algebra under ALG.

**Family A — PROVED.** If `H` is prime, `ord_H(p)>=(H-1)/2`, and `q<=13p/2`, then no source exists in BAND. Nonconstancy gives `r>=ord_H(p)>=n/2`, and (0.2) applies. The order `H-1` subcase still excludes the whole band without the extra `q/p` restriction.

**Family B — PROVED.** Suppose

\[
 H=\ell^a,\quad \ell\text{ an odd prime},\quad
 \operatorname{ord}_H(p)=\varphi(H),\quad
 \operatorname{ord}_{G}(p-1)=\ell.
 \tag{4.7}
\]

Then `r>=H-H/ell`. To prove this, write `g^b=±(p-1)`. The last condition gives `b=(H/ell)v` with `ell` not dividing `v`. The Fourier inversion of (2.5) is a nonzero sum containing factors `1-xi^(-b)`. For every exact order dividing `H/ell` this factor is zero; therefore a primitive `H`th-root coefficient is nonzero. All primitive `H`th roots form one Frobenius orbit by (4.7), so all `phi(H)=H-H/ell` of them are nonzero.

For `ell>=5`, this is more than `3n/4`, and closes the whole band. For `ell=3`, it is at least `2n/3`, and closes `q<=(13/2)p^(4/3)`. This enlarges the sufficient range of the C33 prime-power argument. It does not assert the existence of infinitely many primes `q=2ell^a+1` or qualifying primes `p`.

**Exact order-only fixture for Family A.** At

\[
 (p,q,H)=(100003,600827,300413),
\]

all three numbers are prime and

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_H(p)=150206=(H-1)/2,
 \quad150206=2\cdot7\cdot10729.
\]

The powers at exponents `150206/2`, `150206/7`, `150206/10729` are respectively `300412`, `160902`, `234589 mod H`, none one, while the full exponent gives one. Also `3p/2<q<13p/2`. Thus Family A applies without computing a degree-300412 polynomial gcd. Both DW residues differ from one, as recorded in the checks: this is a theorem/implementation control, not a new surviving source example.

## 5. Directions (b): the two Wieferich conditions and the band's size

### 5.1 PROVED: both trivial values, and what is removed from the rank

Set `n_j=[-jp^-1]_q` and `k_j=(j+pn_j)/q`. The `k_j` permute `1,...,p-1`, because `qk_j=j mod p`. Multiplying in `Z/p^2Z` gives

\[
 q^{p-1}\equiv1+p\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}n_j/j\pmod{p^2}.
\]

Consequently

\[
 f(0)=Q_p(q):=(q^{p-1}-1)/p\pmod p.
\]

The defining residues also give `sum_(a mod q) f(a)=0`, so

\[
 2F(1)=-Q_p(q).
 \tag{5.1}
\]

Forward DW therefore kills the trivial character, which is omitted from `M_H`. The harmonic-profile proof in solRank §4.3 gives, for least representatives `0<=a<q`,

\[
 f(a)=Q_p(q)+h_{[-aq^{-1}]_p},\qquad
 h_s=\sum_{j=1}^sj^{-1}\pmod p.
 \tag{5.2}
\]

A direct proof follows the additive orbit `a=[kp]_q`: the nonzero jumps occur when the quotient in `kp=a+lq` increments through `l=1,...,p-1`, each jump being `1/l`. This proves (5.2). Subtracting the constant `Q_p(q)` changes `F` by a multiple of `M_H`, and changes no nontrivial spectral component.

This is a precise limitation on **using DW only through (5.1)**. It does not assert statistical or logical independence of all the other coefficients from the exponent-pair restriction.

Now interchange the primes. Define

\[
 \widetilde f(b)=\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}j^{-1}[(b-j)q^{-1}]_p\in\mathbb F_q.
\]

The same permutation/product proof, valid without the size ordering of the primes, gives

\[
 \widetilde f(0)=Q_q(p),\qquad
 2\widetilde F(1)=-Q_q(p)\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_q.
 \tag{5.3}
\]

Thus reverse DW kills a trivial component in a **different** coefficient characteristic and a different character group, of size `(p-1)/2` rather than `H`. There is no unital field map `F_q -> F_p`; an additive homomorphism between their additive groups is zero, because its image would be killed by both distinct primes. This rules out treating the two arrays as the same linear algebra. It does not rule out a new nonlinear integer identity relating them.

### 5.2 What is already implied by Frobenius, and what has not been derived

At the fixed residue place, (2.2) and `F(xi^p)=F(xi)^p` give all the automatic congruences within a Frobenius orbit. They imply simultaneous vanishing within that orbit. They do not propagate a zero to inverse characters or to different Frobenius orbits of the same order. The actual controls `(5,17)` and `(7,37)` in solRank demonstrate those distinctions; the script independently verifies their gcds.

Reverse DW also lifts `p^e=±1 mod q` to `p^e=±1 mod q^2`, where `e` is the order of `p` modulo `q` up to sign: write a possible first-order error and raise to the divisor `(q-1)/e` (or use the full order), which is a unit modulo `q`. This concerns `p` as an **argument modulo q**. The Bernoulli orbit sizes are `ord_m(p)` for `m|H`, not this order and not `ord_p(q)`.

The size `q<p^(3/2)` does not identify these order notions. It does imply the very weak fact that an exact order `m>p-1` cannot have Frobenius degree one. It gives no proved restriction here on how many of the available degree-two or larger blocks vanish. The existing pole/degree lower bounds of C33 and solRank remain quantitatively far below a fixed fraction of `H` in the outer band.

**Exact stopping point of (b):** no additional relation between the nontrivial values in (2.2) and the swapped values in (5.3), sufficient to bound `d`, is obtained. Equations (5.1)–(5.3) are all proved; promoting them to such a relation would add an unproved step.

## 6. Direction (c): why a zero Bernoulli component is not itself a source contradiction

### 6.1 The local operator has the same zero

Let `E` be the augmentation algebra `F_p[G]`, and let `V` be the source radical module supplied by RAD. The source map identifies `V` with `E`. On circular exponent vectors, the local Fermat quotient is multiplication by `F`, up to its fixed nonzero scalar and the explicit real basis.

The augmentation part of the reflected operator is

\[
 D=\frac2q(1-\sigma_{p-1}^{-1})F.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

This is the finite CRT formula of C26–C28. Therefore, on any simple factor `phi` where `F=0`, one also has `D=0`. A nonzero source radical in that component is **not** an element on which this operator has a nonzero image.

The finite Kummer covariance can be checked directly. If `tau` multiplies a chosen pth root of `v` by `zeta_p^a`, conjugating both the radical and the root automorphism by `sigma` multiplies that exponent by the cyclotomic character `omega_p(sigma)`. Consequently the adjoint of an ideal-side operator is its inverse-indexed, cyclotomic-character-twisted operator on radicals. For the present operator this is exactly `Xi_c`, whose augmentation part is (6.1).

Thus on a zero-F component the identity

\[
 \langle\Theta_c\tau,v\rangle=\langle\tau,Dv\rangle
\]

has right member one **because `Dv=0`**. Perfectness of the pairing does not turn this equality into `v=0`. It would do so only if an invertible operator acted trivially there; `D` is not invertible on this component.

This explains why merely combining RAD with the reflected pairing does not supply the nonvanishing needed for PACK. It also avoids reversing the classical direction of reflection: vanishing of an annihilator's eigenvalue is compatible with a nonzero module.

### 6.2 A finite module model of the exact interface boundary — PROVED

This model tests only the algebraic interfaces just used, not the full integer-source equations.

Because `E` is semisimple, write

\[
 E=Z\oplus W,\qquad Z=\ker(F:E\to E),
\]

where `F` is invertible on `W`. Then `dim Z=d`, `dim W=r`. Define a formal unit space `U=E` with quotient map `delta_U=F`; its primary subspace is exactly `Z`. Take a formal divided-divisor target `C=W` and a larger radical space `S=U direct-sum W`, with divisor map `(u,w)->w` and local map `(u,w)->F u`.

Embed an independent source copy `V=E=Z direct-sum W` into `S` by

\[
 (z,w)\longmapsto(z,w).
\]

Every source vector is locally primary; its dimension is `n`; the kernel of its divided-divisor map is `Z`, represented by primary units; its image has dimension `r`. Give `V` its full dual with the usual evaluation pairing, twisting the group action by `omega_p` when desired. This pairing is perfect and has exactly the covariance above. Finally the operator (6.1) is zero on `Z`.

All these finite statements hold for **any** specified zero set of `F`, including the actual zero set at `(5,17)`. There is no contradiction on a zero component. This shows precisely that RAD's independence, primaryness, the unit-dimension upper bound, and the finite pairing **as those interfaces alone** do not force more nonzero Bernoulli components.

The model does not realize algebraic integers, the affine source factors, their simultaneous valuations, or the source size in a number field. It therefore does not disprove a theorem that uses those additional arithmetic features. Those features are where a genuinely new source-specific argument would have to enter.

### 6.3 What the source really forces in the present argument

The source yields a **lower** bound `p^r` for the number of distinct divided-ideal classes, and the finite ideal count yields (3.16), a **lower** bound on `d` under a hypothetical source. It does not yield an upper bound for the Bernoulli irregularity count. The desired contradiction would come from proving that the actual exponent polynomial cannot have that much deficiency.

A different proof that a zero-F source component principalizes would still have to remove the resulting primary-unit obstruction. On such a component the circular Fermat quotient is zero, so the nonzero-local-quotient argument used on nonzero components in C29 cannot simply be repeated. No such principalization or unit-removal identity is constructed here.
