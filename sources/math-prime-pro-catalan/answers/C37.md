## P. Ordinary Padé: exact contact and a bound covering every denominator degree

### P.1. Named hypotheses and the polynomial to which the results apply

Use the following hypotheses.

* **PRIMES:** `p<q` are distinct odd primes.
* **EXPONENTS:** `η` is a primitive `q`-th root of unity, `K=Q(η)`, and the integers `n_c`, `1≤c<q`, satisfy `0≤n_c<p`, are not all zero, and have sum `pm` for an integer `m≥2`.
* **BAND-DEGREE:** `q<p²`.

The evenness conditions `n_c=n_{-c}` and `m≤H` are available for the source but are not needed for the degree theorem once `m≥2` has been established. Indeed, C26 §6.2 represents a nonzero augmentation class by paired exponents, possibly replacing it by its inverse. This gives `2≤m≤H`: the sum is positive, `m` is even, and the inverse choice gives the upper bound. The same positive-residue choice is available when the field class is represented by a primary unit times a `p`-th power; it does not assume the unit is itself a power.

Write

\[
 \mathcal P(Z)=\prod_{c=1}^{q-1}(Z-\eta^c)^{n_c},\qquad
 P(t)=\prod_{c=1}^{q-1}(1-\eta^ct)^{n_c},\qquad
 \mathcal P(Z)=Z^{pm}P(1/Z).
\]

Thus `P(0)=1`. Let

\[
 P(t)^{1/p}=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nt^n,\qquad
 y(Z)=Z^m\sum_{n\ge0}b_nZ^{-n},\qquad y^p=\mathcal P.
 \tag{P1.1}
\]

These are formal series over `K`. Their coefficients are obtained by finite polynomial expressions; no analytic continuation or complex integration is used. Put

\[
 P_1=[t]P(t)=-\sum_c n_c\eta^c,\qquad
 N=\#\{c:n_c\ne0\}\le q-1.
 \tag{P1.2}
\]

For the determinant argument it is enough to use the order `R=Z[η]`, rather than the full ring of integers. Its quotient

\[
 R/pR=\mathbb F_p[T]/(\Phi_q(T))
\]

is reduced. Indeed, `T^q-1` has derivative `qT^{q-1}` and has no repeated root modulo `p`, so its factor `Φ_q` is squarefree. Also `P_1` is nonzero in this quotient. Otherwise the polynomial `∑ n_cT^c`, of degree at most `q−1` and with constant coefficient zero, would be a multiple of `Φ_q`; the multiple has to be constant, and its constant coefficient has to be zero. This would force all `n_c=0` modulo `p`, contrary to **EXPONENTS**. Consequently every nonnegative power of `P_1` is nonzero modulo `p`.

### P.2. A factorial-safe determinant identity — PROVED

For an indeterminate `α`, define the coefficient polynomial

\[
 B_n(\alpha)=[t^n]P(t)^\alpha
   =\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom\alpha\ell
     [t^n](P(t)-1)^\ell.
 \tag{P2.1}
\]

#### P.2.1 Integral leading coefficients

**PROVED.** `n!B_n(α)` belongs to `R[α]`, has degree at most `n`, and its coefficient of `α^n` is `P_1^n`.

For each term of (P2.1), `n!/ℓ!` is an integer and `ℓ! binom(α,ℓ)` is a monic integral polynomial of degree `ℓ`. The only contribution of degree `n` comes from `ℓ=n`; the coefficient of `t^n` in `(P−1)^n` is `P_1^n`. This also includes `n=0`.

For integers `s≥1`, `M≥s−1`, set

\[
 \Delta_s(M;\alpha)
   =\det\bigl(B_{M+i-j}(\alpha)\bigr)_{0\le i,j<s},\qquad
 C_s(M;\alpha)=\left(\prod_{i=0}^{s-1}(M+i)!\right)
                      \Delta_s(M;\alpha).
 \tag{P2.2}
\]

Multiplying row `i` by `(M+i)!` makes every entry integral. Every determinant term has degree at most

\[
 \sum_{i=0}^{s-1}(M+i-\sigma(i))=sM.
\]

The coefficient of `α^{sM}` is

\[
 P_1^{sM}\det\bigl((M+i)_j\bigr)_{0\le i,j<s}
   =\left(\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}j!\right)P_1^{sM},
 \tag{P2.3}
\]

where `(x)_j=x(x−1)⋯(x−j+1)`. To see the last equality directly, replace each falling-factorial column by the corresponding monomial column using triangular column operations with diagonal entries one. The resulting determinant is the Vandermonde product

\[
 \prod_{0\le i<k<s}((M+k)-(M+i))
   =\prod_{k=1}^{s-1}k!.
\]

#### P.2.2 Nonvanishing after `α=1/p`

**PROVED.** For `1≤s≤p` and `M≥s−1`,

\[
 \boxed{\Delta_s(M;1/p)\ne0.}
 \tag{P2.4}
\]

In fact `C_s(M;α)` is integral and has degree `sM`. Therefore

\[
 p^{sM}C_s(M;1/p)\in R,
 \qquad
 p^{sM}C_s(M;1/p)
   \equiv\left(\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}j!\right)P_1^{sM}\pmod {pR}.
 \tag{P2.5}
\]

Every `j!` in this product is prime to `p`, because `j≤s−1<p`. The right side is nonzero by §P.1. This proves (P2.4).

There is **no restriction `M<p`**. The factors `(M+i)!` were retained exactly, and may contain large powers of `p`. This is the point that prevents a false extension of a denominator-free computation past the first `p` coefficients. For `s>p`, (P2.3) contains `p` and this proof does not assert determinant nonvanishing.

From now on write `Δ_s(M)=Δ_s(M;1/p)` and set the empty determinant `Δ_0(M)=1`.

### P.3. Exact ordinary Padé contact for every denominator degree `b<p` — PROVED

Let `0≤b<p`. A normalized polynomial denominator of degree `b` has the form

\[
 B(Z)=Z^b\widehat B(1/Z),\qquad
 \widehat B(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{b}c_jt^j,\qquad c_0=1.
\]

Take `A(Z)` to be the polynomial part of `B(Z)y(Z)`. It is monic of degree `m+b`. The coefficient of `Z^{-i}`, for `i≥1`, in `By−A` is

\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{b}c_jb_{m+b+i-j}.
 \tag{P3.1}
\]

Demanding its vanishing for `1≤i≤b` gives a square system for `c_1,…,c_b`. Its matrix determinant is `Δ_b(m+b)`, which is nonzero by (P2.4). Thus there is a unique normalized pair with these `b` vanishing coefficients.

For this pair, the next coefficient is exactly

\[
 r=(-1)^b\frac{\Delta_{b+1}(m+b+1)}{\Delta_b(m+b)}\ne0.
 \tag{P3.2}
\]

For completeness, the numerator matrix has rows indexed by `i=1,…,b+1` and columns by `j=0,…,b`, with entry `b_{m+b+i-j}`. Add to its first column the linear combination `∑_{j=1}^b c_j` times column `j`. The top `b` entries of that column become zero, and the bottom entry becomes `r`. Expansion along it gives determinant `(-1)^b rΔ_b(m+b)`, proving (P3.2).

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{B(Z)y(Z)-A(Z)=rZ^{-(b+1)}+\text{lower powers of }Z,\quad r\ne0.}
 \tag{P3.3}
\]

The contact order `b+1` is maximal among **all** linear polynomial auxiliaries having denominator degree `b`, even if their coefficients are allowed in an arbitrary fixed characteristic-zero extension of `K`. If a nonzero denominator gave contact at least `b+2`, the `b+1` equations obtained from (P3.1) would have coefficient matrix `Δ_{b+1}(m+b+1)` and would force `c_0=⋯=c_b=0`. A nonzero determinant over `K` remains nonzero over an extension.

The normalized pair is automatically coprime. If a common polynomial factor had degree `g>0`, dividing by it would give denominator degree `b−g` and contact `b+1+g`, contradicting the just-proved maximum `b−g+1` for that denominator degree.

#### P.3.1 Exact norm degree

Extend the constant field to contain the `p`-th roots of unity for the following finite factorization. For the selected branch in (P3.3), the difference has degree at infinity `−(b+1)`. At every other branch `ζ_p^ay`, `a≠0`,

\[
 B(Z)\zeta_p^ay(Z)-A(Z)
   =(\zeta_p^a-1)Z^{m+b}+\text{lower powers}.
\]

Their product is

\[
 R(Z):=B(Z)^p\mathcal P(Z)-A(Z)^p.
\]

Multiplying the nonzero leading terms proves

\[
 \boxed{\deg R=(p-1)(m+b)-(b+1)
                   =(p-1)m+(p-2)b-1.}
 \tag{P3.4}
\]

This is the smallest possible norm degree at this `b`. If the degrees of `A^p` and `B^p\mathcal P` differ, there is no leading cancellation. If their leading coefficients do not cancel, the norm has degree `p(m+b)`. In the remaining case scale the leading coefficients, and choose the one branch on which they cancel. If any nonnegative power survives there, its contribution is no smaller than zero; otherwise the maximum-contact assertion bounds the negative degree below by `−(b+1)`. The other `p−1` branches still have degree `m+b`. These alternatives prove the lower bound (P3.4) for every pair of that denominator degree, with equality attained by the normalized Padé pair.

In particular the degree rises by exactly `p−2` whenever `b` is increased by one within `0≤b<p`. Ordinary Padé cancellation of the first `b` tail terms cannot improve the single-truncation norm degree `m(p−1)−1` from C36 §8.

### P.4. A finite derivative bound for arbitrary denominator degree — PROVED

Here is the elementary derivative lemma needed for the large-degree range, with its full proof.

#### P.4.1 Three-polynomial lemma

Let `U,V,W` be nonzero pairwise coprime polynomials over a characteristic-zero field, with `U−V=W`, and suppose they are not all constant. Let `rad(UVW)` be the product of their distinct monic irreducible factors. Then

\[
 \max(\deg U,\deg V,\deg W)\le\deg\operatorname{rad}(UVW)-1.
 \tag{P4.1}
\]

Pass to a splitting field to check multiplicities. Set `H=U'V−UV'`. It is nonzero: otherwise coprimality gives `U|U'` and `V|V'`, forcing both to be constant in characteristic zero, and then `W` is constant too. At a zero of `U` of multiplicity `a`, the polynomial `H` has multiplicity at least `a−1`; the same holds for zeros of `V`. Since `H=W'V−WV'`, a zero of `W` of multiplicity `a` also contributes at least `a−1`. Pairwise coprimality therefore gives

\[
 UVW/\operatorname{rad}(UVW)\mid H.
\]

Its degree is at most `deg U+deg V−1`, so `deg W≤deg rad(UVW)−1`. The identical Wronskian, up to sign, can be written using either other pair; the same argument proves the bounds for `U` and `V`. This proves (P4.1) by finite differentiation and divisibility alone.

#### P.4.2 Application to `B^p\mathcal P−A^p`

Suppose `A,B` are coprime, `deg B=b`, `deg A=m+b`. They can have coefficients in any fixed characteristic-zero extension of `K`. Put

\[
 G=\gcd(A^p,B^p\mathcal P)=\gcd(A^p,\mathcal P),\qquad g=\deg G.
\]

Every multiplicity in `\mathcal P` is strictly between zero and `p`. Thus `G` contains the entire factor `(Z−η^c)^{n_c}` precisely when `A(η^c)=0`. Set

\[
 U=A^p/G,\qquad V=B^p\mathcal P/G,\qquad W=(A^p-B^p\mathcal P)/G.
\]

These are pairwise coprime. At a root of `A` also occurring in `\mathcal P`, the exponent remaining in `U` is at least `p−n_c>0`, whereas the factor is removed entirely from `V`. No root is shared elsewhere because `A,B` are coprime. The zeros of `UV` are therefore exactly the zeros of `AB\mathcal P`.

The polynomial `W` is nonzero. If `A^p=B^p\mathcal P`, the rational function `A/B` would have `p`-th power `\mathcal P`, contrary to any one of its root multiplicities `n_c∈{1,…,p−1}`. The same observation excludes `U,V,W` all being constants.

Write `c=deg(B^p\mathcal P−A^p)`. Applying (P4.1) gives

\[
 p(m+b)-g\le\deg\operatorname{rad}(AB\mathcal P)+(c-g)-1,
\]

and hence the stronger root-sensitive bound

\[
 \boxed{c\ge p(m+b)-\deg\operatorname{rad}(AB\mathcal P)+1.}
 \tag{P4.2}
\]

Since `deg rad(AB\mathcal P)≤m+2b+N`, it follows that

\[
 \boxed{c\ge(p-1)m+(p-2)b-N+1.}
 \tag{P4.3}
\]

When a selected branch has contact `By−A=rZ^{-L}+⋯`, the same leading-term multiplication as in §P.3 gives `c=(p−1)(m+b)−L`. Therefore (P4.3) is equivalently the contact bound

\[
 \boxed{L\le b+N-1.}
 \tag{P4.4}
\]

This derivative bound requires no congruence restriction on `b`. It is generally weaker than the determinant bound for `b<p`, but is useful after that range ends.

### P.5. All denominator degrees throughout `q<p²` — PROVED

**Theorem.** Under **PRIMES**, **EXPONENTS**, and **BAND-DEGREE**, let `C` be any fixed characteristic-zero constant field containing `K`. For every `A,B∈C[Z]` with `B≠0`,

\[
 \boxed{B(Z)^p\mathcal P(Z)-A(Z)^p\text{ is nonconstant}.}
 \tag{P5.1}
\]

**Proof.** If `A=0`, or if the degrees of the two terms differ, the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise `deg A=m+deg B`. Remove a common polynomial factor `C_0`, if present. This multiplies the norm of the resulting coprime pair by `C_0^p`; it is enough to prove a positive degree for that pair. Let its denominator degree be `b`.

For `b<p`, §P.3 gives

\[
 \deg R\ge(p-1)m+(p-2)b-1\ge2(p-1)-1>0.
\]

For `b≥p`, §P.4 gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \deg R
 &\ge(p-1)m+(p-2)b-N+1\\
 &\ge2(p-1)+(p-2)p-(q-1)+1\\
 &=p^2-q>0.
 \end{aligned}
\]

This proves the theorem. No height bound or unproved assertion about Padé normality beyond `b=p−1` enters. ∎

The restriction `B≠0` is essential: a constant polynomial auxiliary independent of `y` has constant norm. The theorem concerns genuinely linear auxiliaries. The restriction `q<p²` makes the uniform coarse floor `p²−q` positive; the sharper root-sensitive bound can also be useful outside that range; it is satisfied throughout the programme's remaining band.

#### P.5.1 Precise arithmetic scope

For the actual untwisted root `ρ=θβ`, with `θ^p=ε` and `\mathcal P(X)=εβ^p`, the normal Padé pair has coefficients in `K`, so it is fixed by the relative `p`-extension over `K(ζ_p)`. Its actual relative norm is exactly `R(X)`. The auxiliary `B(X)β−θ^{-1}A(X)` has norm `ε^{-1}R(X)`. Multiplication by the unit `ε^{-1}` does not create a missing favorable power of `X`.

The displayed polynomial theorem remains true over an arbitrary enlarged constant field, but that is a statement about the **function-field norm**. If coefficients contain `θ`, the actual automorphisms over the original base conjugate those coefficients as well. They cannot be frozen while only the `ρ` branch is changed. An actual arithmetic norm calculation with such coefficients must explicitly include that action.

Clearing fixed coefficient denominators multiplies the norm by a fixed constant and does not change its degree. A rational approximation `y−A/B` can have a norm with negative degree because its denominator is `B^p`; the usual polynomial clearing of the denominator restores exactly `R`. A different proof that the rational auxiliary is integral at a particular source would be additional arithmetic input and is not ruled out here.

There is an additional actual-source nonvanishing statement. In a nonzero unit-overlap relation, `ε` is not a `p`-th power in `L=K(ζ_p)`, and `ρ=θβ` is not in `L`. For coprime `A,B∈L[Z]`, `R(X)` cannot be zero: if `B(X)≠0`, its vanishing would make `\mathcal P(X)=(A(X)/B(X))^p` and hence make `ε` a `p`-th power; if `B(X)=0`, coprimality forces `A(X)≠0`. Thus actual normalized linear Padé auxiliaries already have nonzero relative norm. Their missing ingredient is favorable absolute-size, integrality, and coefficient-height control, not this nonvanishing.

Positive degree alone does not say that `R(X)` is large for every specified integer `X`, or that coefficients may be chosen as functions of the unknown `X` without cost. The theorem shows precisely that fixed ordinary Padé approximants cannot turn C36's one-small-branch argument into an integral norm whose asymptotic power of `X` is negative or zero. Any use of exceptional values or coefficients depending on `X` needs an additional height analysis. More general coefficient extensions or other auxiliary classes also require their actual evaluated nonvanishing to be checked.

### P.6. General Hermite auxiliaries and all `p` branches — PROVED budget, not NPZ

Let `C` be a fixed characteristic-zero constant field containing `K` and `ζ_p`. In the function field `C(Z,y)` with `y^p=\mathcal P(Z)`, take a nonzero reduced polynomial auxiliary

\[
 A(Z,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}A_j(Z)y^j,\qquad A_j\in C[Z],\qquad
 D=\max_{A_j\ne0}(\deg A_j+mj).
 \tag{P6.1}
\]

The word *reduced* means that powers `y^p` have already been replaced by `\mathcal P`. A nonzero reduced polynomial is nonzero in this function field. Here is an elementary irreducibility proof to justify that assertion. If `Y^p−\mathcal P` had a proper factor of degree `d`, `0<d<p`, the product of that factor's roots would be a constant root of unity times `y^d`, and would lie in `C(Z)`. Bézout's identity for `d,p` would then imply `y∈C(Z)`, because `y^p=\mathcal P∈C(Z)`. That is impossible at any root of `\mathcal P`, whose multiplicity lies between one and `p−1`.

For each branch define the degree at infinity

\[
 \delta_a=\deg_\infty A(Z,\zeta_p^ay),\qquad 0\le a<p.
\]

These are finite integers, possibly negative, and `δ_a≤D`. The norm is a nonzero polynomial

\[
 \mathcal N_A(Z)=\prod_{a=0}^{p-1}A(Z,\zeta_p^ay)\in C[Z]\setminus\{0\}.
 \tag{P6.2}
\]

For a completely finite reason it is polynomial: multiplication by `A` in the free basis `1,y,…,y^{p−1}` has entries in `C[Z]`, using `y^p=\mathcal P`, and its determinant is (P6.2). It is nonzero because multiplication by a nonzero element of a field is invertible. Equivalently one may diagonalize this multiplication matrix over the formal Laurent series field by evaluating at the `p` distinct branches.

Products of nonzero leading Laurent terms do not cancel. Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\deg\mathcal N_A=\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}\delta_a\ge0.}
 \tag{P6.3}
\]

Writing the cancellation defect at branch `a` as `ν_a=D−δ_a≥0` gives the exact budget

\[
 \boxed{\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}\nu_a=pD-\deg\mathcal N_A\le pD.}
 \tag{P6.4}
\]

Consequently fixed polynomial auxiliaries cannot gain more total decay at infinity than their `p` weighted degree contributions. A constant norm is the equality case in (P6.4), and this budget alone does not exclude it for a general Hermite auxiliary.

There is an additional direct finite Fourier check. For `0≤j<p`,

\[
 \sum_{a=0}^{p-1}\zeta_p^{-aj}A(Z,\zeta_p^ay)=pA_j(Z)y^j.
 \tag{P6.5}
\]

This follows by summing the geometric progression in each power `y^i`. If every branch were `O(Z^{-1})`, the left side would have only negative powers, whereas a nonzero right side has degree `deg A_j+mj≥0`. Thus every `A_j` would have to vanish. Finite character sums that isolate the branches do not make all their polynomial contributions decay.

As a check on the scope, for the unrelated curve `y^p=Z^p−1`, the auxiliary `y−Z` has constant norm. One branch has degree `−(p−1)` and the other `p−1` branches have degree one, so (P6.3) is sharp. This is not a source polynomial satisfying our `m≥2` hypotheses and is not an arithmetic counterexample to Catalan.

These identities apply to fixed coefficient fields. If the coefficients are conjugated in the actual number-field norm, or depend on the source value `X`, that action and the coefficient heights must be counted separately. For rational auxiliaries, multiplying by a polynomial denominator of degree `e` adds `e` to every `δ_a` and adds `pe` to the norm degree. It removes an apparent total gain obtained by suppressing that denominator.

## H. Nonlinear Hermite auxiliaries through weighted degree \(m+p-1\)

Keep **PRIMES** and **EXPONENTS** from §P.1: `p<q` are odd primes, `K=Q(η)`,

\[
 P(t)=\prod_c(1-\eta^ct)^{n_c},\quad
 \sum_cn_c=pm,\quad m\ge2,\quad 0\le n_c<p,
\]

with nonzero exponent vector. Write `y(Z)=Z^mP(1/Z)^{1/p}` and `\mathcal P(Z)=Z^{pm}P(1/Z)`. As proved in §P.1, `P_1=[t]P` is nonzero modulo `p` in the reduced ring `Z[η]/pZ[η]`.

### H.1. The finite coefficient theorem — PROVED

For a nonnegative integer `D`, put

\[
 ν=\min(p-1,\lfloor D/m\rfloor),\qquad
 n_j=D-mj+1\ (0\le j\le ν),\qquad
 N_D=\sum_{j=0}^{ν}n_j.
 \tag{H1.1}
\]

The weighted-degree-at-most-`D` polynomial auxiliaries

\[
 A(Z,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{ν}A_j(Z)y^j,
 \qquad \deg A_j\le D-mj,
\]

correspond, after multiplication by `t^D` and setting `Z=1/t`, to the span of the `N_D` formal series

\[
 t^sP(t)^{j/p},\qquad
 0\le j\le ν,\quad 0\le s<n_j.
 \tag{H1.2}
\]

**Theorem.** If `D≤m+p−1`, the square coefficient matrix of (H1.2) in degrees `0,…,N_D−1` is invertible. Therefore every nonzero auxiliary of weighted degree at most `D` has

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{t=0}\bigl(t^DA(1/t,t^{-m}P(t)^{1/p})\bigr)
       \le N_D-1.
 \tag{H1.3}
\]

There is a unique auxiliary for which the coefficients in degrees `0,…,N_D−2` vanish and the coefficient in degree `N_D−1` is one. Thus (H1.3) is sharp. Coefficients may belong to any fixed characteristic-zero extension of `K`; nonzero determinants over `K` remain nonzero there.

#### H.1.1 Proof retaining all factorials

Replace `1/p` by an indeterminate `α`. For `j≥1` the coefficient in row `n` and column `(j,s)` is

\[
 [t^{n-s}]P(t)^{j\alpha}=B_{n-s}(j\alpha),
\]

where `B_r(α)` is the finite coefficient polynomial of (P2.1). The columns with `j=0` are simply `1,t,…,t^D`. Their first `D+1` rows form the identity and their remaining rows vanish. Hence it is enough to consider the remaining square matrix

\[
 M_D(\alpha)=
   \bigl(B_{n-s}(j\alpha)\bigr)_{
       D+1\le n<N_D; 1\le j\le ν, 0\le s<n_j}.
 \tag{H1.4}
\]

The empty matrix case is harmless. Multiply row `n` by `n!`. Every entry is then in `Z[η][α]`. Its highest possible power of `α` is `n−s`, with coefficient

\[
 (n)_s\,j^{n-s}P_1^{n-s}.
\]

Every determinant term has the same maximum total degree

\[
 E=\sum_{0\le i<j\le ν}n_in_j.
 \tag{H1.5}
\]

Indeed this is `∑_{n=0}^{N_D−1}n−∑_j∑_{s=0}^{n_j−1}s`; deleting the `j=0` block removes the same sum from both parts. The coefficient of `α^E` in the factorial-cleared determinant is

\[
 \boxed{
 P_1^E
 \left(\prod_{j=1}^{ν}\prod_{s=0}^{n_j-1}s!\right)
 \left(\prod_{0\le i<j\le ν}(j-i)^{n_in_j}\right).
 }
 \tag{H1.6}
\]

Here is a finite proof of the confluent determinant used in (H1.6). Consider the full matrix of derivatives

\[
 \left(\frac{d^s}{dX^s}X^n\bigg|_{X=j}\right)_
     {0\le n<N_D; 0\le j\le ν, 0\le s<n_j}.
\]

Replace the monomial rows by the monic polynomials

\[
 Q_{j,s}(X)=\left(\prod_{i<j}(X-i)^{n_i}\right)(X-j)^s,
 \qquad 0\le s<n_j.
\]

Their degrees are consecutively `0,…,N_D−1`, so this row change has determinant one. The transformed evaluation matrix is block triangular. Its diagonal entry corresponding to `(j,s)` is

\[
 s!\prod_{i<j}(j-i)^{n_i}.
\]

The determinant is their product. The `j=0` derivative block has diagonal `0!,…,D!` and zeros beneath it; deleting that block divides the product by precisely those factorials. This gives (H1.6). Factoring `P_1^E` from determinant terms accounts for the powers of `P_1` above.

If `D≤m+p−1`, every nonconstant block has

\[
 n_j\le n_1=D-m+1\le p.
\]

Thus every factorial remaining in (H1.6) is prime to `p`. Also `1≤j−i≤ν≤p−1`. Consequently (H1.6) is nonzero modulo `p`; no restriction is imposed on `n_0=D+1` or the row indices. The large factorials from the polynomial block have been removed exactly, not discarded modulo `p`.

If `C_D(α)` denotes the factorial-cleared determinant, finite evaluation gives

\[
 p^EC_D(1/p)\in\mathbb Z[\eta],
 \qquad
 p^EC_D(1/p)\equiv[\alpha^E]C_D(\alpha)\not\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Therefore the determinant in (H1.4) is nonzero at `α=1/p`. The full coefficient matrix is invertible as well. Solving it with right side `(0,…,0,1)` proves the existence and uniqueness assertion, and injectivity proves (H1.3). ∎

### H.2. Exact one-branch order and the degree of its norm — PROVED

Suppose `m≤D≤m+p−1`, and take the normalized maximal-contact auxiliary from §H.1. Its weighted degree is exactly `D`. To check this, suppose it had weighted degree `D'<D`. Formula (H1.3) at `D'` would give order at most

\[
 D-D'+N_{D'}-1<N_D-1.
\]

The strict inequality follows because each increase in weighted degree adds at least one polynomial coefficient, and the interval ending at `D≥m` includes an increase that adds at least two. This contradicts its normalization.

Its selected branch therefore has exact degree at infinity

\[
 \boxed{\delta_0=D-N_D+1.}
 \tag{H2.1}
\]

For coefficients in `K`, every other `p`-th-root branch has degree exactly `D`. To justify the field fact used here, the finite cyclotomic degree proof in §D.2 (also read in C26 §1.2) gives `[K(ζ_p):K]=p−1`. Thus `Φ_p` is irreducible over `K`.

Now let

\[
 h(V)=\sum_{\deg A_j+mj=D}\operatorname{lc}(A_j)V^j.
\]

It is a nonzero polynomial over `K` of degree at most `p−1`. Since (H2.1) is negative, `h(1)=0`. If `h(ζ_p^a)=0` for any `a≠0`, irreducibility of `Φ_p` would make `h` a constant multiple of `Φ_p`; evaluation at one would then make that constant zero, contradicting `h≠0`. This proves the asserted full degree at all other branches.

The nonzero product of the leading Laurent terms now gives

\[
 \boxed{\deg N(A)=(p-1)D+\delta_0=pD-N_D+1.}
 \tag{H2.2}
\]

This is an exact formula for the Hermite auxiliary, not an estimate from counting unknown coefficients.

#### H.2.1 Uniform nonconstant-norm boundary in the range needed here

For `p≥5`, `m≥2`, and `1≤D≤m+p−1`, **every** nonzero auxiliary over `K` of exact weighted degree `D` has

\[
 \boxed{\deg N(A)\ge pD-N_D+1>0.}
 \tag{H2.3}
\]

Indeed `ν≤p−2`: otherwise `m(p−1)≤D≤m+p−1`, hence `m(p−2)≤p−1`, contradicting `m≥2` and `p≥5`. The leading polynomial `h` therefore has degree less than `p−1`, so `h(ζ_p^a)≠0` at every `a≠0`, regardless of its value at one. Those `p−1` branches have degree `D`. Formula (H1.3) bounds the remaining branch below by `D−N_D+1`, proving the first inequality.

For positivity use

\[
 pD-N_D+1
   =(p-ν-1)D-ν+\frac{mν(ν+1)}2.
\]

For `ν=0` this is `(p−1)D>0`. For `ν≥1`, `p−ν−1≥1` and `m≥2` make it at least `D+ν²>0`. For `D≥m`, the maximal-contact auxiliary of §H.1 attains equality in (H2.3).

Unlike the all-degree **linear** theorem, this nonlinear result has the explicit range `D≤m+p−1` and coefficients in `K` for its uniform norm bound. It does not assert a result for all weighted degrees or for all larger coefficient fields. The one-branch coefficient theorem (H1.3) does hold over arbitrary fixed extensions. If `m>p−1`, this range contains only constant and linear powers of `y`; the genuinely nonlinear part requires `m≤p−1` and `D≥2m`.

## T. Polynomial auxiliaries over the unit field, and the denominator cost

### T.1 A nontrivial constant unit twist prevents leading cancellation — PROVED

Let \(L\) be a characteristic-zero field containing \(\mu_p\). Let \(\epsilon\in L^\times\setminus L^{\times p}\), choose \(\theta^p=\epsilon\), and let \(\mathcal P(Z)\in L[Z]\) be monic of degree \(pm\), with \(m\ge1\). Consider the twisted polynomial equation
\[
\epsilon Y^p=\mathcal P(Z)
\]
and a nonzero reduced polynomial
\[
\mathcal B(Z,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}A_j(Z)Y^j,\qquad A_j\in L[Z],
\quad D=\max_{A_j\ne0}(\deg A_j+mj).
\]
Finite recursion gives the formal branches
\[
Y_a=\zeta_p^a\theta^{-1}Z^m(1+b_1Z^{-1}+b_2Z^{-2}+\cdots).
\]
At branch \(a\), the coefficient of \(Z^D\) in \(\mathcal B\) is
\[
c_a=\sum_{\deg A_j+mj=D}\operatorname{lc}(A_j)\zeta_p^{aj}\theta^{-j}.
\]
The elements \(1,\theta^{-1},\ldots,\theta^{-(p-1)}\) are linearly independent over \(L\). Hence \(c_a\ne0\) for every \(a\). Every branch has exactly degree \(D\) at infinity, and the multiplication determinant, equivalently the product of the branch values, satisfies
\[
\boxed{\deg N_{\mathrm{function}}(\mathcal B)=pD.}
\tag{T1}
\]
It has degree zero only when \(\mathcal B\) is a nonzero constant. This proof does not need an additional assertion that \(\mathcal P\) is irreducible.

For a nonzero source class in \(J\), positive-residue normalization gives
\[
\mathcal P(\mathcal X)=\epsilon\beta^p
\]
with \(\epsilon\) a positive primary unit which is not a \(p\)-th power, and \(\beta\) integral. Take \(L=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{pq})\). Nonpower persists from \(k\) to \(L\): its degree \(2(p-1)\) is prime to \(p\), so taking the norm of a proposed root and using Bézout would give a root already in \(k\). The distinction between this **function-field norm** and an actual number-field norm matters. At the source, \(\beta\) itself lies in the base field; the formal values \(\zeta_p^a\beta\) are not distinct Galois conjugates of \(\beta\) over that field. In contrast, \(\rho=\theta\beta\) has degree \(p\) over \(L\), and its actual conjugates are \(\zeta_p^a\rho\). §§P–H use that latter extension for their arithmetic interpretation. If coefficients contain \(\theta\), they must also be conjugated in an actual norm; one cannot freeze those coefficients while rotating only \(\rho\).

Equation (T1) is an exact boundary for fixed polynomial coefficients in the twisted variable. It is not a lower bound at every specialized integer. For example, selecting the source-dependent coefficient \(\beta\) in \(Y-\beta\) forces zero at that source and introduces precisely the uncontrolled generator one was trying to eliminate. An arithmetic argument must control coefficient heights and evaluated nonvanishing as well.

The paired source exponents give \(\mathcal P(0)=1\), and DIV gives \(p^2\mid\mathcal X\). Thus \(\mathcal P(\mathcal X)\) is a unit at every prime over \(p\). Since \(\epsilon\) is a unit, \(\beta\) is also a unit there. With valuations normalized by \(v(p)=1\),
\[
v\!\left(\frac{\beta}{\mathcal X^a}\right)=-a\,v(\mathcal X)<0
\qquad(a>0).
\tag{T2}
\]
So this apparent simultaneous smallness cannot enter an algebraic-integer norm contradiction. A more elaborate rational auxiliary must prove its *evaluated* denominator divides its numerator in the relevant ideals. A favorable degree at infinity does not prove that divisibility.

## L. Positive units, divided-ideal generators, and logarithmic smallness

### L.1. The correct logarithmic equation and a source-specific bound

Let `k=Q(zeta_q)^+`, `H=[k:Q]`, `n=H-1`, and let `U^+` be the totally positive units. Put

\[
 W=\{x\in\mathbb R^H:\sum_\sigma x_\sigma=0\},\qquad
 \ell(z)=(\log\sigma(z))_\sigma
\]

for a totally positive norm-one element z. Let

\[
 \Lambda=\ell(U^+)\subset W.
\]

For odd p, the positive-unit quotient `U^+/(U^+)^p` is exactly `U/U^p`. Surjectivity follows by replacing u with `u^(p+1)` and absorbing `u^p`; injectivity follows because a pth root of a positive unit is itself positive and a unit.

If a positive norm-one source representative s lies in the unit class represented by a positive unit epsilon, write

\[
 s=\epsilon\beta^p,\qquad \beta\in k^\times.
 \tag{L1}
\]

The sign and norm equations make beta positive and norm one. The precise logarithmic equation is

\[
 \boxed{\ell(\epsilon)=\ell(s)-p\ell(\beta)
 =p\bigl(p^{-1}\ell(s)-\ell(\beta)\bigr).}
 \tag{L2}
\]

Replacing beta by `beta*u`, with `u in U^+`, replaces epsilon by `epsilon*u^(-p)`. Thus its logarithm changes by **minus p times** a lattice vector. The invariant is the class of `ell(epsilon)` in `Lambda/p Lambda`, not its selected real vector.

#### L.1.1 PROVED explicit source logarithm bound

In the C26 source notation,

\[
 B_a=(\mathcal X-\eta^a)(\mathcal X-\eta^{-a}),\qquad
 Y=|\mathcal X|>1.
\]

Every conjugate of every B_a lies in `[(Y-1)^2,(Y+1)^2]`. For any integer exponent vector r with sum zero, put `s=prod B_a^(r_a)` and `L=sum |r_a|`. The positive and negative exponent sums are both L/2, so

\[
 \boxed{\|\ell(s)\|_\infty
 \le L\log\frac{Y+1}{Y-1}
 \le\frac{2L}{Y-1}.}
 \tag{L3}
\]

Every augmentation class modulo p has a lift with sum zero and `|r_a|<=p-1`: start with residues in `[0,p-1]`, whose sum is `pa`, and subtract p from any a positive entries. There are more than a positive entries if a>0. Therefore each source class admits such a representative with the uniform bound

\[
 \boxed{\delta_Y:=H(p-1)\log\frac{Y+1}{Y-1}
 \le\frac{2H(p-1)}{Y-1}.}
 \tag{L4}
\]

Its norm is one because the B_a have equal norms and the exponents sum to zero. Under C26's `Y>p^(8H)/8`, delta_Y is extremely small. This is an actual use of the affine source, not a hypothesis introduced for the counterfixture.

### L.2. PROVED: a sharp universal positive-unit separation

Set

\[
 R_0=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2},\qquad \lambda_0=\log R_0.
\]

**Lemma.** In every totally real number field, every nonidentity totally positive unit u satisfies

\[
 \boxed{\|\ell(u)\|_\infty\ge\lambda_0.}
 \tag{L5}
\]

**Proof.** The element

\[
 v=u+u^{-1}-2=(u-1)^2/u
\]

is an algebraic integer. If u is not 1, every conjugate of v is strictly positive: an embedding taking u to 1 would make u itself equal to 1. Its norm is consequently a positive integer, at least 1. If every `|log sigma(u)|<lambda_0`, every conjugate of v is less than

\[
 R_0+R_0^{-1}-2=1,
\]

which would give norm less than 1. This contradiction proves the lemma. The constant is sharp across totally real fields: the positive unit `(3+sqrt5)/2` in `Q(sqrt5)` has conjugates `R_0,R_0^(-1)` and attains equality. This last assertion follows directly from its monic polynomial `T²-3T+1`. ∎

Applying the lemma to a quotient gives uniform separation of distinct points of Lambda. It improves the C29/C36 trace-based constant `log(1+1/H)`, without any statement about a regulator or the full rank of the unit group.

#### L.2.1 PROVED repulsion criterion for a nontrivial unit class

Suppose (L1) holds, `[epsilon]!=0 in U/U^p`, and `||ell(s)||_infty<=delta`. For every positive unit u, `epsilon*u^(-p)` is a nonidentity positive unit. Equations (L2) and (L5) give

Thus

 \ge\frac{\lambda_0-\delta}{p}.}
 \tag{L6}

If an independently constructed generator of this principal divided divisor, after multiplication by a positive unit, violates (L6), that component cannot principalize through a nontrivial unit class. Applied to every nonzero source class, this would prove h=0.

This is **repulsion from the zero unit coset**, not contraction toward it. Modulo Lambda, equation (L2) reads

As the source logarithm tends to zero, beta tends to the p-division lattice coset determined by the unit class. A nonzero coset is perfectly compatible with an arbitrarily small source logarithm. Nothing in discreteness moves that coset to zero.

#### L.3.1 A stronger finite-subgroup criterion: uniformly small generators force h=0

### L.4. PROVED constructive density with the unit class retained

Here is a precise source-free limitation that uses actual number-field elements.

Let k be the real q-cyclotomic field, let sigma generate its cyclic Galois group, and let epsilon be a totally positive primary unit. Write

\[
 \epsilon'=\sigma(\epsilon)/\epsilon.
\]

Fix an integer M>=2. Finite local lifting gives an element v_0, integral and a unit at p, such that

\[
 \epsilon v_0^p\equiv1\pmod{p^M}.
 \tag{L9}
\]

This is a finite congruence at every factor over \(p\). Here is the lifting step explicitly. Primaryness supplies \(v_2\), a unit at \(p\), with \(\epsilon v_2^p\equiv1\bmod p^2\). If \(\epsilon v_r^p\equiv1\bmod p^r\), \(r\ge2\), put \(v_{r+1}=v_r+p^{r-1}t\). The binomial theorem gives
\[
\epsilon v_{r+1}^p
 \equiv \epsilon v_r^p+p^r\epsilon v_r^{p-1}t
 \pmod{p^{r+1}}.
\]
Every term of binomial index at least two is divisible by \(p^{r+1}\), because \(p\) is odd and \(r\ge2\). The coefficient \(\epsilon v_r^{p-1}\) is invertible modulo \(p\), so a finite residue choice of \(t\) kills the remaining error. Repeating up to the chosen \(M\) proves (L9). Finite Chinese remaindering makes the choices simultaneously at all factors over \(p\). The cyclotomic order has index prime to \(p\), by the read discriminant calculation in C36 §2.1, so the residue class has an integral coefficient lift in the power basis used below. The actual fixture also supplies and checks that lift directly.

Choose the real power basis `1,theta,...,theta^(H-1)`, where `theta=eta+eta^(-1)`. Its real embedding matrix is invertible by the Vandermonde determinant. Consequently there are real coefficients c_j for which

\[
 \sum_j c_j\sigma(\theta)^j=\sigma(\epsilon)^{-1/p}
\]

at every real embedding, with the positive pth roots chosen by IVT. For every sufficiently large positive integer D congruent to 1 modulo p^M, choose integers a_j in the coefficient residue classes of v_0 such that

\[
 |a_j-Dc_j|\le p^M/2.
\]

Put

\[
 a=\sum_j a_j\theta^j,\quad v=a/D,\quad
 \beta=\sigma(v)/v=\sigma(a)/a,
\]
\[
 \boxed{\alpha=\epsilon'\beta^p
 =\frac{\sigma(\epsilon v^p)}{\epsilon v^p}.}
 \tag{L10}
\]

For D sufficiently large, all conjugates of v are positive. The construction gives:

- alpha and beta are totally positive and norm one, by telescoping the conjugates.
- `alpha=1 mod p^M` at every factor over p, by (L9).
- `[alpha]=[epsilon']`, independent of D.
- Every finite valuation of alpha is a multiple of p; its divided divisor is exactly the principal divisor of beta.
- If epsilon is circular, alpha has explicit circular descent with circular part epsilon'.
- Every conjugate of `epsilon*v^p` tends to 1; hence all logarithms of alpha tend to zero.

These are direct finite approximation statements. No analytic approximation theorem is being imported.

### D.2. Named inputs and notation

The named inputs are those of C26 §1:

Put

\[
k=\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)^+,\quad \ell=\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)^+,\quad
M=k\ell,\quad H=[k:\mathbb Q],\quad m=[\ell:\mathbb Q].
\]

Here is the finite degree proof, following the argument actually read in C26 §1.2. Let `zeta` be a primitive `N`th root and let `f` be its monic irreducible factor in `Z[X]`. If a prime `r∤N` took `zeta` to a root of a different irreducible factor `h` of `Phi_N`, then `f(X)` would divide `h(X^r)`. Modulo `r`, `h(X^r)=h(X)^r`, so the reductions of `f` and `h` would share a nonconstant factor. But `fh` divides `X^N−1`, whose reduction is squarefree because its derivative is `N X^(N−1)` and `r∤N`. This is a contradiction. Iterating over the prime factors of every integer coprime to `N` shows that all primitive `N`th roots are roots of `f`; thus `Phi_N` is irreducible of degree `phi(N)`.

Apply this to `N=p,q,pq`. The compositum of the two full cyclotomic fields is `Q(zeta_(pq))`, by Bezout on `p,q`, and has degree `(p−1)(q−1)=4Hm`. ### D.6. PROVED: mixed powers and ideal classes remain independent

#### D.6.1 An injective mixed-power map

For a real field `K`, write `K^1` for its norm-one multiplicative subgroup. There is an injective map

\[
\begin{split}
\mathcal J:\quad
k^1/(k^1)^p\ \times\ \ell^1/(\ell^1)^q
&\longrightarrow M^*/M^{*pq},\\
([a],[b])&\longmapsto[a^q b^p].
\end{split}
\tag{D10}
\]

It is well defined because changing either representative by its defining prime power changes the product by a `pq`th power.

Suppose `a^q b^p=gamma^(pq)`. Taking the norm to `k` gives

\[
a^{qm}=N_{M/k}(\gamma)^{pq},
\]

because the cross norm of `b` is one. Since `gcd(qm,p)=1`, choose integers `u,v` with `u q m+v p=1`. Then

\[
a=\left(N_{M/k}(\gamma)^{qu}a^v\right)^p.
\]

Its root has norm one because its `p`th norm is one and `p` is odd. The norm to `ell` gives the corresponding conclusion for `b`, since `gcd(pH,q)=1`. This proves injectivity. No `ALG` condition is needed in this mixed-prime argument.

For unit classes the same assertion holds in the unit subgroups: a global prime-power root of a unit has zero finite valuations. Any real unit class modulo an odd prime can, if needed, be represented by a totally positive norm-one unit: replace `epsilon` by `epsilon^(p+1)` or `epsilon^(q+1)`, respectively, without changing its class.

A same-prime variant is also useful. If `a∈k^1`, `b∈ell^1` and `ab∈M^{*p}`, norms show that `a∈k^{*p}` and, under `ALG`, `b∈ell^{*p}`. The scalar norm degrees are `m` and `H`, both prime to `p` under that condition. For `q`th powers both degrees are automatically prime to `q`.

#### D.6.2 Product injection for the divided ideal-class images

Let `A_p⊂Cl(k)[p]` and `A_q⊂Cl(ell)[q]` be the two source divided-divisor images. Extension to `M` is injective on each. For example, if an ideal `a` in `k`, with `[a]^p=1`, becomes principal in `M`, its norm shows `[a]^m=1`. Since `gcd(m,p)=1`, `[a]=1`. The proof for the second image uses `gcd(H,q)=1`.

The two extended images intersect trivially: any element in their intersection is killed by both `p` and `q`. Therefore the compositum contains

\[
|A_p|\,|A_q|=p^{n-h_p}q^{s-h_q}
\tag{D11}
\]

distinct classes, assuming the first RAD input as well as the second theorem above.

This is a positive count, but not an overlap upper bound. Multiplying the two existing base-field representative counts only adds their already-known logarithmic inequalities. If one instead counts ideals directly in `M`, the degrees of extended norms must be charged:

\[
N_{M/\mathbb Q}(\mathfrak a\mathcal O_M)
=N_{k/\mathbb Q}(\mathfrak a)^m,
\qquad
N_{M/\mathbb Q}(\mathfrak b\mathcal O_M)
=N_{\ell/\mathbb Q}(\mathfrak b)^H.
\]

No improved compositum count is supplied here. Equation (D11) must not be combined with an unproved count having only one base-field degree.

### D.7. PROVED: what unit cancellation and coprime tensors do not do

#### D.7.1 Within one source overlap

Choose independent source classes `alpha_1,...,alpha_h` in the unit overlap, represented as

\[
\alpha_i=\epsilon_i\beta_i^p.
\]

Their unit classes `[epsilon_i]` are independent modulo `p`th powers. Otherwise a nonzero coefficient relation among the units would give the same relation among the source classes, contradicting RAD. Thus any linear combination that cancels all unit classes already cancels the same source vector. The analogous statement holds for exponent `q`.

Large `h` does not change this argument: the unit-rank budget is `n` in the first field and `s` in the second, so `h` independent units can fit whenever `h≤n` or `h≤s`. An additional arithmetic restriction on which units occur is required.

#### D.7.2 Between fields

C26 proves `gcd(u,v)=1`. The ordinary divisors of the two augmentation ratio families are supported, respectively, above the rational primes dividing `u` and `v`; the ramified conductor valuations cancel. Hence a literal cross-field product cannot cancel nonzero valuations between the two families. If it is a unit, both component products already have zero divisor. This concerns the literal source representatives; arbitrary subsequent prime-power adjustments can introduce further supports and must be counted separately.

For exact totally positive units, `epsilon∈k`, `eta∈ell`, and `epsilon eta=1` imply `epsilon=eta=1`, because the intersection of the fields is rational. The stronger modulo-power statement is (D10).

For additive groups `A` killed by `p` and `B` killed by `q`, every biadditive pairing from `A×B` is zero: its value is killed by both primes. Equivalently, `A tensor_Z B=0`. Choose integers `u,v` with `up+vq=1` and apply this identity to an elementary tensor. Thus a determinant or tensor construction built only from these two torsion modules has no mixed information. Integer lifts or a nonlinear identity involving the common `x,y` are extra input, and are not ruled out.

#### D.7.3 No uncharged archimedean cancellation for separated products

Let `epsilon∈k` and `eta∈ell` be units, and write

\[
a_i=\log|\sigma_i\epsilon|,\qquad
b_j=\log|\tau_j\eta|.
\]

Their sums are zero. Since every pair of embeddings occurs independently in `M`,

\[
\sum_{i,j}(a_i+b_j)^2
=m\sum_i a_i^2+H\sum_j b_j^2.
\tag{D12}
\]

The mixed term is exactly zero. Also `max_{i,j}(a_i+b_j)=max_i a_i+max_j b_j`, and likewise for minima. In particular the house of the separated product equals the product of the houses. This elementary identity explains why selecting a common unit factor across linearly disjoint fields gives no free smallness. It says nothing about a nonseparated sum with coefficients; C26 §3.2 explains the additional denominators and degrees for that problem.

### D.8. A simultaneous model in one integral unit lattice

The following construction proves a precise limitation of the listed interfaces. It does not model the affine equation or any actual prime-ideal norm count.

Let

\[
E_p=\Lambda_H/p\Lambda_H,\quad
E_q=\Lambda_m/q\Lambda_m,\quad
\Lambda_N=\{z\in\mathbb Z^N:\sum z_i=0\}.
\]

Let `F_p,F_q` be the actual cyclic multiplication operators. Choose arbitrary invariant summands

\[
I_p\subseteq\ker F_p,\qquad I_q\subseteq\ker F_q.
\]

Semisimplicity supplies projections `P_p,P_q` onto these summands; put `Q_p=1−P_p`, `Q_q=1−P_q`. Write `i_p=dim I_p`, `i_q=dim I_q`.

#### D.8.1 The two base structures

Use the full base unit lattices `Lambda_H` and `Lambda_m`. Put

\[
P_k=\ker(\Lambda_H\xrightarrow{Q_p\bmod p}Q_pE_p),\qquad
P_\ell=\ker(\Lambda_m\xrightarrow{Q_q\bmod q}Q_qE_q).
\]

Define formal multiplicative element groups additively as `Lambda_H⊕P_k` and `Lambda_m⊕P_ell`, with ordinary divisor equal to the second coordinate. The unit kernels are exactly the first summands. Their formal class quotients are

\[
C_p=Q_pE_p,\qquad C_q=Q_qE_q.
\]

The singular classes modulo the relevant prime power are respectively

\[
S_p=E_p\oplus C_p,\qquad S_q=E_q\oplus C_q.
\]

For instance, `p`-singular elements have second coordinate in `p Lambda_H`, and dividing by `p`th powers identifies their divisor coordinate with `Lambda_H/P_k=C_p`.

The source maps and local maps are

\[
x\longmapsto(P_px,Q_px),\quad\delta_p(u,c)=F_pu;
\qquad
y\longmapsto(P_qy,Q_qy),\quad\delta_q(v,d)=F_qv.
\tag{D13}
\]

Both source maps are injective and primary; their overlaps are exactly `I_p,I_q`. Their divided image dimensions are `n−i_p,s−i_q`. The units have one copy of each nontrivial character, satisfying the elementary multiplicity budget with equality. One may take the entire unit module as circular, as in C36's model. The reflected operator identity `D=(2/conductor)(1−T^(−b))F` kills each chosen overlap automatically; it gives no inverse on a zero component.

#### D.8.2 One global integer unit lattice

Use

\[
\Lambda=\left\{(z_{ij})\in\mathbb Z^{H\times m}:\sum_{i,j}z_{ij}=0\right\},
\]

of rank `Hm−1`. Embed base units by repeated rows and columns:

\[
\operatorname{res}_k(x)_{ij}=x_i,\qquad
\operatorname{res}_\ell(y)_{ij}=y_j.
\]

Define the two norms by row and column sums. Then

\[
N_k\operatorname{res}_k=m\,\mathrm{id},\quad
N_\ell\operatorname{res}_\ell=H\,\mathrm{id},\quad
N_k\operatorname{res}_\ell=0,\quad
N_\ell\operatorname{res}_k=0.
\tag{D14}
\]

The two characteristic reductions thus come from the same integral unit lattice; they are not unrelated vector spaces with independently invented norm maps. The orthogonal CRT idempotents in `Z/(pq)` keep the two coefficient characteristics distinct. The integer version of (D12) also holds in this lattice.

#### D.8.3 Disjoint integer divisor supports and a common class quotient

Use two tagged, disjoint summands

\[
D_u=\Lambda_H\otimes\mathbb Z^m,
\qquad D_v=\mathbb Z^H\otimes\Lambda_m.
\]

Elements of `D_u` are arrays with every column sum zero; those of `D_v` have every row sum zero. The tags encode the disjoint `u` and `v` rational supports. Define

\[
\theta_p(A)=m^{-1}Q_p(\operatorname{rowsum}A\bmod p),
\qquad
\theta_q(B)=H^{-1}Q_q(\operatorname{colsum}B\bmod q).
\]

The scalar inverses exist. Set

\[
P_M=\ker\bigl(D_u\oplus D_v\xrightarrow{(\theta_p,\theta_q)}C_p\oplus C_q\bigr).
\tag{D15}
\]

Both maps are surjective (a single chosen row or column realizes any desired vector). Thus the common formal class quotient is exactly

\[
(D_u\oplus D_v)/P_M\simeq C_p\oplus C_q,
\]

with cardinality `p^(n−i_p)q^(s−i_q)`. Take the global element group to be `Lambda⊕P_M`; its divisor kernel is exactly the common unit lattice.

Extension of a base divisor repeats it across rows or columns. The factor `m^(-1)` or `H^(-1)` makes extension induce the identity on its own class quotient. Row/column norm maps induce multiplication by `m`/`H`, respectively, and the cross norms are zero. These maps preserve principal divisors and commute with cyclic shifts. All these assertions hold on the full integer lattices, not merely on their class quotients.

The models therefore retain both local zero spaces, both full source modules, a common multiplicity-one integral unit representation, exact divided-divisor kernels, disjoint supports, and the norm relations (D14), for every independently chosen pair `I_p,I_q`. They show that these interfaces alone do not force one overlap to disappear when the other is present.

They do **not** show that actual arithmetic overlaps can be arbitrary. In particular, no actual number-field class-number cap, actual prime norms, height restrictions, or affine source equation is included in (D15). It would be incorrect to combine these small formal models with an actual cyclotomic ideal count as though they were number fields.
