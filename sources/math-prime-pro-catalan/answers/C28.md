## 2. The rational-integer reductions: exact losses and retained components

### 2.1 Finite residue covariance and named conditions

Let p and q be distinct odd primes, F=Q(eta), eta=zeta_q, K=Q(zeta_p), L=FK. Let j be complex conjugation on L. Let H_p=Gal(L/F), whose element tau_a fixes eta and sends zeta_p to zeta_p^a. Let H_q=Gal(L/K). Put H=(q-1)/2.

**REAL:** z lies in F^+ and has norm one from F^+ to Q. **SEM:** p does not divide q-1. For the actual C26 source these hold for both alpha and z=alpha^Xi_c, using the named source premises and BAND+DW from C26. **NP:** z is not a p-th power in L; this is the C26/C27 conclusion for z under those premises. **SING:** div(alpha)=pA for an explicitly determined integral-coefficient fractional divisor A. All denominator divisors in the following symbol identities avoid p and the supports of the relevant numerator and its conjugates. For the source, rational primes not dividing pqu suffice.

For a prime P of L away from p and a P-unit z, define (z/P)_p as the unique element of mu_p reducing to z^((NP-1)/p). Extend multiplicatively to signed finite divisors. Finite-field conjugation proves

    (z/sigma D)_p = (sigma^-1 z/D)_p^omega_p(sigma).           (R1)

This is the entire finite covariance input for the rationalization arguments below. It is not a reciprocity law: it just transports one residue congruence by an automorphism.

### 2.2 PROVED: every rational comparison for the source is already 1=1

**Proposition 2.1.** If jz=z and jD=D, then (z/D)_p=1. More generally

    (z/(D+jD))_p=1,
    (z/(D-jD))_p=(z/D)_p^2.                                (R2)

Proof. Equation (R1), with omega_p(j)=-1, gives (z/jD)_p=(z/D)_p^-1. Multiply, or divide, the two symbols. If D is fixed, its symbol equals its inverse; mu_p has odd order, hence that symbol is one. This also covers primes individually fixed by j. No pairing of distinct primes is being assumed.

Consequences: every divisor consisting of all primes above a chosen rational prime, with equal multiplicities, has trivial detecting value for z. So does every rational principal divisor (n). This applies to every real element, not just a singular or primary one. Enlarging the rational-prime bound cannot fix the loss.

**Proposition 2.2.** Under REAL and SING, for every rational integer n coprime to p and to the full support of alpha,

    (alpha/(n))_p = 1 = (n/(alpha))_p.                       (R3)

Proof. The first equality is Proposition 2.1. For the second, div(alpha)=pA, so (n/(alpha))_p=(n/A)_p^p=1. Signed multiplicities cause no problem. Thus the source-specific rational-integer reciprocity comparison holds by two separate finite calculations. It cannot yield the nontrivial detecting value needed in C27 §8.3. The stronger strong-primary condition is unused in this assertion.

The same calculation applies after exchanging the two arguments when the algebraic denominator is alpha and the other argument is an admissible algebraic number J: (J/(alpha))_p=1 by SING. The missing implication is that this equals (alpha/(J))_p. Eisenstein's rational-integer law does not authorize that equality for this pair.

### 2.3 PROVED: norms and rationalization modulo powers

#### 2.3.1 The useful norm compatibility has zero target

For a prime Q of K unramified in L and avoiding the relevant support,

    (N_(L/K) z / Q)_p = product_(P|Q) (z/P)_p.             (R4)

Proof directly in residue fields. Reducing the field norm gives the product, over P|Q, of the finite-field norms of z mod P. Raise this product to (NQ-1)/p. The P-th factor becomes z^((NP-1)/p), since the finite-field norm exponent is (NP-1)/(NQ-1). Every factor is the reduction of the corresponding member of mu_p; reduction is injective there. This proves (R4) without global reciprocity or an Artin map.

Under REAL,

    N_(L/K) z = N_(F/Q) z = N_(F^+/Q)(z)^2 = 1.            (R5)

The first equality follows by listing the q-1 automorphisms fixing K; the disjoint conductor degrees give [L:K]=q-1. Thus the right side of (R4) is one for every Q. Passing to K, or using divisors extended from K, discards exactly the information that was to be detected.

The other norm is N_(L/F)z=z^(p-1). It retains the inverse p-power class, but F does not contain mu_p and is not the field in the exponent-p Eisenstein statement below. Adjoining mu_p again does not fix averaging: if a divisor comes from F, it is fixed by tau_-1, which fixes z and inverts mu_p; the proof of Proposition 2.1 again gives symbol one. This second argument does not require z to be real, only z in F.

#### 2.3.2 A stronger obstruction: no K-valued representative of the target class

**Proposition 2.3 (PROVED).** Assume REAL and SEM. If

    z=t w^p,  t in K*, w in L*,

then z is already a p-th power in L. Hence under NP the class of z cannot be represented by an element of `K*`, and in particular cannot be represented by a rational integer or rational number.

Proof with explicit exponents. Put n=q-1 and v=N_(L/K)w. By (R5), 1=t^n v^p. Choose integers A,B with An+Bp=1 (for example A is the least inverse of n mod p and B=(1-An)/p). Then

    t=t^(An+Bp)=(t^B v^-A)^p,

No descent theorem is imported. This proves more than the failure of literal field membership: multiplying by an arbitrary p-th power cannot repair the field mismatch. The same result applies to alpha, whose class is nonzero by C26's injective source augmentation map.

#### 2.3.3 The standard Jacobi denominator also resists K-rationalization

Assume p<q. Let ell be completely split in L, and let P be one specified prime above ell. C27 constructs J_P with

    div(J_P)=Theta_c P,
    Theta_c=sum_(b,pq)=1 floor(cb/(pq)) sigma_b^-1,

where c=1 mod p and c=p-1 mod q. Under SEM, J_P is not in `K*L*^p`.

Proof. Suppose J_P=t w^p with t in K. At the ell-primes, its valuation vector modulo p must be constant on each H_q-orbit, because t has the same valuation at every prime above a fixed prime of K. Thus floor(cb/(pq)) mod p would depend only on b mod p. Weighting these coefficients by b^-1 mod p and projecting sigma_b to Gal(F^+/Q) would give a constant vector on Gplus. But C26's explicitly proved CRT identity gives that projected vector as D_(p-1) plus a constant vector; D has augmentation zero and identity coefficient -2. If D itself were constant, SEM would force it to be zero (H is invertible mod p), contradicting -2. This disproves the assumed representation. It does not use the source equation, only the exact Jacobi valuations and finite CRT identity.

In particular neither member of the proposed pair (alpha,J_P) can be made K-valued by removing p-th powers under the source hypotheses. Averaging J_P to a rational norm instead changes its divisor into a detecting-zero divisor.

\tag{2.1}

Thus its rational conjugate norm is already a pth power. In the power-class group, `[j(J_P)]=-[J_P]`, whereas `J_P/j(J_P)` represents `2[J_P]`. ### 2.4 PROVED: weighted averaging retains detection but is not rational averaging

Define the integer divisor

    D_omega=sum_(a=1)^(p-1) [a^-1]_p tau_a D.

For z in F, equation (R1) gives

    (z/D_omega)_p
       = product_a (z/D)_p^(a [a^-1]_p)
       = (z/D)_p^(p-1) = (z/D)_p^-1.                     (R6)

So D_omega detects exactly when D does. It is an explicit useful alternative to ordinary symmetrization. It does not invoke the unknown existence of D: it transforms any supplied D, leaving the existence/bound question unchanged.

Modulo p divisors, tau_b D_omega=b D_omega. To check the orientation, set u=ba in the sum; a^-1=b u^-1. Thus this is the omega eigenspace, not the invariant eigenspace. For b=-1 it is anti-invariant. If the detecting symbol is nontrivial, D_omega cannot also be invariant modulo p: since p is odd the intersection of these two eigenspaces is zero, and a p-multiple divisor has trivial symbol. The Jacobi product

    J_(D_omega)=product_a tau_a(J_D)^([a^-1]_p)

has divisor Theta_c D_omega, because the Galois group is abelian. It retains the required twist, but it is precisely a nonrational denominator of the kind for which REC is still needed.

The simple signed version D-jD has the same feature: by (R2) it squares the detecting value, an invertible operation on mu_p. Rationalization via D+jD sets it to one. The exact boundary is therefore constructive, not merely a warning that norms might lose information.

## 3. PROVED: a restricted Eisenstein law, with a complete finite proof

This theorem is a genuine subcase of the classical rational-integer law, proved here. We do not import the full law. Its restriction is made explicit; in particular the proof does not pretend the conductor-pq source is an element of Q(zeta_p).

### 3.1 Statement and hypotheses

Let p be an odd prime, K=Q(zeta), zeta=zeta_p, and lambda=1-zeta. Let a be a nonzero algebraic integer in Z[zeta] with:

* **ER-local:** a is prime to p and a is congruent to some rational integer a0 modulo lambda^2.
* **ER-split:** every prime in div(a) lies above a rational prime ell satisfying ell=1 mod p.

Let n be a rational integer prime to p and coprime to a. Then

    (a/(n))_p=(n/(a))_p.                                  (ER)

Here ER-local means exactly the displayed congruence modulo lambda^2. It is named separately here because it is weaker and different in formulation from C26's strong-primary condition (local p-th roots at every p-place). For the stated field K, a strong local root does imply ER-local: choose an integer v0 with the same lambda residue as a local root v; expansion of v^p gives v^p=v0^p modulo lambda^2, since every remaining term contains either p*lambda or lambda^p. The converse is not asserted or needed. Neither observation moves the actual source alpha from F into K.

### 3.2 Explicit Gauss products

For each prime P in div(a), of rational norm ell, let chi_P(t)=(t/P)_p^-1, using the finite-field definition. Put

    G_P=sum_(t in F_ell*) chi_P(t) zeta_ell^t,
    Phi(P)=G_P^p.

Descent and integrality can be proved directly in a free monic polynomial quotient, without an unmentioned integral-basis assertion. Put R=Z[zeta] and S=R[T]/Phi_ell(T). Represent G_P by the displayed finite character sum with T instead of zeta_ell. Substitution T->T^u, for u in F_ell*, multiplies G_P by chi_P(u)^-1, so fixes G_P^p. Every invariant of these substitutions in S lies in R: choose its degree-ell-2 representative and append zero as the T^(ell-1) coefficient. A substitution permutes the nonzero-index coefficients. If the difference represents zero in S, it is a scalar multiple of 1+T+...+T^(ell-1); its constant coefficient is zero, so that scalar is zero. Invariance then makes all nonzero-index coefficients equal; the appended zero makes them all zero. Thus G_P^p is a constant Phi(P) in R. Evaluating T=zeta_ell gives the claimed field element and proves its integrality. Let

    A=sum_(r=1)^(p-1) r sigma_r^-1 = p theta_p.

The finite binomial proof of C27 §2.2.2, with n=p, gives

    div(Phi(P))=A P,
    |sigma(Phi(P))|^2=ell^p  for every sigma in Gal(K/Q).  (ER1)

Here is the exact leading-term calculation, so the imported split-prime statement is visible. At P the character chi_P(t) reduces to t^(-d), d=(ell-1)/p. Put pi=zeta_ell-1. The order normalization is finite: the split evaluation of K at ell lifts by the simple-root digit recurrence, and ell=product_(j=1)^(ell-1)(1-zeta_ell^j) is pi^(ell-1) times a unit, because each quotient (1-zeta_ell^j)/(1-zeta_ell) reduces to j. Thus an ell-divisible base coefficient has pi-order at least ell-1. In G_(chi_P^r), the coefficient of pi^k is sum_t chi_P(t)^r binom(t,k). Its reduction is zero for k<rd, by finite power sums; at k=rd it is -1/(rd)!, nonzero modulo ell. Since the pi-order of ell is ell-1, every earlier coefficient contributes order greater than rd. Thus v_pi(G_(chi_P^r))=rd and v_P(sigma_r Phi(P))=r. This gives the inverse sigma_r^-1 in (ER1). Finite additive-character orthogonality gives G_chi conjugate(G_chi)=ell. The resulting rational norm excludes any other prime divisors. These are all finite identities and valuations; no class-group annihilator is used.

Write D=div(a), and define Phi(D)=product_P Phi(P)^(v_P(a)). Then

    eps=Phi(D)/a^A

has zero finite divisor. Here its integrality and that of its inverse admit the following explicit denominator proof. The integer multiplication matrix of a^A on the power basis of R has determinant N(a^A); its adjugate writes eps with a rational integer denominator supported only on primes dividing N(a). These primes all split by ER-split. For each such ell and each denominator precision ell^e, the distinct nontrivial p-th roots modulo ell lift uniquely to roots modulo ell^e; their pairwise differences are units. Zero component orders for eps mean its numerator vanishes at every lifted root modulo ell^e. The unit-Vandermonde evaluation matrix therefore forces every coefficient of that numerator to be divisible by ell^e. Cancel the denominator at each ell. This proves eps in R. Apply the same argument to eps^-1, whose adjugate denominator divides a power of N(Phi(D)), again supported only on the same split primes. Hence eps is a unit in R, without an appeal to a global unit theorem or a class group. Moreover A(1+j)=p sum_sigma sigma. Therefore, for every embedding sigma,

    |sigma(a^A)|^2=N_(K/Q)(a)^p
                   =|sigma(Phi(D))|^2,

so every conjugate of eps has modulus one.

### 3.3 The residual unit is explicitly removed, not assumed to be a power

First prove the elementary finite-order lemma. If an algebraic integer u has all conjugates of modulus one, the coefficients of the monic polynomial formed from all conjugates of u^m are integers bounded in absolute value by the corresponding binomial coefficients, independently of m. Only finitely many such polynomials exist. Their roots form a finite set, so two positive powers of u coincide. Hence u is a root of unity. Applied to eps, this proves finite order without importing a unit theorem or Kronecker's theorem as an unexplained input.

For each G_P, reducing zeta to one makes chi_P(t)=1, so

    G_P = -1 mod lambda,
    Phi(P)=G_P^p = -1 mod lambda^2.

The second assertion follows by the binomial expansion: mixed terms have a factor p*lambda, and the last term lambda^p; all have lambda-order at least two. Compute this in the free monic quotient S from §3.2. Since Phi(P) has already been proved to be a constant in R, its congruence modulo lambda^2 contracts coefficientwise in the free R-basis of S. Thus Phi(D) is congruent to 1 or -1 modulo lambda^2.

ER-local is preserved by every sigma_r. Consequently

    a^A = a0^(p(p-1)/2) mod lambda^2.

Finite Fermat gives a0^(p-1)=1 modulo p, so a0^((p-1)/2) is one of the two roots of T²-1 in F_p, namely 1 or -1. Since p is divisible by lambda^(p-1), with p-1>=2, the last display is 1 or -1 modulo lambda^2. Hence eps is congruent to 1 or -1 modulo lambda^2.

Finally, a root of unity u congruent to 1 modulo lambda^2 must be 1. If its order m is prime to p, (u^m-1)/(u-1) is congruent to m modulo lambda and is nonzero, forcing u=1. If p divides m, then u^(m/p) is a primitive p-th root and remains congruent to 1 modulo lambda^2; but every zeta^r-1 with 1<=r<p has exactly lambda-order one, since its quotient by zeta-1 reduces to r. This is impossible. Apply this argument to eps or -eps. It proves

    eps=1 or -1,
    Phi(D)=± a^A.                                        (ER2)

In particular the residual unit has been dealt with completely. This is why the restricted theorem is an element calculation rather than an ideal assertion with a hidden unit gap.

### 3.4 The finite Frobenius calculation and the rational integer

Let Q be a prime of K over a rational prime r not dividing p*N(a), with residue degree f. Frobenius sends zeta_p to zeta_p^r, so f=ord_p(r); hence NQ=r^f and f divides p-1. Reduce G_P at a prime above Q in the ring also containing zeta_ell. The finite binomial Frobenius map gives

    G_P^(NQ)
      = sum_t chi_P(t) zeta_ell^(t*NQ)
      = chi_P(NQ)^-1 G_P mod Q.

The characters are unchanged since NQ=1 mod p. Reindexing t by t*NQ is legitimate because r differs from ell. Divide by the nonzero G_P, use Phi(P)=G_P^p, and identify mu_p under its injective reduction. The result is

    (Phi(P)/Q)_p=(NQ/P)_p.

Multiplying over P in D and applying (ER2) gives

    (a^A/Q)_p=(NQ/(a))_p.                                (ER3)

The left side is

    product_(s=1)^(p-1) (sigma_s^-1 a/Q)_p^s
       = product_s (a/sigma_s Q)_p
       = (a/(r))_p^f.

The middle identity is the elementary covariance (R1), including its cyclotomic twist. In the last identity each prime over r occurs exactly f times in the full Galois orbit, so the product divisor is (r)^f. The right side of (ER3) is (r/(a))_p^f. This proof pinpoints the rational integer in the calculation: it is NQ=r^f, not an arbitrary algebraic Jacobi element J_D. Replacing NQ by J_D is not a permissible step in the Frobenius reindexing. The proof makes no assertion for non-split prime factors of a, nor for an arbitrary primary element of L. Neither extension is needed to establish the rationalization failures in §2.

## 4. Detection: a bounded supply, and the missing nontrivial residue

### 4.1 PROVED: a bounded eligible Jacobi prime

Named hypotheses: `p,q` are distinct odd primes, `N=pq`; `u` is a nonzero integer; source factors, when used, satisfy `CASS-Q: Phi_q(X)=q*u^p`. Put `t=N*abs(u)`, and

\[
V=\Phi_N(t)=\frac{(t^N-1)(t-1)}{(t^p-1)(t^q-1)}.
\]

The quotient is an integer polynomial evaluation: the roots of `Phi_q(T)` remain primitive qth roots after raising to p, so monic integer division of `Phi_q(T^p)` by `Phi_q(T)` has zero remainder. Its quotient is the displayed polynomial and has constant coefficient 1. This is a finite polynomial argument, not irreducibility or a theorem on the distribution of primes.

All factors in the displayed rational expression are positive. Since `(t^p-1)(t^q-1)<t^(p+q)`, while `t^N-1 >= (t-1)t^(N-1)`, we have

\[
V>(t-1)^2 t^{N-p-q-1}>1
\]

for distinct odd primes p,q (the least `N-p-q-1` is 6). Because `t` is divisible by every prime dividing `Nu`, the constant coefficient gives `gcd(V,Nu)=1`.

Choose the least prime divisor `ell` of this explicit positive integer. Then

\[
\ell\le V<t^N=(pq|u|)^{pq}.
\tag{D1}
\]

The strict last bound follows from `(t^p-1)(t^q-1)>(t-1)^2` and `(t^N-1)/(t-1) <= t^N-1` for `t>=2`. The factorization V=product_(a,N)=1 (t-zeta_N^a), with 1<=a<N, also gives V<=(t+1)^((p-1)(q-1)) by the triangle inequality. Bound (D1) uses only the displayed rational-integer expression.

Modulo ell, `T^N-1` is squarefree because `ell` does not divide N. Its factors `Phi_d(T)` for d dividing N therefore have disjoint roots. Since `Phi_N(t)=0`, the order of `t mod ell` is exactly N, not a proper divisor. Consequently `ell=1 mod N`. This order argument can equally be checked from `t^N=1`, `t^p!=1`, `t^q!=1`; separability prevents a root of the Nth factor from entering either proper factor.

Specify a degree-one prime of `L=Q(zeta_N)` by `zeta_N -> t mod ell`. This also specifies the qth root `eta -> t^p` and the pth root `zeta_p -> t^q`. Under CASS-Q, all conjugate source factors are units at every prime over ell: their product is `q*u^p`, prime to ell. Thus this is a fully eligible prime for C27's split Jacobi construction and its finite residue symbol.

This construction is genuinely bounded. It does **not** prove that the target symbol at the chosen prime is nontrivial.

It also yields arbitrarily many distinct eligible primes without an unproved termination claim. If `ell_1,...,ell_j` have been chosen, use `t_j=N|u| product_i ell_i`. The new value is 1 modulo every earlier ell and modulo every prime dividing Nu, so any prime factor is new and eligible. The same explicit bound holds with `t_j`. Nothing in this recursion controls the new symbol.

### 4.2 PROVED: the exact linear detection obligation

Retain C26 RAD and write `E` for the source augmentation module, with `r=H-1` and `i:E -> L*/L*^p` injective. Let `e != 0` be C26's target exponent. For any explicit finite set `S` of split primes away from the conjugate source supports, the residue symbols define a linear map

\[
\operatorname{ev}_S:E\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{P\in S}\mathbb F_p,
\quad v\longmapsto\bigl(\operatorname{ind}_P(B_1^v)\bigr)_P.
\]

This is a finite map with computable entries; multiplication of symbols proves linearity. A detector supported on S exists if and only if `ev_S(e) != 0`: a single nonzero coordinate supplies one; if every coordinate is zero, every signed divisor supported on S has symbol 1. This elementary equivalence uses no prime-distribution theorem.

RAD proves `e != 0` in the global radical space. It does not prove `e notin ker(ev_S)`. The perfect Kummer pairing says that exactly `p^(r-1)*(p-1)` abstract elements of `T=Gal(M/L)` detect e, because a nonzero linear functional has a kernel of cardinality `p^(r-1)`. It does not count how many of the explicitly constructed primes have any of those substitutions as their Frobenius. A list of arbitrarily many primes can repeat elements of the same kernel; no injectivity of prime-to-Frobenius assignment is available. Thus a pigeonhole count on the number of candidate primes does not fill this gap.

Finite character orthogonality over all elements of one finite field averages the numerator value or a free parameter. Here `X=-y` is fixed, and one needs a statement about its reductions as ell varies. Averaging over a new free X is a different quantifier. No character-sum theorem is imported to exchange them.

The narrow missing detection statement is an explicit finite formula `B(p,q,X,u,...)` together with a proof that `ev_{S(B)}(e) != 0`, where S(B) consists of every chosen split component above eligible rational primes <=B. The bounded supply lemma §4.1 does not imply this assertion. No allowed proof of it is obtained here.

## 5. Circular descent: the proved theorem and a smaller open source target

### 5.1 PROVED: the circular-primary calculation

**Hypotheses CIRC.** Distinct odd primes p<q; F=Q(eta), eta a primitive qth root; H=(q−1)/2; choose g generating G+=(Z/q)* / {±1}. Put C_i=(1−eta^(g^i))(1−eta^(−g^i)). Let E=(E_i) be an integer vector with sum E_i=0, and epsilon=product C_i^E_i. Assume epsilon is locally a pth power at every prime above p. No source, DW, or p∤H assumption is needed in this section.

The following reproduces the finite proof actually read in astraSR. It is not an invocation of a general reflected-annihilation theorem.

Work in the cyclotomic ring localized at p, with phi=σ_p. For a unit u set

\[
\delta(u)=\frac{u^p/\phi(u)-1}{p}\pmod p.
\]

Frobenius modulo p makes the quotient defined, and multiplication modulo p² gives delta(uv)=delta(u)+delta(v). A local pth power has zero delta. Conversely, if delta(u)=0, iterate phi through f0=ord_q(p) to get u^(p^f0−1)=1 modulo p². Choose e with pe=1 modulo p^f0−1; then (u^e)^p=u modulo p². The quotient 1+p²z has a pth root: the finite polynomial

\[
\frac{(1+pT)^p-1}{p^2}-z,
\]

is T−z modulo p and has derivative 1 modulo p. Successively correcting one digit constructs compatible roots to every finite precision. This is the needed elementary local criterion; no local/global principle follows from it.

Put n(a,r)=[(a−r)p^(−1)]_q and

\[
f(a)=\sum_{r=1}^{p-1}r^{-1}n(a,r)\pmod p.
\]

Binomial coefficients give

\[
\delta(1-\eta)=-\frac{\sum_{r=1}^{p-1}\eta^r/r}{1-\eta^p}.
\]

The exact finite inverse identity

\[
\frac1{1-\eta^p}=-\frac1q\sum_{n=1}^{q-1}n\eta^{pn}
\]

follows by multiplying by 1−eta^p. Moreover delta(−eta^(−1))=0, so

\[
\delta(C_0)=\frac2q\sum_{a=0}^{q-1}f(a)\eta^a.
\]

Pairing r with p−r proves f(−a)=f(a). Write F_i=f(g^i) and Fvec=(F_i); the star denotes cyclic convolution. Since sum E_i=0, the a=0 contribution cancels under multiplication, yielding

\[
\delta(\epsilon)=\frac2q\sum_i(E*F_{\rm vec})_i(\eta^{g^i}+\eta^{-g^i}).
\]

The coefficient ring modulo p is F_p[T]/Phi_q(T). Indeed the power-basis discriminant is, up to sign, q^(q-2), by multiplying Phi_q'(eta)=q eta^(q-1)/(eta-1) over its conjugates; its integral-order index is therefore a p-unit. Also Phi_q is squarefree modulo p because p differs from q. This identifies the p-local integral ring used in the local test with the stated quotient. These H real sums are independent over F_p: a relation gives a polynomial of degree at most q−1, constant coefficient zero, divisible by Phi_q; it must be a scalar multiple of Phi_q, and its zero constant coefficient forces that scalar to be zero. Thus

**epsilon is primary if and only if `E*Fvec`=0 modulo p.**

For the reverse implication use the finite local criterion just proved. This equivalence describes the exponent presentation, not the whole real-unit group.

For the specified c=1+pk, c=p−1 modulo q, let Xi be the literal integer coefficient vector of Xi_c restricted to F+. C26's finite floor calculation, also checked directly in the script, is

\[
\Xi=D-\frac{2k}{q}N\pmod p,
\]

\[
D=\frac2q(1-\sigma_{p-1}^{-1})F_{\rm vec},\qquad N=\sum_{a\in G^+}\sigma_a.
\]

If `E*Fvec`=0 and sum E=0, then `E*Xi`=0 modulo p. Every entry of the integer vector `E*Xi` is therefore divisible by p, and

\[
\boxed{W_\epsilon=\prod_i C_i^{(E*\Xi)_i/p},\qquad W_\epsilon^p=\epsilon^{\Xi_c}.}
\]

is an explicit element of F with W_epsilon^p=epsilon^Xi. All exponents may be signed. This proves the circular reflected-root assertion, including nonsemisimple pairs.

#### Why the same local calculation supplies no source descent

At the actual source X=−y, p² divides X. Each affine plus factor is

\[
B_a(X)=1-X(\eta^a+\eta^{-a})+X^2\equiv1\pmod{p^2}.
\]

Consequently every product of B_a with any integer exponents has delta zero. Its primaryness imposes no exponent equation analogous to `E*Fvec`=0. This is true to arbitrary local precision: each factor 1+p²z has compatible pth roots by the digit polynomial above. The circular calculation at X=1 cannot be substituted at X=0 modulo p².

### 5.2 PROVED: source and circular modules meet only in the annihilated part

**Hypotheses RAD.** Retain the named C26 source inputs `SRC,CASS,DIV,HALF,LEDGER,DW,BAND`, with p<q<p². Their proved consequences used here are: p∤H; each A_a=B_a/B_1 is strong primary and real norm one; and the map

\]

is injective, where R=F_p[G+] and E is its augmentation ideal. C26 states the injection in `L*/L*^p`; the injection in F follows immediately. Conversely F-class nonpower persists in L because [L:F]=p−1 and the elementary norm identity descends pth roots. No extra global theorem is being asserted.

Let K_circ=ker(multiplication by the coefficient vector Fvec on E), and let C_prim=kappa(K_circ). By §5.1, this is exactly the subspace of primary classes in the norm-one circular presentation. ### 5.4 Exactly where an element construction of CD stops

The p-multiple divisor of alpha does not produce a field element whose divisor is one pth of it. A CD identity would provide precisely such a beta, since a norm-one circular product has zero finite divisor. Once a candidate beta with the required divisor is constructed, alpha/beta^p is a unit. One still must explicitly express its class by a norm-one circular product, including any needed real and norm normalizations; mere principality has not supplied this expression. These are two separate element tasks. Equality of valuations and local pth roots do not discharge either. The ordinary Jacobi operator on this real norm-one element is the tautology already proved in C27, so it does not construct the needed beta.

The finite Kummer pairing does not force module containment. It supplies the contragredient/cyclotomic-twist identity between V and its dual. One can take the explicit source-shaped module E, its twisted dual, and an independent module kappa(K_circ) killed by D; all those finite pairing and circular annihilation identities hold while the required containment E⊂kappa(K_circ) fails. This is an algebraic scope test, not a model of all arithmetic source hypotheses.

The stronger C27 generic-field obstruction applies directly to CD. Recall A=k(t), beta_a=t−eta^a, and the field M generated by pth roots of t−1, Phi_q(t), and all beta_a/beta_(-a); let M' be any finite tower of qth roots. Include in the number field k every algebraic constant and every circular or Gauss/Jacobi constant used in a proposed formula, together with their pth roots. [C27 §6][C27]'s finite radical-kernel proof says an A-element can become a pth power in M only if its class is generated by the defining radicands. Their branch-valuation vectors are: one vector at t=1; one constant vector at the nontrivial qth roots; and all odd vectors. A nonzero even augmentation vector is in none of their span, because p∤H. A q-power extension preserves this nonpower by the norm and Bezout identity. Thus no nonzero even augmentation product becomes a pth power modulo a constant in M'.

Every circular product is a constant, so full and projected CD fail in this formal allocation model. All ordinary half roots are already in M by the identity f_T²=Phi_q(t) product(beta_a/beta_(-a))^(sign a), and C27 explicitly realizes both algebraic Cassels allocations after qth-root adjunctions. A purely formal formula from those root equations and constant identities therefore cannot supply CD. This does not exclude integer specialization, source-dependent prime arithmetic, fixed-cyclotomic-field root membership, integrality, or a new reciprocity calculation. Those are precisely the substantive escape points.

## 6. Exact stopping points and the narrowest remaining obligations

### 6.1 The two residue certificates that would suffice

Retain the full named source inputs of §1. Let `D_aux` be a finite signed divisor supported on specified degree-one primes of `L` above rational primes `ell=1 mod pq`, `ell` not dividing `pqu`. The finite Jacobi construction of C27 §2.2 gives an explicitly computed element `J_(D_aux)` with

\[
\operatorname{div}(J_{D_{\rm aux}})=\Theta_cD_{\rm aux}.
\]

The covariance (R1), multiplied term by term, gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\left(\frac{\alpha^{\Xi_c}}{D_{\rm aux}}\right)_p
=\left(\frac{\alpha}{\Theta_cD_{\rm aux}}\right)_p
=\left(\frac{\alpha}{(J_{D_{\rm aux}})}\right)_p.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

The notation on the right means the product over the finite divisor of the displayed algebraic number, with signed valuations. Its factors are all defined: the support is at the selected auxiliary rational primes, where every conjugate source factor is a unit.

The following two statements are still **CONJECTURED / OPEN as elementary source consequences** here:

- **`DET` with a proved bound:** construct such a divisor, with an explicit bound on its rational-prime support, for which the left member of (6.1) is not one. Section 4 constructs primes with the required support conditions, but not the nontrivial value.
- **Restricted nonrational `REC`:** calculate the right member of (6.1) and prove it is one for that divisor and the actual source. Section 3 proves a rational-integer theorem with fully named hypotheses; §2 proves why the proposed norms and symmetrizations do not reduce this right member to a useful instance of that theorem.

These are separate requirements. `REC` for every eligible prime would still need a proved detecting-prime statement. A detecting prime alone would merely reproduce the nonzero side of C26's target. A direct construction of a reflected root, or a direct proof of the single `ENTRY` value, could avoid both.

### 7.2 PROVED correction of the certificate-level two-lift target

The new [sol2Lu §6][SOL2LU] changes the interpretation of the earlier order-four obstruction. Use its named **ONV31** test and half-column policy:

\[
d=p-1,\quad R\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\quad
s=\left\lceil\frac{Rd}{2q}\right\rceil,\quad
m=s+1+\lceil s/2\rceil,\quad J=m-1,
\]

\[
\theta=2R/q,\quad Q_J=q^{J+v_q(J!)},\quad
C_J=(\theta)_J/J!,\quad M_p=\frac{p}{2\sin(\pi/p)},\quad S_0\ge1.
\]

The sufficient nonvanishing inequality is

\[
[S_0Q_JC_JM_p^J]^d
 <p^{d(2q-2)+(J+1)(p-2)}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

**PROVED, for precisely this policy and inequality:** if `q>=11` and (7.2) passes, then `m<=2q`.

If `theta<1`, then `s<=ceil((p-1)/4)<=(p+1)/4`, and therefore

\[
m\le (3p+15)/8<q.
\]

If `theta>=1`, then `C_J>=1`. The elementary bounds `sin t<t` and `pi<4` give `M_p>p^2/8`, so `qM_p>p^2` for `q>=11`. Thus the left side of (7.2) is greater than `p^{2Jd}`. But `m>2q` would give `J>=2q` and

\[
2Jd-\{d(2q-2)+(J+1)(p-2)\}
=Jp-d(2q-2)-(p-2)\ge2q+p>0,
\]

contradicting (7.2). This proves the claim; it does not apply to a different height policy without recalculation.

**Updated interpretation.** The proved order-four ceilings `C_2<=2q^2` globally and `C_2<=2q` on an aligned phase remain correct. Their `m>2q` consequence does **not** force several components in the parameter range where the current certificate succeeds. C27's broad recommendation to pursue multiple components must be narrowed accordingly. A source-uniform one-component theorem for the height-admissible region has not been proved either. The remaining task is actual phase-fiber capacity, with the exact ONV/population tests, rather than an unnecessarily stronger selection theorem outside the usable range.

The new lead also proves selection when a component has full rank, or when suitable rank-`r-1` component rowspaces have zero intersection. Its low-rank binary target remains open. ### 7.3 PROVED finite arithmetic exclusion inherited from the new lead, independently checked here

Under the named source congruences `DW`, if `f=ord_p(q)=4` and `p<q<3p`, order lifting gives `p^2 | q^2+1`. Thus

\[
q^2+1=kp^2,\qquad k\equiv2\pmod8,\qquad 1<k<10,
\]

so `k=2`. Modulo `q^2` this says `2p^2=1`, whence

\[
p^{q-1}\equiv1\pmod{q^2}
\iff 2^{(q-1)/2}\equiv1\pmod{q^2}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

All positive integer solutions of `q^2-2p^2=-1` arise from `(1,1)` by

\[
(q,p)\longmapsto(3q+4p,2q+3p).
\]

For completeness, when `p>1` there is no solution with `p=2`, and `p>=3` allows the inverse step `(q',p')=(3q-4p,3p-2q)`. The inequalities `q<3p/2`, `q>p`, and `9q^2-16p^2=2p^2-9>0` give `q',p'>0` and `p'<p`; the equation is preserved. Descent reaches `(1,1)` and proves completeness of the increasing enumeration.

**TESTED exact finite certificate.** The script beside this answer enumerates all `130` solutions with `1<p<=10^100`, checks every inverse step, and checks (7.3) by both Python modular exponentiation and a separate left-to-right square-and-multiply routine. All `130` fail the required congruence. The first following `p` exceeds the bound. Thus the finite source exclusion reported in [sol2Lu §7.1][SOL2LU] is reproduced without primality assumptions or probabilistic primality tests. It excludes this source subcase through the stated bound; it is not a uniform assertion about Pell numbers or Wieferich primes.
