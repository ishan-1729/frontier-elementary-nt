# C30: Source-prime residues and the splitting obstruction
written: 2026-10-29T02:10:00Z
question: C30: QP_phi at the source's own primes
status: partial — explicit residue and finite splitting obstruction; no proof of QP_phi.

## 1. Hypotheses
Retain SRC, CASS, DW, RAD and the projected class A and circular unit ε of C29. Put X=−y, q odd, p<q odd, and F=Q(η), k=F⁺. At a rational prime ℓ|u, ℓ≠q, the source allocation Φ_q(X)=q u^p implies ord_ℓ(X)=q, hence ℓ≡1(mod q). The prime above ℓ specified by η↦X^t is denoted λ_t. Its real restriction identifies t and −t.

## 2. Explicit residue — PROVED
For b∈(Z/qZ)* define
 C_b=(1−η^b)(1−η^(−b)).
At λ_t, C_b reduces to (1−X^{tb})(1−X^{−tb})=2−X^{tb}−X^{−tb} in F_ℓ*. Every factor is nonzero, since X has order q. Let P_φ=Σ_j n_j σ_{g^j}, with σ_a(η)=η^a. Then
 ε=(C_g/C_1)^{P_φ}
and its reduction is the explicit rational-integer residue
 E_t=∏_j [(2−X^{t g^{j+1}}−X^{−t g^{j+1}})/(2−X^{t g^j}−X^{−t g^j})]^{n_j} (mod ℓ).
Negative exponents mean modular inverses. This expression is unchanged by replacing t with −t. If ℓ≡1(mod p), the pth-power residue test is exactly E_t^((ℓ−1)/p)≡1(mod ℓ). Otherwise gcd(p,ℓ−1)=1 and **every element of F_ℓ* is a pth power**; hence no nontrivial pth-power residue character exists at this degree-one prime. This is a direct finite cyclic-group calculation, not a Chebotarev assertion.

A useful independent simplification is C_b=−X^(−tb)(X^{tb}−1)^2, so E_t is also an explicit product of powers of X and cyclotomic differences. The Cassels allocations constrain X, u, x, y globally but do not, by their displayed congruences alone, force the above exponent to be nonzero. In particular the source valuation condition for A and the circular residue character are logically different finite tests.

## 3. Splitting and derivative — PROVED
The source equation yields ℓ≡1(mod q), **not** ℓ≡1(mod p). In the finite field F_ℓ, an order-p multiplicative character exists iff p|(ℓ−1). More strongly, a degree-p subfield of Q(ζ_ℓ) exists iff p|(ℓ−1), since its Galois group is (Z/ℓZ)* of order ℓ−1. Thus replacing the full ζ_ℓ extension by a degree-p subextension cannot work at a source prime with p∤(ℓ−1). The C29 derivative uses an m=p divisibility of ℓ−1, and its local residue-log computation is unavailable there. Extending the base residue field to F_(ℓ^f), f=ord_p(ℓ), introduces μ_p, but does not create an order-p subextension of Q(ζ_ℓ); nor does it make the source prime split completely in L.

There is no implication ℓ≡1(mod p) from ord_ℓ(X)=q alone: take p=5,q=7,ℓ=29 and X=16 mod29 (16 has order 7). This is a **local model only**, not a solution to Catalan or the complete Cassels allocations. The stronger claim that all actual source primes avoid 1 mod p is not proved.

## 4. Support divisor and the exact gap
C29's A is a signed fractional divisor supported on primes over u, and [A] has a nonzero φ-component. Thus at least one prime of its support has nonzero φ-image, with ℓ≤|u|. This conclusion neither asserts that its rational prime is 1 mod p nor that ε is a p-nonresidue there. A product of support primes can represent a prescribed linear combination of their classes, but an Euler-system derivative at a product is not obtained by replacing the single prime ℓ in Q(ζ_ℓ) by a formal divisor. One can apply separate derivatives at each qualifying prime and add their class relations; the required nonzero residue-log coefficient still has to be established at at least one prime, or a noncancelling linear combination. There is no elementary proof of that selection here.

For a source support prime with ℓ≡1(mod pq), define h_t=ind_{μ_p}(E_t^((ℓ−1)/p)). The narrowest finite arithmetic target is: **find a support prime whose φ-projected class is nonzero in A_p/pA_p and for which h_t≠0**, or prove an explicit linear combination of the derivative relations has nonzero φ-coefficient on [A]. The source support supplies the first property somewhere, not both properties at the same prime. The obstruction is simultaneous selection, not calculation of E_t.

## 5. What the equations do and do not force
The two Cassels allocations give p- and q-adic valuations and the order-q residue of X at ℓ|u. Double Wieferich constrains p and q modulo each other's squares, not ℓ modulo p. The size bounds constrain |X| and |u|, not the discrete logarithm of E_t. At ℓ|x or ℓ|y the source ratios often specialize to 1 or to circular ratios, but this does not force a nontrivial circular residue at a prime of the divided-divisor support. C27's source-prime local transfer concerns a *different* source-radical character; it is not a nonzero statement for h_t. No logical inference between these characters is supplied by the existing finite identities.

## 6. Closure map
The rank-one plus route still needs QP_φ or PA_φ; C30 makes the former a finite simultaneous support-prime test and proves the exact residue formula, but not existence. The alternative REC+bounded detector and the height-admissible two-lift/jet route remain open. The filed HHalf and denominator/ONV formalization gaps remain separate. No new unconditional Catalan exclusion is claimed.

## 7. Computation
The companion standard-library script checks the local order-q example, the circular identity, the residue test, and the impossibility of a degree-p subfield when p∤ℓ−1. Its output is stored beside it. These are **TESTED** finite local controls, not actual Catalan sources. No theorem is imported beyond the finite cyclicity of F_ℓ*, which follows by the elementary root-count argument: a finite subgroup of a field's multiplicative group is cyclic, since for each prime-power divisor of its exponent one can choose an element of that order, and their product has full exponent.
