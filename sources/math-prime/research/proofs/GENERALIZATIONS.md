# Generalizing the ghost methods

This is a design note, written 2026-10-15. Each item is tagged **[done]** (in a completed proof), **[lit]** (established
in the literature), **[proposed]** (a design not yet proved) or **[open]**. Method IDs refer to
[methods/](methods/README.md); the targets are in [targets/](targets/README.md).

## 1. The common skeleton
Each ghost proof fixes eight pieces:
- **Ghosts G**, carrying a **Ghost Property P** that the moves preserve;
- **moves M**;
- a **height h** that a parent strictly lowers outside a finite **seed set S**;
- an **index group Γ** that turns the descent trees into orbits;
- a **marker μ**, a fixed-value condition that picks out the original solutions;
- an **exclusion engine E** that rules out the marker at all but finitely many indices;
- a **reconstruction ρ** back to the original solutions.

| Piece | Ljunggren (ghost) | x²+7=y³ | x²+k=y³, k<0 | (t+1)x⁴−ty²=1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G, P | nodes r⁴+q²=2s⁴, primitive | reduced rational points of Y²=X³−7 | integer triples with N(z)=m | primitive (t+1)B⁴−tQ²=C⁴ |
| M | Pythagorean/Vierzahlensatz parent; chord maps A, D | isogenies Φ, Ψ; chord S | multiplication by a unit ε | quartic parents |
| h | s | A (numerator) | u²+v²+w² | per member |
| Γ | Z (one signed index) | Z | Z (orbits Tⁿβ) | Z |
| μ | reduced numerator −1 | denominator 1 | w=0 | C=1 (a_l²=C²) |
| E | 3-adic exact lifting | 5-adic exact lifting | 2-adic three-coefficient count | 2-adic drift law |

## 2. Axes of generalization

### 2.1 What a ghost is
- **[done]** rational points; integer points of a norm form; reduced Somos windows.
- **[proposed]** points on coverings (2- and 4-coverings), and points of a whole family, with the parameter t as a coordinate.
- **[proposed]** nodes of trees generated by involutions: Vieta jumping on Markov- or Hurwitz-type
  surfaces, and Descartes/Apollonian quadruples (Team 1 met these).
- **[proposed]** exponent-indexed ghosts: powers of a fixed pair, where the exponent itself is the index
  (Ramanujan–Nagell type).

### 2.2 Ghost Properties along other axes
A Ghost Property is any arithmetic condition that the moves preserve. Besides parametrization, our proofs
already use properties along six axes, and each axis can be pushed further.

| Axis | Properties already used | How a property is used | Generalization |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Algebraic** | the defining equation, the norm, the Somos invariant I(a,b,c,d)=0 (Ljunggren) | integrality of the next term (Bézout on two formulas) | invariants of other recurrences, such as elliptic nets for two indices **[proposed]** |
| **Local, periodic** | residues mod 8, 3, 25, 256 along the index | restrict the marker to finitely many residue classes | any modulus; combine several primes as a sieve **[lit]** |
| **Local, exact** | drift laws: v₃ (Ljunggren), v₅(s_{c+5q}−s_c)=2+v₅(q) (k=7), v₂(a_n−a_k)=v₂(n−k)+3 (t=7) | exclude a whole infinite class from one finite residue | multi-index drift laws **[proposed]** |
| **Divisibility** | strong divisibility of denominators; adjacent and distance-two coprimality of Somos terms | primitive-numerator lemma: the marker becomes a fixed value | gcd laws on two-index lattices (elliptic nets) **[proposed]** |
| **Order** | signs, sectors, the sign of q (q≡1 mod 4) | exhaustive signed descent; sector reduction | inequality markers; positivity regions **[proposed]** |
| **Transport** | parity of A_n ⇔ 3∣n, through the image of T (k=7) | carry information between ghost families linked by isogenies or coverings | transport markers across coverings, so that a square marker becomes a fixed value **[proposed]** |
| **Index arithmetic** | composite index ⇒ marker at a smaller index (Team 3, handover G1) | a second descent, on the index itself, down to prime indices | combine with drift laws at prime indices **[open]** for t=6 |
| **Parameter** | identities polynomial in m (the 5-block exclusion for every m) | uniform statements over a family | a "generic ghost" over Z[t]; lifting uniform in t **[open]** |

### 2.3 Moves and index structure
- **[done]** one move, Γ=Z.
- **[proposed]** commuting moves, Γ=Z^r (higher rank; see §3).
- **[proposed]** involutions generating free products, i.e. trees. Here the marker question becomes a question about words in the
  generators. The Markov uniqueness conjecture has exactly this shape **[open; famous]**.
- **[done]** isogeny moves between two ghost families (Φ, Ψ for k=7).

### 2.4 Markers
- **[done]** a single fixed value.
- **[proposed]** several simultaneous fixed values. A degree-n Thue equation gives n−2 vanishing coordinates.
- **[proposed]** S-integrality, meaning the denominator is supported on a fixed set S of primes.
- **[lit]** nonlinear markers ("is a square") cannot be excluded at fixed precision: a square modulo p lifts to
  every power of p. Pass to a covering on which the square becomes a coordinate. This is how
  Ljunggren's r⁴ became a node coordinate, with marker r=1.

### 2.5 Exclusion engines
- **[done]** one-index exact lifting on Somos sequences ([lifting.somos](methods/lifting.somos.md)).
- **[done]** the one-index finite-binomial zero count ([lifting.binomial](methods/lifting.binomial.md)).
- **[done, conditional]** the two-index finite-polynomial count [lifting.binomial.multi](methods/lifting.binomial.multi.md).
  It bounds the common zeros by Σ mult ≤ d₁d₂ when the two stable reductions are coprime, or after a saturation step,
  and by 2 per orbit under a frame hypothesis (§3.2).
- **[done]** the kernel-lattice sieve of [ghost.unit.multi_unit](methods/ghost.unit.multi_unit.md).
  - Classes of exponents are taken modulo Λ_q = ker(units → (O/q)*) for several prime powers q.
  - Each known zero is isolated by the Jacobian criterion (Proposition 17 of lifting.binomial.multi).
  - The other classes are removed by congruences.
  - It solved all twelve Thue equations of 1611.a1 ([triangular_tetrahedral](theorems/triangular_tetrahedral/README.md)).
- **[lit]** sieves over several primes (Mordell–Weil sieve): they cut the candidate classes but never prove finiteness alone.
- **[lit]** archimedean gap principles ([approx.*](methods/approx.normsign.md)), which Ishan counts as classical.

### 2.6 Effectivity
**[lit]** A sharp count, equal to the number of known solutions, certifies a complete list. This is how Skolem's method is
used in practice. Our counts (≤ν₂(mΔ₀)+2 per orbit) become complete classifications when they are sharp.

## 3. Rank 2 and beyond

### 3.1 Two different ranks
- A rational ghost proof has as many indices as the **Mordell–Weil rank r_E** of the curve, and one marker
  (denominator 1).
- A unit ghost proof has as many indices as the **unit rank r_U** of its number system. For a degree-n Thue
  equation it has **n−2 markers** (vanishing coordinates).

An exclusion engine of the lifting type can isolate solutions only when **markers ≥ indices**. This is Skolem's condition.
- For rational ghosts it means r_E≤1. Every completed case has r_E=1; both stuck cases have r_E=2.
- For unit ghosts it means r_U≤n−2, which holds exactly when the Thue field has a complex place
  (r₁+2r₂=n and r_U=r₁+r₂−1, so the condition is r₂≥1). **This does not involve r_E.**
- **It does involve a sign [done, 2026-10-15].** A quartic Thue form F from a 2-descent is a 2-covering of a curve
  E_F, and disc F has the sign of Δ(E_F). So the Thue fields are totally real or definite when Δ(E_F)>0, and of
  signature (2,1) when Δ(E_F)<0.
  - All four rank-2 targets have Δ(E_F)>0 ([rank2_signatures_2026-10](targets/rank2_signatures_2026-10.md)).
  - 1611.a1, 8604.a1 and y²=x³+17 have Δ<0.
  - The rule covers the 2-coverings of E_F and of its twists. The isogeny classes of t=6 and t=12 also contain
    Δ<0 curves, the Jacobians of the problem curves, but no reduction of the integral points through them is
    known.

Literature: Skolem's method needs a pair of complex roots (Borevich–Shafarevich; Tzanakis–de Weger 1989, p. 99).
- **Thue equations.** For the module Z+Zα with K=Q(α), finiteness holds whenever K is not totally real, with no
  Galois hypothesis (Skolem 1935, per the review of Chabauty 1938). Chabauty's extra condition, prime degree or
  symmetric Galois group, is for general modules.
- **Where to count markers.** Count them inside the field spanned by the units. An overfield such as L(i) adds
  no units and no real conditions.
- **Several indices.** Lombardo (2022) treats a family of quintic Thue equations whose fields have unit rank 2, and
  Lido–Mauri (2026) prove a *multivariate Strassmann theorem*, the analytic tool for several indices.

### 3.2 Strategy R1: trade rational ghosts for unit ghosts with enough markers [tested 2026-10-15]
1. **Reduce to Thue equations.** A 2-descent turns the integral points of the rank-2 curve into finitely many Thue equations: cubic for Mordell
   curves, quartic in general. Ghost properties: the norm, and the unit orbits βε₁^aε₂^b.
   **Done** for the four targets and three comparison curves
   ([rank2_signatures_2026-10](targets/rank2_signatures_2026-10.md)).
2. **Check the signature.** For each Thue field with a complex place, quartic with signature (2,1) for example, there are two markers in two
   indices.
   **Result:** every Thue field of the four targets is totally real, or its form is definite. That is 3 units
   against 2 markers, so R1 as stated fails on all four. The reason is the sign rule of §3.1.
3. **Prove the two-index lemma** (`lifting.binomial.multi`). The plan follows the one-index proof:
   1. Truncate: G_N(X,Y) = Σ_{i,j<N} p^{i+j} b_{ij} (N!²/(i! j!)) X^{(i)} Y^{(j)}, with falling factorials, for each of the two markers.
   2. Bound the p-content by finite differences at a few small points, as in the three-coefficient lemma.
   3. Show that high-degree coefficients carry extra factors of p, so the reductions R̄₁, R̄₂ mod p have bounded degree.
   4. Apply a unimodular change of variables that makes the projection injective on the finite zero set S. The resultant
      Res_Y(R₁,R₂)(X) then vanishes at |S| distinct integers, so the monic product over S divides it in Z[X].
      Reducing mod p gives |S| ≤ deg R̄₁ · deg R̄₂, provided R̄₁ and R̄₂ have no common factor.

   The crux is the hypothesis that R̄₁, R̄₂ have no common factor mod p. It replaces "one of b₀, b₁, b₂ has small valuation", and must be
   forced by a determinant identity, as Δ₀≠0 was for k<0.

   **Result [done, conditional]:** see [lifting.binomial.multi](methods/lifting.binomial.multi.md) and its
   [proof](methods/lifting.binomial.multi/proof.md).
   - The lemma is proved under the coprimality hypothesis. The resultant step was replaced by a colength argument:
     the evaluation image on the zero set is free of rank |S|.
   - No determinant identity forces the hypothesis. The analogue det[w,K₁w,K₂w,K₁K₂w] = const holds in an example
     with infinitely many common zeros. In Q(2^{1/4}) at p=7, natural seeds have reductions with a common factor.
   - What works is saturation: the finite-precision form of Lido–Mauri's saturated ideal. A frame criterion also
     works: if det[v,K₁v,K₂v,(c₁K₁+c₂K₂)²v] has bounded valuation in every direction c, one explicit saturation
     step bounds the orbit by 2 zeros. Both are finite checks on the seed.
   - The frame criterion is in practice for S4 fields. In a D4 field the unit of the real quadratic subfield
     defeats it.
4. **First test.** Compute the Thue fields and their signatures for each rank-2 target (quartic t=6 and t=12, the odd branch of
   Fibonacci =y²−y−1, the Avanesov curves) and pick one with a complex place.
   **Result:** none of the four has one.
   - The reductions themselves are classical: Tzanakis–de Weger 1989 (916.a1, a problem they attribute to S. P. Mohanty;
     it is not Avanesov's), de Weger 1995 (the Fibonacci odd branch) and Bennett–Togbé–Walsh 2006 (t=6, 12).
   - The curves with a complex place are 1611.a1 and 8604.a1 (Avanesov's two problems) and y²=x³+17.
   - Avanesov already used Skolem's method on a (2,1) quartic for 1611.a1. His quartic is a sub-case of one of
     the 2-descent classes of 1611.a1, so R1 there would be a completion-free form of his argument, not a new mechanism.
5. **R1 carried out on 1611.a1 [done, 2026-10-15]:** [triangular_tetrahedral](theorems/triangular_tetrahedral/README.md).
   - *Coverage* is elementary and needs no unit theorem: an explicit cone certificate for the least-E element of a
     unit orbit, then a small exact enumeration. It gives 16 orbit seeds and proves the two units fundamental.
   - *Exclusion* used Proposition 17 with a congruence sieve over kernel lattices, at q ≤ 41. Theorem 18 and
     saturation were not needed, and no spurious p-adic zero blocked the sieve.
   - *Result.* All twelve Thue equations are solved completely and elementarily.
   - *Remaining input, now proved.* The step from integral points to Thue equations rested on the 2-descent class
     list, that is rank E(Q) ≤ 2 (LMFDB), and on standard facts about O_K. Both are now proved elementarily in
     [hypothesis_H.md](theorems/triangular_tetrahedral/hypothesis_H.md) (2026-10-15).
     - Z[λ], λ³ = 4λ − 6, is not norm-Euclidean (M ≥ 121/109).
     - A division with multipliers {1, 2}, certified by 398 exact boxes, still gives Bézout and square allocation.
     - With a one-unit descent and a congruence modulo 4, this gives (H).

     So the theorem on C(n,2)=C(m,3) is WRITTEN.
   - *Novelty.* None. The mechanism is Avanesov's (read in full). Its congruence-only form has a precedent on
     8604.a1 (Uchiyama 1984).
6. **R1 carried out on 8604.a1 and y²=x³+17 [done, 2026-10-15]:**
   [triangular_square_pyramidal](theorems/triangular_square_pyramidal/README.md) and
   [mordell_k_minus17](theorems/mordell_k_minus17/README.md), with the same scripts and new data.
   - *A new wrinkle: field forms.* For three of the six class forms, the marker lemma fails as stated: (1+α)/2 is
     integral (8604.a1, class (1,0)), α/2 is integral (y²=x³+17, class (0,1)), or the form is not monic
     (y²=x³+17, class (1,1)). The Thue equations are solved for the monic norm form G of a generator β with
     O ∩ (Q+Qβ) = Z+Zβ, and a short substitution lemma returns to the class form. The extra solutions of G that give
     no class-form solution are certified like the others.
   - *Coverage* gives 12 orbit seeds for 8604.a1 and 17 for y²=x³+17 (six orbits each at c = 9 and c = 36 in one
     field), and proves both unit pairs fundamental in all six fields.
   - *Exclusion* again used only Proposition 17 with the kernel-lattice sieve, at q ≤ 49. One instance on y²=x³+17
     has λ = 1.
   - *A 2-adic obstruction.* On 8604.a1, orbit (0,1), c = 36, the markers have a common 2-adic zero that is not an
     integer zero (Hensel certificate), so no sieve by powers of 2 can finish that orbit; the odd moduli 43 and 13
     pass it. For the same reason Uchiyama's Prop. 8 cannot be completed modulo powers of 2.
   - *Result.* All twenty Thue equations are solved completely and elementarily. The integral points rest on the class
     list (H), implied by rank E(Q) ≤ 2; for y²=x³+17 it is also Cassels's theorem (1950, §22), which is not
     elementary.
   - *(H), proved the same day* ([8604.a1](theorems/triangular_square_pyramidal/hypothesis_H.md),
     [y²=x³+17](theorems/mordell_k_minus17/hypothesis_H.md)), by the method of 1611.a1:
     - Z[λ], λ³ = λ − 3 (discriminant −239), is norm-Euclidean: 116 exact boxes, and the point fixed by the unit
       needs 8/9. A congruence modulo 4 then removes the unit's coset, as for 1611.a1.
     - The ring of integers of Q(∛17) is not norm-Euclidean (a point fixed by −η needs 1115/1028); multipliers
       {1, 2} work, with 795 boxes. There the norm condition alone leaves the four classes.
     - Units by the one-unit descent in both.

     So both theorems are WRITTEN (in-session review; the proofs of (H) are round 3 of the cards' review records,
     2026-10-26).

**A way around Δ>0 [lit; open lead].** Ljunggren (1942) met the totally real obstruction for t=1. According to its
JFM review, he passed to an octic overfield of signature (4,2), whose units of relative norm 1 carry a two-index
Skolem argument. Chabauty (1939) and Stroeker–Tzanakis (1988) treat totally real fields through such overfields.
- **t=6 and t=12.** Analogous octics of signature (4,2) exist and contain the Thue field. Two obstacles are found:
  the unit √(t+1)+√t lies outside Q(√(t(t+1))), and the analogue of Ljunggren's λ₁ is not a unit.
- **The Fibonacci odd branch.** Its field Q(ζ₂₀)⁺ is Ljunggren's own field for D=5, but the relative-norm identity
  is inhomogeneous.
- **916.a1.** No route: S4, no quadratic subfield, isogeny class of size 1.
- **What is unchecked.** In each case, whether the solutions fall into 2 fixed-value markers in 2 indices. Data:
  [rank2_signatures_2026-10](targets/rank2_signatures_2026-10.md), "Consequences for R1".

### 3.3 Strategy R2: rational ghosts with two indices [open]
Coverage works at any rank: the Mordell–Weil descent by halving or isogeny is still a strict descent. The
denominators along mP+nQ form an **elliptic net** (Stange 2011), the two-index analogue of the Somos sequences.
But integrality is one marker in two indices, so a single prime's lifting leaves a whole curve of p-adic
candidates. R2 therefore needs a second fixed-value marker, for example from a covering, or a combination with R1.

### 3.4 Strategy R3: cut the index rank by symmetry [proposed]
Suppose an involution of the problem forces every marked ghost into a coset of a rank-1 subgroup of E(Q). Then the problem
is rank 1 again. Test: write the known integral points of 1525.a2, 916.a1 and 14112.c1 in terms of
Mordell–Weil generators, and see whether they lie on one line.
**Tested for 916.a1 (2026-10-15):** its integral points lie on no single line in Mordell–Weil coordinates, for any
choice of signs ([r3_916_collinearity.py](targets/rank2_signatures_2026-10/r3_916_collinearity.py)). R3 fails there
in this simple form. 1525.a2 and 14112.c1 are not tested.

### 3.5 Strategy R4: descent on the index [partial, Team 3]
A marker at a composite index forces a marker-type condition at a divisor. This reduces the problem to prime indices,
which then need their own exclusion (open endpoint G1 of the t=6 handover).

### 3.6 Known dead ends (do not retry unchanged)
- R1 through the classical 2-descent when the descent quartics have Δ>0. The Thue fields are totally real, with 3
  units against 2 markers. This covers all four rank-2 targets.
- Forcing coprimality of the two reductions in `lifting.binomial.multi` by a determinant identity (Example 14 of its
  [proof](methods/lifting.binomial.multi/proof.md)).
- A finite congruence sieve alone: large indices imitate small ones. For r-th power markers in the unit Lucas
  sequences this is a theorem: every fixed finite set of moduli is passed by a whole index progression on which at
  least one term in every three is not an r-th power ([lucas_power_triples](theorems/lucas_power_triples/README.md),
  D2).
- A square marker at fixed precision.
- One prime's lifting with one marker in two indices.
- The single-orbit transfer for t=6 (counterexample node (1189,1475,2277967)).
- Lifting at the prime 3 for x²+x+1=y³ (stride 3). The class k ≡ 5 (mod 9) has s_k ≡ 1 (mod 3⁵) at every
  k < 3600 checked, which looks like a 3-adic zero away from the known indices. The prime 2 works
  ([x2_x_1_y3](theorems/x2_x_1_y3/README.md)).
- Ghost Descent run backwards on an existence problem: Pollock's seven octahedral numbers, October 2026. Every N
  has a rational "source" (a seven-slot rational point with the same target), and the Ghost moves were used to
  descend from it towards an integral nonnegative endpoint. Closed descents exist only on special source classes.
  Entry into them from an arbitrary N became a rational-point existence question on a genus-one curve with a
  prescribed denominator. That question is harder than the original, and no Ghost tool produces points. See §3.7.
- A fixed Pell translation as a move for a power marker. On X² − D·Z^{2m} = ε (m ≥ 2, D rational and nonzero,
  ε = ±1), fix rational u, v with u² − Dv² = 1 and v ≠ 0. No rational map on the curve lifts
  (X,Y) ↦ (uX + DvY, vX + uY) through Y = Z^m. The reason: with x, z the coordinate functions, vx + uz^m is not an
  m-th power in the function field Q(z)(x).
  - *Proof.* The norm from Q(z)(x) to Q(z) is multiplicative and sends vx + uz^m to z^{2m} − εv². That polynomial is
    squarefree and nonconstant, so it is not an m-th power of a rational function.
  - *Consequence.* A move must be a map of the curve of genus ≥ 1 itself, as the chord maps of
    [ghost.rational](methods/ghost.rational.md) are. On the conic, such a map acts as a translation that depends on
    the point. The doubling map on X² − DZ⁴ = 1 is an example. For squarefree D, its rational inputs with integral
    image occur only for D = 3 and D = 1785. D = 1785 is the classical exceptional case of X² − DY⁴ = 1
    (Ljunggren 1942; Togbé–Voutier–Walsh, Acta Arith. 120 (2005) 39–58).
  - *Sources.*
    - Branch `cloud-agent/elementary-connections`, `cloud_agent/conics/proof_draft.md` §2 (C2; one external review,
      PASS) and `rational_doubling_classification.md` (C5–C7).
    - The one- and two-step case for a product-square marker is already in
      [team1/imo_foundation_scion_second_ghost.md](../shared/prior/ghost_parametrization/team_work/team1/imo_foundation_scion_second_ghost.md).

### 3.7 Ghost Descent on an existence problem (Pollock, 2026-10) [tested; dead as a transfer]
Ishan asked on 24 Oct 2026 that the Ghost Parametrization and Ghost Descent of the Ljunggren and Mordell proofs be
transferred to the open return quartic of the Pollock lane. The Codex captain's partner did this in about 55
packages, checked by the captain only, from 24 to 25 Oct. Report:
`research/waring/experiments/CONS26_ghost_descent_transfer/`; packages:
`research/waring/experiments/CONS26_src_codex_pollock/pollock_astra/`.

The eight pieces of §1, as they were instantiated:

| Piece | Pollock version | What was proved |
|---|---|---|
| G, P | rational seven-slot points X with ΣO(Xᵢ) = N for the FIXED target N; three pairs about centres such as 67T, 71T, 79T, and a seventh slot | every N ≥ 0 has such a source under the three-square theorem (`source_coverage`) |
| M | chords and tangents of the cubic fibre, Vieta and isotropic exchanges, cubic norm-square ghosts (Cremona–Fisher covariants) | all moves preserve N; all-prime gcd laws for every move |
| h | complete primitive height H = max(B, \|nᵢ\|), X = n/B | a nonnegative integral endpoint of a source with B ≥ 2, H ≥ 4 automatically has smaller H |
| μ | B = 1 and all seven coordinates ≥ 0 | — |
| descent | a parent map that lowers B, H and is defined on every source | only on closed classes: `three_adic_parent` (B = 3ᵏ → 3ᵏ⁻¹, H' ≤ 2H/3, positive throughout), the dyadic chain D → 16 → 8 → 4 (the last step eventual, by Duke–Schulze-Pillot) |
| entry | reach a closed class from the source of an arbitrary N | open even for one compulsory source (N = 1,099,106,222): a point with W = 3ᵏ on the intersection of two quadrics in P³ (genus one), soluble everywhere locally, k < 10^5,000,000 by an effective S-integral theorem, no point found |

Why it does not transfer:
1. **The direction is reversed.**
   - The Ghost proofs prove *coverage of solutions*: every original solution lies on a descent tree from finitely many
     seeds. Then the marker is *excluded* at all but the known indices.
   - Pollock needs *attainment*: every source must reach a marked point.
   - An exclusion engine (lifting, drift laws) works against that goal. Descent was available; a total parent map whose
     fixed points are integral was not.
2. **The moves preserve invariants that the marker needs to change.**
   - Vieta and sum-preserving words keep the total index sum, which is nonintegral for the canonical prime sources.
   - Fixed-centre secant and tangent words obey a binary displacement lock.
   - The centre form 67x²+71y²+79z² is anisotropic at 2: v₂(Q(Z)) = −2f+δ with δ ∈ {0,1}.
3. **No expanding unit.**
   - The return norms are positive definite, so their integral automorphism groups are finite.
   - The `ghost.unit` mechanism needs a unit of infinite order, so it has nothing to iterate. Rational rotations pay
     the full denominator cost.
4. **Rational families grow.** On a fixed section the complete height of a rational family grows like
   (P²+Q²)² in its parameter (also order 6 and order 24 in other sections). A large parameter is not a descent.
5. **Genus counts decide nothing.** Genus 2 and genus 4 return curves exclude rational one-parameter sections only.
   They neither exclude nor supply a discrete descent.

**What a working version would need.** A move set M that is defined on every source and changes the invariant
classes in (2), together with a well-founded height whose minima are integral and nonnegative. Equivalently: an
existence mechanism for marked points, which the Ghost toolkit (descent plus exclusion) does not contain. Local
successes (targets 14, 29, 63, 254, 4578; the exponent orbit 5650·2^(3h)+140·2^(2h)+24·2^h, h ≥ 6) show that
returns exist case by case. No uniform selector was found.

Status: exploration, with the captain's checks only. Rev3 (2026-10-26) spot-checked `three_adic_parent` and found
no error. The referee observed that this descent adds no coverage: its endpoints stay in a fixed family that misses
3,214 of the N ≤ 20,000 ([branch_review_2026-10-26.md](../shared/consolidation_2026-10-26/branch_review_2026-10-26.md), §3.3).
