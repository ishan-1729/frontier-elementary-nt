# involution.vieta: Vieta jumps as reflections in conjugate diameters
Parent: involution     Children: none

## What it does
For F = x² − kxy + y² + dx + ey + f with k ≥ 3, the two Vieta jumps s₁(x,y) = (ky − d − x, y) and
s₂(x,y) = (x, kx − e − y) are the affine reflections of the conic in the diameters conjugate to the two axes. s₁
fixes the line F_x = 0, which bisects every horizontal chord, and s₂ fixes F_y = 0. Together they generate an
infinite dihedral group acting on the integer points. Put N = d² + kde + e² + (k²−4)f.
1. If N > 0, every orbit meets the closed arc between the points of horizontal and vertical tangency exactly once.
   Its lattice points are the minimal solutions, those at which neither jump lowers a coordinate, and explicit
   bounds confine them to a box.
2. If N < 0, the jumps exchange the two branches. The lines through the centre parallel to the axes cut out a set
   that each orbit meets once or twice.
3. Existence of positive solutions becomes a finite search on the arc; a tangent line separates the branches, so
   positivity is decided by the branch.
4. A quantity can be carried along the orbits. For (a+r)/b + (b+r)/a = k, the gcd invariant
   Q = r(a+b)/gcd(a,b)² is the value of kxy − x² − y² at the primitive direction, and each jump multiplies it by
   (k+2)/e², with e = gcd(k+2, a′+b′).

## Requirements: when it applies
- A conic x² − kxy + y² + linear and constant terms with integer coefficients and |k| ≥ 3; k ≤ −3 reduces to k ≥ 3
  by (x,y) ↦ (x,−y). The parabolic case k = 2 is treated separately in T1.

## Limits: where it stops, with examples
- It turns each equation into a finite search, not into a closed form. For x² − kxy + y² + lx + my = 0 the set of k
  has the explicit part D1 ∪ D2, but the rest T has no closed form ([vieta_conics](../theorems/vieta_conics/README.md);
  7 ∈ S(10,3) comes from the arc point (2,3)).
- Three variables: for (a+r)/b + (b+r)/c + (c+r)/a = k, Vieta jumping need not preserve integrality
  (Miller–Nikolakopoulos–Srinivasan, §4.2), and no jump rule is known.
- Mixed signs lm < 0: only the finite arc test applies.

- The reduction of all integer points of such a conic, with any linear terms, to finitely many fundamental points is
  Mills's (Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 5 (1954); Canad. J. Math. 8 (1956)). For d = e = 0, Lemmermeyer (arXiv 2601.15229,
  2026) uses the tangency box.
- That the chord involution in a fixed direction is the affine reflection in the conjugate diameter is classical
  geometry (Coxeter, Introduction to Geometry, 1969, p. 203).
- Not found elsewhere: the exact fundamental-arc formulation, and the jump rule of the gcd invariant. This card
  therefore records a classical reduction in a new form, plus one new invariant; it is not a new reduction.
