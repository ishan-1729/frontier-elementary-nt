# Literature check: triangular = tetrahedral, and R1 on 1611.a1

Written 2026-10-15. Section 1 is from a full reading of Avanesov's paper. Section 2 is the literature sweep, and
section 3 gives the verdict.

## 1. Avanesov (1966/67), read in full
**Source.** È. T. Avanesov, *Решение одной проблемы фигурных чисел* (Solution of a problem on figurate numbers),
Acta Arith. 12 (1966/67) 409–420, DOI 10.4064/aa-12-4-409-420. It is open access under CC-BY at IMPAN
(https://www.impan.pl/pl/wydawnictwa/czasopisma-i-serie-wydawnicze/acta-arithmetica/all/12/4/96317/reshenie-odnoj-problemy-figurnyh-chisel).
The PDF is a scan with no text layer. Ishan saved it, and the session read its rendered pages on 2026-10-15. Nothing
is quoted beyond the equations.

**What the paper does.**
1. *Problem.* It answers Sierpiński's problem P²₉₈ (*A selection of problems in the theory of numbers*, 1964, p. 121):
   the tetrahedral numbers that are also triangular are exactly 1, 10, 120, 1540, 7140. Equivalently,
   3y(y+1) = x(x+1)(x+2) in positive integers.
2. *Method, as the introduction states it.*
   - Mordell's work on factorization in an arbitrary cubic field;
   - reduction to representation by binary biquadratic forms;
   - Billevich's method for fundamental units;
   - Skolem's local method, which embeds the field in its completions.
3. *Model.* X = 2x+2, Y = 2y+1 gives 6Y² = X³ − 4X + 6, equation (16).
   - The cubic field is K₂ = Q(λ), λ³ − 4λ + 6 = 0, with power integral basis, class number h = 1 (Delone–Faddeev's
     table) and discriminant −2²·179.
   - Its fundamental unit is ε₀ = 40λ² − 101λ + 95 (by Billevich's method); he uses ε = 1/ε₀ = λ² − 5λ − 19.
4. *Factorization.* 6Y² = (X − λ)(X² + λX + λ² − 4).
   - Mordell (1923) is cited only for the finiteness of the common ideal factors of (X − λ) and (X² + λX + λ² − 4).
     That these factors divide 2, 3 or 3λ² − 4 is his own deduction, and the passage from ideals to elements uses
     h = 1.
   - With h = 1 and 2 = −(λ²+λ−4)³ε⁻¹, 3 = (λ−1)(2λ+5)(λ²−3λ+3), 3λ² − 4 = (λ²+λ−4)²(45λ²−114λ+107), he gets
     X − λ = ±ε^{q₁}(λ²+λ−4)^{q₂}(λ²−3λ+3)^{q₃}(λ−1)^{q₄}(2λ+5)^{q₅}(45λ²−114λ+107)^{q₆}(a+bλ+cλ²)².
   - Here q₂ = 1, and q₆ = 0 by an argument mod 8. That leaves eight cases (q₁, q₃, q₄, q₅).
5. *Cases.* Equating coefficients gives ternary quadratic systems. Read against pp. 414–419:
   - Cases 3 and 6 fail mod 2, and case 5 fails mod 4.
   - Case 4 fails because its system contains 3f₂ = ±1, which has no integer solution.
   - Case 8 is only asserted to fail "as in case 4"; nothing is printed.
   - Case 1 gives a² = 12bc with (b,c) = 1. Coprime factorization (b = n², c = 3m² or b = 3n², c = m², a = 6mn) leads
     to Lemmas 2 and 3.
   - Cases 2 and 7 give the conics a² + 3(2c)² = t² and a² − 3(2c)² = t². His Lemma 1 parametrizes them, and they lead
     to Lemmas 4–7.
   - Lemma 1 as printed is incomplete. It claims that all primitive solutions of u² + 3v² = w² come from
     (3m² − n²)² + 3(2mn)² = (3m² + n²)², so v is always even. Primitive solutions with v odd exist, for example
     (u,v,w) = (1,1,2) and (11,5,14). The slip is harmless, because he applies the lemma only with v = 2c even.
   - The six quartic Thue equations, all with right side 1, are:

     | Case | Lemma | Equation | Solutions | X |
     |---|---|---|---|---|
     | 1 | 2 | n⁴ + 36nm³ + 36m⁴ = 1 | (1,0), (1,−1) | 0, 42 |
     | 1 | 3 | 9n⁴ + 12nm³ + 4m⁴ = 1 | (1,−1) | 18 |
     | 2 | 4 | n⁴ − 12n³m + 18n²m² + 9m⁴ = 1 | (1,0), (2,1) | 4, 70 |
     | 2 | 5 | n⁴ + 18n²m² + 36nm³ + 9m⁴ = 1 | (1,0) | −2 |
     | 7 | 6 | n⁴ − 12n³m − 18n²m² + 9m⁴ = 1 | (1,0) | 8 |
     | 7 | 7 | n⁴ − 18n²m² − 36nm³ + 9m⁴ = 1 | (1,0) | 2 |

   - So X ∈ {−2, 0, 2, 4, 8, 18, 42, 70}, x = X/2 − 1, and the positive x are 1, 3, 8, 20, 34.
6. *Proof of Lemma 2*, the only one printed.
   - The field is K₁ = Q(η), η⁴ − 36η + 36 = 0, with integral basis [1, η, η²/6, η³/6] (Billevich–Shulikina),
     Galois group S4 and discriminant −2⁴·3²·179. It has two real roots, so two fundamental units:
     ε₁ = η³/6 + η²/6 − 6 and ε₂ = (8/3)η³ + (23/3)η² + 22η − 33, by Billevich's method.
   - The units of the ring Z[η] are generated by ε̄₁ = −ε₁⁻² = 1 − η and ε̄₂ = −ε₂⁻⁶. So the equation becomes
     n + mη = ε̄₁^α ε̄₂^β.
   - At p = 3: ε̄₁⁹ = 1 + 3A with A ≡ 2η³, and ε̄₂ = 1 + 3B with B ≡ η² (mod 3). A table mod 3 of ε̄₁^r ε̄₂^β,
     r = 0..8, leaves r = 0 and r = 1.
   - In both cases the linear terms of the two vanishing coordinates have a determinant ≡ −1 (mod 3). Skolem's
     theorem (his 1934 paper, p. 179) then gives the single solution β = γ = 0.
   - This is exactly the Jacobian criterion, Proposition 17 of
     [lifting.binomial.multi](../../methods/lifting.binomial.multi/proof.md), with λ = 0 and ν₃(J) = 0.
7. *Lemmas 3–7* "are proved similarly". No details are printed.
8. *His remark.* The fields of η⁴ − 36η + 36, η⁴ − 12η³ + 18η² + 9 and η⁴ − 18η² − 36η + 9 are three different
   quartic fields with the same discriminant −2⁴·3²·179. 00_fields.py checks this claim; see section 4.

**Relation to the present proof.**
- In terms of his (X, Y), the coordinates of our E are X_E = 6X and Y_E = 36Y. His reduction covers only the points
  of his model, those with 6 | X_E.
- His three fields are ours: Lemmas 2–3 go with class (1,0), 4–5 with (1,1), and 6–7 with (0,1). The isomorphisms are
  checked in [00_fields.out.txt](scripts/00_fields.out.txt).
- His right-side-1 equations are sub-cases of our right sides {1,4,9,36}. For example our F(N, −6M) = 36·(his L2).
- The mechanism is the same: factorization in a cubic field, then (2,1) quartic Thue equations, then units, then
  Skolem's method in two indices. The present proof differs in four ways:
  - it does not use ideals or class numbers, at the price of hypothesis (H), rank 2;
  - it does not assume fundamental units. Lemma 3.2 of [proof.md](proof.md) gives coverage, and fundamentality is
    only a by-product (Remark 3.2(iii)). That by-product confirms his claim that ε₁, ε₂ of Lemma 2 are fundamental,
    since his units and ours generate the same group modulo ±1. His further claim (5), that ε̄₁, ε̄₂ generate
    Z[η]^× modulo ±1, is not checked by a recorded script;
  - it uses no completions: Proposition 17 is a finite-polynomial statement, used with a congruence sieve;
  - all twelve equations are proved, with re-runnable exact certificates, where Avanesov printed one of six.

## 3. Verdict
- **Theorem.** Classical: Avanesov 1966/67, re-solved by Stroeker–de Weger 1999 and by Gallegos-Ruiz et al. 2020.
- **Mechanism.** Avanesov's: a cubic-field descent, then (2,1) quartic Thue equations, then two-index Skolem. This
  proof is a finite reconstruction of it.
- **The finite form has precedent.** Its congruence-only shape was already used for the sister curve 8604.a1 by
  Uchiyama (1984).
- **The coverage lemma** is an effective form of classical reduction theory (Koecher).
- **Novelty.** None is claimed, by the project's criterion: the same proof, or a rewording of it, of this theorem.
- **What this card adds is presentation.**
  - All twelve equations are proved, where Avanesov printed one of six.
  - The certificates are exact and re-runnable.
  - The units are not assumed fundamental; they are proved fundamental, without the unit theorem.
  - The p-adic analysis is replaced by the finite-polynomial Proposition 17 and a congruence sieve.
- **Avanesov's slip** in the remark on discriminants is recorded (section 4).

## 4. Checks of Avanesov's statements
From [00_fields.out.txt](scripts/00_fields.out.txt) §6, by exact arithmetic: 27 statements of the paper were tested,
and 26 are true. All six lemma solution lists also agree with a box search (|N|,|M| ≤ 300), which is a confirmation,
not a proof.

**True:**
- the integral basis and discriminant of K₁(η);
- the cubic resolvent;
- the unit identities (5);
- the Skolem data (7) and table (8);
- the cubic-field identities (18), (19), (21), (22) and the factorization of 3λ² − 4.

**False: his p. 413 remark.** He says the three quartic fields have discriminant −2⁴·3²·179. The field of his Lemmas 4–5,
η⁴ − 12η³ + 18η² + 9 = 0, has discriminant −2²·3²·179 = −6444. Its maximal order is
[1, η, (η²+3)/6, (η³+η²+3η+3)/12], of index 72 over Z[η]. The slip has no visible consequence, since Lemmas 4–5 are
printed without proof.

**Correspondences (exact):**
- His fields are ours through η = 6/α, 3/(α+1) and 3(1+α)/(1−α).
- Each of his equations is one of ours: L2 ↔ ((1,0), 36), L3 ↔ ((1,0), 4), L4 ↔ ((1,1), 9), L5 ↔ ((1,1), 1),
  L6 ↔ ((0,1), 4), L7 ↔ ((0,1), 36).
- His units ε₁, ε₂ of K₁ and our class (1,0) units generate the same group modulo ±1.

**5.1 The field of discriminant −716** (LMFDB 3.1.716.1).
- LMFDB [full]: polynomial x³ − 4x − 6 (x = −λ), monogenic, class number 1, fundamental unit x² + 5x − 19 = λ² − 5λ − 19
  = ε, regulator 6.40536…; 2 totally ramified, 3 split, 179 = 𝔭𝔮².
- S. Cavallar, F. Lemmermeyer, *The Euclidean algorithm in cubic number fields*, Number Theory (Eger 1996), de Gruyter
  1998, 123–146; arXiv:1202.6019 [full, arXiv version]. §7, table of complex cubic fields: the row "−716 N 121/109",
  where N means class number 1 but not norm-Euclidean.
  - Method: a floating-point cover of half a fundamental domain, a unit to isolate the exceptional points, and exact
    arithmetic at those points. The authors trust the results through agreement with earlier tables; they do not
    present them as proofs.
  - They note that for unit rank 1 the minima tend to sit at points α/(ε − 1). ξ* is such a point:
    N(ε − 1) = −654 = −2·3·109.
- F. Lemmermeyer, *The Euclidean algorithm in algebraic number fields*, Expo. Math. 13 (1995) 385–416; updated copy of
  14 Feb 2004 [pages 6–7, 10, 15, 27–29]. The same row is on p. 28. Prop. 3.4 (van der Linden): for unit rank 1 the
  Euclidean minimum equals the inhomogeneous one.
- F. Lemmermeyer, *Euclidean Rings* (Diplomarbeit 1989), arXiv:2608.23216 [pages 145–148]. Row 85 lists −716 with
  h = 1 and the unit 23 + 3ϑ − ϑ² = −ε (ϑ = λ − 1), with the minimum left blank.
- E. M. Taylor, PhD thesis (Royal Holloway, 1975) [pages, OCR and page images]. Her table skips −716; her covering
  method stopped at −687. Taylor, J. London Math. Soc. (2) 14 (1976) 49–54 [review].
- S. Cavallar, F. Lemmermeyer, *Euclidean windows*, LMS J. Comput. Math. 3 (2000) [pages]: −716 is not among the 58
  conjectured norm-Euclidean complex cubic fields. OEIS A187773 [full] agrees.
- P. Lezowski, Math. Comp. 83 (2014) 1397–1426 [pages; the text was searched]: −716 is not mentioned. His online
  tables, with critical points, were [not accessed]; they would show whether the orbit of ±ξ* is the only critical one.
- **Verdict.** The literature gives M = 121/109 by computation. Remark 2.5 of hypothesis_H.md proves the lower bound
  exactly. The upper bound is not proved here, and not needed.

**5.2 Division with a multiplier (Dedekind–Hasse).**
- R. Dedekind, Supplement XI to Dirichlet's *Vorlesungen über Zahlentheorie*, 4th ed. 1894, §159, p. 451 [pages]: for
  Z[(1+√−19)/2] the division fails but unique factorization holds. His criterion, *Charakteristische Eigenschaft
  einklassiger Körper*, Werke II 373–375 [not accessed], is cited by Lemmermeyer.
- H. Hasse, J. reine angew. Math. 159 (1928) 3–12 [not accessed; metadata]. The criterion in Lemmermeyer's survey,
  Prop. 2.4 [pages]: R is a principal ideal ring iff some f: R → N, vanishing only at 0, satisfies the following.
  Whenever β ∤ α, there are λ, μ with 0 < f(λα − μβ) < f(β).
- A. Hurwitz, Math. Z. 3 (1919) 123–126 [not accessed; statement from Lemmermeyer's thesis]: some m depending on K makes
  every x have an integer 1 ≤ j < m and y with |N(jx − y)| < 1.
- Hand coverings with multipliers 1 and 2 for Z[(1+√−19)/2]:
  - O. A. Cámpoli, Amer. Math. Monthly 95 (1988) 868–871 [pages 869–871];
  - R. A. Wilson, note of 2016 [full].
- D. W. Dubois, A. Steger, Canad. J. Math. 10 (1958) [full]; F. Lemmermeyer, arXiv:1205.1147 [full] (the
  Dedekind–Hasse step for quadratic fields, iterated to a Bézout algorithm, which is the least-norm idea of (3.1)).
- Two-stage divisions for complex cubic fields: −199 and −351 (Lemmermeyer's thesis), −307 and −335 (Lezowski 2014,
  §4.6) [pages]. Weighted norms: −199, −327, −351, −367 (Euclidean windows) [pages].
- Not found: a published multiplier certificate for −716, or for any cubic field.

**5.3 The box principle.**
- Dedekind 1894, Suppl. XI, §181, Satz I [pages]: two of the integers with coordinates 0..k are congruent modulo the
  ideal, and their difference has norm at most H times the norm of the ideal. Satz II: every class contains an ideal of
  norm at most H. Section 6 of hypothesis_H.md is this argument, for a single prime lattice, with an explicit table.
- Hurwitz, Göttinger Nachr. 1895 [abstract]; S. Wright, arXiv:1507.07520, Lemma 70 [pages]; a Bar-Ilan exercise sheet
  (88-798, 5779, sheet 2, ex. 8) [full]; Brauer–Reynolds, Canad. J. Math. 3 (1951) [abstract].
- Not found: a published box-principle proof of h = 1 for a specific cubic field.
