# Notebook methods for the Schäffer investigation

## Finding reviewed statements and gaps

The [permanent catalogue](catalogue/README.md) gives each source item and project consequence an RN tag. Its proof links distinguish an identity used in a derivation, an adaptation of a method, an obstruction to transfer and a historical cross-reference. The [volume-by-volume reading backlog](catalogue/COVERAGE.md) links every individual item without a registered research proof path and lists the physical-page gaps. The [coverage query](source_coverage.py) retrieves those records by volume, page or status. From this directory run:

~~~sh
python3 source_coverage.py
python3 source_coverage.py --volume notebook-5 --page 22
python3 source_coverage.py --volume notebook-1 --page-card 35
python3 source_coverage.py --volume notebook-1 --tex-gaps
python3 source_coverage.py --volume lost-3 --unlinked
python3 source_coverage.py --volume lost-1 --gaps
~~~

A page card returns the original image, the accepted TeX and its receipt when available, every individual source assertion on the page, and the permanent tags of all project results reachable from each assertion on proof edges. The reviewed TeX backlog includes front matter. Pages with one or more recorded statements may still need further extraction.

The summary gives physical pages with individual statement locators and counts individual source tags with or without a dependency path to a registered research result. A path may lead to an identity, a failed-transfer theorem or a partial result. The page query also returns older audit tags. The gaps query includes front matter and pages covered only by earlier audits. Absence of a path means that the register has no recorded proof relying on the item. No total theorem count is known for the ten volumes. For the actual inputs of one particular theorem, run e.g. `python3 catalogue/catalogue.py usage RN-00KY`.


The [permanent statement catalogue](catalogue/README.md) records individual entries, source attributions, research applications and unresolved transfers across the ten books. Each RN tag has a stable link and a navigable dependency record. The earlier 29-passage ledger remains one source of evidence within this catalogue.

The ten tables record a first retrieval pass across the 4,526 PDF pages. Each row gives the physical page, its extraction method, the topics detected in its searchable text, and the five-page source chunk. The [indexer](index_pages.py) specifies the English search terms. Headers, bibliographies and OCR can produce false matches or missed entries. An empty topic field means that the scan found none of these terms on that page. The table below records individual passages examined and what each offers the argument.

The [entry and equation inventory](entries/coverage.tsv) adds a second, independent pass over every page. The ten volume directories in [entries](entries/) contain short TSV files, one per ten PDF pages. Each record locates a possible statement heading, proof opening or numbered formula, with the line relative to the page's first text line and the five-page source filename. This pass found 3,889 heading candidates, 12,887 formula-label candidates, 2,802 proof-opening candidates and 404 likely cross-references across 3,874 pages. It also explicitly retains the 640 nonblank pages without a recognized marker and the 12 confirmed blank pages. The [indexer](index_entries.py) checks continuous page coverage and enforces a direct-read size budget. A heading can be a reference, a label can be damaged by OCR, and different books number entries differently. These are locations to examine, with no assumption that a candidate has been read, proved or applied. Page images take precedence when a formula and its text disagree. The [passage ledger](passages.tsv) gives an individual disposition and a research destination for 29 distinct page images cited in the research notes, including the corrected Bernoulli attribution. Several rows record limitations or analogies.

The [fixed-endpoint certificates](../15_fixed_endpoints/README.md) use complete orbits with one shared exponent residue. Additional page-image readings record the five twists in Lost Notebook I, printed p. 18, the constrained choices of seventh roots in Notebook IV, printed p. 29, and the exact integer-power formula in Notebook I, printed p. 151. The [cubic unit sums](../15_fixed_endpoints/cubic_limit.md) specialize that last formula before removing multiples of a prime.

A targeted second search of the text in all ten volumes looked for Pell equations, norm equations and fundamental units. The page image in [Lost Notebook IV, printed p. 174](../../../notebooks/lost-4/pages/0189.webp) treats a fixed norm of six by a unit orbit. The [index-13 proof](../16_relative_units/README.md) first enumerates the possible ideals of a fixed relative norm and then restricts the resulting unit orbits by their local logarithms. [Lost Notebook II, printed p. 215, Theorem 9.6.1](../../../notebooks/lost-2/pages/0222.webp) and [Notebook V, printed p. 217, (4.5)–(4.6)](../../../notebooks/notebook-5/pages/0225.webp) instead relate fundamental units to class-number formulas in quadratic fields. Those identities would require additional hypotheses to supply the units of the degree-ten field used here.
The [all-index root proof](../17_all_indices/roots.md) uses the power-sum formula and the standard translation and half-value identities in the editorial discussion of Lost Notebook IV, Entry 8.4.1. The series evaluation (25.5) in Notebook II contains products of half-values. Its following page explains the origin of the general formula. [Lost IV, printed p. 195](../../../notebooks/lost-4/pages/0209.webp) · [Lost IV, printed p. 196](../../../notebooks/lost-4/pages/0210.webp) · [Notebook II, printed pp. 294](../../../notebooks/notebook-2/pages/0303.webp) and [295](../../../notebooks/notebook-2/pages/0304.webp). [Notebook IV, printed p. 135](../../../notebooks/notebook-4/pages/0143.webp) discusses primes in intervals. Nagura supplies the narrower interval needed to bound the degree of a repeated factor. The complete root-of-unity orbit in the [odd-exponent norm calculation](../17_all_indices/README.md) separates the logarithmic derivatives into distinct characters.

A search of all ten volumes for binomial expansions, Bernoulli numbers and their denominators led to [Notebook IV, printed p. 75](../../../notebooks/notebook-4/pages/0083.webp) and [Lost Notebook III, printed p. 123](../../../notebooks/lost-3/pages/0132.webp). The first page states the exact squarefree denominator rule and corrects an entry in the accompanying table. The second uses the denominator at index p−1 in a congruence for a Lambert series. The [shifted root estimate](../21_shifted_roots/README.md) charges each prime denominator to at least p−1 orders in the series. This reduces the height bound for rational leading roots as the source index grows.

The same two Bernoulli denominator pages control the leading and higher terms separately at a prime dividing \(x\) or \(x+1\). Combining that computation with Kummer's congruence gives the [exact regular-prime valuations](../26_radical_bounds/README.md). Translating the expansion to \(x-1\) and \(-x-2\) then gives a divisibility restriction on \(2k-3d\) for a proposed triangular power. The [finite calculation](../26_radical_bounds/triangular.md) combines the two marked pairs with an integral estimate.

A pass over all ten text collections for continued fractions and convergents located the numerator and denominator recurrences in [Notebook II, printed p. 130, (17.18)](../../../notebooks/notebook-2/pages/0139.webp). The [joint exponent certificate](../27_fixed_bases/README.md) uses ordinary convergents to give a verified rational separation between the logarithms of the endpoint and base.

The [smooth-source calculation](../28_smooth_sources/README.md) uses the positive norm-one orbit of an integral Pell equation. The fixed norm-six orbit in Lost Notebook IV, printed p. 174, supplies a comparison. Exact ranks of appearance at the allowed primes bound the orbit exponent in the norm-one calculation.

A search of all ten text collections for determinants, Vandermonde identities and quadratic residues led to two page-image checks. [Notebook V, printed p. 15](../../../notebooks/notebook-5/pages/0024.webp) constructs the five shifted coefficient rows for eliminating a common root of the cubic (1.4) and quadratic (1.5). The [growing-index proof](../31_endpoint_sieve/growing_indices.md) uses precisely this resultant operation after obtaining an auxiliary polynomial by 2-adic interpolation. [Lost Notebook III, printed p. 302](../../../notebooks/lost-3/pages/0310.webp) expands the different three-by-three determinant (8.5.25.17) to recover a Rogers–Ramanujan identity. The [shifted divisor proof](../31_endpoint_sieve/balanced.md) instead uses the formal root coefficients from Notebook I with a variable denominator in the approximating rational function.

Three additional page readings lead to a bound in the other direction, counting the source exponents \(k\) for which some endpoint may work. In [Notebook III, Chapter 16, printed p. 37, (22.3)](../../../notebooks/notebook-3/pages/0052.webp), Euler's odd-parts/distinct-parts identity lets the argument split the ordinary partition product by powers of two and bound its coefficients. The proof of [Lost Notebook II, Entry 7.2.1, printed p. 150, (7.2.5)](../../../notebooks/lost-2/pages/0157.webp) identifies exponents with partition weight, number of parts and largest part. [Lost Notebook III, printed p. 13, following (2.1.24)](../../../notebooks/lost-3/pages/0022.webp) identifies the ordinary partition series by specializing the rank variable. The [source-index proof](../32_power_neighbors/source_indices.md) tracks the weight of an exponential monomial through multiplication. This gives at most \(K^{o(1)}\) exceptional indices through \(K\) when all targets and endpoints through \((\log(2k))^{2-\epsilon}\) are allowed.

The [cyclotomic order argument](../32_power_neighbors/cyclotomic.md) uses the complete groups of conjugate roots from Notebook IV and Lost Notebook I to select local branches. Its rational projector uses primitive-root traces from Ramanujan's 1918 paper. That paper and the modern tools in the proof are separately identified.

## Volume-specific mathematical audits

* [Notebooks I–II](deep/regular-1-2/README.md) record all chapters, 52 clusters and two checked limits on continued fractions and inverse-series arguments at index 17.
* [Notebooks III–V](deep/regular-3-5/README.md) record all 24 chapters and eleven image-checked formula pages. The audit relates the branch-prime norm at all odd indices to a Bernoulli number and verifies exactly what a degree-17 dissection detects.
* [Lost Notebooks I–II](deep/lost-1-2/README.md) give a rational spacing bound for large target exponents and a cyclotomic moment calculation that specifies the information retained by finite root-of-unity data.
* [Lost Notebooks III–V](deep/lost-3-5/README.md) cover the 50 numbered chapters and identify seven newly checked pages. The midpoint calculation restricts every prime divisor of \(2x+1\) for a square at index 17, and two ramified primes exclude every target on explicit progressions. A signed-rank identity supplies an exact square test for the odd part of a power sum.

The chapter and candidate totals in these reports denote different units of coverage. Each report records its remaining reading backlog.

The [midpoint calculation](../34_midpoint/central-values.md) combines the centered Bernoulli expansion, the denominator theorem and the Lost III–V splitting argument. On every odd index from 7 through 2,001 the midpoint gives a positive-density set of prime obstructions for each prime target. The [companion progression theorem](../34_midpoint/README.md) reuses the simple-root count deduced from the centered expansion.

The [Lost IV, Chapter 20 statement audit](deep/lost-4-5/second/README.md) accounts for eight entries and a manuscript table. Its moment recurrence gives exact common divisors in blocks of even or odd source indices at an arbitrary endpoint.

The [regular IV.31 audit](deep/regular-3-5/second/README.md) accounts for all thirty entries and checks both the coefficient recurrence and the hypotheses of the limiting arguments against the supplied pages.
