# P17: Rational contact ladders and prime-support sign synchronization
written: 2026-10-29T03:55:25Z
question: P17
status: partial (stronger unconditional S13 regions and complete prime-support regions for both Thue equations are proved; unrestricted S13, S-003, and Thomas a=7 remain unproved here)

## 0. Outcome and scope

There are two useful advances, neither of which is a logarithmic-form argument.

**PROVED: a rational-contact ladder with bounded residual degree.** Replacing a polynomial contact by an integer pair of polynomials A,B changes the k=13 contact comparison from

    3^e < constant * h^5

to a family of inequalities

    3^((s+1)e) < 10^kappa_s * h^(s+5).

Here |n-1|=h*3^e, or |n+2|=h*3^e. Ten completely checked contacts are supplied, through s=16. More generally, for every rational alpha<1, a finite, effective contact construction excludes all sufficiently large e with h <= 3^(alpha e). The constants depend on alpha; no assertion that they are small is made. This is an all-height theorem on growing-cofactor regions, not a finite scan and not an unrestricted classification.

Four particularly simple certified exclusions are:

| Either endpoint N=n-1 or n+2; write N=h*3^e, 3 does not divide h | No S13 square solution when |
|---|---|
| h^2 <= 3^e | e >= 161 |
| h^3 <= 3^(2e) | e >= 405 |
| h^4 <= 3^(3e) | e >= 971 |
| h^5 <= 3^(4e) | e >= 6068 |

The finite cofactor region is also enlarged:

**PROVED:** if n>1 and min(h_3(n-1),h_3(n+2)) <= 100000, then S13(n) is not a square. The proof covers every exponent, using the contact bounds and a complete replay of 3,082,919 exponent rows / 6,165,838 endpoints. All even power exponents are excluded on these same regions.

**PROVED: contact really does synchronize an unbounded collection of primes for the Thue equations.** For z != 0 define

    b_q(z) = product of p^v_p(|z|) over all primes p == 1 mod q,

with an empty product equal to 1. This is the full prime-power part, not the radical or squarefree kernel. Put H=10^100.

* For Thomas a=7, every solution of |x^3-6x^2y-9xy^2-y^3|=1 with one nonzero member of {x,y,x+y} satisfying b_3(z)<=H is one of the six trivial signed pairs.
* For S-003, every solution of

      w^4-6uw^3-29u^2w^2+54u^3w-19u^4=1

  with one nonzero member of {u,u-w,u-2w} satisfying b_4(z)<=H is one of

      +/-(0,1), +/-(1,1), +/-(2,1).

  A zero member already gives a known point. The same complete classification holds whenever one of these three members is a nonzero signed prime power, with no restriction on its prime.

These are genuine global regions, permitting arbitrarily many primes and arbitrarily large exponents in the complementary residue classes. The finite boxes used to finish them are consequences of the new divisibility theorems: |y|<=11*10^200 for Thomas, and |u|<=8*10^306 for the quartic. Exact continued-fraction certificates exhaust those boxes. A finite box is not being advertised as a global bound without the stated prime-support hypothesis.

**What is not proved:** an unrestricted sign-synchronization theorem for S13, a bound on all its endpoint cofactors, or complete exclusion of the remaining balanced Thue tails. A degree-budget theorem below identifies the precise limit of the naive balanced-allocation argument. It is not a theorem that no new elementary descent or cover can work.

## 1. Exact S13 normalization and the two marked points

Put

\[
P(T)=30T^5-175T^4+574T^3-1180T^2+1382T-691,
\qquad T=n(n+1).
\]

The polynomial difference identity

\[
[T^2P(T)]_{T=n(n+1)}-[T^2P(T)]_{T=n(n-1)}=420n^{13}
\tag{1}
\]

and the zero value at n=0 prove

\[
420S_{13}(n)=T^2P(T).
\tag{2}
\]

The verifier checks every coefficient in (1), so no summation formula is assumed without its finite certificate.

For n>=1 a square solution is equivalent to

\[
P(T)=105r^2,
\quad r\in\mathbb Z,
\quad T=n(n+1),
\quad y=Tr/2.
\tag{3}
\]

Indeed r=2y/T is initially rational. If r=a/b in lowest terms, (3) gives b^2|105, hence b=1. Conversely T is even, so y=Tr/2 is integral. Replacing r by its negative has no effect on the square problem.

There is an especially short proof of the required restriction at three. If n is 0 or -1 modulo 3, then T=0 modulo 3, and P(T)=-691=2 modulo 3. This contradicts (3). Thus

\[
\boxed{n\equiv1\pmod3.}
\tag{4}
\]

Set X=n-1 and W=105r. Then

\[
W^2=f(X),\qquad f(X)=105P((X+1)(X+2)),
\quad f(0)=105^2,
\quad \deg f=10,
\quad \operatorname{lc}(f)=3150.
\tag{5}
\]

The leading coefficient 3150=2*3^2*5^2*7 is not a rational square, so f is not a square in Q[X]. Reflection is the exact identity

\[
f(-3-X)=f(X).
\tag{6}
\]

Consequently the same contact certificate applies at X=n-1 and X=-n-2, with the same integer W. These are the two lifts of T=2; they are not two independent marked points on the T-quotient. The two endpoint distances differ by three, and for a possible nontrivial solution their 3-valuations satisfy

\[
e_1,e_2\ge1,\qquad \min(e_1,e_2)=1.
\tag{7}
\]

One must not count the same 3-power twice when combining the endpoints.

## 2. A general finite rational-contact theorem

### 2.1. Contact order can be raised without assuming nonzero Hankel determinants

**PROVED.** Let f in Z[X] be nonsquare in Q[X], of degree d>=1, with f(0)=c^2 and nonzero integer c. Put g0=ceil(d/2). For every integer s>=0 there are nonzero integer polynomials A,B, an integer nu with

\[
0\le\nu\le\min(s,g_0-1),
\]

and a nonzero integer polynomial R such that

\[
A(0)\ne0,\quad B(0)=cA(0),
\quad \deg A\le s-\nu,\quad \deg B\le s+g_0-\nu,
\]

\[
\boxed{A(X)^2f(X)-B(X)^2=X^{\tau}R(X),}
\quad \tau=2s+g_0+1-\nu,
\quad \deg R\le g_0-1-\nu.
\tag{8}
\]

In particular, with g=s+g0-nu,

\[
\tau-g=s+1.
\tag{9}
\]

The theorem is effective using finite rational linear algebra. It does not assume nonsingularity of a prescribed square Padé system.

**Proof.** Work only in Q[X]/(X^t), with t=2s+g0+1. Construct the finite list q0,...,q_(t-1), starting with q0=c, by

\[
2c q_j=f_j-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}q_iq_{j-i}.
\tag{10}
\]

It satisfies q^2=f in this finite quotient. Let l=s+g0. For A0 of degree at most s, impose that the coefficients of A0*q in degrees l+1,...,l+s are zero. These are s homogeneous linear equations in s+1 unknowns, so they have a nonzero rational solution. Let B0 be the truncation of A0*q through degree l. Then

\[
B_0-A_0q=0\pmod{X^t}.
\]

Let nu be the order of A0 at zero. Since q0!=0 and nu<=s<=l, B0 has the same order. Divide both polynomials by X^nu. Their norm difference vanishes to order at least t-nu, because after division the other factor B+Aq has a nonzero constant term. Equivalently this follows by multiplication in the finite quotient; no convergent square root has been introduced.

That norm difference is nonzero. Otherwise f=(B/A)^2 in Q(X); reducing the fraction B/A and using polynomial gcd/division makes its denominator constant, which would make f a polynomial square. Its degree is at most 2(s+g0-nu). Thus

\[
t-\nu\le2(s+g_0-\nu),
\]

which gives nu<=g0-1. Clear the finitely many rational coefficients of A,B by a common positive integer. Their norm difference now has integral coefficients, and division by the monomial X^(t-nu) leaves R in Z[X]. The displayed degree calculation gives its bound. This proves every assertion. ∎

An explicit implementation is: form the rational coefficient matrix, row-reduce it, choose a nonzero rational kernel vector, remove its initial common power of X, and clear denominators. All operations terminate on finite matrices. A certificate consists of the actual A,B,R and their coefficient identity; no numerical rank decision is accepted.

For d=10, the residual degree is at most four **at every level s**. This is the useful feature of the ladder: raising contact does not create an unbounded-degree residual root problem.

### 2.2. Divisor-versus-size theorem with exact constants

Assume (8). Let

\[
L=1+\left\lfloor\sqrt{\sum_i|f_i|}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
C=L\sum_i|A_i|+\sum_i|B_i|+1.
\tag{11}
\]

At an integer point W^2=f(N), |N|>=1,

\[
|A(N)W|+|B(N)|<C|N|^g.
\tag{12}
\]

This follows from |f(N)| <= (sum|f_i|)|N|^(2g0), the bound deg A<=g-g0, and the coefficient bound for B. It is a size estimate, not an assertion that B/A approximates the real square root at large N.

Fix a prime p and put c_p=v_p(B(0)). If |N|=h p^e, p does not divide h, e>c_p, and R(N)!=0, then

\[
\boxed{p^{(s+1)e}<C p^{v_p(2B(0))} h^g.}
\tag{13}
\]

**Proof.** B(N)=B(0) modulo p^e, so v_p(B(N))=c_p. The two nonzero integers

\[
E_-=A(N)W-B(N),\qquad E_+=A(N)W+B(N)
\]

have product N^tau R(N). Their smaller valuation is at most v_p(2B(0)), because their difference is 2B(N). One factor therefore has valuation at least tau e-v_p(2B(0)). Compare its absolute value with (12), and use tau-g=s+1. ∎

This proof covers p=2 as written; the loss is v_p(2B(0)), not just v_p(B(0)). No p-adic series or limit is used.

For an all-N version let R0=1+sum|R_i|. Every integer root of R has absolute value <=R0: beyond this the integer leading term dominates the sum of lower terms. Set

\[
K=\max\{C p^{v_p(2B(0))},\ p^{(s+1)c_p}+1,\ R_0^{s+1}+1\}.
\tag{14}
\]

Then **every** such square value, with e>=1, satisfies

\[
p^{(s+1)e}<K h^g.
\tag{15}
\]

The second term covers shallow e, and the third covers residual roots. When a finite root-exclusion certificate proves R(N)!=0 for every integer N, omit the third term. A rootless reduction of the primitive polynomial R modulo one integer is sufficient.

### 2.3. Cofactors growing with the exponent

**PROVED.** Under the preceding hypotheses, fix a prime p and a rational alpha=a/b with 0<=alpha<1. An explicit finite computation produces E such that no integer square point has

\[
|N|=hp^e,\quad p\nmid h,\quad e\ge E,\quad h^b\le p^{ae}.
\tag{16}
\]

Choose s so that (s+1)/(s+g0)>a/b. In (8), g<=s+g0, so

\[
D=b(s+1)-ag>0.
\]

If (16) held, raising (15) to the b-th power would give

\[
p^{De}<K^b.
\]

Choose E by integer multiplication until p^(DE)>=K^b. Such an E exists, for example by the elementary bound p^(Dj)>j. This proves the theorem and its effective quantifiers.

This is the advertised uniform argument. It approaches, but does not cross, the balanced threshold h roughly p^e. No estimate on all n follows by deleting the cofactor condition. Nor is the theorem asserting an affordable constant as alpha approaches one.

## 4. Combining the two contacts: a complete sign ledger and its limitation

### 4.1. Full factor allocation for one rational contact

The prime-power comparison can be retained as an exact global ledger, rather than applied to one prime at a time.

**PROVED.** Fix one pair A,B from (8), and a square point W^2=f(N), N!=0, R(N)!=0. There are positive integers b,U,V with

\[
|N|=bUV,\quad b\mid |B(0)|,\quad \gcd(U,V)=1,
\tag{22}
\]

such that

\[
U^{\tau}\mid 2B(0)(A(N)W-B(N)),
\qquad
V^{\tau}\mid 2B(0)(A(N)W+B(N)).
\tag{23}
\]

Consequently

\[
U^{\tau},V^{\tau}<2|B(0)|C|N|^g.
\tag{24}
\]

**Proof.** At each prime p with a=v_p(N)<=v_p(B(0)), put the whole p^a into b. These contributions make b divide |B(0)|. At every other prime, B(N) has the fixed valuation v_p(B(0)), and the shared p-part of the two factors is at most v_p(2B(0)). Assign the whole p^a to U or V according to a factor with valuation at least tau*a-v_p(2B(0)). Multiplication over the finitely many primes proves (23). Each prime is assigned once, so U,V are coprime. Neither target factor is zero because R(N)!=0. Apply (12). ∎

This is a terminating ghost allocation: the pending unassigned prime multiplicity decreases at every assignment; the endpoint and its square witness are unchanged. The terminal state has two colors, not one. Termination of allocation is not termination of the original Diophantine problem.

Applied at both endpoints, it gives

\[
n-1=b_1U_1V_1,\quad n+2=b_2U_2V_2,
\qquad b_2U_2V_2-b_1U_1V_1=3,
\tag{25}
\]

with the exact two-factor divisibilities and fixed b-lists at each selected contact level. This is a cover of the full factor ledger. Nothing in its proof bounds U_i or V_i independently of n.

At primes not dividing 2*105*A(0), the color has a particularly transparent meaning: r=+1 or -1 modulo p. For all contacts at the same branch this sign agrees; it is not a new independent binary choice for each approximant.

### 4.3. A precise degree-budget obstruction to naive balanced allocation

**PROVED.** For the polynomial f in (5), and arbitrary nonzero rational polynomials A,B, put

\[
g=\max(\deg A+5,\deg B).
\]

Then

\[
\deg(A^2f-B^2)=2g.
\tag{28}
\]

If the leading degrees differ this is immediate. If they agree, cancellation would require the square of a rational leading-coefficient ratio to equal 3150, which is impossible. In particular the norm is never zero.

Suppose a single such norm has contacts of orders t1,...,tj at distinct finite rational points. Polynomial division gives

\[
\sum_i t_i\le2g.
\tag{29}
\]

When the endpoint differences all have size comparable to n, and their prime powers may divide the two signs in a balanced way, their total forced contact divisor has size on the scale n^(sum ti). Pigeonhole between two factors guarantees only an exponent (sum ti)/2<=g in one factor. But both factor size bounds have exponent g. There is therefore **no automatic strict exponent saving** from the total multiplicity and the two-factor pigeonhole alone. A nonnegative weighted combination of these total-degree inequalities cannot change this conclusion: sum ti<=2g holds for each norm before taking the weighted sum. This observation does not exclude deductions that use additional correlations between the markers.

This is a limitation of that specified inference, not an assertion that constants, arithmetic correlations, or other moves can never help. The rational-contact ladder improves the concentrated-prime region precisely because a whole prime power cannot split between signs. It does not prove that the full endpoint cannot split.

The infinity contact has the same rational leading-coefficient obstruction in this model. Passing to T changes the degree to five, where the two possible leading degrees have opposite parity. Thus no rational polynomial Pell unit or rational leading cancellation has been created merely by taking more terms.

### 4.4. Neither the endpoint difference nor local contact forces a common sign

There are explicit balanced factorizations with the correct endpoint difference. For k=24j+9, j>=0, set

\[
N_1=k(k+4),\qquad
N_2=(k+1)(k+3)=8\frac{k+1}{2}\frac{k+3}{4}.
\]

Then N2-N1=3, gcd(k,k+4)=1, and the two odd factors (k+1)/2,(k+3)/4 are coprime. Also n=N1+1 is 1 modulo 3. Every displayed nonconstant factor is comparable to sqrt(n). Removing a bounded collection of shallow factors at the finitely many bad primes preserves this balanced size pattern. For the actual ladder, tau=2g-4, so these size patterns satisfy the powers permitted by (24) for all sufficiently large j. They are **not claimed to satisfy S13(n)=y^2**. They prove that the endpoint relation and the size-balance tests alone do not bound a cofactor.

There is also an exact local independence statement. Fix an integer n and pairwise distinct odd primes dividing (n-1)(n+2) but not 2*630. In (26), X is divisible by each prime. Both choices

\[
Y\equiv+A_2(X)\quad\hbox{or}\quad Y\equiv-A_2(X)
\pmod{p^{3v_p(X)}}
\]

satisfy the square congruence at that prime. The two choices are distinct units. The ordinary Chinese remainder construction combines arbitrary choices at the distinct primes, also with Y=0 modulo 630. Thus no sign synchronization follows just from these congruences.

The checker exhibits all eight patterns at n=562: the good primes 11,17 divide n-1 and 47 divides n+2. It uses moduli 11^3,17^3,47^3 and 630, constructs the residue by modular inverses, and verifies both the root choices and the square congruences. This is a finite independence certificate, not an integer solution of the exact norm equation.

The missing S13 statement is therefore still substantive: a move or a new cover must use the exact square equation to constrain the balanced color assignment or the quadratic debt, rather than infer it from the local contacts alone.

## 5. Contact on Thue curves: prime-support sign synchronization

The Thue setting has an extra piece of algebra that the S13 square-root marker does not: the scalar coordinate at a known homogeneous solution is itself a cube root or a square of a fourth root. Certain residue classes of primes force its branch. This permits simultaneous allocation of an arbitrary number of prime-power blocks to one integer marker.

The elementary fact used throughout is Fermat's congruence for a nonzero residue: multiplication permutes the p-1 nonzero residues, and cancellation of their product gives z^(p-1)=1. Thus the cube map is one-to-one modulo p when p=2 modulo 3, by Bezout for 3,p-1. Also -1 is not a square modulo a prime p=3 modulo 4, since raising such a square to (p-1)/2 would give both 1 and -1. No quadratic reciprocity is used.

### 5.1. Thomas a=7: all primes other than 1 modulo 3 go to one factor

Write

\[
F_7(x,y)=x^3-6x^2y-9xy^2-y^3=\epsilon,\qquad\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}.
\]

A solution is primitive. Put B=x-2y. Exact expansion gives

\[
\boxed{B^3-\epsilon=7y^2(3x-y).}
\tag{30}
\]

Assume y!=0. For a prime p=2 modulo 3 dividing y, primitivity gives p does not divide x, and x^3=epsilon modulo p forces x=epsilon modulo p. Hence B=epsilon modulo p. In

\[
B^3-\epsilon=(B-\epsilon)(B^2+\epsilon B+1),
\]

the second factor is 3 modulo p, a unit. Therefore

\[
p^{2v_p(y)}\mid B-\epsilon.
\tag{31}
\]

The prime three can be included, not discarded. If 3|y, then x=epsilon and B=epsilon modulo 3. Substituting B=epsilon+3z gives

\[
B^2+\epsilon B+1=3(1+3\epsilon z+3z^2),
\]

whose 3-valuation is exactly one. The right side of (30) has valuation at least 2v3(y)+1 because 3|(3x-y). Thus (31) holds at p=3 as well.

Let h=b_3(y), and D=|y|/h. Multiplying (31) gives

\[
\boxed{D^2\mid x-2y-\epsilon.}
\tag{32}
\]

The marker is nonzero. If x-2y=epsilon, (30) gives 3x=y; together these imply -5x=epsilon, impossible for integer x.

If |x|>=8|y|, the leading term of F7 dominates the other terms, since

\[
8^3-6\cdot8^2-9\cdot8-1=55>1.
\]

For larger ratios the domination follows by dividing by |x|^3; every lower-term ratio decreases. Thus |x|<8|y| at a solution. Consequently

\[
0<|x-2y-\epsilon|<11|y|.
\]

Together with (32), this proves the global bound

\[
\boxed{|y|<11b_3(y)^2.}
\tag{33}
\]

The signed cyclic transformations generated by

\[
(x,y)\mapsto(y,-x-y)
\]

preserve |F7| and put any one of x,y,x+y, up to sign, in the second coordinate. Therefore (33) holds with each nonzero member z of this triple in place of y.

This is a ghost content descent, not a descent of |y| through other Thue solutions. Start with a pending factor |y| and the marker x-2y-epsilon. Remove each whole prime power whose prime is not 1 modulo 3 from the pending factor and divide the marker by its square, which (31) guarantees to be exact. The pending prime multiplicity strictly decreases. The terminal pending factor is h, and the original endpoint is hD. The integer size bound then gives (33). The rational branch is the same for every transferred prime, so their divisibilities multiply.

In particular, arbitrary powers and arbitrary products of primes 2,3,5,11,17,... are covered with h=1. This is not a sieve using only those finitely many example primes.

### 5.2. The same mechanism for the Thomas parameter family

There is a useful uniform version. For a>=1 put

\[
F_a=x^3-(a-1)x^2y-(a+2)xy^2-y^3,
\quad D_a=a^2+a+7,
\quad B=3x-(a-1)y.
\]

Exact expansion gives

\[
\boxed{B^3-27\epsilon=D_a y^2(9x+(4-a)y)}
\quad(F_a=\epsilon).
\tag{34}
\]

For primes p=2 modulo 3 dividing y, the same argument gives p^(2vp(y))|(B-3epsilon). At three, set e=v3(y)>=1 and U=B/3, an integer. If e=1, 3(B-3epsilon)=9(U-epsilon) is already divisible by 3^(2e). If e>=2, then U=x=epsilon modulo 3, and (34) gives

\[
v_3(U^3-\epsilon)\ge2e-1,
\]

since 9x+(4-a)y is divisible by 9. The quotient (U^3-epsilon)/(U-epsilon) has valuation exactly one, so 9(U-epsilon) is divisible by 3^(2e). Thus for h=b_3(y), D=|y|/h,

\[
D^2\mid3(B-3\epsilon).
\]

If B!=3epsilon, leading domination at |x/y|=a+3 and then coefficient bounds give

\[
|x|<(a+3)|y|,\quad
|B-3\epsilon|<(4a+11)|y|,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{|y|<3(4a+11)b_3(y)^2.}
\tag{35}
\]

The zero-marker case is completely explicit: (34) forces

\[
(2a+1)y=-9\epsilon,\qquad9x=(a-4)y.
\tag{36}
\]

For a>=1 it can occur only at a=1 or a=4; the integral pairs are directly recovered and checked. A point with y=0 is separate and trivial. The cyclic transformations again give the analogous inequalities for the other two linear coordinates. Thus Sol/Astra can attach a prime-support contact leaf to every fixed Thomas fibre, with a proved cutoff, without invoking a unit group or a logarithmic estimate. It is not a complete uniform family theorem: the b_3-cofactors can still grow.

### 5.3. S-003: three known anchors and one quadratic contact at each

Write the quartic in the norm-one sign convention

\[
F(u,w)=w^4-6uw^3-29u^2w^2+54u^3w-19u^4=1.
\tag{37}
\]

This is exactly the equation in the question multiplied by -1. Every solution is primitive. For a=0,1,2, put

\[
p=u-aw,\quad q=w.
\]

These are integral unimodular coordinate changes. The exact contacts are:

| a | B_a(p,q) | B_a^2-F(aq+p,q) |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | q^2-3pq-19p^2 | 20p^3(19p+3q) |
| 1 | q^2+11pq-51p^2 | 20p^3(131p-55q) |
| 2 | q^2-41pq-921p^2 | 20p^3(42413p+3781q) |

Assume p!=0. Neither B_a-1 nor B_a+1 is zero at an integral solution. Such a zero would make the displayed residual zero. Primitivity then leaves, up to common sign, only these (p,q) pairs and norm values:

| a | primitive residual-zero pair | F(aq+p,q) |
|---:|---|---:|
| 0 | (3,-19) | 130321 |
| 1 | (-55,-131) | 3347663881 |
| 2 | (3781,-42413) | 22971045324058561921 |

None has norm one. The table follows by solving the residual linear factor and taking its primitive integer vector; the verifier checks the norm exactly.

Let l=3 modulo 4 be a prime dividing p. Then q is a unit and q^4=1 modulo l, by (37). Since -1 is not a square modulo l, q^2=1 modulo l. Hence B_a=1 modulo l, and l does not divide B_a+1. The contact identity therefore gives

\[
l^{3v_l(p)}\mid B_a-1.
\tag{38}
\]

There is a fixed-cost treatment of the prime two. If v2(p)=e>=1, q is odd. The coefficient of pq in every B_a is odd. Thus B_a is 3 modulo 4 if e=1, and 1 modulo 4 if e>=2.

For e=1, v2(B_a-1)=1, so 4(B_a-1) is divisible by 2^(3e). For e>=2, B_a+1 has valuation one, while the product B_a^2-1, by its displayed residual, has valuation at least 3e+2. Thus B_a-1 has valuation at least 3e+1, more than required. Combining this with (38), and putting h=b_4(p), D=|p|/h, proves

\[
\boxed{D^3\mid4(B_a-1).}
\tag{39}
\]

The only unsupported primes left in h are those 1 modulo 4, with their full exponents. This is the required sign synchronization for the complementary, unbounded set of primes.

The following integer bounds are exact:

| a | K_a with |q|<K_a|p| | C_a with |B_a-1|<C_a p^2 |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 10 | 150 |
| 1 | 23 | 834 |
| 2 | 84 | 11422 |

To verify the first column, write F(aq+p,q)=q^4+b p q^3+c p^2q^2+d p^3q-19p^4. The respective coefficient quadruples (b,c,d,-19) are

    (-6,-29,54,-19), (22,19,-22,-19), (-82,-161,-98,-19).

For the stated K, K^4-|b|K^3-|c|K^2-|d|K-19>=2. Dividing by |q|^4 shows that the leading term dominates for every |q/p|>=K. Such a value cannot be 1. The second bound is the finite triangle inequality, with

    C_a=K_a^2+|[pq]B_a|K_a+|[p^2]B_a|+1.

Equations (39) and the size bound give the global contact restriction

\[
\boxed{|u-aw|<4C_a\,b_4(u-aw)^3\qquad(a=0,1,2),}
\tag{40}
\]

whenever the left side is nonzero.

The ghost move is explicit: transfer a prime-power block at a prime not 1 modulo 4 from |p| into D, and divide the integer marker 4(B_a-1) by the cube of that block. The exact divisibility is (38)-(39); the constant four pays the exceptional binary branch. The unassigned prime multiplicity decreases. At termination the full original solution and p=hD are retained. Unlike the unrestricted S13 ledger, this transfer has a single target sign for all the transferred primes.

If p=0, (37) gives q^4=1, so the point is already +/- (a,1).

**Prime-power corollary, including primes 1 modulo 4.** If |p|=l^e, the two nonzero factors B_a-1,B_a+1 have gcd dividing 2. For odd l one factor is divisible by l^(3e), so |p|<C_a. At two the product valuation is at least 3e+2 and the smaller factor valuation is one; hence |p|<C_a/2. These bounds also reduce to the finite certificate in the next section. No restriction on l is needed in this corollary.

## 6. Complete finite certificates for the Thue regions

### 6.1. The proved finite boxes

Take H=10^100. If one nonzero Thomas coordinate x,y,x+y has b_3<=H, cyclically move it to y. Equation (33) proves |y|<11H^2. By a common sign change assume y>0. The case y=0 already gives (+/-1,0).

\[
|u|\le |p|+a|q|<(1+aK_a)4C_aH^3.
\]

The largest coefficient is

\[

\]

Thus |u|<8*10^306. A common sign makes u>0. If u=0, w=+/-1. These are actual deductions from the prime-support hypotheses, not heuristic height estimates.

### 6.2. Root intervals and approximation inequalities

Use the monic polynomials

\[
f_3(z)=z^3-6z^2-9z-1,
\quad
f_4(z)=z^4-6z^3-29z^2+54z-19.
\]

IVT and direct rational sign tests give one root in each of these intervals:

\[
f_3:\quad(-2,-1),\ (-1/8,-1/9),\ (7,8);
\]

\[
f_4:\quad(-5,-4),\ (0,1/2),\ (1,2),\ (8,9).
\tag{41}
\]

Their number is the polynomial degree, so they are all the roots, each simple and real. The monic rational-root test excludes rational roots: test +/-1 for f3, and +/-1,+/-19 for f4.

Choose a root nearest to a real number z. For any other root, its distance from z is at least half its distance from the chosen root, by the triangle inequality and nearest-root choice. Using the interval gaps in (41), the product of these other distances is at least 14/9 for f3, and 15/8 for f4. The verifier checks these rational products for every possible chosen root.

Consequently a Thomas solution with y>0 satisfies, for some root beta,

\[
|x/y-\beta|\le\frac9{14y^3},
\]

and a quartic solution with u>0 satisfies, for some root beta,

\[
|w/u-\beta|\le\frac8{15u^4}.
\]

For denominators at least two, each is strictly smaller than 1/(2q^2). The denominator-one cases are checked directly using |x|<8|y| and |w|<10|u|. This is the complete reduction to finitely many convergents; semiconvergents are not silently being ignored.

### 6.3. Finite proof of the continued-fraction coverage criterion

For completeness, if alpha is irrational, gcd(p,q)=1, q>0, and

\[
|\alpha-p/q|<1/(2q^2),
\tag{42}
\]

then p/q is one of its continued-fraction convergents.

Define complete quotients by floor, subtraction and reciprocation. Define the convergents by

\[
p_j=a_jp_{j-1}+p_{j-2},\quad q_j=a_jq_{j-1}+q_{j-2},
\quad(p_{-2},p_{-1};q_{-2},q_{-1})=(0,1;1,0).
\]

Finite induction gives adjacent determinant +/-1, eventually strictly increasing positive denominators, and opposite signs of adjacent errors delta_j=p_j-q_j alpha. The sign assertion follows also from the exact finite identity

\[
\alpha=\frac{p_j\alpha_{j+1}+p_{j-1}}{q_j\alpha_{j+1}+q_{j-1}}.
\]

The denominator recurrence q_(j+2)>=q_(j+1)+q_j ensures that some denominator exceeds any prescribed q after finitely many steps.

Choose the last j with q_j<=q<q_(j+1). If p/q is not p_j/q_j, express the integer pair in the adjacent unimodular basis:

\[
(p,q)=A(p_j,q_j)+B(p_{j+1},q_{j+1}),\quad A,B\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Neither coefficient is zero, and they have opposite signs: equal positive signs violate q<q_(j+1), while equal negative signs violate q>0. Therefore A delta_j and B delta_(j+1) have the same sign, so |p-q alpha|>=|delta_j|.

But (42) is strictly better in this error than every distinct rational r/s with 0<s<=q. Otherwise the nonzero integer cross determinant would satisfy

\[
1\le|ps-qr|\le s|p-q\alpha|+q|r-s\alpha|
<(s+q)/(2q)\le1,
\]

a contradiction. Apply this to p_j/q_j. This proves the criterion using only finite identities and inequalities.

### 6.4. What the exact replay checks

For each root interval, the verifier makes a signed dyadic bracket by integer bisection. It checks opposite endpoint signs. At each continued-fraction step it requires the two rational bracket endpoints to have the same floor. It then subtracts that common integer and reverses endpoints on reciprocation. Every recorded quotient is therefore proved to be a quotient of the actual algebraic root. It stops only after the next denominator exceeds the proved bound. If a chosen precision were insufficient, the code would increase precision and redo the finite calculation; in the delivered run this was not needed.

Every convergent within the box is evaluated in the original homogeneous form by integer arithmetic. Results:

## 7. Why this does not yet remove the remaining tails

### 7.1. The remaining prime classes have real branch choices

For Thomas at a prime p=1 modulo 3, x^3=epsilon can have nonrational cube-root branches in the residue field. For example, modulo 7 and epsilon=1 the choices x=2,4 are not 1. At a prime dividing y they put the divisibility into B^2+epsilon B+1, not necessarily into B-epsilon. Thus the b_3-cofactor in (33) is not dispensable.

For the quartic, primes p=1 modulo 4 can have q^2=-1. For example q=2 modulo 5 gives q^4=1 but q^2=-1. At a transverse coordinate p=0 modulo 5 this selects B_a=-1 instead of +1. Thus the b_4-cofactor is not dispensable either.

These alternatives persist at every finite precision at primes not dividing the displayed derivative coefficient: expanding (z+p^e t)^d by the finite binomial theorem gives a unique next digit for each simple root. This is a finite root-lifting proof, not an appeal to p-adic analysis. Different primes' choices can be combined by ordinary CRT. The global equation may exclude them, but the contact identities alone do not already do so.

The residual sets are explicit:

* Any still-unknown Thomas a=7 solution has x,y,x+y all nonzero, b_3(x),b_3(y),b_3(x+y)>10^100, and satisfies (33) at each coordinate. The finite certificate also excludes every solution for which a cyclically selected second coordinate has absolute value at most 11*10^200.
* Any still-unknown S-003 solution has u,u-w,u-2w all nonzero, each corresponding b_4>10^100, and satisfies (40). It is outside the verified |u|<=8*10^306 box. None of the three transverse coordinates can be a signed prime power.

The elementary unit coverage from P15 remains compatible with these restrictions, but is not needed to prove them. The new restrictions can be transported into its exponent ledger; they do not yet bound every exponent on every tail.

### 7.2. Direct contact in a unit index is a different comparison

A fixed-order finite local contact in an integer unit index t typically guarantees a divisor of the form p^(j*v_p(t-r)), with j fixed. This is at most |t-r|^j. A raw orbit-height estimate of the form C*lambda^|t|, lambda>1, is exponentially larger. Comparing only this guaranteed divisor with that upper bound cannot yield a large-index contradiction.

The elementary comparison needs no infinite series: for integer T>=2(j+1), the single binomial term in (1+c)^T gives

\[
(1+c)^T\ge\binom{T}{j+1}c^{j+1}
\ge\frac{(T/2)^{j+1}c^{j+1}}{(j+1)!}.
\]

It exceeds any fixed multiple of T^j after an explicit finite threshold. This is a limitation of the proposed divisor-versus-that-size-bound argument, not a lower bound on a marker that might cancel. An independently proved small marker, or a moving-modulus sign obstruction, could change the comparison.

Our Thue contacts operate instead on the prime content of a transverse **coordinate**, already of the scale of the solution height, and synchronize all transferable primes into one factor. That is why (33) and (40) are effective whereas the naive fixed-prime contact in a large unit index is not.
