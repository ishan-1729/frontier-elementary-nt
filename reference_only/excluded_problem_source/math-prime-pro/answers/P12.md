## 1. Q1(a): determinant windows and the signed inner frame

### Source verification

The primary source is W. F. Lunnon, *The Number-Wall Algorithm: an LFSR Cookbook*, Journal of Integer Sequences 4 (2001), Article 01.1.1, §§3–4, pp. 8–15:
https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL4/LUNNON/numbwall10.pdf

The paper does state and prove square zero windows over an integral domain, geometric inner edges, the signed inner relation, and an outer-frame relation. It does **not** assert that zero sets over arbitrary rings with nilpotents have that shape. The field/integral-domain distinction is essential below.

This note proves the square-window and inner-frame statements independently and completely. It does not import the outer formula as a proved module. Nothing below relies on that outer formula.

### Conventions

For a doubly infinite sequence s over a commutative ring R, put

    W_(m,n) = det[s_(n+j-i)]_(0<=i,j<=m),   m>=0,
    W_(-1,n)=1.

Reversing rows turns this into a Hankel determinant, with the known sign (-1)^(m(m+1)/2). Desnanot–Jacobi, valid as a polynomial identity over every commutative ring, gives

    W_(m,n)^2 = W_(m-1,n) W_(m+1,n)
                 + W_(m,n-1) W_(m,n+1).                    (DJ)

For completeness, (DJ) follows from the two-row/two-column minor identity for the (m+2)-square Toeplitz matrix. That identity can be proved first for a matrix of independent variables by eliminating an invertible central block, taking the determinant of the resulting 2-by-2 Schur complement, and multiplying by the central-block determinant squared. After clearing denominators it is a polynomial identity over the integers, hence remains valid after every specialization, including singular matrices and rings with zero divisors.

### Unit-pivot recurrence lemma — PROVED

Suppose

    W_(m,n)=...=W_(m,n+g-1)=0

and W_(m-1,n-1),...,W_(m-1,n+g-1) are units. Then there are coefficients J_0,...,J_m with J_m=1 and J_0 a unit such that

    sum_(j=0)^m J_j s_(a+j)=0
        for every n-m <= a <= n+g-1.                       (R)

For m=0 the assertion is just that the displayed source entries vanish. Suppose m>0. Solve the first m equations (a=n-m,...,n-1) with J_m=1. Their coefficient matrix has determinant, up to sign, W_(m-1,n-1), a unit. The determinant W_(m,n)=0 then forces the next equation, at a=n: eliminate the first m entries of the last column; the full determinant becomes the unit pivot determinant times that last residual. Comparing the first and last m columns gives

    J_0 = (-1)^m W_(m-1,n)/W_(m-1,n-1),

so J_0 is a unit. Each subsequent zero determinant W_(m,n+j), with its unit upper-left m-square minor, extends the same relation by one equation. This proves (R), with no division by a zero or nonunit.

The same proof works with nonzero pivots over a field, or after passing from an integral domain to its fraction field.

### Finite square-window theorem over a field — PROVED

Every finite connected component of zero entries in the part m>=0 of a number wall over a field is a square with nonzero immediate frame. Connectivity here is horizontal/vertical adjacency. Infinite components require separate endpoint conventions; the finite statement is all that is used.

**Proof of rectangular shape.** If two diagonally adjacent entries in a 2-by-2 square vanish, (DJ) at each of the other two entries says its square vanishes, so both vanish. This implication uses the field hypothesis. A finite connected component is a rectangle: grow a rectangle from one element along a path in the component. Whenever a new zero touches a side, repeated applications of the diagonal completion rule fill the entire new row or column. If a rectangular component had a zero at a corner of its immediate frame, diagonal completion would connect it to the component. Thus its complete immediate frame, corners included, is nonzero.

Let the component have top-left (m,n), height h, and width g. Its top row and nonzero row immediately above yield, by (R), a proper order-m relation spanning source indices n-m,...,n+m+g-1, or relation starting indices n-m,...,n+g-1.

For the following two kernel arguments, reverse the rows of each Toeplitz matrix to use its Hankel form; its row starting indices are then increasing. Row reversal changes only the determinant sign and hence preserves zeros, units, and kernel dimensions.

If g>h, the polynomial E^h J(E), padded to degree m+h, supplies a nonzero kernel vector for W_(m+h,n): its row equations are precisely (R) at starts n-m,...,n+h, all of which belong to the permitted interval because h<=g-1. This makes the bottom-frame entry W_(m+h,n) zero, a contradiction.

If h>g, the entry W_(m+g,n) is zero inside the component, and its minor W_(m+g-1,n-1) on the left frame is nonzero. Hence its first m+g rows have a one-dimensional kernel and the full matrix has the same kernel. The vector E^g J annihilates those first m+g rows because (R) holds up to start n+g-1. It must therefore annihilate the final row as well. Thus (R) holds also at start n+g, which forces W_(m,n+g)=0 on the right frame, a contradiction. Consequently h=g. This proves the theorem.

Over the integers, apply the theorem in Q. The same exact zero set results.

### Signed unit inner-frame theorem — PROVED over every commutative ring

Assume an **exact** g-by-g square of zeros, g>=1, with top-left entry W_(m,n), and assume every entry of its immediate frame is a unit. Define k=0,...,g+1 by

    A_k=W_(m-1,n-1+k),       B_k=W_(m-1+k,n-1),
    C_k=W_(m+g-k,n+g),       D_k=W_(m+g,n+g-k).

Thus A_0=B_0 is the NW corner and C_0=D_0 is the SE corner; C and D are indexed upward and leftward from the SE corner. Set

    P=A_1/A_0, Q=B_1/B_0, R=C_1/C_0, T=D_1/D_0.

Then all four edges are geometric and

    PT=(-1)^g QR,
    A_k D_k=(-1)^(gk) B_k C_k       (0<=k<=g+1).          (IF)

All quotients here are only by units. In particular the theorem applies over Z/p^N whenever the exact zero square has a unit frame. Over an integral domain with merely nonzero frame, pass to its fraction field; the cross-multiplied equations (IF) are identities in the original domain.

**Proof.** Applying (DJ) at each non-corner frame entry gives

    A_k^2=A_(k-1)A_(k+1),

and the analogous three equations. Unit cancellation proves the four geometric-progressions assertions. Put a=A_0=B_0.

It remains to determine the sign connecting the four ratios; (DJ) on an abstract array alone does not suffice. Perturb the **source sequence** by

    s_(m+n+g-1) <- s_(m+n+g-1)+(-1)^m X,

over R[X]. Mark perturbed wall entries with a tilde. The unit-pivot lemma gives an order-m relation (R). After the perturbation it still holds for every start n-m,...,n+g-2. The upper-left (g-1)-square remains exactly zero, as follows. A cell in that square has indices (m-1+r,n-1+c), where 1<=r,c<=g-1, and its Hankel matrix has row starts n-m+c-r,...,n-1+c. Choose

    q=max(0,r-c).

Then 0<=q<=r-1 and q<=g-1-c. The coefficient vector of E^q J, padded with zeros to length m+r, annihilates every row: the shifted starting indices all lie in n-m,...,n+g-2. This vector has a unit coefficient. Invertible column operations therefore make a column zero, so the determinant is zero over R, even when R has zero divisors.

Write the new east and south edges of the shrunken square as

    M_k=tilde W_(m+g-k,n+g-1),
    N_k=tilde W_(m+g-1,n+g-k),       1<=k<=g+1.

They share M_1=N_1. Cofactor expansion at the single perturbed top-right matrix entry gives exactly

    M_(g+1)=A_g=aP^g,
    M_g=A_(g-1)X=aP^(g-1)X,
    N_(g+1)=B_g=aQ^g,
    N_g=(-1)^(g-1)B_(g-1)X=(-1)^(g-1)aQ^(g-1)X.

Along these edges, the vanishing shrunken square and (DJ) give M_k^2=M_(k-1)M_(k+1) and the analogous equation for N, for k=2,...,g. Work temporarily in R((X)): X, a, P, Q are units there. Downward induction yields the exact monomials

    M_k=a P^(k-1) X^(g+1-k),
    N_k=a Q^(k-1) (-1)^((g-1)(g+1-k)) X^(g+1-k).

They are actually polynomial identities, so no problematic specialization of a Laurent series is involved. Apply (DJ) to their shared endpoint M_1=N_1. Its north, west, east, south neighbors are M_2,N_2,tilde C_1,tilde D_1. Therefore

    a^2 X^(2g)
      = aP X^(g-1) tilde D_1
        + (-1)^(g-1) aQ X^(g-1) tilde C_1.

Cancel the unit a and the non-zero-divisor X^(g-1) in R[X], then set X=0. This gives

    P D_1=(-1)^g Q C_1.

Since C_0=D_0 is a unit, PT=(-1)^g QR. Finally, the geometric progressions give

    A_k D_k/(B_k C_k)=(PT/(QR))^k=(-1)^(gk),

which is (IF). This completes the proof.

### The prime-power square claim is false — exact counterexample

Let N>=2, let q=p^ceil(N/2), r=p^floor(N/2), and take source values

    s_2=q, s_3=0, s_4=r;

s_1 can be anything. Modulo p^N, the actual determinant wall has the 2-by-2 block

    W_(0,2)=q != 0,     W_(0,3)=0,
    W_(1,2)=q^2=0,     W_(1,3)=-qr=0.

The three connected zeros form an L around a nonzero entry, so their connected component cannot be a square. This disproves a blanket square-window assertion modulo p^N. Already p=2,N=2 gives values (2,0;0,0).

Correct usage: determine zero-window shape modulo p, where the quotient is a field; retain the full nonzero higher-precision interior values when lifting. An exact zero square with unit frame at precision p^N satisfies (IF), but entries merely zero modulo p cannot be substituted as zero modulo p^N. No signed inner-frame identity alone supplies the omitted p-adic digits.

### What this establishes for the tau programme

This closes the **ring-correct determinant/inner-frame component** of A7. It does not identify an arbitrary Somos-k orbit with a number-wall slice, does not prove a finite bound on window size, and does not construct a fixed-dimensional scalar atlas from increasing-order Hankel determinants. Those require separate identities and coverage arguments. Kronecker rationality is a finite-rank hypothesis, not a consequence of isolated zero windows.

There is also a raw-state ambiguity even for Somos-4 with alpha=beta=1. At state (0,1,1,-1), the defining equation leaves tau_4=t arbitrary; the next three values are t+1, t^2-t-1, and -t^3-t-1. A deterministic lift must retain information beyond those four scalar entries, such as the invariant J or an appropriate regular chart. A gauge alone does not specify the missing continuation.

## 2. Q1(b,c): a finite polynomial suspension atlas

The useful replacement for division by a tau term is to retain a regular earlier anchor and a scale ledger. A phase tag says which raw window is currently being observed. This is a finite cover of the completed orbit, not a claim that a singular raw window itself determines the missing continuation.

### 2.1. PROVED: polynomializing a finite Laurent block

Let $p$ be prime. Suppose a block of a homogeneous recurrence has been certified by homogeneous degree-one Laurent polynomials

$$
 L_0(x),\ldots,L_{q+d-1}(x)\in\mathbb Z_p[x_0^{\pm1},\ldots,x_{d-1}^{\pm1}],
 \qquad L_i=x_i\quad(0\le i<d).
$$

Here $q\ge1$, the recurrence identities hold identically in these Laurent polynomials, and

$$F(x)=(L_q(x),\ldots,L_{q+d-1}(x))$$

maps a specified union $U$ of unit residue polydiscs bijectively to itself. Require an integral analytic inverse with integral Taylor expansions on these unit discs. A cancelled Laurent inverse is one sufficient certificate. These are hypotheses to verify, not consequences of the word Somos.

Choose a positive integer $E$ divisible by $p(p-1)$ and large enough to clear every negative exponent in every $L_i$. Put

$$
 D(X)=(X_0\cdots X_{d-1})^E,\quad P_i(X)=D(X)L_i(X),\quad h=dE+1.
$$

Every $P_i$ is a polynomial of degree $h$. On homogeneous anchors $(X,S)$ with $S$ a unit and $X/S\in U$, define

$$
 \Phi(X,S)=\big(P_q(X),\ldots,P_{q+d-1}(X),\ S D(X)\big). \tag{A1}
$$

This is a polynomial map, over $\mathbb Z$ when the Laurent coefficients are integers, and over $\mathbb Z_p$ otherwise. It is the same polynomial at every precision $p^N$. No zero tau value is a denominator in (A1).

**Exact transport.** With $x=X/S$, homogeneity gives

$$
 \frac{P_i(X)}{S D(X)}=\frac{L_i(X)}S=L_i(x).
$$

Thus the raw anchor advances by $F$. The auxiliary scale changes by $S'=S D(X)$ and remains a unit. In coordinates $(x,S)$, the map is exactly

$$ (x,S)\longmapsto\big(F(x),S^hD(x)\big). \tag{A2}$$

**Invertibility over $\mathbb Z_p$ and every $\mathbb Z/p^N$.** Since $h\equiv1\pmod{p(p-1)}$, raising a unit to $h$ is a bijection modulo $p$. If a unit $s_n$ solves $s_n^h=u\pmod{p^n}$, the possible lifts $s_n+p^n a$, $0\le a<p$, satisfy

$$ (s_n+p^na)^h\equiv s_n^h+h s_n^{h-1}p^na\pmod{p^{n+1}}.$$

The derivative factor is a unit, so exactly one digit $a$ works. Induction proves the unique root at every precision and in $\mathbb Z_p$. On each residue disc its dependence is integral analytic: solve the Taylor identity recursively, dividing only by its constant unit derivative; after rescaling by $p$, the terms of increasing degree tend to zero. Given $(x',S')$, first recover $x=F^{-1}(x')$, then the unique unit root of $S^h=S'/D(x)$. This proves the asserted inverse of (A1). A polynomial inverse over $\mathbb Q$ is neither asserted nor needed.

**The finite phase atlas.** Take $q$ labelled copies of this lifted anchor domain, with phase $r=0,\ldots,q-1$. The shift is the identity on $(X,S)$ while $r$ increases, and at $q-1\to0$ it is (A1). In phase $r$ the raw window is

$$
 \left(\frac{P_r(X)}{S D(X)},\ldots,
       \frac{P_{r+d-1}(X)}{S D(X)}\right). \tag{A3}
$$

All transitions of the lifted state are polynomial. Equivalently the raw window is carried as polynomial numerators and one unit scale. The projection (A3) intertwines the shift because the $L_i$ satisfy the recurrence identities. It remains defined when any numerator is exactly zero. At a wrap, the matching identities first hold as rational identities on the nonsingular locus; their denominators involve only anchor units, so they hold on the whole stated domain and after reduction modulo every $p^N$.

The raw condition $\tau=w$ in phase $r$ is precisely

$$ M_{r,w}(X,S)=P_r(X)-wS D(X)=0. \tag{A4}$$

Moreover $v_p(M_{r,w})=v_p(\tau-w)$ because its denominator is a unit. This is an exact raw-value ledger, not a gauge-invariant substitute for the raw condition.

The same construction works for finitely many certified blocks joining finitely many unit anchor discs: retain the block label and its phase. A finite itinerary/coverage certificate must prove that every relevant orbit state belongs to one of them. Neither a bound on zero-window lengths nor this coverage is supplied by the construction itself.

### 2.2. PROVED: chart lifting, including the actual raw marker

Here is the local theorem used on every regular returning chart, with all needed hypotheses. Let

$$ H(z)=z+p^e K(z),\qquad K\in\mathbb Z_p\langle z_1,\ldots,z_s\rangle^s,$$

coefficientwise on the full unit polydisc, where $e\ge1$ for odd $p$ and $e\ge2$ for $p=2$. Let $z_*$ be the base point and $f$ an integral restricted analytic marker. Suppose the raw difference is a unit times $p^b(f(H^t z_*)-f(z_*))$, with $b\ge0$ fixed and the unit allowed to depend on $t$. This last relation must be proved from the ledger.

Then the following statements hold.

* If $v_p(f(Hz_*)-f(z_*))=e$, the raw difference has valuation $b+e+v_p(t)$ for every nonzero $t\in\mathbb Z_p$.
* Suppose an analytic involution $\sigma$ fixes $z_*$ and $f$, and $\sigma H\sigma=H^{-1}$. If
  $$v_p(f(H^2z_*)-2f(Hz_*)+f(z_*))=2e,$$
  the raw difference has valuation $b+2e-v_p(2)+2v_p(t)$ for every nonzero $t\in\mathbb Z_p$.

**Proof, including interpolation and the tail.** On the algebra of integral restricted series let $Uf=f\circ H$ and $\Delta=U-I$. Substitution and integral Taylor coefficients show that $\Delta$ increases coefficient valuations by at least $e$. The operator

$$ \mathcal L=\log(I+\Delta)=\sum_{k\ge1}(-1)^{k+1}\Delta^k/k$$

converges and increases valuations by at least $e$, since $ek-v_p(k)\ge e$. Its exponential also converges under the stated threshold. Formal logarithm/exponential identities, valid after truncation and then passage to the valuation limit, give $\exp(\mathcal L)=U$. Hence

$$
 f(H^t z_*)=\sum_{r\ge0} a_r t^r,
 \qquad a_r=(\mathcal L^r f)(z_*)/r!,\qquad
 v_p(a_r)\ge er-v_p(r!). \tag{A5}
$$

For nonnegative integers this follows from $\exp(t\mathcal L)=U^t$; it extends to all $t\in\mathbb Z_p$ by convergence. The inverse of $H$ is obtained by the contraction $x\mapsto y-p^eK(x)$, also in the restricted-series algebra, so negative integer iterates agree as well.

For $r\ge2$, $er-v_p(r!)\ge e+1$. Therefore the first-difference hypothesis makes $v_p(a_1)=e$, and $a_1t$ strictly dominates all higher terms at every $t\ne0$. This proves the first law.

Under the symmetry hypothesis the interpolated function is even, first on integer indices and then by continuity. All odd $a_r$ vanish. For even $r\ge4$, the bound in (A5) is at least $2e+1$. Consequently

$$
 f(H^2z_*)-2f(Hz_*)+f(z_*)
 =2a_2+\sum_{\substack{r\ge4\\r\text{ even}}}(2^r-2)a_r
$$

has valuation $v_p(2a_2)$, yielding $v_p(a_2)=2e-v_p(2)$. The quadratic term strictly dominates the tail at every $t\ne0$. Multiplication by the specified $p^b$ and unit proves the second law. These arguments prove the laws rather than infer them from sample values.

**Producing a near-identity return.** If a regular block $T$ and its inverse have integral Taylor expansions in the unscaled coordinates on $z_*+p\mathbb Z_p^s$, and $T(z_*)\equiv z_*\pmod p$, its rescaled reduction is affine:

$$ (T(z_*+pw)-z_*)/p\equiv b+Jw\pmod p.$$

Here $J$ is invertible. An integer power equal to the order of this affine permutation is coefficientwise the identity modulo $p$. At $p=2$, square once more. For $V=I+2K$, Taylor expansion gives

$$V^2=I+2K+2K\circ(I+2K)\equiv I\pmod4.$$

The higher Taylor terms in the rescaled return have the required factors of $p$ because of the unscaled Taylor integrality hypothesis. Evaluating a map on finitely many residue points without that fact would not certify a coefficientwise congruence.

### 2.3. The quadratic gauge survives a zero-aware lift

For a homogeneous bilinear recurrence of order $d$, write $R$ for its rational shift and $G_{A,B}(x)_i=AB^ix_i$. Homogeneity and index weight give $RG_{a,b}=G_{ab,b}R$. For $H=G_{A,B}^{-1}R^N$, induction therefore gives

$$
 R^{Nt}=G_{A^tB^{N\binom t2},\,B^t}\,H^t. \tag{A6}
$$

One proves this first on the nonsingular rational domain. In a certified suspension atlas all the raw coordinates in (A6) have unit denominators; clearing them extends the identity to zero observations. A lifted return need only intertwine the normalized return under projection. Its extra scale need not obey the same torus commutation law, because (A6) is an identity for the projected raw state.

To retain a raw value when $A,B$ are $p$-units, here is the complete augmentation from P03 in this setting. Let $H=I+p^eK$, choose $E_0$ with $A^{E_0}\equiv B^{E_0}\equiv1\pmod{p^{2e}}$, and put $L=2E_0$. For $0\le b<L$, set

$$
 C_b=A^bB^{N\binom b2},\quad
 D_b=A^LB^{N(Lb+\binom L2)},\quad Q=B^{NL^2}.
$$

Both $D_b,Q$ are $1$ modulo $p^{2e}$. The map

$$
 T_b(x,s,r)=\big(H^Lx,\ s(1+p^er),\ Qr+(Q-1)/p^e\big) \tag{A7}
$$

is coefficientwise $I$ modulo $p^e$. Starting at $(H^bz_*,1,(D_b-1)/p^e)$, its iterates satisfy

$$
 1+p^er_t=D_bQ^t,\quad s_t=D_b^tQ^{\binom t2},\quad
 \tau_{c+N(b+Lt)}=C_b s_t\,\iota_0(x_t).
$$

The last identity follows by substituting $b+Lt$ into (A6); the exponent difference is $N[Lbt+(L^2t^2-Lt)/2]$. If $\iota_0=P/V$ is the atlas observable with $V$ a unit, use the raw polynomial/analytic numerator

$$C_b sP(x)-wV(x).$$

Its valuation equals that of the raw difference. To isolate the fixed value $w$ by §2.2, apply that theorem at a base point where this numerator vanishes, or after a separately certified translation to a root, and remove any certified common $p^b$ factor. At a base point with nonzero numerator, the theorem only controls changes from that nonzero initial value; excluding or locating a zero then requires a separate argument. The actual marker's drift and symmetry must be checked anew. An involution preserving a normalized observable does not automatically preserve the augmented raw marker.

### 2.4. PROVED explicit five-phase atlas for the standard Somos-4 sequence

Take

$$ \tau_{n+4}\tau_n=\tau_{n+3}\tau_{n+1}+\tau_{n+2}^2,

### 3.1. The precise one-variable fact

**PROVED.** Let

$$
 f(s)=\sum_{j=1}^m a_j e^{\lambda_j s},\qquad
 \lambda_1<\cdots<\lambda_m,\quad a_j\ne0.
$$

Then $f$ is not identically zero and has at most $m-1$ real zeros counted with multiplicity. In fact it has at most the number of sign changes in $(a_1,\ldots,a_m)$.

For completeness, put $g=e^{-\lambda_1s}f$. Its derivative has $m-1$ terms, in the same coefficient-sign order as $a_2,\ldots,a_m$. The ordinary Rolle theorem, counting a zero of multiplicity $q$ as $q-1$ derivative zeros at the same point, gives $Z(g)\le Z(g')+1$. This proves the $m-1$ bound by induction and also rules out infinitely many zeros: any finite selection of more than $m-1$ would give a contradiction. For the sign refinement, if $a_1,a_2$ have opposite signs, adding $a_1$ increases sign variation by one and the preceding inequality suffices. If they have the same sign, multiply by $-1$ if needed. Then $g(s)>0$ and $g'(s)>0$ for all sufficiently negative $s$. If $g$ has a first zero $z$, the mean-value theorem gives a point with negative derivative before $z$; hence there is an additional derivative zero before the first zero of $g$. Together with the zeros between successive zeros and at multiple zeros this proves $Z(g)\le Z(g')$. This completes the induction.

If frequencies coincide, first merge their coefficients, remove zero merged coefficients, and then apply the theorem. If every coefficient disappears, the restriction is identically zero and has infinitely many zeros. That case must be recorded separately in any arithmetic certificate. Negative real recurrence roots are handled by splitting the integer index modulo two before applying this statement; zero characteristic roots require treating the finite transient separately.

### 3.2. A completely elementary multivariate counting theorem: simplex and circuit supports

Here is a useful full answer to Q2(a) on a sharply stated support class. It is also a finite certificate for positive-dimensional degeneracy, rather than a genericity assumption that hides it.

Let

$$
 M_h(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}c_{hj}e^{\langle\ell_j,t\rangle},
 \qquad h=1,\ldots,r,\quad t\in\mathbb R^r.
$$

Normalize by $e^{-\langle\ell_0,t\rangle}$, set $d_j=\ell_j-\ell_0$, and let $D$ be the matrix with rows $d_j$, $j\ge1$. Write $C'$ for the coefficient matrix with its zeroth column removed and $c_0$ for that column. Assume $\operatorname{rank}D=r$ and $\operatorname{rank}C'=r$. (If either rank is smaller, no zero can be nondegenerate, since the Jacobian factors through that matrix.)

#### 3.2.1. Simplex support

If $m=r+1$, then $D,C'$ are square and invertible. A common zero requires

$$
 z=-C'^{-1}c_0>0,\qquad Dt=\log z.
$$

There is exactly one real common zero if every coordinate of $z$ is positive, and none otherwise. Its Jacobian is $C'\operatorname{diag}(z)D$, so it is nondegenerate. This is an exact linear-algebra certificate.

#### 3.2.2. Circuit support

If $m=r+2$, the affine solution space of $C'z=-c_0$ is a line

$$
 z=p(s)=(p_1(s),\ldots,p_{r+1}(s)),\qquad p_j(s)=\alpha_j+\beta_js,\quad\beta\ne0.
$$

Its positive part is a single open interval $I=\{s:p_j(s)>0\text{ for all }j\}$, possibly empty, unbounded, or all of $\mathbb R$. Choose a nonzero vector $b\in\ker D^T$, which is one-dimensional. A positive point $p(s)$ is in the exponential image of $D$ exactly when

$$
 g(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}b_j\log p_j(s)=0. \tag{C1}
$$

The corresponding $t$ is unique: solve $Dt=\log p(s)$ by any left inverse. Thus this gives a bijection of the entire real solution set with $g^{-1}(0)\cap I$.

Differentiate:

$$
 g'(s)=\frac{P(s)}{\prod_jp_j(s)},\qquad
 P(s)=\sum_j b_j\beta_j\prod_{h\ne j}p_h(s). \tag{C2}
$$

The numerator has degree at most $r$.

**PROVED circuit bound.** If $P\not\equiv0$, there are at most $r+1$ real common zeros counted with the multiplicity of $g$. In particular, at most $r+1$ nondegenerate zeros exist. Rolle proves this directly from $\deg P\le r$. If $P\equiv0$, $g$ is constant on $I$. A single exact evaluation distinguishes no solutions from the whole curve

$$
 t(s)=D^{\dagger}\log p(s),\qquad s\in I. \tag{C3}
$$

In the latter case this parametrizes the complete positive-dimensional component; there are no extra components.

The nondegeneracy claim is also exact. At a common zero, $g'(s)=0$ means

$$
 \operatorname{diag}(p(s))^{-1}\beta\in\operatorname{im}D.
$$

Thus $Dv=\operatorname{diag}(p)^{-1}\beta$ for a nonzero $v$, and $C'\operatorname{diag}(p)Dv=C'\beta=0$. Conversely a nonzero null vector of the Jacobian gives a nonzero vector in $\ker C'=\mathbb R\beta$ and therefore $g'=0$. Multiplication of the original equations by the nonzero normalizing exponential does not change Jacobian rank at a common zero.

**Certificate format.** Record $D,C',c_0,p,b$; exact matrix identities $C'p=-c_0$, $D^Tb=0$, and rank witnesses; the positive interval $I$; and the polynomial $P$. If $P\not\equiv0$, its degree gives the count, while Sturm or explicitly certified sign/monotonicity intervals can sharpen it. If $P\equiv0$, give an exact proof of that identity and one exact evaluation of $g$. With algebraic unit bases the logarithms are exact symbols; proving identities among polynomial expressions in logarithms is a certificate obligation, not something floating point can decide. A rational interval excluding zero proves a claimed nonzero determinant or coefficient. Equalities must come from proved algebraic multiplicative identities or other exact arguments.

**Sharp two-index example, exact certificate in P12_real.py.** Put $x=e^u,y=e^v$ and impose

$$
 x+y=\tfrac32,\qquad x^2y-\tfrac{33}{50}x+\tfrac2{25}=0.
$$

There are four common support vectors $0,(1,0),(0,1),(2,1)$. Elimination gives

$$
 -x^3+\tfrac32x^2-\tfrac{33}{50}x+\tfrac2{25}
 =-(x-\tfrac15)(x-\tfrac12)(x-\tfrac45).
$$

All three roots have $y=3/2-x>0$, and all are simple. Hence the bound $r+1=3$ is attained. A degenerate example on the same support size is $y-1=xy-x=0$, whose entire real solution set in $(u,v)$ is $v=0$; its master function is $\log s+\log1-\log s=0$.

#### 3.2.3. General support: what has and has not been proved

For $m=r+k+1$ the same linear algebra gives an open convex polyhedron $\Delta\subset\mathbb R^k$ with positive affine functions $p_j$, and $k$ master functions $\psi_h=\sum_jb_{hj}\log p_j$. This reduction itself is elementary and exact. A general numerical fewnomial bound is **not** proved in this note by mentioning Khovanskii's name. The circuit proof above is the case $k=1$, where all remaining work is ordinary Rolle and a polynomial derivative.

There is nonetheless a valid general conditional Rolle certificate. On a smooth one-dimensional complete intersection $C=\{f_1=\cdots=f_{k-1}=0\}\subset\Delta$, put $J=\det D(f_1,\ldots,f_k)$. Assume the zeros under consideration are finite and no component of $C$ has $f_k\equiv0$. A compact circular component has at least as many critical points as zeros; a noncompact component has at least one fewer. Hence

$$
 \#\{f_1=\cdots=f_k=0\}
 \le \#\{f_1=\cdots=f_{k-1}=J=0\}+b(C), \tag{C4}
$$

where $b(C)$ is the number of noncompact components. Proof: parametrize each component locally by an oriented curve coordinate. The derivative of $f_k\vert_C$ vanishes exactly when $J=0$. Apply Rolle on the arcs between consecutive zeros, and on the closing arc for a circle. This proves (C4) without importing a fewnomial theorem.

Successive Jacobians clear to polynomials because $d\psi_h=\sum_jb_{hj}\,dp_j/p_j$. To obtain a **complete** finite certificate, provide the critical-point leaves, a verified bound for every boundary component $b(C)$, and a decomposition of all singular loci. Components on which the last function is identically zero must be output as components with exact parametrizations or equations and a separate integer-point obligation, never counted as isolated roots. A finite list of approximate isolated roots without these data is not a completeness certificate. The universal production and size bound for that full decomposition remains TARGET here.

Primary-source orientation, not an imported proof: Bates–Sottile, *Khovanskii–Rolle continuation for real solutions*, https://franksottile.github.io/research/pdf/Rolle.pdf; Khovanskii, ICM 1983 report, https://www.math.toronto.edu/askold/1983%20Congress%20Warsaw.pdf. The latter explicitly explains the Rolle induction for univariate exponential sums. The proof needed above has been written out.

### 3.3. Exact planar end theorem with checkable hypotheses

The phrase “fewer markers than indices, so the zero set is a curve” requires $r-s=1$ and an appropriate rank condition. In particular one marker in three indices usually gives a surface. The following theorem handles one marker in two indices, including Thomas's family. Its finite-certification assertion assumes exact representations and certified comparisons for the finitely many real constants involved; arbitrary unspecified real-number inputs do not furnish an algorithm.

Let

$$
 M(t)=\sum_{j=1}^m c_j e^{\langle\ell_j,t\rangle},\qquad t\in\mathbb R^2,
$$

where the $\ell_j$ are distinct, the $c_j$ are nonzero, and their convex hull $P$ is two-dimensional. For every edge $E$ of $P$, choose a unit outward normal $v_E$ and a unit tangent $w_E$. Write $h_E=\langle\ell_j,v_E\rangle$ for $j\in E$. Define the one-variable face sum

$$
 g_E(z)=\sum_{\ell_j\in E}c_j e^{\langle\ell_j,w_E\rangle z}.
$$

Assume every real zero of each $g_E$ is simple. This is automatic for an edge containing just its two vertices: it has one simple root if the endpoint coefficients have opposite signs, and none if they have the same sign.

**PROVED.** For an effectively certifiable $S$, the zero set $M=0$ is the union of a bounded part and the following pairwise disjoint tails

$$
 t_{E,a}(s)=s v_E+z_{E,a}(s)w_E,\qquad s\ge S,
$$

one for each real root $a$ of $g_E$. Each $z_{E,a}$ is real analytic and

$$
 |z_{E,a}(s)-a|\le C_{E,a}e^{-\delta_Es},\qquad
 \delta_E=\min_{\ell_j\notin E}(h_E-\langle\ell_j,v_E\rangle)>0. \tag{E1}
$$

Thus a two-term edge gives exactly one ray tail or none. Multiple terms on a face require the face sum; replacing them by arbitrarily chosen balancing pairs would be incorrect. The number of tails is at most $\sum_E(\#(E\cap\{\ell_j\})-1)$.

**Complete proof of end coverage.** Choose small disjoint angular neighborhoods of the finitely many outward edge normals. On the complement, the maximum of $\ell_j\cdot v$ over $\ell_j\in P$ is attained at a unique vertex, with a uniformly positive gap from all others. Compactness of that complement provides a positive lower bound for the gap. Along $t=Rv$, its term therefore dominates the sum of all other absolute values for all sufficiently large $R$, excluding zeros.

In a sufficiently small cone about $v_E$, write $t=sv_E+zw_E$, with $s>0$, $|z|\le\varepsilon s$. Choose $\varepsilon>0$ so small that every point off $E$ is lower than every vertex on $E$ by at least $\delta_Es/2$, after allowing the tangent contributions. This follows from the finite inequalities

$$
 (h_E-\ell_j\cdot v_E)s-(\ell_j-\ell_i)\cdot w_E\,z
 \ge\delta_Es/2
$$

for $j\notin E$ and $i$ an endpoint. For $z$ large positive, one endpoint term dominates every other term on $E$; for $z$ large negative, the other does. Since all endpoint tangent exponents are distinct from the remaining face exponents, a finite choice $K$ makes that endpoint exceed, say, four times the sum of the other face terms whenever $|z|\ge K$. A further finite choice of $S$ makes all off-face terms together less than one quarter of the endpoint term. Consequently every zero in this cone with $s\ge S$ has $|z|\le K$.

On this fixed strip, divide $M(sv_E+zw_E)$ by $e^{h_Es}$. The result is

$$
 g_E(z)+R_E(s,z),\qquad
 R_E=\sum_{j\notin E}c_j e^{-\delta_js+(\ell_j\cdot w_E)z},\quad\delta_j\ge\delta_E.
$$

Both $|R_E|$ and $|\partial_zR_E|$ are bounded by explicit constants times $e^{-\delta_Es}$ on $[-K,K]$. The univariate theorem gives finitely many roots of $g_E$. Around each root $a$, choose a closed interval with disjoint interior on which $|g_E'|\ge\eta_a>0$. On the complement of these intervals in $[-K,K]$, $|g_E|$ has a positive minimum. Choose $S$ large enough that the residual is smaller than half this minimum, its derivative smaller than half each $\eta_a$, and its endpoint values smaller than half the corresponding nonzero face values. The intermediate-value theorem and strict monotonicity now give exactly one zero $z_{E,a}(s)$ in each root interval and no others. The real analytic implicit-function argument gives analyticity, and the mean-value theorem gives

$$
 |z_{E,a}(s)-a|\le 2\sup_{|z|\le K}|R_E(s,z)|/\eta_a.
$$

**Explicit certificate for the cutoff.** Supply a rational $K$, disjoint rational root intervals $I_a\subset[-K,K]$, opposite signs of $g_E$ at their endpoints, lower bounds $|g_E'|\ge\eta_a>0$ on each $I_a$, and $|g_E|\ge b_E>0$ on the complement of their interiors. These are hypotheses on the supplied face-root enclosures; the theorem does not assume that floating-point roots certify them. Put

$$
 B_0=\sum_{j\notin E}|c_j|e^{|\ell_j\cdot w_E|K},\qquad
 B_1=\sum_{j\notin E}|c_j|\,|\ell_j\cdot w_E|e^{|\ell_j\cdot w_E|K}.
$$

Let $m_E>0$ be the smaller of $b_E$ and all certified nonzero endpoint magnitudes. After meeting the previously stated finite cone-dominance inequalities, it suffices to choose $S$ so that

$$
 B_0e^{-\delta_ES}<m_E/2,\qquad
 B_1e^{-\delta_ES}<\tfrac12\min_a\eta_a.
$$

A displayed rational upper bound on each logarithmic cutoff, or simply a certified integer $S$ satisfying these inequalities, is finite data. When there are no face roots only the first inequality is needed. One may take $C_{E,a}=2B_0/\eta_a$ in (E1).

This is (E1). The cones are disjoint outside a box, so these are all tails. Every estimate uses only finitely many strict inequalities and one-variable root/derivative certificates. The bounded remainder can be enclosed in an explicitly certified box. The zero set inside that box need not be resolved into real components for the integer problem: its integer points form a finite list to enumerate exactly. The resulting box together with the tails is an exhaustive cover; overlaps at the truncation boundaries are harmless. For logarithms of positive algebraic numbers, rational root intervals and the identity

$$
 \log x=2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{q^{2j+1}}{2j+1}+R_N,
 \quad q=\frac{x-1}{x+1},\quad
 |R_N|\le\frac{2|q|^{2N+1}}{(2N+1)(1-q^2)}
$$

give exact rational interval certificates for strict inequalities (using monotonicity at the rational interval endpoints). Exact equalities among exponent vectors still need algebraic or symbolic proofs.

For a three-term sum whose exponent vectors are affinely independent, more is true. If all three coefficients have the same sign, there are no zeros. Otherwise normalize by the term with the exceptional sign and make the invertible affine-linear change in $t$ given by the two exponent differences. The equation becomes

$$
 A e^x+B e^y=1,\quad A,B>0,
$$

whose complete curve is $x<-\log A$, $y=\log((1-Ae^x)/B)$. It has exactly two tails, both with exponential convergence to their asymptotes, and no hidden compact component.

### 3.4. Rational asymptotes force exact lattice lines

**PROVED rational-asymptote theorem, in any dimension.** Let a tail have a parametrization

$$
 x(s)=b+sv+\epsilon(s),\quad s\ge s_0,\quad
 \|\epsilon(s)\|\le Ce^{-\kappa s},\quad C,\kappa>0,
$$

where the direction $\mathbb Rv$ is rational. Rescale $s$ so that $v$ is a primitive integer vector. The Euclidean algorithm gives a unimodular $U\in\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb Z)$ with $Uv=e_1$. Let $Q$ consist of its last $r-1$ rows. Then $Q\mathbb Z^r=\mathbb Z^{r-1}$ and

$$
 Qx(s)=Qb+O(e^{-\kappa s}).
$$

If $Qb\notin\mathbb Z^{r-1}$, its distance $\delta>0$ from that lattice is positive, so there are no lattice points once $\|Q\|Ce^{-\kappa s}<\delta$. If $Qb\in\mathbb Z^{r-1}$, once the error is less than $1/2$, every lattice point on the tail satisfies $Qx=Qb$ exactly. All such points lie on the one affine lattice line $a+\mathbb Zv$, with $a\in\mathbb Z^r$ chosen so $Qa=Qb$. This also proves the user's weaker “finitely many lattice lines” claim: the preceding compact portion has only finitely many integer points, each lying on its own translate of $\mathbb Rv$.

Restrict every marker to this line:

$$
 M_h(a+nv)=\sum_j c_{hj}e^{\ell_j\cdot a}\left(e^{\ell_j\cdot v}\right)^n.
$$

Merge equal frequencies. Any nonzero restriction supplies the univariate sign-variation budget for the common zeros. If all restrictions vanish identically, the entire lattice line satisfies the markers; this is a genuine positive-dimensional arithmetic leaf, not an excluded leaf. Mere convergence to the asymptote, at any effective rate tending to zero, would already force the eventual exact line; exponential convergence supplies an explicit convenient cutoff.

The same proof applies to a fixed parity class or any finite-index affine lattice: replace $\mathbb Z^r$ by that lattice before choosing the unimodular coordinates.

#### 3.4.1. The exact irrational obligation

In the plane an irrational asymptote can be written $y=\alpha x+\beta$, $\alpha\notin\mathbb Q$. A tail estimate gives

$$
 |n-\alpha m-\beta|\le C e^{-\kappa H(m,n)} \tag{D1}
$$

for its integer points in its specified cone. A completing arithmetic certificate must prove an effective eventual lower bound excluding (D1), and separately handle exact equality. Equivalently, in the exponent-coordinate form for a two-term edge it must exclude

$$
 0<|\langle\ell_i-\ell_j,t\rangle-\log|-c_j/c_i||
 \le C e^{-\kappa H(t)}. \tag{D2}
$$

For higher-dimensional one-dimensional tails, replace this by exponentially small distance of $Qt-Qb$ from zero for a real rank-$(r-1)$ map $Q$ with kernel $\mathbb Rv$. Irrational direction alone supplies no adequate quantitative lower bound: arbitrary irrational slopes can have extremely strong rational approximation. In the arithmetic application the left side is a linear form in logarithms of specified algebraic numbers. An elementary conjugate norm sometimes gives an exponential lower bound, but it closes the tail only if its exponent beats the error exponent $\kappa$. When it does not, this is precisely the cancellation wall; real zero counting has not removed it.

### 3.5. Thomas's family: every actual end is irrational

Use the precise P03 §8 notation

$$

$$

Their column determinant on the first two rows is $L^2-LM+M^2>0$; their sum is zero, so the three row points are affinely independent. Lagrange interpolation gives the exact coefficient of $\theta^2$ marker

$$

The signs of $f_a'(\theta_i)$ are $+,-,+$ in this labeling. Consequently:

| parity $(A,B)$ | coefficient signs | ends, indexed by balancing embedding pair |
|---|---|---|
| $(0,0)$ | $+,-,+$ | $01, 12$ |
| $(0,1)$ | $+,-,-$ | $01, 02$ |
| $(1,0)$ | $+,+,-$ | $02, 12$ |
| $(1,1)$ | $+,+,+$ | no real zeros |

$$

with its outward ray chosen so the two terms dominate the third.

**PROVED irrationality of every listed direction.** If a nonzero rational direction existed, clear denominators to obtain $(h,k)\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0}$ with $(\ell_i-\ell_j)\cdot(h,k)=0$. This uses no logarithmic-form theorem or reciprocity.

Thus the rational-tail mechanism closes **none** of the Thomas tails. It converts each into one exact inhomogeneous exponential approximation obligation. This is a curve in three indices. A single marker in all three indices would instead be a surface, and a classification by finitely many rays would not apply. Full arithmetic unit coverage remains a separate, unproved input here.

If $|u|+|w|\to\infty$ while the homogeneous product stays $\eta$, divide by $\max(|u|,|w|)^4$; some factor must tend to zero. $$
 r\prod_{i\ne k}(\beta_k-\beta_i+r/u^4)=\eta.
$$

Near $u^{-4}=0$, the derivative in $r$ is $D_k\ne0$, so elementary analytic implicit inversion gives exactly one branch,

$$

$$

To justify that this local branch exhausts that slope neighborhood, if $|\delta/u|$ is small, all three other factors have absolute size between fixed positive multiples of $|u|$. Among those three there is a pair whose indices differ by an odd number; squaring makes them equal, so $u^2$ is fixed by a generator of the cyclic order-four group and is rational. The unit property forces $u^2=1$. Independence of (K1) forces the index vector to be zero, contradiction.

Pair balance alone does not certify an actual end. These rational directions are **not** rational ends of the actual two-marker Thue curve, which has the triple-balance directions (K3).

### 4.1. PROVED: an integral representation of the same Padé problem cannot change its primitive pair

Let K be a field of characteristic zero, let M(x)=sum(v_n x^n), and suppose Delta_N=det(v_(i+j))_(0<=i,j<N) is nonzero. There is a unique pair

    Q(0)=1, deg Q<=N, deg P<N,
    Q(x)M(x)-P(x)=O(x^(2N)).

Indeed, writing q(t)=t^N Q(1/t), the coefficients at x^N,...,x^(2N-1) are exactly

    sum_(j=0)^N q_j v_(j+r)=0, 0<=r<N,

with q_N=1. The nonsingular moment matrix gives a unique q_0,...,q_(N-1). The coefficients of P are then the coefficients below x^N of QM. This proves existence and uniqueness by ordinary linear algebra.

Consequently, a Hermite or Euler integral construction that has these same degrees, normalization and order conditions produces precisely the same pair, irrespective of how the integral is evaluated. A fixed rational target transformation (A+B P/Q)/C also produces the same pair up to a common scalar. At a rational Gaussian specialization its primitive Gaussian integer pair is unique up to a unit: after clearing denominators, divide the two coordinates by their Gaussian gcd. Thus this representation change cannot improve the exact arithmetic multiplier a_N of P08/P09. It can reveal a previously unrecognized common divisor and thereby help *prove* a content theorem, but it cannot create new content in an already primitive pair.

In the four P08 transported systems the required nonsingularity is proved by their coercivity argument, including the two quadratically repaired weights. This result therefore applies to each of those four systems separately. It does not apply to a changed Padé type, additional independent remainder conditions, a new approximation function, or a different arithmetic lattice.

There is no mathematically defined class called “all integral-type constructions” in the question. Even an arbitrary polynomial can be represented as an integral of a constant integrand. A universal no-go theorem requires an explicit class of kernels, allowed coefficient denominators, degree/index schedules, and error conditions. No such universal no-go is asserted here.

### 4.2. PROVED: sparse-index packets incur a spacing cost

The following theorem is useful independently of the Fibonacci calculation. Let beta be real, Q,E>1, C_Q,C_E>0, A,D>=0, and suppose an infinite strictly increasing sequence of positive integers N_j satisfies

    N_(j+1)<=rho N_j+A, rho>=1.

At each N_j assume there are two integer pairs (p_(j,0),q_(j,0)) and (p_(j,1),q_(j,1)), at indices n_(j,s) in [N_j,N_j+D], satisfying

    |q_(j,s)|<=C_Q Q^(n_(j,s)),
    |q_(j,s) beta-p_(j,s)|<=C_E E^(-n_(j,s)),
    p_(j,0)q_(j,1)-p_(j,1)q_(j,0) != 0.

Then for every integer q>=1 with 2 C_E q>E^(N_0), and every integer p,

    |beta-p/q| >= c q^(-mu),
    mu=1+rho log(Q)/log(E),
    c=[2 C_Q Q^(A+D)(2 C_E)^(rho log(Q)/log(E))]^(-1).

Proof. Choose j minimal with N_j>=log_E(2 C_E q); the displayed condition ensures j>0. Then N_j<rho log_E(2 C_E q)+A. The two pair determinants ensure that for at least one s, pq_(j,s)-qp_(j,s) is a nonzero integer. Its absolute value is at least one. On the other hand,

    1 <= |q_(j,s)| q |beta-p/q|+q C_E E^(-n_(j,s))
      <= C_Q Q^(N_j+D) q |beta-p/q|+1/2.

Rearrangement gives the stated constant and exponent. No irrationality theorem is used.

For the P08 rates put

    L=Lambda_+=(11+5sqrt(5))/2, Q=gamma L, E=L/gamma.

Provided Q,E>1, the sufficient strict quartic condition is

    mu<4 iff gamma<L^((3-rho)/(3+rho)).

Indeed, log E=log L-log gamma>0, so this is exactly

    rho(log gamma+log L)<3(log L-log gamma),
    (rho+3)log gamma<(3-rho)log L.

The initial `1+` in mu is retained: it arises when the lower bound for |q beta-p| is divided by q. This is the rational-approximation exponent, not the linear-form exponent.

For all consecutive degrees rho=1 this is gamma<sqrt(L), the existing approximately 3.330 threshold. For dyadic packets at N_j=2^j, rho=2 and it becomes

    gamma<L^(1/5)=(1+sqrt(5))/2.

The last identity follows by expanding the fifth power of phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2 with phi^2=phi+1, which gives phi^5=(11+5sqrt(5))/2. Thus a content gain at powers of two is not automatically usable at the old threshold.

The packet hypothesis is substantive. A favorable bound at N=2^j alone gives no favorable bound at N+1 and does not supply a nearby nonzero determinant. If only isolated sparse pairs and their consecutive-*sparse* determinants are known, the same proof with N_j,N_(j+1) gives the weaker sufficient exponent

    mu=1+rho^2 log(Q)/log(E).

This is a sufficient bound from that argument, not an impossibility theorem or a claim that sharper exploitation of the arithmetic cannot improve it. At rho=2 and gamma>=1 it is at least five and therefore does not give the quartic gain. A sparse construction must state its partner and spacing certificate along with its content estimate. The classical use of sparse indices merely to prove irrationality does not automatically prove an irrationality exponent below four.

Since that same Bezout identity proves (p_N,q_N) is primitive even over Z[i], every Gaussian gcd eta_N of the raw coordinates is a Gaussian unit times H k_N z. Therefore

    H k_N/|eta_N|=1/|z|=q_N/|d_N|,

exactly P08's multiplier, independently of H,k_N and the gcd associate. There is no hidden square-root or associate cost. When q_N=0 the argument uses p_N=1 and z=b_N. This proof also explains why merely changing a denominator-clearing convention cannot improve this multiplier.

The standard three-term recurrence is an exact way to compute r_N; its validity is elementary. Multiplication by z is symmetric for the moment bilinear form. Orthogonality shows z r_N-r_(N+1) is orthogonal to r_0,...,r_(N-2), leaving only r_N and r_(N-1). Their coefficients are <z r_N,r_N>/h_N and h_N/h_(N-1). Consequently the recurrence uses rational arithmetic only. The second-kind identity is

    r_N a_(N-2)-r_(N-1)a_(N-1)=-h_(N-1).

It follows either by applying the three-term recurrence to both polynomial sequences, starting at N=1, or by expanding their defining integrals. Multiplying by the two exact normalization factors gives the target determinant

    b_N d_(N-1)-b_(N-1)d_N
       =U^(2ell)2^(-(2N-1)) B F C h_(N-1),

which is nonzero. This is the same determinant as P08 T10 in centered coordinates, with every scalar retained.

### 4.4. Exact finite results, and what they do not prove

It verifies orthogonality, nonzero norm with its expected sign, the full second-kind polynomial determinant, the exact target determinant, rationality, primitiveness, and the squared multiplier. For N<=4 it independently reconstructs the original t-polynomials and their original Gaussian moments and checks both specialized coordinates. `--json` emits the complete exact integer pairs and rational a_N^2; floating-point logarithms are labelled diagnostic only.

    C_0>2^1271, C_1>2^962, C_2>2^989, C_3>2^1402.

**Catalogue/evaluation:** this closes neither the Fibonacci multiplier gap nor S-003. It supplies a small exact arithmetic compiler and closes a logical gap in proposals that transfer the old approximation threshold unchanged to sparse indices.

**Next finite certificates:** (1) for any proposed integral formula, verify its exact order conditions and whether uniqueness identifies it with the existing pair; (2) compute Smith/cofactor content for the centered rational Hankel matrices, with every prime dividing the output included; (3) for a sparse content conjecture, compute the partner determinants and index gaps, not just the selected gcd; (4) if a genuinely different multiple-orthogonality construction is proposed, verify its coefficient denominators and both its target transport and two-index determinant before evaluating its apparent gamma. None of these finite tests substitutes for the required all-index content proof.

## 5. Programme consequences and the next finite certificates

### 5.1. What is closed, and what the evaluations gain

**A7 — closed for certified finite blocks and the explicit Somos-4 test.** A zero observation no longer forces division by zero. The polynomial suspension stores an anchor that actually determines the orbit and a scale that preserves the original arithmetic marker. Its transition polynomials are uniform in $N$, so finite-precision passage and $p$-adic lifting are compatible. The standard Somos-4 test provides a complete raw-value progression, and P07 now has a literal polynomial raw continuation at $7$.

**A7 — still open in its universal form.** The missing theorem is existence and coverage of regular independent scalar coordinates for the desired higher-genus orbits. The inner-frame theorem does not produce those coordinates, and a redundant scalar continuation map with singular ambient Jacobian does not qualify as a return chart. P07's already completed rational-point evaluation remains proved by its projective chart; this answer advances its scalar-continuation layer without claiming a new proof of its scalar lifting layer.

**A9 — closed for simplex/circuit support and rational tails of the proved planar class.** Real zero counting now has a fully elementary multi-index instance with a sharp bound and an exact degenerate component. The rational-tail theorem is stronger than a finite collection of approximate lattice lines: every sufficiently large lattice point must lie on the exact asymptote. The branch classifications show that this shortcut closes none of the actual Thomas or S-003 tails. Those evaluations still need the inhomogeneous exponential lower bound stated in §3.

**Fibonacci multiplier — remains open.** The exact integral identities and normalizations make the arithmetic target more explicit. The finite growth in §4 cannot refute an estimate with an unrestricted constant. The sparse theorem does refute transferring the old numerical threshold to dyadic indices without a spacing calculation and partner determinant.
