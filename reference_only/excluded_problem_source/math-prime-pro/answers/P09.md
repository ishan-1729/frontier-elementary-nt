## Part I. Arithmetic of the coupled Fibonacci Padé construction

### 0. The exact rate thresholds


Retain the monic Padé normalization from P08. For a fixed branch and a fixed presentation U=A+iB, A>0 and B!=0, write

    R=sqrt(A^2+B^2),
    Lambda_+=(R+A)/2, Lambda_-=(R-A)/2.

The all-index analytic estimates proved in P08 have the form

    |q_N| <= C_Q a_N Lambda_+^N,
    |q_N beta-p_N| <= C_E a_N Lambda_-^N,

with explicit positive constants C_Q,C_E independent of N and a nonzero consecutive determinant. Here (p_N,q_N) is the primitive integral pair; a_N is its arithmetic multiplier relative to the specified monic pair.

If one proves a_N<=C gamma^N, then put

    Q=gamma Lambda_+, E=1/(gamma Lambda_-).

Under Q>1 and E>1, the determinant lemma gives a rational-approximation exponent

    mu=1+log(Q)/log(E).

For a quartic Thue equation the exponent-comparison height argument gains a strict power when mu<4. The borderline mu=4 would need a separate comparison of the actual constants; it is not ruled out abstractly. In the present presentations |B|=2 and therefore Lambda_+ Lambda_-=1. Elementary algebra gives the exact strict-gain condition

    mu<4  iff  gamma<sqrt(Lambda_+).

Thus the thresholds are indeed

    U=1+2i:  gamma<sqrt((1+sqrt5)/2),
    U=11-2i: gamma<sqrt((11+5sqrt5)/2).

For the latter presentation this must hold for the correctly transported four branches, with their actual coefficient fields and normalization factors. It is not enough to prove it for an untransported positive weight at the same numerical U. P08's phase factors remain part of the data. Also a uniform bound with gamma<1 is impossible here: the analytic bounds would make the nonzero integer consecutive determinant O(gamma^(2N-1)), tending to zero. Thus every possible geometric bound of this form has gamma>=1.

PROVED limitation. Positivity or coercivity of every Hankel matrix proves normality and the nonzero determinant. It does not bound the heights of its rational solutions after clearing denominators. In particular it cannot, by itself, justify a geometric bound for a_N. The following arithmetic section proves exactly what extra content estimate is required.

### 1. Exact scope

PROVED below: the original positive moment system has integer Hankel determinants growing like 2^(N^2), and their odd parts have that growth on an explicit infinite subsequence. Symmetry reduces the relevant coefficient denominator determinant to size about 2^(N^2/2). The primitive arithmetic multiplier has an exact expression in terms of that determinant and one completely specified integer content. There is also an unconditional quadratic-exponential upper bound.

UNPROVED: a subquadratic upper bound for that content, the true growth of the primitive multiplier a_N, or the useful geometric bound demanded in P08. Large raw Hankel determinants do not alone prove that a_N grows rapidly. The specialization content must be controlled, and the distinction cannot be omitted.

The computations in the certificate are diagnostic and check the exact identities. No asymptotic assertion about a_N is inferred from their finite range.

### 2. An integer algebraic moment series

Let m_n be the positive moments in P08 II.2:

    m_n = sum_(alpha=1/4,1/2,3/4)
              c_alpha alpha(1-alpha)(2-alpha)_n/(n+2)!,
    (c_(1/4),c_(1/2),c_(3/4))=(4,6,4).

Set

    c_n=2^(3n+2)m_n,
    C(X)=sum_(n>=0)c_n X^n=4M(8X).

PROVED: every c_n is an integer. An exact useful presentation is

    v=(1-(1-8X)^(1/4))/2,
    X=v-3v^2+4v^3-2v^4,
    C(X)=(6v^2-12v^3+7v^4)/X^2.                    (A1)

To prove integrality, solve

    v=X+3v^2-4v^3+2v^4

recursively in Z[[X]], with v(0)=0. The coefficient of X^n for n>=2 on the right depends only on previously computed coefficients. The unique solution therefore has integer coefficients. Since v is divisible by X, (A1) proves C(X) is integral. This avoids a denominator estimate for generalized binomial coefficients.

For completeness, the identity follows by putting z=(1-8X)^(1/4)=1-2v in

    M(x)=(7z^2+10z+7)/[(1+z)^2(1+z^2)^2],
    z=(1-x)^(1/4).

The relation X=v(1-v)(1-2v+2v^2) gives the displayed numerator in (A1).

The initial integers are

    6,24,121,684,4142,26264,172125,1156396,7920858,... .

### 3. A completely explicit 2-adic determinant subsequence

Reduce (A1) modulo 2. Then

    v=X+v^2,
    v=sum_(j>=0)X^(2^j) in F_2[[X]],
    C(X)=v^4/X^2=sum_(j>=2)X^(2^j-2).              (A2)

Thus

    c_n is odd iff n=2^j-2 for some j>=2.           (A3)

Define the integer Hankel determinant

    D_N=det[c_(i+j)]_(0<=i,j<N), D_0=1.

PROVED. If N=2^r-1 or N=2^r, with r>=2, then D_N is odd.

Proof for N=2^r-1. The largest index i+j in this matrix is 2^(r+1)-4. By (A3), the largest index carrying an odd moment is 2^r-2=N-1. Every antidiagonal entry i+j=N-1 is one modulo2, and every entry strictly beyond that antidiagonal is zero. Reversing the columns makes a triangular matrix with diagonal entries one. Hence the determinant is one modulo2.

For N=2^r, the last row and column have only their bottom-right entry nonzero modulo2, since their off-corner indices lie between 2^r-1 and 2^(r+1)-3. The corner index is 2^(r+1)-2 and its entry is one. Removing that row and column leaves the preceding size 2^r-1 matrix. This proves the second case.

This is an infinite exact congruence theorem, not a numerical pattern extrapolation.

In fact the parity can be determined at every index. Let Cat_j=binom(2j,j)/(j+1). Their generating series satisfies Ccat=1+X Ccat^2, so in characteristic two Cat_j is odd exactly when j=2^e-1, including j=0. Consequently the centered even moments in Section 5 satisfy

    r_(2j) = Cat_j - delta_(j,0) modulo2.

The elementary Catalan Hankel identities are

    det[Cat_(i+j)]_(i,j<m)=1,
    det[Cat_(i+j+1)]_(i,j<m)=1,
    det[Cat_(i+j+2)]_(i,j<m)=m+1.                   (A3a)

Here is a direct proof to make the ingredient explicit. Put

    L_(i,j)=binom(2i,i-j)-binom(2i,i-j-1),
    B_(i,j)=binom(2i+1,i-j)-binom(2i+1,i-j-1),

with zero entries for j>i or negative binomial lower indices. The reflection principle counts nonnegative paths with steps +1,-1 and proves the Gram factorizations [Cat_(i+j)]=LL^t and [Cat_(i+j+1)]=BB^t: cut a Dyck path after respectively 2i or 2i+1 steps. Both finite triangular factors have diagonal one, proving the first two identities. To prove the third, take rows 1,...,m and columns 0,...,m of L. Its last m columns have determinant one, and its kernel is spanned by (1,-1,...,(-1)^m), since the binomial formula gives sum_j(-1)^j L_(i,j)=0 for i>=1. Its m+1 maximal minors therefore all have absolute value one. Cauchy--Binet gives the third determinant as m+1.

Subtracting one only from the upper-left entry of the first Catalan Hankel matrix now gives

    E_m = 1-m modulo2,   O_m = 1 modulo2.

Using the factorization (A5), this strengthens the preceding subsequence to

    D_N is odd iff N=0 or 3 modulo4.                (A3b)

### 4. Quadratic-exponential growth of the raw Hankel numerator

Let h_n be the squared monic orthogonal-polynomial norm for the positive measure mu in P08. Its mass is 3/2, and

    dmu(t)>=(6/pi)sqrt(t(1-t))dt.

The shifted Chebyshev polynomial U_n(2t-1)/4^n is monic and has squared norm (pi/8)16^(-n) for sqrt(t(1-t))dt. The minimizing property of monic orthogonal polynomials therefore gives the lower bound

    h_n >= (3/4)16^(-n).

The monic shifted Chebyshev T polynomial has supremum at most 2*4^(-n). Using it as a competitor gives

    h_n <= 6*16^(-n).

These inequalities also hold at n=0. Since

    Delta_N=det[m_(i+j)]=product_(n=0)^(N-1)h_n,
    D_N=2^(3N^2-N) Delta_N,

we obtain, for every N>=1,

    2^(N^2)(3/2)^N <= D_N <= 2^(N^2)12^N.          (A4)

In particular log(D_N)/N^2 tends to log2. For N=2^r or 2^r-1, Section 3 says that this entire determinant is odd. Consequently the reduced rational Hankel determinant Delta_N has denominator exactly 2^(3N^2-N) and odd numerator D_N on those indices. That odd numerator has rigorous quadratic-exponential growth.

PROVED obstruction to one proposed shortcut: clearing only the dyadic denominators of the moments does not leave uniformly bounded or merely geometric Hankel numerators. A prospective denominator-savings proof must address these growing odd integers.

This is not yet an obstruction for primitive specialized approximants. There can be common cofactors and specialization content, and those have not been bounded by (A4).

### 5. Symmetry gives the correct coefficient-denominator scale

Push 4dmu forward by z=8t-4, and define its integer moments

    r_n=4 integral (8t-4)^n dmu(t)
       =sum_(j=0)^n binom(n,j)(-4)^(n-j)c_j.

Since mu is symmetric about 1/2, r_n=0 for odd n. The first even moments are

    r_0=6, r_2=25, r_4=206, r_6=2109, r_8=24090.

There is also a short exact algebraic generating function for these moments. With all square roots expanded from their value 1 at s=0, set R=sqrt(1-16s^2). Then

    sum_(n>=0) r_(2n) s^(2n)
       =[7-3R-2sqrt(2R(1+R))]/(8s^2).              (A5a)

The apparent pole is removable. To derive it, translation of the moment series gives

    sum r_n s^n=C(s/(1+4s))/(1+4s).

Write a=(1+4s)^(1/4), b=(1-4s)^(1/4). Substitution of (A1), or directly of x^2M(x)=14-7x-4z-6z^2-4z^3, reduces the right side to

    [7-2a^3b-3a^2b^2-2ab^3]/(8s^2).

Now a^2b^2=R, and ab(a^2+b^2)=sqrt(2R(1+R)), which proves (A5a). This identifies the exact parity-reduced moment series: it combines an ordinary square root and a nested fourth-root term. The formula does not itself give a product evaluation of its Hankel determinants.

Put

    E_m=det[r_(2i+2j)]_(0<=i,j<m),
    O_m=det[r_(2i+2j+2)]_(0<=i,j<m),
    E_0=O_0=1.

Integer translation preserves Hankel determinants, and parity gives

    D_(2m)=E_m O_m,
    D_(2m+1)=E_(m+1) O_m.                            (A5)

Let p_N(z) be the monic orthogonal polynomial for the pushed-forward measure. It has parity N. Hence

    p_(2m)(z)=S_m(z^2),
    p_(2m+1)(z)=z T_m(z^2).

Cramer's rule applied to the defining equations shows that E_m clears every coefficient of S_m, and O_m clears every coefficient of T_m. Define

    L_N=E_(N/2) if N is even,
    L_N=O_((N-1)/2) if N is odd.                     (A6)

This is a specified positive integer. The monic polynomial for mu is

    q_N(t)=8^(-N)p_N(8t-4).

Consequently the coefficient of X^k in Q_N(X)=X^N q_N(1/X) has denominator dividing L_N 8^k. Since m_j=c_j/(4*8^j), the coefficient of X^k in P_N has denominator dividing 4L_N8^k for 0<=k<N. Thus the least coefficient-clearing integer k_N of P08 satisfies the exact divisibility

    k_N divides 8^N L_N.                            (A7)

The centered monic norm is

    hhat_n=4*8^(2n)h_n,
    3*4^n <= hhat_n <=24*4^n.

Since E_m is the product of the even-index hhat norms, and O_m the product of the odd-index norms,

    3^m4^(m(m-1)) <= E_m <=24^m4^(m(m-1)),
    3^m4^(m^2) <= O_m <=24^m4^(m^2).                 (A8)

It follows that

    log(L_N)=(log2)N^2/2+O(N),

with the explicit two-sided constants in (A8). For N=2^r, r>=2, both factors in D_N=E_(N/2)O_(N/2) are odd; in particular L_N is odd. This is the determinant at the correct half-dimensional scale for the original symmetric Padé sequence.

### 6. The exact primitive-content problem

Use precisely P08's specialization at U=1+2i, with k_N the least coefficient denominator and eta_N the Gaussian gcd of its raw target pair. Since that pair is purely imaginary, |eta_N| is a positive ordinary integer. Put

    g_N^coef=8^N L_N/k_N in Z_(>0),
    G_N=g_N^coef |eta_N| in Z_(>0).

Then the arithmetic multiplier in the question is EXACTLY

    a_N=8^N L_N/G_N.                                (A9)

Thus the requested useful geometric bound a_N<=C gamma^N is equivalent to

    G_N >= C^(-1)L_N(8/gamma)^N.                    (A10)

Because L_N has size 2^(N^2/2+O(N)), a useful bound requires quadratic-exponential cancellation in the combined coefficient and specialization content. A denominator saving that is only exponential in N relative to this determinant normalization is insufficient.

Conversely, the following implication is PROVED:

    if log G_N=o(N^2), then
    lim_(N->infinity) log(a_N)/N^2=(log2)/2.          (A11)

In particular a bound G_N<=exp(CN log(N+2)) would prove that this original coupled sequence has no geometric multiplier bound at any fixed base gamma. This would be a precise obstruction, stronger than merely missing the threshold sqrt((1+sqrt5)/2).

But the hypothesis in (A11) is UNPROVED here. It is the cofactor/content theorem still needed. Neither a finite prime sieve nor the parity theorem above supplies it.

There is a stronger exact 2-adic assertion. For every positive N divisible by 4,

    v_2(k_N)=3N,  v_2(|eta_N|)=2N+2,
    v_2(a_N)=N-2.                                  (A11a)

Proof. Write N=2m with m even. By (A3a), L_N=E_m and O_m are odd. Cramer's rule for the constant term of S_m gives E_m S_m(0)=(-1)^m O_m, which is odd. Thus p_N(-4)=S_m(16) is a 2-adic unit with odd denominator. The constant coefficient of q_N is p_N(-4)/8^N. Together with (A7), this proves v_2(k_N)=3N.

At U=1+2i and delta=4i, homogenization gives the exact identity

    U^N Q_N(delta/U)=(i/2)^N p_N(-2i).

Here p_N(-2i)=S_m(-4) is again a 2-adic unit. Since N is divisible by 4, this homogeneous value is real and has valuation -N. The raw d_N=k_N delta U^N Q_N(delta/U) therefore has valuation 2N+2 after removing i. The first summand of b_N is 20 i k_N U^N Q_N(delta/U), since 20U-10delta=20, and has the same valuation. For the remaining term, the coefficient of X^j in P_N has valuation at least -3j-2, so U^(N-1)P_N(delta/U) has valuation at least -N-1. Multiplication by k_N delta^2 raises this to at least 2N+3. Therefore b_N also has valuation exactly 2N+2. The common gcd has that valuation, proving (A11a).

This still is not an archimedean obstruction. The rational number a_N need not be an integer: exact arithmetic gives denominator 5 at N=11,12,33,34 and denominator 3 at N=17. A large positive v_2(a_N) alone is not a real lower bound. If one could prove that the odd denominator of a_N is exp(o(N)) along N=0 modulo4, then (A11a) would force liminf a_N^(1/N)>=2 along that subsequence, excluding the original threshold sqrt((1+sqrt5)/2). That odd-denominator statement is UNPROVED. It would be a weaker sufficient arithmetic obstruction than the full cofactor estimate in (A11).

The denominator in that weaker target has a small, explicit parity-reduced description. Let nu be the pushforward of the centered measure by z to z^2, so its moments are s_j=r_(2j). Define the second-kind polynomial

    A_(m-1)(z)=integral [S_m(z)-S_m(t)]/(z-t) dnu(t).

Both E_m S_m and E_m A_(m-1) have integer coefficients. A change of variables in the Stieltjes transform gives exactly

    beta_N=5-4 A_(m-1)(-4)/S_m(-4), N=2m,

and hence

    a_(2m)=2^(2m-2)E_m /
       gcd(E_m S_m(-4),4E_m A_(m-1)(-4)).           (A11b)

For even m, E_m S_m(-4) is odd, so the factor 4 may be removed from the gcd. Writing g_m=gcd(E_m S_m(-4),E_m A_(m-1)(-4)), the reduced denominator is

    b_(2m)=g_m/gcd(g_m,E_m),  m even.

The consecutive second-kind determinant is

    S_m A_(m-2)-S_(m-1) A_(m-1)=-E_m/E_(m-1).

After multiplying by E_m E_(m-1), every entry is integral and the determinant is -E_m^2. Therefore g_m divides E_m^2 and b_(2m) divides E_m. This exact divisibility is still too weak: E_m itself has quadratic-exponential size. Nor is the reduced denominator supported only at the first few exceptional primes: exact parity-reduced computation gives b_80=b_88=7, in addition to b_12=b_56=5. Any claimed bounded-prime support must be proved and cannot use the initial small sample.

In this simpler normalization, the requested bound a_N<=C gamma^N, restricted to N divisible by 4, is exactly equivalent to

    g_(N/2)/E_(N/2) >= (2/gamma)^N/(4C).            (A11c)

Thus even an upper bound g_(N/2)/E_(N/2)<=exp(o(N)) would exclude every gamma<2 on this subsequence. No such bound has been proved here. It is not a consequence of positivity alone: at degree one a positive moment system with s_0=6,s_1=12 gives E_1=6, E_1S_1(-4)=-36 and E_1A_0(-4)=36, hence g_1=36>E_1. Such first moments can occur in a nondegenerate positive measure with integer moments at every order (take six times the shifted Catalan probability measure). Any effective bound for (A11c) must use more of the specific algebraic series (A5a).

For completeness, the transform identity used above is elementary. Put F(z)=integral d(4mu)(t)/(z-(8t-4)). Then xM(x)=2F(8/x-4). At x=4i/(1+2i), the argument is -2i. Evenness gives F(z)=z integral dnu(s)/(z^2-s), and beta=i[(5/2)z-2F(z)] becomes beta=5-4 integral dnu(s)/(-4-s). Replacing the last Stieltjes function by A/S gives the stated approximant. The scale of its homogeneous pair is i(-1)^m 2^(2-N)k_N/E_m, which proves (A11b).

An unconditional upper bound follows immediately from G_N>=1 and (A8):

    a_N <= 2^(N^2/2)(8sqrt(6))^N for N>=1.           (A12)

For N=2m this is exactly 8^(2m)24^m4^(m(m-1)); the odd case is smaller than the same displayed bound. This is a proved quadratic-exponential upper bound, not the claimed true asymptotic of a_N.

### 7. Why a scalar renormalization cannot change this question

PROVED. At a fixed index, replace both raw approximant coordinates by a common nonzero rational or Gaussian factor and then take the primitive Gaussian pair. The resulting primitive pair is unchanged up to a Gaussian unit. Therefore the multiplier governing its size and linear error relative to the fixed monic Padé pair is unchanged.

Indeed, the intersection of the one-dimensional Q(i)-space spanned by the raw pair with Z[i]^2 has a primitive generator unique up to units. Equivalently, clear the numerator and denominator of the common factor and cancel the resulting common Gaussian gcd. The same primitive generator remains.

Changing the normalization may expose a previously overlooked common divisor, but cannot improve beyond the exact gcd already present in a_N. A different Padé index/type, an additional independent remainder condition, or a different approximation function is a new construction; it should not be described as a harmless normalization of this one.

### 8. What extends to the transported complex weights

For each fixed weight V in P08 II.10 there are explicit c0>0, a monic real polynomial chi of degree d0<=2 (specifically 1 or t^2-t+b^2), and A2>0 such that

    Re V(t)>=c0 chi(t)^2 sqrt(t(1-t)),
    integral |V(t)|^2/[chi(t)^2 sqrt(t(1-t))]dt=A2.

Let h_N be its monic bilinear norm. The reflection symmetry makes h_N real, while orthogonality to q_N-conjugate(q_N) gives h_N=integral |q_N|^2 V. Thus

    [c0 pi/(8*16^d0)]16^(-N) <= h_N
        <= [4A2/c0]16^(-N).                         (A13)

The lower bound follows because chi q_N is monic of degree N+d0 in the Chebyshev reference measure. The upper bound follows from the coercive estimate and orthogonality against the monic Chebyshev competitor, exactly as in P08.

All moments of the fixed weight have the form v_n in Q(i), with H 8^n v_n in Z[i] for some fixed positive integer H: the unmodified moments have that property by (A1) applied separately to the three quarter-power terms; the repaired moments are fixed rational linear combinations of two shifts. Let

    D_N^V=det[H 8^(i+j)v_(i+j)]_(0<=i,j<N).

Then

    |D_N^V|=2^(N^2+O(N)),                            (A14)

with explicit constants from (A13) and H. This follows from the exact scaling H^N8^(N(N-1)) product h_n. It is an integer determinant, and its reflection symmetry in the cases at hand makes it real up to a fixed scalar phase.

Unlike the original positive weight, the transported complex weights need not have vanishing odd centered moments, so the parity factorization reducing N^2 to N^2/2 is unavailable. Their corresponding coefficient and specialization contents remain a separate arithmetic problem. Formula (A14) is not a proof of their primitive multiplier growth.

TARGET: determine the exact Smith/cofactor content and the specialization gcd for these concrete moment matrices. Proving an exponential or exp(O(N log N)) upper bound for the combined content would give a genuine quadratic-growth obstruction. Proving cancellation of essentially the entire quadratic determinant height could instead validate P08's desired geometric separator. The current all-index results establish the size of the cancellation required; they do not establish which alternative occurs.

## Part II. General covariant covers and finite arithmetic orbit theorems

### A general covariant cover for every Thomas parameter

For an integer a, define

    F_a(x,y)=x^3-(a-1)x^2y-(a+2)xy^2-y^3,
    d=a^2+a+7,
    K=x^2+xy+y^2,
    L=-(2a+1)x^3-3(a+5)x^2y+3(a-4)xy^2+(2a+1)y^3.

PROVED, by polynomial expansion:

    L^2+27F_a(x,y)^2=4d K^3.                         (COV.1)

Hence every integral solution |F_a(x,y)|=1 gives integers K,L with K>=1 and

    L^2=4dK^3-27.                                    (COV.2)

This is a necessary cover, not an equivalence: a solution of (COV.2) must still satisfy the covariant image conditions to come from x,y. When the cover has only K=1, that distinction disappears for the final enumeration, since K=1 is a finite positive-definite equation.

There is a useful uniform congruence refinement. A solution |F_a|=1 is primitive. If 3|K, then x=y mod3 and y is a unit modulo3. Writing x=y+3z gives

    F_a(x,y)=-(2a+1)y^3 mod9.

Since y^3=+/-1 mod9, this can equal+/-1 only when a=0 or8 mod9. Therefore a not in{0,8} mod9 forces 3 not dividing K. Also d=0 mod3 exactly when a=1 mod3. Combining these observations with (COV.1) shows that

    a in{2,3,5,6} mod9 and |F_a|=1
       imply 3 does not divide K or L.              (COV.2a)

For a=1,4,7 mod9, L is divisible by3 despite the forced 3-adic unit K. That case requires its own allocation; the a=5 coprimality argument must not be extended to it unchanged.

Suppose 4d is not a rational cube and let theta be its positive real cube root. In any explicitly given order O containing theta, set

    alpha=theta K-3,
    alpha'=theta^2K^2+3theta K+9.

Then

    alpha alpha'=L^2,
    Norm(alpha)=L^2.                                 (COV.3)

If a prime lattice divides alpha and alpha', it divides 27: modulo alpha one has theta K=3, so alpha'=27. This calculation has no hidden unique-factorization assumption. It is an identity in the order, and can be checked in each of its finite local quotient rings.

Consequently, if 3 does not divide L and the order has certified local uniformizers and factorization into their powers at every relevant prime, the scalar lattice alpha O is the square of an invertible stable lattice. At a prime factor of alpha the complementary factor is a unit, and the valuation in (COV.3) is twice the valuation of L. An arbitrary division procedure in a nonmaximal order would not suffice for this step; the explicit local structure in the a=5 proof below does. If 3 divides L, the factors above 3 require a separate finite allocation. One may not discard that case just because it was absent for a=5.

The pure-cubic field has signature (1,1). Its units can be covered without quoting a unit theorem: provide one explicit positive norm-one unit epsilon, balance the real embedding by its powers, and enumerate the resulting finite coefficient box. The general proof is given below. This is why the covariant route can be easier than the original totally real cubic, whose elementary unit coverage has two directions.

If 4d is a cube, the cubic-field construction is inapplicable as stated; one must instead work directly with (COV.2). More generally cube factors in 4d should be removed and a smaller order/basis certified before a lattice calculation. The normalized a=4 Fermat cover illustrates why this preliminary simplification matters.

### PROVED theorem: square allocation from an odd relation determinant

Here is a statement that covers a=5 and is useful independently.

Let O be a specified order in a number field, equipped with explicit multiplication in a Z-basis. A stable lattice is a full-rank Z-submodule I of the field such that OI is contained in I. Write I~J when I=cJ for a nonzero field element c. Assume the following data have been proved or supplied as finite exact certificates.

1. Every stable lattice needed in the argument is invertible, and its local divisions and products can be checked by integer lattice operations.
2. Every such lattice is scalar-equivalent to a product of specified lattices P_1,...,P_s and Q_1,...,Q_r. A volume bound and an exhaustive finite list of small prime lattices are one way to prove this coverage.
3. For j=1,...,r there is an explicitly checked equality Q_j^2=delta_j O, with delta_j nonzero.
4. There are s scalar relations

       product_i P_i^(a_ki) product_j Q_j^(b_kj)=c_k O

   whose s-by-s exponent matrix A=(a_ki) has odd nonzero determinant D. Every c_k is explicit and every equality is verified as equality of lattices, not merely of their indices or norms.

Then every I with I^2 scalar-equivalent to O is scalar-equivalent to one of the at most 2^r lattices

    Q_1^e1 ... Q_r^er,  e_j in {0,1}.                (SA.1)

Moreover, if I is integral and alpha O=I^2, then there are an explicitly allowed e, a unit u of O, and an element gamma in the inverse of the corresponding representative lattice such that

    alpha=u delta_1^e1 ... delta_r^er gamma^2.        (SA.2)

Proof. Multiply the verified relations using the integer coefficients of the adjugate of A. This expresses P_i^D as scalar-equivalent to a product of the Q_j. Thus I^D is scalar-equivalent to such a product. Write D=2h+1, with h an integer, including the case D<0. Since I^2~O,

    I~I^D(I^2)^(-h)~product_j Q_j^f_j.

Reduce each f_j modulo2 using Q_j^2=delta_j O. This proves (SA.1) and produces an actual scalar gamma with I=gamma product Q_j^e_j. Squaring and comparing with alpha O shows that alpha/(gamma^2 product delta_j^e_j) generates O, hence is a unit. If I is integral, gamma times its representative lattice is contained in O, exactly the inverse-lattice condition in (SA.2).

All operations in this proof are multiplication, inversion, adjugates, and equality tests for specified lattices. The theorem does not assume that O is a PID, or infer that it is one from an odd-rank test. A surviving Q_j is a real additional cover; it must not be silently replaced by O.

For higher-power allocation the same proof works with an integer m>=2: replace squares by m-th powers, require gcd(D,m)=1, and supply Q_j^m=delta_j O. If I^m~O, choose integers h,k with hD+km=1 and use I~(I^D)^h(I^m)^k. Exponents of the Q_j then reduce modulo m. This is an elementary generalization beyond 2-descent.

### PROVED theorem: an explicit unit certificate in signature (1,1)

Let O be a specified order in a cubic field with one real embedding sigma_1 and a conjugate pair sigma_2,sigma_3. Suppose epsilon is an explicitly given unit with

    0<sigma_1(epsilon)<1, Norm(epsilon)=1.

Choose rational bounds U>sigma_1(epsilon)^(-1/2) and V>sigma_1(epsilon)^(-1/4). Let B be a finite coefficient box which contains every element z in O satisfying

    |sigma_1(z)|<=U, |sigma_2(z)|<=V, |sigma_3(z)|<=V.

The box can be obtained by interval bounds for the inverse embedding matrix. If exhaustive exact enumeration shows that the only elements of B with norm 1 or -1 are 1 and -1, then every unit of O is of the form +/-epsilon^j, j an integer.

Proof. For any unit z multiply by an integer power of epsilon so that

    sqrt(sigma_1(epsilon))<=|sigma_1(z)|
        <=1/sqrt(sigma_1(epsilon)).

This uses only the ordinary Archimedean property of the powers of a real number in (0,1). Norm(z)=+/-1 gives

    |sigma_2(z)|=|sigma_3(z)|=|sigma_1(z)|^(-1/2)
        <=sigma_1(epsilon)^(-1/4).

The normalized z lies in B, so is +/-1. Undoing the power proves the assertion. No prior rank or finite-generation theorem is used.

This criterion is conditional on a successful finite enumeration, not on a conjectural fundamental unit. If the box contains other units, it certifies a larger generating list or tells us to refine the candidate; it does not authorize treating epsilon as a generator. Its cost depends on the actual unit and embedding matrix, not only on the degree.

There is a sharper normalization for a fixed norm in signature (1,1). Write e=sigma_1(epsilon), let M be a positive integer, and suppose |Norm(alpha)|=M. Multiply alpha by a power of epsilon until

    (M e^2)^(1/3)<=|sigma_1(alpha)|<=(M/e)^(1/3).

The ratio of the upper and lower endpoints is 1/e, so such a power always exists. The norm identity now gives, simultaneously,

    |sigma_1(alpha)|, |sigma_2(alpha)|, |sigma_3(alpha)|
        <=(M/e)^(1/3).                              (UNIT.1)

This asymmetric real interval minimizes the largest of the three embedding bounds. It often shrinks the finite enumeration substantially. For the a=5 order, the rational interval for epsilon certifies e*178^3>1 and e*396^3>11. Consequently norm-one units can be covered in the coefficient box (177,33,12), and elements of absolute norm11 in (395,74,28), using the inverse Fourier formulas in the pure-cubic basis. The latter box is empty and proves that the retained index11 lattice is not principal. The dedicated certificate below checks this assertion exactly.

### PROVED theorem: a primitive conic cover produces finitely many quartic markers

Suppose an allocated square cover has written alpha=lambda gamma^2 in a fixed fractional lattice. In a Z-basis gamma=z_0b_0+z_1b_1+z_2b_2, its coordinate equations have the form

    Q(z_0,z_1,z_2)=0, H(z_0,z_1,z_2)=c,             (PC.1)

where Q,H are specified integer homogeneous quadratic forms and c is a fixed nonzero integer. Denominators must first have been cleared exactly; a finite-index sublattice is not a substitute for the full inverse lattice.

Assume the projective conic Q=0 is nonsingular and has a specified rational parametrization covering all its rational points

    [z_0:z_1:z_2]=[q_0(u,v):q_1(u,v):q_2(u,v)],

where the q_i are integer homogeneous quadratic forms with no common projective zero. Suppose explicit polynomial Bezout identities have been supplied:

    D u^h=sum_i A_i(u,v) q_i(u,v),
    D v^h=sum_i B_i(u,v) q_i(u,v),                 (PC.2)

for a nonzero integer D and an integer h. Then every primitive integral point on the conic is, up to simultaneous sign,

    z_i=q_i(u,v)/g,
    gcd(u,v)=1,  g=gcd(q_0(u,v),q_1(u,v),q_2(u,v)),
    g divides D.                                  (PC.3)

Indeed a rational projective parameter has coprime numerator and denominator u,v. Dividing its integer image by its gcd gives the primitive vector. Evaluating (PC.2) shows g divides both D u^h and D v^h, hence divides D.

For a general integral vector z=d z_primitive, equation H(z)=c forces d^2|c. Thus (PC.1) is covered by finitely many explicit binary quartic equations

    H(q_0(u,v),q_1(u,v),q_2(u,v))=(c/d^2)g^2,
    d^2|c, g|D,                                   (PC.4)

with the corresponding gcd and congruence conditions retained. Missing projective points, if a chosen affine parametrization omitted one, must be checked separately or included by homogeneous parameters. A nonsingular rational conic has the usual line-through-a-rational-point parametrization; (PC.2) is then a finite polynomial certificate that its image coordinates have no common projective zero. No ineffective finiteness theorem is used.

This is a globally necessary cover, not a claim that the original local equations had too few variables. The new square-allocation parameters may have no solution modulo a prime even when the original quadratic markers have smooth local solutions. The unit-odd principal cover for a=5 gives an exact modulo5 example below.

### PROVED theorem: fixed-norm coverage does not require a fundamental unit basis

Let O be a specified order of degree d and signature (r_1,r_2), and put s=r_1+r_2-1. Suppose u_1,...,u_s are explicit units of norm1. Assume their vectors of logarithms of absolute values at the real places and one place from each complex pair are linearly independent. This hypothesis can be certified by interval bounds on a finite real determinant; no theorem about the rank of the unit group is needed.

Fix a nonzero integer c. Use the weight1 at a real place and weight2 at a complex place. The log vector of every alpha with Norm(alpha)=c lies on the affine hyperplane whose weighted coordinate sum is log|c|. Choose the center vector with every coordinate log|c|/d. The log vectors of the u_i form a real basis of the parallel homogeneous hyperplane. Express the difference between log|alpha| and the center in that basis, round each coefficient to an integer, and divide alpha by the corresponding integer powers of u_i. Its new log vector lies in the closed parallelepiped with each basis coefficient between -1/2 and 1/2.

Every embedding absolute value is therefore explicitly bounded. Inverting the embedding matrix gives a finite integer coefficient box B_c. Exhaustively enumerate

    S_c={z in O intersect B_c:Norm(z)=c}.

Then every element of O of norm c belongs to one of the orbits

    z u_1^n1 ... u_s^ns,  z in S_c, n_i in Z.        (NS.1)

Proof is exactly the normalization just described. The enumeration may include extra seeds whose embedding vector is outside the parallelepiped; that only enlarges the finite cover and causes no logical problem. Units of norm -1 can instead be squared or combined to give norm1 units, or a finite norm-sign condition can be carried explicitly.

This is stronger for the present purpose than the assertion that a proposed list generates all units: it covers a fixed norm equation directly, even when the displayed unit subgroup has an unknown index. A larger subgroup index costs more seeds in B_c; it does not create an unproved coverage claim. The proof uses a finite real determinant, inequalities, and integer enumeration, rather than a unit theorem. The one-dimensional nonprincipal-lattice test for norm11 below is an especially small instance.

### PROVED theorem: removing finite residue torsion before lifting

Let p be an odd prime and B a commutative finite free Z_p-algebra. Let e_1,...,e_r be units of B and let m>=1 be coprime to p, with e_i^m=1 modp for every i. Let mu_1,...,mu_k:B->Z_p be Z_p-linear markers with mu_j(1)=0.

There is a unique m-th root U_i of e_i^m in 1+pB, obtained by the integral binomial series

    U_i=(1+(e_i^m-1))^(1/m).

Put zeta_i=e_i/U_i. Then zeta_i^m=1, zeta_i=e_i modp, and U_i=1+pK_i for some K_i in B. Uniqueness follows because raising to the m-th power is invertible on 1+pB; the binomial series for exponent1/m is its inverse.

Suppose a finite congruence sieve proves that every marker-zero orbit index n=(n_1,...,n_r) has

    e_1^n1 ... e_r^nr modp in a specified scalar subgroup C of F_p^*.

Assume each c in C has order dividing m, and let its unique scalar m-th root-of-unity lift to Z_p be denoted by c_tilde. The product zeta_1^n1 ... zeta_r^nr is then exactly the corresponding scalar c_tilde: it is an m-th root of unity with that residue, and two such lifts have a quotient in 1+pB, where there is no nontrivial m-torsion.

Suppose finally that the k-by-r matrix

    J_(j,i)=mu_j(K_i) modp

has rank r. Then the only integer vector n whose orbit point has all markers zero and passes the sieve is n=0. The same conclusion holds with an extra scalar sign, since the markers are homogeneous linear forms.

Proof. For a putative zero, remove the scalar c_tilde; linearity leaves all markers of U_1^n1...U_r^nr equal to zero. If n!=0, let a=min_i v_p(n_i), and write n_i=p^a t_i with some t_i a p-adic unit. Binomial expansion, or induction on a followed by the binomial expansion for each t_i, gives

    U_1^n1 ... U_r^nr
       =1+p^(a+1) sum_i t_i K_i modp^(a+2).

Applying the markers and dividing by p^(a+1) gives Jt=0 modp. Full rank contradicts the primitivity of t. Thus n=0.

For a nontrivial base z with all markers zero the same proof uses J_(j,i)=mu_j(K_i z), provided one has the exact equality zeta_1^n1...zeta_r^nr z=c_tilde z in B for every admissible index. A scalar action merely modulo p on a nonunit base does not by itself prove that equality. The hypothesis is essential: one may not replace an arbitrary finite-order residue component by a scalar. Several primes can supply the finite sieve, while only one prime is used for the analytic isolation.

This theorem explains how a finite sieve can align several apparent exponent residue classes with one analytic zero. It does not produce additional markers or defeat the local dimension barrier when rank r exceeds the number of independent markers.

### Which remaining fibres this architecture can reach

PROVED uniform reduction. The identity (COV.1) applies to every integer a. Subject to explicit local division, square-allocation, and unit-box certificates, it reduces |F_a|=1 to finitely many quartic equations and finite congruence conditions. It does not prove that these quartics are solvable by the existing isolation cards, or that their associated unit groups have an elementary complete finite cover already available.

The orders which appear first are pure-cubic orders in Q(cuberoot(4d)), after removal of rational cube factors and any useful change to a dual pure-cubic generator. For a=1 one can use Q(cuberoot(6)), as in P08. For a=4 the normalized covariant is already a Fermat cubic and there is no reason to build a larger order. For a=5 the order with basis 1,theta,theta^2/2 and theta^3=148 has the explicit certificate below. The quartic orders at the next stage are generated by roots of the particular primitive-conic quartics; their coefficients depend on the allocated scalar and the unit parity. They cannot be chosen from the discriminant alone while ignoring those data.

Here is a useful explicit size benchmark. For a certified maximal pure-cubic order of discriminant D_O<0, the elementary volume argument in the a=5 proof gives a scalar-equivalent integral lattice of index at most

    floor((8/(9pi))*sqrt(|D_O|)).                    (SIZE.1)

The naive order Z[theta], theta^3=4d, has discriminant -432d^2. It is often not the order in which local divisions are valid, so it cannot simply be used as a maximal order. After the required local enlargement, its discriminant decreases by the square of the index; consequently the same numerical expression with 12sqrt3*d is an upper benchmark. It is O(d)=O(a^2), not a uniform small constant. The integer lattices and local bases remain explicit.

Small principalization certificates occur when this norm bound is modest or many low-norm relations generate the needed power classes. Small unit certificates occur when a given unit balances the embeddings in a modest box. These are separate costs. Neither a small parameter nor the cyclic nature of the original real cubic guarantees both. The a=5 certificate also demonstrates that a nonprincipal square class can survive and must be carried through the cover.

For immediate work on the remaining fibres, the correct compiler order is: test normalized covariant shortcuts; take the finite norm-preserving transport closure of every already completed source fibre; simplify the pure-cubic generator/order; certify only the power classes actually required; certify units by a balanced box; perform primitive conic allocation before a large residue sieve; then attack the resulting quartic markers. This order can eliminate a fibre before any new separator is constructed. It does not promise that all the remaining 164 fibres close by the same finite amount of work.

## Part III. Complete Thomas fibres a=4 and a=55

### Status

PROVED. For integers x,y, put

    F_4(x,y)=x^3-3x^2y-6xy^2-y^3.

Then F_4=1 has exactly

    (x,y)=(1,0),(0,-1),(-1,1).

The F_4=-1 solutions are their negatives. Thus |F_4|=1 has exactly the six signed trivial solutions.

This fibre has a simpler covariant cover than the generic pure-cubic/quartic route. Its normalized covariant equation is the Fermat cubic. The elementary descent below solves that equation completely. No class-group, unit-group, or rational-point theorem is used. The finite certificate checks every identity and the finite residue/unit tables used in the descent; the infinite argument is proved in the text.

### 1. The covariant identity and the finite endpoint

Define

    K(x,y)=x^2+xy+y^2,
    G(x,y)=-x^3-3x^2y+y^3.

Direct expansion gives

    F_4(x,y)^2+3G(x,y)^2=4K(x,y)^3.                   (A4.1)

This is the specialization of P08's Thomas covariant identity

    L^2+27F_a^2=4(a^2+a+7)K^3

at a=4: a^2+a+7=27 and L=9G. If |F_4|=1, then

    3G^2+1=4K^3.                                      (A4.2)

Reduction modulo4 shows that G is odd. Set

    A=(1+G)/2, B=(1-G)/2.

Then A,B are integers, A+B=1, and

    A^3+B^3=((1+G)^3+(1-G)^3)/8
             =(1+3G^2)/4=K^3.                        (A4.3)

The elementary exponent-three descent proved in Section2 says that a nonzero integer solution of A^3+B^3=C^3 cannot have ABC!=0. Here K>=1 by (A4.2), so A=0 orB=0. Hence

    G=+/-1, K=1.

The bound

    K=(x+y/2)^2+3y^2/4=(y+x/2)^2+3x^2/4

forces |x|,|y|<=1. The six pairs with K=1 are

    (1,0),(-1,0),(0,1),(0,-1),(1,-1),(-1,1).

Substitution in F_4 gives exactly the claimed signs and completes the theorem.

### 2. A full elementary descent for exponent three

#### 2.1. The only ring facts required

Let omega satisfy omega^2+omega+1=0, with its nonreal complex embedding, and let R=Z[omega]. Write elements as a+bomega. Define

    N(a+bomega)=a^2-ab+b^2.

The norm is the complex absolute value squared, hence multiplicative and a positive integer on nonzero R. The six norm-one elements are

    +/-1, +/-omega, +/-omega^2.

They are all the units. To verify completeness, use

    N(a+bomega)=(a-b/2)^2+3b^2/4.

Norm one forces b=0,+/-1, and the six possibilities follow by substitution. Conversely norm one gives inverse equal to the complex conjugate, which lies in R.

Here is an explicit division algorithm. Given alpha,beta in R with beta!=0, write alpha/beta=r+somega for rational r,s. Round r and s to integers m,n. Then

    |r-m|<=1/2, |s-n|<=1/2,
    N((r-m)+(s-n)omega)
       <=(r-m)^2+|(r-m)(s-n)|+(s-n)^2<=3/4<1.

Thus with q=m+nomega, the remainder alpha-qbeta has strictly smaller norm than beta. Euclid's algorithm supplies Bezout identities. Factorization into irreducibles terminates by the positive norm, and Bezout proves Euclid's lemma. This proves unique factorization of elements, up to the six displayed units, directly in this ring.

Put

    pi=1-omega.

Then

    N(pi)=3,
    pi^2=-3omega,
    3=-omega^2 pi^2.                                  (A4.4)

The reduction map a+bomega -> a+b mod3 identifies R/piR with F_3. For an entirely explicit divisibility check,

    (a+bomega)/pi=[(2a-b)+(a+b)omega]/3.

So pi divides a+bomega exactly when 3 divides a+b. In particular pi is prime, and for an ordinary integer n, pi divides n exactly when 3 divides n.

#### 2.2. A first-order congruence with fourth-order strength

PROVED. If u in R is not divisible by pi, then u^3 is congruent to +1 or-1 modulo pi^4, with the sign equal to the residue of u modulo pi.

To prove it, first take u=1+pi t. Expansion gives

    u^3=1+3pi t+3pi^2t^2+pi^3t^3.

The middle quadratic term is divisible by pi^4 because 3 is a unit times pi^2. The other two nonconstant terms combine as

    3pi t+pi^3t^3=pi^3(t^3-omega^2t).

Modulo pi one has omega^2=1 and t^3=t in F_3. Therefore the bracket is divisible by pi, proving u^3=1 modpi^4. Negating u gives the residue -1 case.

#### 2.3. The valuation descent, with units retained

PROVED stronger lemma. There are no nonzero elements a,b,c in R and no unit epsilon such that

    a^3+b^3=epsilon c^3,
    gcd_R(a,b)=1,
    pi does not divide a or b,
    pi divides c.                                     (A4.5)

Suppose there is such a solution, choosing one with m=v_pi(c) minimal. Because a+b=0 modulo pi, their residues are opposite. Section2.2 shows that a^3+b^3 is divisible by pi^4. Hence 3m>=4 and therefore m>=2.

Factor the left side:

    (a+b)(a+omega b)(a+omega^2b)=epsilon c^3.           (A4.6)

Every factor is divisible by pi. Any common irreducible divisor of two factors divides pi: subtract the two factors and then use gcd_R(a,b)=1. Also the difference of any two factors has pi-valuation exactly1, since pi does not divide b and each difference is a unit times pi b. Consequently the three factor valuations are

    1, 1, 3m-2

in some order. Multiplying b by omega oromega^2 leaves b^3 unchanged and permutes the factors; choose this harmless rotation so that a+b is the factor of valuation 3m-2.

Set

    A=(a+b)/pi,
    B=(a+omega b)/pi,
    C=(a+omega^2b)/pi.

The three elements A,B,C are nonzero and pairwise coprime. Their product is epsilon(c/pi)^3. Unique element factorization therefore gives units u_A,u_B,u_C and nonzero elements a1,b1,c1 in R with

    A=u_A a1^3,
    B=u_B b1^3,
    C=u_C c1^3,
    v_pi(a1)=m-1,
    pi does not divide b1 orc1.

The exact linear identity

    A+omega B+omega^2 C=0

now gives, after division by omega u_B,

    b1^3+eta c1^3=epsilon' a1^3,
    eta=omega u_C/u_B,
    epsilon'=-u_A/(omega u_B).                        (A4.7)

Both eta and epsilon' are units. Since pi divides a1, the right side is divisible by pi^3. Section2.2 makes b1^3 and c1^3 congruent to signs modulo pi^3. Thus pi^3 divides s1+eta s2 for some s1,s2 in {1,-1}.

For the six units eta, every nonzero element s1+eta s2 has norm at most4, whereas a nonzero multiple of pi^3 has norm at least27. Hence s1+eta s2=0, and eta is either1 or-1. Absorb a minus sign into c1 if necessary. Equation(A4.7) is another solution of the form(A4.5), with the new third variable a1 and

    v_pi(a1)=m-1<m.

The new first two variables are coprime because B and C were coprime. All hypotheses are preserved, contradicting minimality. This proves the stronger lemma.

#### 2.4. Fermat's cubic follows

Suppose nonzero integers A,B,C satisfy A^3+B^3=C^3. Divide out their ordinary common divisor to make the triple primitive. Cube residues modulo9 are 0,1,-1. If none of A,B,C were divisible by3, the equation would equate a sum of two signs to a sign modulo9, which is impossible. The primitive triple is pairwise coprime: a rational prime dividing two entries divides the third by the equation. Thus exactly one entry is divisible by3. Move terms between sides, change signs, and if necessary permute them so that the divisible variable is the one called c in

    a^3+b^3=epsilon c^3,

where epsilon is1 or-1. The other two are not divisible by3. An ordinary Bezout identity for a,b is also a Bezout identity in R, so gcd_R(a,b)=1. The explicit criterion for pi-divisibility in Section2.1 now gives all hypotheses of(A4.5), a contradiction.

Thus every integer solution of A^3+B^3=C^3 has ABC=0. This is the only assertion from Fermat's cubic used in Section1, and it has been proved here using explicit ring identities, Euclid's algorithm and a decreasing nonnegative integer valuation.

### 3. A useful complete norm-three corollary

Recall F_1(X,Y)=X^3-3XY^2-Y^3. The exact identity

    F_1(-x+y,2x+y)=3F_4(x,y)                          (A4.8)

has the inverse rational linear map

    x=(Y-X)/3, y=(2X+Y)/3.

If F_1(X,Y)=3, reduction modulo3 gives X=Y mod3, so this inverse is integral. Therefore all solutions of F_1=3 are exactly

    (X,Y)=(-1,2),(-1,-1),(2,-1).

The solutions with right side -3 are their negatives. This is a fully proved additional norm equation; no unjustified preservation of the right-hand side under a change of presentation is used.

### 4. The general covariant shortcut

PROVED criterion. Let F,G be integer homogeneous cubic forms and H a positive definite integer homogeneous quadratic form. Suppose the polynomial identity

    F(x,y)^2+3G(x,y)^2=4H(x,y)^3

holds. Then every integer solution |F(x,y)|=1 satisfies H(x,y)=1 and G(x,y)=+/-1. Consequently all solutions are obtained by the finite, explicitly bounded enumeration H=1.

The proof is exactly(A4.2)-(A4.3) and Section2. Thus a covariant compiler should test this normalized Fermat identity before building a pure-cubic order or a quartic unit cover. In this case the covering curve has an elementary valuation descent available globally, and no positive-rank unit analysis remains.

This does not assert that every small Thomas fibre has this normalization. For a=4 the divisibility of the covariant by9 and a^2+a+7=27 make it exact. Other fibres generally retain a nontrivial twist in the Mordell equation.

### 5. Another complete open fibre: a=55

PROVED, using the complete a=1 theorem proved in P08. For integers x,y,

    F_55(x,y)=x^3-54x^2y-57xy^2-y^3=1

has exactly (1,0),(0,-1),(-1,1). The negative right-hand side has their negatives.

The exact norm-preserving transport is

    F_1(2x-y,x+3y)=F_55(x,y).                         (A55.1)

Its matrix and inverse are

    M=[[2,-1],[1,3]], det M=7,
    x=(3X+Y)/7, y=(-X+2Y)/7.                          (A55.2)

P08 proves that the complete list of integer solutions F_1(X,Y)=1 is

    (1,0),(0,-1),(-1,1),(2,1),(-3,2),(1,-3).          (A55.3)

Every F_55=1 solution maps to this list. The first three displayed pairs do not lie in M Z^2: their inverse coordinate numerators are respectively (3,-1),(-1,-2),(-2,3), which are not both divisible by7. The last three pairs pull back respectively to

    (2,1) -> (1,0),
    (-3,2) -> (-1,1),

### 6. Finite norm-preserving closure of solved Thomas fibres

PROVED transport theorem. Fix an integer a and suppose the finite complete list

    S_a={(p,q) in Z^2:F_a(p,q)=1}

has been proved. For each (p,q) in S_a define

    H=p^2+pq+q^2,
    M_(p,q)=[[p,-q],[q,p+q]],
    b=1+(a-1)p^3+3(a+2)p^2q+9pq^2-(a-1)q^3.         (C.1)

Then H>0, det M_(p,q)=H, and

    F_a(M_(p,q)(x,y))=F_b(x,y).                       (C.2)

Consequently the complete list S_b consists precisely of the integral pairs

    x=((p+q)X+qY)/H,
    y=(-qX+pY)/H                                     (C.3)

as (X,Y) ranges over S_a. This is a finite exact divisibility test. Negatives supply the right-hand side -1.

Proof. Put h=F_a(p,q). Expanding the transformed cubic gives coefficients

    [h,g,g-3h,-h],
    g=-(a-1)p^3-3(a+2)p^2q-9pq^2+(a-1)q^3,

in the order x^3,x^2y,xy^2,y^3. Here h=1 and b=1-g, which proves (C.2). The determinant and inverse are immediate. Necessity and sufficiency of the inverse-list test then follow from (C.2).

The parameter identity

    b^2+b+7=(a^2+a+7)H^3                             (C.4)

also follows. It can be checked by expanding

    g^2-3gh+9h^2=(a^2+a+7)(p^2+pq+q^2)^3

before setting h=1. Thus the target parameter and the index H of the image lattice can be certified without a number-field isomorphism calculation.

There is also the sign-reflection identity

    F_a(-y,-x)=F_(-a-1)(x,y).                         (C.5)

Hence every completed b-fibre immediately completes the (-b-1)-fibre as well.

The list in (C.1) is exhaustive for all integer norm-preserving transformations that commute with the standard order-three action

    R(x,y)=(y,-x-y).

Indeed solving MR=RM over integer entries gives exactly M=M_(p,q), and the leading coefficient condition F_a(M(x,y))=F_b(x,y) forces F_a(p,q)=1. Composing with the reflection (C.5) covers the transformations conjugating R to R^-1. No assertion is made here about other transformations not satisfying this equivariance condition.

For the complete a=1 list in P08, the trivial orbit yields b=1 and the other orbit yields b=55. Thus this closure calculation supplies all new positive parameters obtainable from a=1 by this stated integral equivariant norm-preserving class. For the complete a=3 list in P05 (there called G_2, since G_t=F_(t+1)), the trivial orbit yields b=3 and the nontrivial orbit yields b=-2391, hence the reflected b=2390. These are exact consequences of the complete lists. The new fibre below182 obtained this way is a=55.

This closure is a useful compiler step after each completed Thue fibre. It can finish another equation without constructing a new separator, a unit basis, or a local analytic germ. It is also sharply bounded once the source fibre is solved: the finite list S_a exhausts this equivariant transport class.

### 6. General mechanism isolated by this proof

PROVED (scalar torsion stripping).
Let A be a finite free commutative Z_p-algebra, let p be odd, and let
E1,...,Er be commuting units. Suppose an integer N prime to p satisfies
E_i^N=1 modp for every i. Let mu1,...,muk be Z_p-linear maps with
mu_j(1)=0. Suppose:

1. Every globally admissible exponent vector is forced, by a finite residue
   certificate at p and auxiliary primes, to have E1^s1...Er^sr modulo p
   in a set of scalar residues. Since each has Nth power1 modulo p and
   p does not divide N, each has a unique scalar Nth-root lift in Z_p.
2. The k x r drift matrix whose ith column is
   mu_j((E_i^N-1)/(pN)) modulo p has rank r.

Then every simultaneous marker-zero exponent vector is0. Multiplication
by a separate scalar torsion factor merely reproduces the corresponding
scalar base solutions.

Proof: take unique Nth roots U_i of E_i^N in1+pA, strip zeta_i=E_i/U_i,
and use Hensel uniqueness to turn the finite scalar residue condition
into an exact scalar torsion factor. Linear homogeneous markers ignore
that invertible scalar. The first nonzero p-adic digit of the exponent
vector then contradicts the full-rank drift. This argument needs no
integer exponent representative of each p-adic torsion base point.

The useful distinction is between an arbitrary residue zero and one whose
finite torsion component has been forced into scalar roots. Auxiliary
primes can enforce that scalar condition even when they do not themselves
isolate the exponent vector. In the present example5 supplies both the
scalar residue condition and the invertible differential;7 is optional.

Every norm+/-3 element of O4 is therefore

    +/- seed*U1^m*U2^n                               (M.11)

for a seed from this finite list. Since the two units have norm-1, their exponent parity changes the norm sign. Allowing both signs in the seed enumeration handles this exactly; a final solution must still have norm-3. The marker sieve can safely retain the larger norm+/-3 orbit cover if that simplifies the congruence calculation.

### 5. The exact modulus-nine separation

PROVED. There are no integers r,s satisfying simultaneously

    F(r,s)=-3,
    3 does not divide K(r,s).

Use the two linear markers

    mu1(a,b,c,d)=-88a+337b+130c-327d,
    mu2(a,b,c,d)=252a-448b+277c-357d.

Their values on eta and zeta are both zero, by direct substitution. Thus every target beta=R eta-s zeta has mu1(beta)=mu2(beta)=0.

Modulo3 the two quartics satisfy

    F(r,s)=r(r+s)^3,
    K(r,s)=s(r+s)^3.

The conditions F=-3 and 3 not dividing K therefore force r=0 mod3 and s=+/-1 mod3. Since r is even, R=r/2 is also0 mod3. Consequently beta modulo3 belongs to the two-element set

    {+zeta,-zeta} mod3
      ={(1,2,2,1),(2,1,1,2)}.                        (M.12)

The two units satisfy the exact congruences

    U1^18=U2^18=1 mod9.                              (M.13)

Thus every exponent pair in(M.11) has the same modulo9 state as one with0<=m,n<18. The two markers are homogeneous linear forms, and the set(M.12) is closed under negation, so it is enough to use the nine displayed seeds rather than all eighteen signed seeds.

The complete finite assertion is the following. For each of the nine seeds and each of the324 pairs0<=m,n<18, put

    z=seed*U1^m*U2^n mod9.

Then at least one of these necessary conditions fails:

    mu1(z)=0 mod9,
    mu2(z)=0 mod9,
    z mod3 belongs to the set(M.12).                 (M.14)

The companion `P09_a5_last_separation.py` checks all2916 cases with exact integer multiplication in the displayed order and verifies(M.13). It returns PASS. This finite certificate, together with the proved orbit coverage(M.11), contradicts the existence of any target beta. The theorem follows.

No local analytic isolation theorem is needed at the last step. Nor is a completeness theorem for all quartic solutions with K divisible by3 claimed: those solutions are outside the necessary Thomas condition and may be left in the larger fixed-norm orbit cover.

### 6. The theory gain

Complete fixed-norm orbit coverage can be easier than a complete unit-group theorem. The units need only span the real log space; all possible missing unit cosets are absorbed by the finite seed box. Here a finite norm-three cover with nine signed seed pairs, followed by a modulus-nine sieve, closes a branch whose unparametrized quadratic markers had surviving local points.

This also illustrates why the entire inverse lattice must be transported. The omitted index-two coset contained a real arithmetic branch, including the K=3 point. Correcting that coverage did not require an infinite new descent: an integral conic parametrization, an explicit quartic order and a finite fixed-norm seed box supplied the missing globally necessary conditions.

### 2. What the moment calculation rules out, precisely

The original continuous weight is a sum of three Jacobi densities with endpoint powers1/4,1/2,3/4. It is not a rational-function modification of one fixed Jacobi density. To see this directly, expand near t=0. A nonzero rational multiple of t^alpha(1-t)^beta has exponents all in alpha+Z in its Laurent-Puiseux expansion. Our weight has nonzero terms in three distinct classes modulo Z. They cannot be equal. Thus the usual single-weight polynomial/rational modification formulas do not directly evaluate this Hankel system.

This is a restricted obstruction. It does not rule out a more elaborate product formula, a multiple-orthogonality identity, or a characteristic-p content theorem for the explicit nested-radical moment series. For context, Christian Krattenthaler's paper *Hankel determinants of linear combinations of moments of orthogonal polynomials, II* treats finite combinations of shifts of one moment sequence; that is not automatically the same operation as mixing the three different Jacobi parameters here. See https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04225 and the author's description at https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~kratt/artikel/cigldet3.html . No result from that paper is required in the proofs above.

A scalar change of normalization cannot alter the primitive pair, as proved in Part I. It may reveal a missed gcd; it cannot supply a new gcd after the exact primitive content has already been removed. Likewise a finite list of unusually small denominators is not an all-index denominator theorem: the certificate already finds the new odd denominator7 at N=80 and88.

The strongest useful replacement target would therefore have to control integral heights as part of the construction, rather than first selecting a unique rational orthogonal polynomial and then hoping for cancellation of its determinant.

### 3. A precise elementary alternative to a unique scalar Padé line

PROVED integer-kernel lemma. Let A be an M-by-D integer matrix, with 1<=M<D and every entry of absolute value at most H, H>=1. Put

    L=ceil((3 D H)^(M/(D-M))).

Then there is a nonzero integer vector v with Av=0 and |v_i|<=L for all i.

Proof. The (L+1)^D vectors in {0,...,L}^D have images whose M coordinates each lie between -DHL and DHL. The number of images is at most (2DHL+1)^M. Our choice gives

    (L+1)^D > L^D
       >=(3DH)^M L^M
       >=(2DHL+1)^M,

since DHL>=1. Two vectors have the same image. Their nonzero difference is the desired v.

Consequently, in a Padé/Hermite-Padé system with M_N<=c_1 N, D_N<=c_2 N, D_N-M_N>=epsilon N, and integer coefficient height H_N<=C_0^N, this lemma gives coefficients bounded by

    (3c_2 N C_0^N)^(c_1/epsilon)+1,

which is geometric up to a fixed power of N. A unique scalar Padé line has only one free dimension instead; the same elementary counting argument gives a quadratic-exponential bound in that case. This dimension/height distinction is exact and does not depend on asymptotic orthogonal-polynomial theory.

TARGET, not a completed separator. Build an underdetermined coupled system for the actual Fibonacci algebraic functions with a positive proportion of free integer coefficients, retain enough vanishing to beat the quartic exponent, and certify enough independent approximants for a nonzero determinant. The kernel lemma supplies only one nonzero vector and its height bound. It does not supply the determinant, the sign/branch control, or a satisfactory error rate. Those are the new hard lemmas. This is a genuinely different construction from rescaling the current scalar Padé pair.

For Sol, the arithmetic experiment most directly tied to a theorem is now to compute the exact pair E_m S_m(-4), E_m A_(m-1)(-4), their gcd, and the valuations of that gcd relative to E_m. The half-size formula avoids repeated denominator clearing in the original N-by-N system. Any conjectured prime-support or valuation law should be stated before expanding the range, then challenged by the new primes rather than inferred from the first few exceptional indices. A future proof must control all primes, including prime divisors of the large odd Hankel numerators.

### 4. Five reusable statements supported by the evaluations

The statements in Part II are the theory output, with the a=5 proofs serving as complete evaluations.

- **Power allocation by finite lattice relations.** Complete small-lattice coverage, explicit power relations for the retained twists, and a relation determinant coprime to m give a finite m-th-power allocation. The a=5 square case has an actual nonprincipal retained twist, independently certified by the empty norm11 box.
- **Fixed-norm orbit coverage without unit saturation.** Enough explicit units with independently certified logarithmic vectors give a finite seed cover for a fixed nonzero norm. Missing unit cosets are included in the seed box. In the last a=5 branch, nine signed seed pairs suffice and no quartic principalization theorem is needed.
- **Primitive conic allocation before local separation.** A unimodular conic reduction, or an explicit parametrization with Bezout gcd certificates, turns a square condition into finitely many quartic markers. The new modulo5 obstruction in the principal-odd a=5 branch exists only after that globally justified allocation.
- **Scalar residue torsion followed by lifting.** A finite residue sieve can force the finite residue-torsion part of a unit orbit to act as an exact scalar after Hensel lifting. The remaining principal-unit orbit can then be isolated by a full-rank drift. For the first a=5 quartic,5 alone supplies both the finite sieve and the matrix diag(3,2). The scalar lifts of residues2 and3 lie in Z_5 and need not be rational; Z_5-linearity of the markers is enough. Auxiliary primes remain available when a scalar residue condition is not forced at the lifting prime.
- **Finite transport closure of completed fibres.** Every norm-one seed in a completed Thomas fibre gives an explicit integral equivariant change of presentation and a finite inverse-image test. The complete source list makes this operation finite and exhaustive in its stated transformation class. It gives a=55 from a=1 without any new approximation argument.

The last a=5 branch also clarifies what a global cover contributes. Its raw congruence equations need not be locally empty.
