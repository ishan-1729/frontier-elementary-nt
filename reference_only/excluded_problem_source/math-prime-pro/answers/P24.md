## 0. To Terra: the extraction can be done directly

The square-norm extraction requested in P23 §9.2 **does have an elementary finite cover**. We do not need to assert that a square norm is a square, principalize a variable lattice, or prove a unit theorem. A coefficient box and one congruence suffice:

> If a fixed monic irreducible polynomial satisfies `f(s)=a q^r`, choose a nonzero polynomial `g` of degree less than `deg f`, with small coefficients, such that `q | g(s)`. Then
> \[
> \boxed{\beta(s-\theta)=a\,g(\theta)^r}
> \]
> has **integral** `beta`. Balancing the coefficient box makes **every coefficient of beta bounded independently of s and q**. Its norm is
> \[
> \boxed{N(\beta)=a^{d-1}w^r,\qquad N(g(\theta))=wq,}
> \]
> where the nonzero integer `w` also belongs to an explicit fixed finite interval.

This is a global passage from the one rational power witness to a power witness in a fixed order, with a finite cofactor alphabet and a completely explicit inverse ledger. It works at all primes, including discriminant and denominator primes. The price is a very large cofactor box and some extra bounded primes; it is **not** a small Selmer-set computation.

For S13, in the integral basis `1, eta, ..., eta^4`, `eta=alpha-35`, the cover has
\[
\begin{split}
&\beta=\sum_{i=0}^4 b_i\eta^i,\qquad
\sum_i|b_i|100^i\le525000000000,\\
&N(\beta)=105^4w^2,\qquad
1\le w\le312500000000000000000000.
\end{split}                                                    \tag{0.1}
\]
Every actual square lifts, and the original root is reconstructed exactly. Section 3 prints the full marked system, not just its norm projection.

There is a useful further transport. Each cofactor puts the source on a **fixed relative-norm equation in a quadratic algebra over the quintic field**:
\[
\begin{split}
&\xi^2=3150\beta,\qquad v=2n+1,\\
&\Omega=15\beta v+\gamma\xi,\\
&\boxed{\Omega\overline\Omega=15\beta^2(85+2\eta).}
\end{split}                                                    \tag{0.2}
\]
The bar fixes the quintic field and sends `xi` to `-xi`. There is no moving denominator here. This is an even-degree algebra, so it does not contradict P23's scoped odd-degree polynomial constant-norm obstruction.

**An actual separator:** the part of the cover with `deg beta <= 3` and `deg gamma <= 2` has only `n=1`. The proof is a polynomial factor argument plus a complete exact remainder through `n=132287`. Consequently any nontrivial S13 square would force, in every lift in our finite cover,

This is a statement about the explicitly marked cofactor cover, not a new unconditional square-fibre classification.

## 1. A coefficient norm and an elementary evaluation lemma

### 1.1. Explicit monic order

Let
\[
 f(X)=X^d+\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}f_iX^i\in\mathbb Z[X]
\]
be irreducible over the rationals, with `d>=2`. Put
\[
 K=\mathbb Q[X]/(f),\qquad \mathcal O=\mathbb Z[\theta],\quad f(\theta)=0.
\]
Every element of this order has a unique integer coefficient vector in the power basis. The norm `N(u)` means the determinant of multiplication by `u` on that basis. Its multiplicativity follows from multiplication of matrices. In particular, nonzero elements have nonzero norm, and a nonzero norm gives an inverse by the adjugate matrix. We do not assume that this order is maximal.

Choose integers `R>=1`, `G>=1` satisfying
\[
 \sum_{i<d}|f_i|R^i\le R^d,\qquad G^2\ge R^{d-1}.            \tag{1.1}
\]
Such integers are effective: for example first take `R=1+sum |f_i|`, then the least integer `G` whose square is at least `R^(d-1)`. Smaller choices can be certified by the same finite tests.

For a coefficient vector put
\[
       \left\|\sum u_i\theta^i\right\|_R=\sum |u_i|R^i.
\]
Reduction of `theta^d` by `f` gives
\[
 \|\theta u\|_R\le R\|u\|_R,
 \qquad \|uv\|_R\le\|u\|_R\|v\|_R.                         \tag{1.2}
\]
For the first inequality, each basis vector except the last shifts to the next weight; for the last use (1.1). Iteration and the triangle inequality give the second.

There is also the determinant bound
\[
                         |N(u)|\le\|u\|_R^d.               \tag{1.3}
\]
Indeed conjugate the multiplication matrix by the diagonal matrix with entries `1,R,...,R^(d-1)`. Its j-th column has sum of absolute values at most `||u||_R`, by (1.2). In the determinant expansion the sum over permutations is at most the product of the column sums: the latter expands into all choices of one entry per column, including all permutation choices. Diagonal conjugation changes no determinant. This proves (1.3) without embeddings or a geometric volume theorem.

For every integer `s>=2R`,
\[
 \tfrac12s^d\le f(s)\le\tfrac32s^d,
 \qquad \|(s-\theta)u\|_R\ge(s-R)\|u\|_R.                 \tag{1.4}
\]
For the polynomial estimate, use
\[
 \sum_{i<d}|f_i|s^i
 \le(s/R)^{d-1}\sum_{i<d}|f_i|R^i\le R s^{d-1}.
\]
The other estimate is the reverse triangle inequality and (1.2).

### 1.2. Evaluation divisibility controls a norm — PROVED

If `q>=1`, `q | f(s)`, and `q | g(s)` for `g in Z[X]`, then
\[
                              q\mid N(g(\theta)).          \tag{1.5}
\]
Here no primality, squarefreeness, derivative or discriminant condition is needed.

To prove it, reduce the order modulo `q`. Evaluation at `s` is a homomorphism to `Z/qZ`, because `f(s)=0` there. The basis
\[
 1,\quad X-s,\quad X(X-s),\ldots,X^{d-2}(X-s)
\]
is obtained from the power basis by an integer matrix of determinant one. The span of its last `d-1` members is the evaluation kernel and is stable under multiplication. In this basis the multiplication matrix of `g` is block triangular; its one-dimensional quotient block is `g(s)=0 mod q`. Its determinant is therefore zero modulo `q`. This proves (1.5) over a possibly composite residue ring.

For later use, polynomial division by `X-s` gives
\[
 H_s(X)=\frac{f(X)-f(s)}{X-s}\in\mathbb Z[X],\qquad
                 (s-\theta)H_s(\theta)=f(s).              \tag{1.6}
\]
Consequently `N(s-theta)=f(s)`. One can see the last identity either by the companion determinant, or by expanding `det(sI-M_theta)`; the result is precisely the monic polynomial `f`.

## 2. Bounded-cofactor extraction for any fixed target power

### Theorem 2 — PROVED

Fix the data in §1, and integers `a>=1`, `r>=2`. Suppose
\[
                    s\ge2R,\quad q\ge1,\quad f(s)=a q^r. \tag{2.1}
\]
Set
\[
 W=(dG)^d,\qquad
 B=\begin{cases}2a(dG)^r,&a\ge2,\\4(dG)^r,&a=1.\end{cases} \tag{2.2}
\]
There exist `gamma=g(theta) != 0` in the order, `beta != 0` in the order, and an integer `w != 0`, with
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 &\beta(s-\theta)=a\gamma^r,\qquad \|\beta\|_R\le B,\\
 &N(\gamma)=wq,\qquad N(\beta)=a^{d-1}w^r,\quad |w|\le W,\\
 &q\mid g(s),\qquad
 |g_i|^d R^{id}\le qG^d\quad(0\le i<d).
\end{aligned}}                                               \tag{2.3}
\]
Thus `beta,w` belong to a specified finite set depending only on `f,a,r,R,G`, not on the source point.

Conversely, the first two lines of (2.3), with `N(gamma)/w=q` a positive integer, imply (2.1). Any further endpoint or original-root divisibility conditions are retained separately. The box and evaluation conditions in the last line are additional necessary marks of the constructed cover; they are not needed for this converse.

### 2.1. The finite pigeonhole construction

Let
\[
             L_i=\left\lfloor\left(\frac{qG^d}{R^{id}}\right)^{1/d}\right\rfloor.
\]
All these integers can be obtained by comparisons of integer d-th powers; a real root is only notation. Strictly,
\[
 \prod_{i=0}^{d-1}(L_i+1)
 >\frac{qG^d}{R^{d(d-1)/2}}\ge q.                          \tag{2.4}
\]
Evaluate the polynomials with coefficients `0<=c_i<=L_i` at `s`, modulo `q`. Two have the same residue. Subtract them to obtain a nonzero `g`, of degree less than `d`, satisfying `q | g(s)` and `|g_i|<=L_i`. Irreducibility implies that `gamma=g(theta)` is nonzero. Also
\[
                          \|\gamma\|_R\le dGq^{1/d}.      \tag{2.5}
\]
This is a finite search with a proved collision, not a shortest-vector assumption or an LLL output accepted without verification.

### 2.2. Integrality: the denominator cancels exactly

Write `g(s)=qt` and
\[
 V_s(X)=\frac{g(X)^r-g(s)^r}{X-s}\in\mathbb Z[X].
\]
Define the order element
\[
                   \beta=t^rH_s(\theta)-aV_s(\theta).    \tag{2.6}
\]
Every coefficient is integral, including after reducing by the monic polynomial. Equations (1.6), (2.1), and the definition of `V_s` give
\[
 (s-\theta)\beta
 =t^rf(s)+a(g(\theta)^r-g(s)^r)=a\gamma^r.
\]
This proves the claimed extraction at **every** prime. It has not inferred square factors from coprime integer norms. No unbounded exceptional denominator remains concealed in (2.6).

### 2.3. The uniform cofactor bound

By (1.2), (1.4), and (2.5),
\[
 \|\beta\|_R
 \le\frac{a(dG)^r q^{r/d}}{s-R}
 =\frac{a(dG)^r(f(s)/a)^{1/d}}{s-R}.                       \tag{2.7}
\]
If `a>=2`, (1.4) gives `(f(s)/a)^(1/d)<=s`; and `s/(s-R)<=2`. If `a=1`, it suffices to use `f(s)^(1/d)<=2s`. This proves (2.2).

By (1.5), `w=N(gamma)/q` is an integer. It is nonzero. Equation (1.3) and (2.5) give `|w|<=W`. Taking norms of the extraction identity proves
\[
                              N(\beta)=a^{d-1}w^r.         \tag{2.8}
\]
Conversely, taking norms and dividing this nonzero value gives
\[
                         f(s)=a\left(\frac{N(\gamma)}w\right)^r,
\]
which proves the inverse assertion. All signs are retained: in general `w` may be negative. For odd degree and even `r`, replacing `gamma` by `-gamma` permits `N(gamma)>0` and `w>0`.

### 2.4. Literal cofactor-times-power form with one fixed denominator

The reciprocal orientation of (2.3) is often computationally better, but the requested form follows too. Put
\[
 \delta=a\beta^{r-1},\qquad \zeta=\gamma/\beta.
\]
Then
\[
                              s-\theta=\delta\zeta^r.       \tag{2.9}
\]
The cofactors `delta` belong to a fixed finite set. Moreover the **single point-independent integer**
\[
                         \mathcal D=a^{d-1}(W!)^r          \tag{2.10}
\]
clears the coefficients of every possible `zeta`. Indeed `|N(beta)|=a^(d-1)|w|^r` divides `D`, and the adjugate multiplication matrix expresses `beta^(-1)` with denominator dividing `N(beta)`.

There is no need to compute this enormous factorial. Its divisibility proof is the finite statement `|w|<=W => |w| | W!`. In implementations retain the integral equation (2.3) and the much smaller, fixed-per-label denominator `N(beta)`. Formula (2.10) proves that even their common denominator can be fixed before seeing a source point.

Extra primes can occur in `w`; the theorem does not restrict the cofactor to the original discriminant primes. But they are bounded by `W`. This deliberate finite enlargement is what avoids a principalization claim.

### 2.5. A retained contact congruence

Let `beta(X)` be the reduced polynomial representing (2.6), of degree less than `d`. Then
\[
                \beta(s)\equiv t^r f'(s)\pmod{q^{r-1}}.   \tag{2.11}
\]
The prime denotes the formal derivative of a finite polynomial. Before reduction, evaluation of (2.6) gives
\[
 t^rf'(s)-ar g(s)^{r-1}g'(s).
\]
The second term is divisible by `q^(r-1)`. Reducing a polynomial by `f` changes its value at `s` by a multiple of `f(s)=a q^r`. This proves (2.11).

For squares, if `p | q` and `p` divides neither `N(beta)` nor `f'(s)`, (2.11) implies that `t` is a p-unit and `beta(s)/f'(s)` is a nonzero square modulo p. To justify the first assertion, an invertible multiplication matrix for `beta` modulo p makes its image under evaluation at `s` a unit. This is a useful extra local mark of the cover, not a proof that all its sign choices agree.

### 3.3. A counter-control at the actual seed

A scalar square-norm shortcut already fails at `n=1`:
\[
 N(60-\alpha)=105\cdot900^2,
 \quad N(105(60-\alpha))=105^6\cdot900^2\text{ is a square},
\]
but
\[
                            105(60-\alpha)\notin K^{\times2}. \tag{3.10}
\]
Here is a finite certificate. Modulo 103, `f` is squarefree, has the root 54, and
\[
            105(60-54)\equiv12,\qquad12^{51}\equiv-1\pmod{103}.
\]
To see that this excludes a rational-field square, not just an order square, suppose `h^2` is integral at 103. If a coefficient of `h` has a 103-denominator, multiply by the smallest power `103^e`, `e>0`, making all coefficients integral there. Its nonzero reduction would have square zero in `F_103[X]/(f)`. A squarefree polynomial divides a polynomial square only if it divides that polynomial: factor into irreducibles and compare multiplicities. Thus this quotient has no nonzero square-zero element, a contradiction. So `h` is integral at 103 and may be evaluated at the root 54. It cannot square to 12. This proves (3.10).

The normalized candidate `(60-alpha)/105` has the same square class, since the two candidates differ by `105^2`. Thus even an actual source requires a genuinely nontrivial cofactor. The particular cofactor (3.9) correctly retains it.

## 4. A complete separator on the low-coordinate part of the cover

### 4.1. No-reduction lemma — PROVED

In the setting of §1, suppose
\[
             \beta(s-\theta)=a\,g(\theta)^r,
\quad \deg\beta\le d-2,\quad r\deg g<d,
\quad \beta\ne0.
\]
Then
\[
                                  \beta(s)=0.              \tag{4.1}
\]
Indeed both polynomials `beta(X)(s-X)` and `a g(X)^r` have degree less than `d`. Equality modulo the irreducible `f` is therefore equality as polynomials. Evaluation at `X=s` gives `g(s)=0`. The right side is divisible by `(X-s)^r`, so the left side has multiplicity at least `r` there. Its explicit factor `s-X` has multiplicity one, hence `beta(s)=0` because `r>=2`.

This argument is exactly where reduction by the defining polynomial matters. It is false to compare the unreduced coefficients when either degree reaches `d`.

If `||beta||_R<=B`, no positive integer root of the nonzero integer polynomial `beta` exceeds `B+1`. For `s>B+1`, the absolute value of its leading term exceeds the sum of the lower terms: bound each lower power by the next lower power and use that a nonzero integer leading coefficient has absolute value at least one, while the sum of the other absolute coefficients is at most `B`. Constant nonzero `beta` has no root at all.

### 4.2. An all-height subcover classification for S13 — PROVED + CHECKED

For the cover (3.6)–(3.8), suppose `b4=0` and `g3=g4=0`. Lemma 4.1 applies. Thus `s<=B+1`. But
\[
 30\cdot132288\cdot132289-35=525007416925>B+1,
\]
while the same expression at `n=132287` is `524999479645<=B+1`.
Every point in this subcover has `n<=132287`. The certificate sums `i^13` consecutively over precisely that whole interval and checks the integer square root at every endpoint; the complete hit list is `[1]`. Therefore
\[
 \boxed{\text{(3.6)--(3.8), }b_4=g_3=g_4=0
                   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad n=z=y=1.}    \tag{4.2}
\]
The finite prefix is not used to claim anything about the unrestricted fibre. It completes the bounded remainder proved by the no-reduction lemma.

On the seed cofactor itself, an even shorter argument applies. If `beta=beta0` and `deg gamma<=2`, then
\[
                         g(X)^2=(25-X)(s-X).
\]
Its quadratic leading coefficient gives, after the fourth- and third-degree coefficients force the quadratic coefficient of `g` to vanish,
`b^2=1`, `2ab=-(25+s)`, `a^2=25s`. Hence
`(25+s)^2=100s`, or `(s-25)^2=0`. Thus `s=25,n=1`. This is an exact positive control for a retained-root return, not an unproved degree bound on all extracted roots.

## 5. A fixed-relative-norm transport retaining the endpoint

### 5.1. The quadratic algebra — PROVED

For a fixed S13 cofactor `beta`, define the free rank-ten ring
\[
 \mathcal R_\beta=\mathcal O[\xi],\qquad \xi^2=3150\beta.
\]
It is represented by pairs of five-coordinate vectors. No assumption that its rational algebra is a field is necessary; it may split over `K`. Define conjugation by `(A+B xi)bar=A-B xi`.

With `v=2n+1`, the field equation of (3.7) is exactly
\[
                210\gamma^2=\beta(15v^2-85-2\eta).
\]
Consequently (0.2) holds with
\[
                 C_\beta=15\beta^2(85+2\eta)\ne0.          \tag{5.1}
\]
Conversely, an element of this norm locus returns to (3.7) **only** if its two coordinates have the form
\[
                 \Omega=(15\beta v,\gamma),
\quad v\in2\mathbb Z+1,\quad v\ge3,
\quad n=(v-1)/2,
\]
and the norm, evaluation, short-vector, and original-root divisibility marks in (3.7)–(3.8) hold. The fixed relative norm alone is a necessary projection, not the original problem.

The rational algebra inverse has one fixed label-dependent denominator:
\[
                         \Omega^{-1}=\overline\Omega/C_\beta.
\]
Multiplication by `N(C_beta)` clears its order coefficients, by the quintic adjugate formula. In contrast to normalization by the variable source norm, this denominator no longer grows with `n` on the branch.

The exact determinants are
\[
\begin{split}
 N_K(85+2\eta)&=29028628125=15^5\cdot7\cdot43\cdot127,\\
 N_{\mathcal R_\beta}(\Omega)
     &=N_K(C_\beta)
       =15^{10}\cdot7\cdot43\cdot127\,N_K(\beta)^2.
\end{split}                                                   \tag{5.2}
\]
For the second equality, the multiplication matrix is a two-by-two block matrix of commuting quintic multiplication matrices. Eliminating its invertible first block yields the determinant of multiplication by `A^2-3150 beta B^2`; here `A=15 beta v` is nonzero in `K`. This is finite matrix elimination. The verifier also checks it directly at the seed with a ten-by-ten determinant.

This does not assert that `Omega` is a unit. Its norm is the nonunit constant (5.2).

### 5.3. An exact finite unit-quotient gate

Here is a small lemma needed before applying P23's finite-increment criterion.

**PROVED.** Let `R` be an explicit free commutative integer ring of finite rank. Let `A,X in R` have the same nonzero multiplication determinant `c`. Then `X/A` is an integral unit of `R` if and only if
\[
                       \operatorname{adj}(M_A)X\equiv0\pmod{|c|}. \tag{5.5}
\]
The vector `X` means its coefficient vector; `M_A` is multiplication by the fixed anchor. The displayed condition is precisely integrality of `M_A^(-1)X`. If it holds, the quotient has determinant one, so its integer multiplication matrix has an integer inverse; applying that inverse to 1 gives its inverse in the ring. The converse is immediate. In a quadratic algebra with equal relative norms the quotient additionally has relative norm one.

This finite test can separate classes that equality of integer norms does not. In `Z[sqrt(2)]`, the elements `3+sqrt(2)` and `13+9sqrt(2)` have norm 7 and their quotient is the unit `3+2sqrt(2)`. But `3-sqrt(2)` also has norm 7, and
\[
          \frac{3-\sqrt2}{3+\sqrt2}=\frac{11-6\sqrt2}{7}
\]
is not integral in that order. The verifier checks both outcomes of (5.5).

There are finitely many multiplication-lattice classes at a fixed nonzero determinant `c`: the adjugate identity gives
`cR subset XR subset R`, so `XR` is the inverse image of a subgroup of the finite group `R/cR`. Also `XR=AR` is equivalent to an integral unit quotient. This is an elementary finiteness statement about explicit integer subgroups.

**The additional certificate still required:** for every multiplication-lattice class that can contain a marked source, provide an actual anchor of determinant `c`, or prove that class contains no source. Listing all subgroups modulo `c` is not a proof that each is generated by one element. The present answer does not supply those anchors for the S13 rings (5.1). Once anchors are supplied, (5.5) and a fully covered finite-increment exclusion are legitimate next steps. Before that, the phrase “take the unit quotient” is unjustified.

## 6. Two precise obstructions to automatic separation

### 6.1. The variable rational norm-one normalizer has an unavoidable denominator

**PROVED.** Let `d>=2`, and let `p | f(s)` with `p` not dividing `f'(s)`. If a nonzero integer `L` makes
\[
                 L\,\frac{(s-\theta)^d}{f(s)}
\]
integral at p in the power-basis order, then
\[
                              v_p(L)\ge v_p(f(s)).         \tag{6.1}
\]
Here “integral at p” means rational coefficients with denominators prime to p; no completion is used.

Put `e=v_p(f(s))` and work modulo `p^e`. The polynomial
`H_s=(f(X)-f(s))/(X-s)` is monic of degree `d-1`, and `H_s(s)=f'(s)` is a unit. The quotient
\[
             A_e=(\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z)[X]/(H_s)
\]
is a free, nonzero module. The element `s-X` is invertible in it: subtracting `H_s(s)` from `H_s(X)` and dividing by `X-s` gives its explicit inverse up to the unit `H_s(s)`. Also `f(X)=0` in this quotient.

Reducing the proposed integral equality
`f(s)u=L(s-theta)^d` into `A_e` gives `0=L` times a unit. In the free module `A_e`, a rational p-integral scalar vanishes modulo `p^e` exactly when it is divisible by `p^e`. This proves (6.1).

For example in `theta^3=2`, the source `f(3)=25` gives
\[
             \frac{(3-\theta)^3}{25}
                  =\frac{25-27\theta+9\theta^2}{25},
\]
with norm one and exact denominator 25. In the same example the new extraction works integrally:
\[
 (2+2\theta+\theta^2)(3-\theta)=(2+\theta)^2,
 \quad N(2+2\theta+\theta^2)=4.
\]
The source `3-theta` is not a square in the field: at the simple root `theta=4 mod 31`, it is `-1`, a nonsquare; the integrality argument from §3.3 applies.

Equation (6.1) is pointwise. It does not assume or assert that there are infinitely many S13 sources. It says exactly what denominator is forced **if** a source contains the indicated prime. It rules out using a fixed normalizer on all such points without proving a bound on these source prime powers.

### 6.2. A fixed cofactor still has a smooth family of finite local marks

**PROVED.** Suppose a fixed cofactor equation
\[
              \beta(s_0-\theta)=a\gamma_0^2
\]
has an integral seed, and `p` is odd with `p` not dividing `a N(gamma0)`. For every integer `s=s0 mod p`, and every `N>=1`, there is a unique coefficient vector `gamma=gamma0 mod p` modulo `p^N` satisfying
\[
                         a\gamma^2=\beta(s-\theta)\pmod{p^N\mathcal O}. \tag{6.2}
\]
If a root is known modulo `p^j`, replacing it by `gamma+p^j h` changes the next digit of the square by `2a gamma h`. Multiplication by `2a gamma` is invertible modulo p, by its nonzero determinant. Exactly one next digit vector solves the equation. Finite induction proves (6.2). The other initial sign is available as well.

On the S13 seed label (3.9), take `p=11`. The ledger divisors `900w0` are also units there. Every `n=1 mod 11` gives `s=s0 mod 11`; taking the norm of (6.2) reconstructs the original square congruence with
\[
                     z\equiv N(\gamma)/(900w_0)\pmod{11^N}.
\]
The replay constructs this explicitly at `N=8` for `n=12,23,34,122`, and checks that their actual integer sums are not squares. These are local controls, not claimed solutions.

Thus extraction is not already a full-rank local isolation theorem on all the cover variables. The multiplication matrix of `2a gamma0` is invertible while the endpoint remains free. A successful P23 application needs an independently covered **unit orbit with the right number of indices and all retained markers**, not merely the nonsingular square-root equation (6.2).

## 7. Independent audit of Terra's contact and its surviving signs

This section checks the proposed constants rather than assuming them. It does not replace Terra's work on the mixed-colour return.

### 7.1. The monochrome-block bound is valid — PROVED

For an S13 square, `n=1 mod 3`: if `3 | T`, then `P(T)=-691 mod 3` is a unit and (3.1) has odd 3-valuation `2v3(T)-1`. For `n>1` write
\[
 n=3v-2,\quad u=v-1\ge1,\quad X=T-2=9uv.
\]
Set
\[
\begin{split}
 Q(X)&=781X^3+1551X^2+3150X+1890,\quad M=1890,\\
 R(X)&=609961X^2+1402062X+3073401,\\
 C&=2^4 3^{12}5^4 7^4=12759898410000.
\end{split}
\]
The exact identity is
\[
                   Q(X)^2-M^2P(2+X)/105=X^4R(X).          \tag{7.1}
\]
At an actual square, `D_-=Q-Mz` and `D_+=Q+Mz` are strictly positive and less than `2Q`; their product is `X^4R(X)`.

For each prime `p | uv`, at least one sign has
\[
                         p^{4v_p(uv)}\mid C D_{\pm}.       \tag{7.2}
\]
If `p` does not divide `2M`, the two factors are coprime at p because `Q(X)=M mod p`. If `p | 2M` and `v_p(X)>v_p(M)`, then `v_p(Q(X))=v_p(M)` and the shared valuation is at most `v_p(2M)`. If instead `v_p(X)<=v_p(M)`, the exponent supplied by `C` is at least `4v_p(uv)` already. These are exactly the losses built into the displayed `C`.

Assume both blocks admit monochrome choices, with the two block signs either equal or opposite. If they are equal, coprimality gives `(uv)^4 | C D_sign`. Since for `X>=1`, `Q(X)<=7372 X^3`,
\[
                              uv<10748376 C.              \tag{7.3}
\]
If the block signs are opposite, write `CD_1=u^4 A`, `CD_2=v^4 B` with positive integers `A,B`. Their sum is `2CQ`. Reducing it modulo `v^4` gives
\[
\begin{split}
 A&\equiv C H(v)\pmod{v^4},\\
 H(v)&=-900774v^3+118962v^2-41580v+3780.                  \tag{7.4}
\end{split}
\]
Indeed the inverse of `(v-1)^4` modulo `v^4` is the finite polynomial
`sum_{j=0}^3 binom(3+j,j)v^j`; the verifier multiplies this by `2Q(9v(v-1))` and keeps exactly its first four coefficients.

For every `v>=1`, `H(v)<0` and `|H(v)|<900774v^3`. The first follows by grouping the cubic with the positive quadratic term and the negative linear with the constant. For the second use `118962v^2-41580v+3780>0`. Thus for
\[
                         v\ge1801548 C,                   \tag{7.5}
\]
the least nonnegative residue in (7.4) is greater than `v^4/2`.
On the other hand `u=v-1>=v/2` for `v>=2`, so
\[
\begin{split}
 A&<\frac{2CQ(9uv)}{u^4}\\
  &\le C(2277396v^2+1005048+113400+3780)\\
  &\le2557953 C v^2.                                    \tag{7.6}
\end{split}
\]
The integer inequality `1801548^2 > 2*2557953` contradicts (7.4)–(7.6) at (7.5). The same threshold contradicts (7.3), since `uv>=v^2/2` and `1801548^2>2*10748376`. We have independently proved the claimed weaker bound
\[
          \boxed{v\le22987569460738680000}
\]
for every block-monochrome source.

This is an effective bound, not a completed search of that interval. Mixed blocks are not covered by its hypothesis.

### 7.2. Mixed signs persist even with the seed cofactor fixed — CHECKED control and general finite explanation

At `n=1717`, `u=572=4*11*13`, `v=573`. Modulo 11 and 13, the tentative root `z=12` respectively has signs `+1` and `-1` relative to the contact at `T=2`. The two primes lie in the **same** block.

Lifting the two orientations to fourth powers and combining them gives
\[
\boxed{
 n=1717,\quad z=776931\pmod{418161601},\quad
 418161601=11^4 13^4.}
\]
The exact checks are
\[
\begin{split}
 &P(T)=105z^2\pmod{11^4 13^4},\\
 &11^4\mid Q(X)-1890z,\qquad13^4\mid Q(X)+1890z,\\
 &P(T)\ne105\cdot776931^2\quad\text{as integers}.
\end{split}
\]
More strongly, this same local control lifts to the **same cofactor** `beta0` of (3.9). At 11 start with `gamma0`, and at 13 with `-gamma0`, apply the finite digit construction in §6.2, and combine the five coefficient vectors by CRT. The norm reconstruction gives exactly the displayed `z` modulo `11^4 13^4`.

Therefore neither a finite original-sum contact ledger nor fixing this cofactor automatically forces block monochromy. This is a precise local obstruction; it does not show that a genuine integer S13 square with mixed signs exists, or that a global marked return cannot rule one out.

## 8. The even/odd interface and an honest next certificate

The common mechanism now has an actual extraction theorem rather than a square-norm slogan:

1. Retain the original Faulhaber root and every scalar/anchor divisibility mark.
2. Pass to a monic irreducible factor equation and apply the finite coefficient-box theorem, with its bounded cofactor and norm-debt `w`.
3. Use either a polynomial no-reduction separator, the fixed-relative-norm lift, or another **proved** marked transition.
4. Before applying a unit-increment argument, pass the integral quotient gate and cover all its remaining multiplication-lattice classes. Check the actual endpoint/root marker on every terminal.

This is a finite cover of the sources; step 4 is not yet a complete global separator. The bound on cofactor coordinates is not a decreasing rank on the source endpoint.

For even k this can be added to a surviving row of the existing graph without losing its fourth root. It is useful precisely when area projection alone has lost information. The positive controls `q=330` and `q=14066106,n=38034750624` from P21/P22 remain untouched: a right-triangle or three-anchor point is not a Faulhaber square unless its last root and reconstruction also pass. Our elementary norm-7 control in §5.3 illustrates the analogous error at the unit-quotient stage.

There is **no new proof of infinitely many complete even fibres** in this answer. A precise broadened sufficient input remains: infinitely many actual Bernoulli/Mersenne data sets have a complete source graph in which every row is the cannonball terminal or has a finite, terminating marked certificate, now permitted to use Theorem 2 and (5.5). This is a certificate specification, not a proved arithmetic-distribution assertion, and is broader but less purely arithmetic than P23's fixed explicit predicate `G(ell)=0`. The latter predicate and its unproved infinitude target remain the clean single arithmetic statement. This answer does not infer their infinitude from the finiteness of each new cofactor cover.
