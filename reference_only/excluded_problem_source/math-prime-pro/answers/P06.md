## Part I. The general cubic seed theorem

### I.1. Precise verdict

PROVED: every irreducible binary cubic has an explicit quadratic-algebra presentation suitable for the binomial Pade construction. A primitive seed determines an exact quality test. If that test holds, the construction gives an effective irrationality exponent below 3 for one specified real root. For a cubic with negative discriminant there is only one real root, so this supplies all Archimedean root coverage. For a totally real cyclic cubic, the quadratic algebra is Q(omega), and multiplication by cube roots of unity supplies all three branches. For a totally real non-Galois cubic, one must separately cover its three real-root cones; one successful branch does not automatically cover the other two.

UNPROVED here: that every fixed cubic field has a seed passing this particular test, or that such a seed always occurs below a universal effective search bound. Neither the Kummer representation nor Dirichlet's quadratic approximation scale proves this. The spectral obstruction only rules out specified regular unit-ray constructions; it does not rule out an isolated exceptional continued-fraction seed.

The theorem is deliberately explicit about its denominator cost. A large ratio of real and imaginary parts, without its integral lattice, is insufficient.

### I.2. Cubic covariants and the correct quadratic algebra

Let

    F(X,Y)=A X^3+B X^2Y+C XY^2+D Y^3

be irreducible over Q, with integer coefficients and discriminant

    Delta=B^2 C^2-4AC^3-4B^3D-27A^2D^2+18ABCD != 0.

Define

    H(X,Y)=h_0 X^2+h_1 XY+h_2 Y^2,
    h_0=B^2-3AC,
    h_1=BC-9AD,
    h_2=C^2-3BD,
    J=F_Y H_X-F_X H_Y.

Explicitly,

    J=(2B^3-9ABC+27A^2D)X^3
      +3(B^2C-6AC^2+9ABD)X^2Y
      +3(6B^2D-BC^2-9ACD)XY^2
      +(9BCD-27AD^2-2C^3)Y^3.

Direct multiplication proves

    h_1^2-4h_0 h_2=-3Delta,
    J^2+27Delta F^2=4H^3.                         (C1)

These are polynomial identities over Z; an exact symbolic verifier is supplied separately.

Write

    -3Delta=d w^2,

where d is a nonzero squarefree integer and w is a positive integer. Use the quadratic algebra

    K_2=Q[rho]/(rho^2-d),     bar(rho)=-rho,
    s=w rho, so s^2=-3Delta.

If d=1 this is the split algebra Q x Q; this case must not be discarded. Its two evaluations are rho=1 and rho=-1. Otherwise it is a quadratic field. The explicit integer lattice O is Z[(1+rho)/2] when d=1 mod4, and Z[rho] otherwise. No ideal theory or class group is involved.

The compositum of the cubic’s normal closure with the following base is a cyclic cubic extension of that base:

    Q(sqrt(Delta),omega)=Q(sqrt(-3Delta),omega).

That is the Kummer base. The coefficient identities below already live over K_2, so adjoining both quadratic radicals is unnecessary for the actual Pade computation. When Delta is a rational square, K_2=Q(omega); this is the cyclic case. For nonsquare positive Delta, K_2 is a different imaginary quadratic field and does not contain omega.

Here is the diagonalization in a form requiring no abstract Kummer theorem. If h_0!=0, put

    ell(X,Y)=2h_0 X+(h_1+s)Y,

Direct multiplication, reducing s^2 to -3Delta, gives

This displays the two cubes and their common quadratic coefficient field. At a root of F, their quotient gives a cube-root equation.

### I.3. The seed and its exact arithmetic score

Take a primitive integer seed v=(p,q). Let

    f=F(p,q), h=H(p,q), j=J(p,q).

Assume h>0 and j!=0, and change v to -v if necessary so that j>0. For Delta>0 the condition h>0 is automatic: the Hessian is positive definite. For Delta<0 it is a genuine seed condition, satisfied by sufficiently close approximations to the real root.

Since F is irreducible, f!=0. Choose an integer complement v' so that N=(v,v') has determinant 1. This is just Bezout in Z. Replacing F by F composed with N does not change Delta, and its h_0 and J(1,0) become h and j. The covariance identities follow directly by differentiating the change of variables. Denote the middle Hessian coefficient of this transformed cubic by b_H.

Put

    U_*=(j+3wf rho)/2.

It belongs to O. Indeed its trace j and norm h^3 are integers, and explicitly j^2-9d w^2f^2=4h^3 forces the necessary parities in the displayed basis of O.

Let g be the positive gcd of the two integer coordinates of U_* in that basis. Define

    U=U_*/g, V=bar(U),
    L=U+V=j/g,
    D_0=|U-V|^2=27|Delta|f^2/g^2,
    lambda=L+2sqrt(UV)=(j+2h^(3/2))/g.             (C3)

The notation |U-V| in (C3) means ordinary complex absolute value when Delta>0 and ordinary real absolute value when Delta<0. D_0 is a positive integer. Also UV=h^3/g^2>0.

For Delta>0, U and V are complex conjugates with positive real part, and

    lambda=L+sqrt(L^2+D_0).

For Delta<0, U and V are positive real numbers in the two evaluations, and

    lambda=L+sqrt(L^2-D_0).

Define the exact denominator-loss rate

    v_3=v_3(D_0),
    gamma=3^(max(0,3-v_3)/2).                    (C4)

The score for this certified construction is

    score(v)=lambda/(gamma^3 D_0^2).             (C5)

PROVED THEOREM. If score(v)>1, then the construction below gives an effective rational approximation bound for a specified real root z of F composed with N, in the coordinate Y/X:

    |z-P/Q| >= 1/[K Q^(1+kappa)]

for all integers P and positive Q satisfying an explicit small-denominator cutoff, where

    Q_rate=gamma lambda,
    E_rate=lambda/(gamma D_0),
    kappa=log(gamma lambda)/log(lambda/(gamma D_0)) < 2.  (C6)

The constant K is explicit from the initial conditions and the root projection. The displayed kappa is the exact logarithmic ratio of the certified exponential bases for this normalization; it is not asserted to be the best possible irrationality exponent or the optimal denominator normalization. In particular the strict inequality kappa<2 is equivalent to the exact seed test

    lambda>gamma^3 D_0^2.                       (C7)

The remaining finitely many small denominators can be checked directly. Thus this is an effective separator for that root. The constants may be deliberately generous in a fixed equation; the exponent threshold is the decisive point.

#### Presentation invariance of the normalized score

PROVED. Let F'=c F composed with M be another integer binary cubic, with c a nonzero rational number and M in GL2(Q). Transport a rational projective seed by M. After clearing its coordinates to a primitive integer pair and normalizing U_* by its full quadratic-order content as above, the pair (U,V) is unchanged up to interchange. In particular lambda, D_0, gamma and the score are unchanged. The fixed projection constants and the pullback lattice can change; the exponential quality test does not.

Proof. The chain rule and the displayed covariants give

    H_(F')=c^2 det(M)^2 H_F composed with M,
    J_(F')=c^3 det(M)^3 J_F composed with M,
    Delta_(F')=c^4 det(M)^6 Delta_F.

Choose the coherent square root s'=c^2 det(M)^3 s. Then

    U_*'(v)=c^3 det(M)^3 U_*(Mv).

Writing Mv as a nonzero rational scalar times its primitive integer representative introduces a further rational cube. Thus U_*' is a nonzero rational multiple of the original U_*. If an integer coordinate vector is primitive, every rational multiple of it that is again integral has an integer scaling factor; this follows from an ordinary integer Bezout relation between its coordinates. Dividing by the full content therefore removes this scale. Choosing positive trace removes a possible minus sign, and choosing the other sign of s merely interchanges U and V. This proves the assertion.

Every pair of nonrational generators theta,eta of a cubic field is related by a rational fractional-linear transformation: the four elements 1,theta,eta,theta*eta are linearly dependent in a three-dimensional Q-vector space. Solving the resulting relation for eta gives such a transformation; its determinant is nonzero because otherwise eta would be rational. Therefore, for the precise fully normalized binomial construction in this theorem, existence of a passing seed is independent of the chosen cubic presentation. Re-presenting the field can make a certificate easy to find and compute, but does not create a passing score without a corresponding projective seed in the original presentation. Nothing here proves that a passing seed exists in every cubic field.

### I.4. The Pade construction and its denominator certificate

Let alpha=1/3 and define, for n>=0,

    P_n(z)=sum_(i=0)^n binom(n-alpha,i)binom(2n-i,n)z^i,
    Q_n(z)=sum_(i=0)^n binom(n+alpha,i)binom(2n-i,n)z^i.

The letter Q_n in these two polynomial formulas is unrelated to the final rational denominator. Put

    zeta=(V-U)/U,
    A_n=U^n P_n(zeta),
    bar(A_n)=U^n Q_n(zeta).

The conjugation formula follows from

    Q_n(z)=(1+z)^n P_n(-z/(1+z)).

The exact remainder, with the branch W=(V/U)^(1/3) chosen near 1 in the complex-conjugate case and positive in the real case, is

    W P_n(zeta)-Q_n(zeta)
      =c_n zeta^(2n+1)
         integral_0^1 t^n(1-t)^n(1+zeta t)^(-n-2/3)dt,

    c_n=(1/3) product_(r=1)^n(1-1/(9r^2)), 0<c_n<=1/3.    (C8)

Appendix A proves this by expanding a finite polynomial and integrating each power, so it is an exact elementary identity. The same finite antiderivative permits the path change used below whenever U and V are in the right half-plane. The consecutive polynomial determinant is

    P_n Q_(n+1)-P_(n+1)Q_n=d_n z^(2n+1),
    d_n=2c_n/(n+1)>0.                              (C9)

Consequently A_n bar(A_(n+1))-A_(n+1)bar(A_n) is nonzero.

The integral coefficients of the quadratic algebra require a specific correction when primitive content has removed some powers of 3. Define

    N_n=3^floor(max(0,3-v_3(D_0))*n/2),
    B_n=N_n A_n.                                  (C10)

Then B_n belongs to O, and N_n<=gamma^n.

Proof. A coefficient of A_n is an integer binomial factor times

    (V-U)^i product_(r=0)^(i-1)(3n-1-3r)/(3^i i!).

At every prime ell!=3, the arithmetic progression with step 3 supplies at least v_ell(i!) factors, so no denominator at ell remains. Put delta=V-U. Since delta^2 is either D_0 or -D_0, and delta is a trace-zero integer in O, delta^i is divisible in O by

    3^floor(i v_3(D_0)/2).

This is also immediate from delta=c rho and the squarefreeness of d. The remaining denominator needs at most i+v_3(i!) powers of 3, and

    v_3(i!)<=floor(i/2).

For v_3(D_0)=0,1,2 or >=3, respectively, the exponent in N_n supplies the deficit for every i<=n. These four cases are a direct floor inequality. This proves (C10). No unique factorization in O is used.

The multiplier is safe, not necessarily optimal. A further exact gcd certificate can improve it; such improvement must be recorded rather than assumed.

The homogeneous polynomial A_n obeys

This is a finite coefficient identity. One direct verification uses

    A_n=sum_(r=0)^n binom(n-1/3,r)binom(n+1/3,n-r)V^r U^(n-r).

### I.7. Root coverage is a separate hypothesis

If Delta<0, there is one real root. Outside its bounded neighborhood the norm form cannot be small relative to the cube of the coordinate height. The separator therefore bounds all solutions of F(x,y)=h_0 for every fixed nonzero h_0.

If Delta>0 is a square, K_2=Q(omega). Multiplying B_n by omega^r, r=0,1,2, supplies the three cube-root branches. It preserves the integer lattice and the exponential bases. Consequently one good seed supplies effective bounds in all three real-root cones, with separately recorded constants. The Thomas automorphism R is an especially simple way to express this branch transport. Explicitly, if W_r=omega^r W and B_(n,r)=omega^r B_n, then W_r B_(n,r)-bar(B_(n,r))=omega^(-r)(W B_n-bar(B_n)), so the same error bound holds. The conjugation identity M R M^-1=R^2 checked in the question is precisely what justifies transporting the finite normalized P05 representatives and then taking their complete automorphism orbits.

If Delta>0 is nonsquare, multiplication by omega leaves K_2. The two-coordinate rational extraction in (C17) then no longer applies. The theorem proves one specified branch only. A complete elementary solution may use three independently certified seeds, or any other explicit mechanism covering the remaining cones. Merely adjoining omega and retaining the same one-place estimates would omit extra coordinates and embeddings; it is not a proof.

Once every real-root cone is covered, combine the lower bound with the elementary norm upper bound

    |theta-x/y|<=C |h_0|/|y|^3.

Because kappa<2, this bounds |y| explicitly. The finitely many other cones and small denominators are bounded directly. Continued fractions and exact integer evaluation then give a complete finite certificate, as in P05. This argument needs neither unit coverage nor a unit theorem.


#### I.7a. A precise obstruction to an automatic rational root cycle

PROVED. If an irreducible rational cubic has nonsquare discriminant, no nonidentity rational fractional-linear transformation permutes its three roots. The permutation induced by such a transformation must commute with every Galois permutation, because its coefficients are rational. The Galois permutations form S_3, whose centralizer in S_3 is its trivial center. Thus the transformation fixes all three roots. A nonidentity fractional-linear map cannot fix three distinct points, so it must be the identity. This elementary observation explains why the cyclic all-root automorphism is extra structure. It does not prohibit three separate seeds or a construction with a larger, explicitly controlled coefficient lattice.

### I.8. Exactly how exceptional a good seed must be

The content g cannot provide an unbounded free improvement for primitive seeds of one fixed form. Indeed g divides gcd(J(v),3wF(v)). The homogeneous resultant identity is

    Res(F,J)=8Delta^3.

To verify the constant, for f(x)=F(x,1) one has

    H(x,1)=f'(x)^2-(3/2)f(x)f''(x).

At a root alpha of f,

    H(alpha,1)=f'(alpha)^2,
    J(alpha,1)=-2f'(alpha)^3.

The ordinary product formula for the resultant then gives 8Delta^3. The Sylvester-adjugate identities show, for primitive p,q, that gcd(F(p,q),J(p,q)) divides this fixed resultant. Consequently

    g divides 24wDelta^3, and in particular g<=24w|Delta|^3.   (C19)

This rough bound suffices for the following precise asymptotic statement; better fixed contents can be used in computations.

PROVED. Fix a real root theta of F(x,1) and a compact rational interval I around it containing no other root, on which H(x,1)>0. There are explicit positive constants c_1,c_2 and a finite threshold such that, for primitive p,q with q>0, p/q in I and q beyond the threshold:

    |theta-p/q|<c_1 q^(-9/4) implies the seed score exceeds 1;

    seed score exceeds 1 implies |theta-p/q|<c_2 q^(-9/4).

    m_1 q^3 |theta-p/q|<=|F(p,q)|<=m_2 q^3 |theta-p/q|

    (|J|+2H^(3/2))g^3

    c_1=[c_L/(Gamma_max^3 C_D m_2^4)]^(1/4),
    c_2=[C_L G_max^3/(C_D m_1^4)]^(1/4)

follow by using 1<=g<=G_max and 1<=gamma<=Gamma_max. Polynomial root isolation and rational upper/lower bounds make all constants effective; no logarithmic-form estimate occurs. The exponent is 9/4 because q^3 competes with q^12 times the fourth power of the rational approximation error.

Here is the promised identification of the nearby branch, including the choice of complement. First take q>0 and choose p s'-q r=1 with |s'|<=q/2; for q=1, take s'=0 and r=-1. Since p/q stays in the fixed compact interval I, both r and s' are O(q), with explicit constants. Orient the whole matrix N or -N so that j>0; this does not change its projective coordinate. Put epsilon=p/q-theta. The coordinate of theta in the N-chart is exactly

    z=(p-theta q)/(theta s'-r)
      =q^2 epsilon/(1-s' q epsilon).

For epsilon=O(q^(-9/4)), the denominator tends to1, and z=O(q^(-1/4)) tends to0. Since h is bounded above and below by positive multiples of q^2 and b_H=O(q^2),

    [2h+(b_H+s)z]/[2h+(b_H-s)z] tends to1.

Its cube equals V/U by (C2), so for all sufficiently large q it is exactly the cube-root branch near1 used in (C16). All cutoffs follow from the displayed interval bounds. This identification is independent of the complement: replacing v' by v'+kv changes z to z/(1-kz) and each of ell and bar(ell) by the same nonzero real factor 1/(1-kz), so their ratio does not change.

In particular every sufficiently large good seed is a continued-fraction convergent: the necessary bound c_2 q^(-9/4) is below 1/(2q^2) once q>(2c_2)^4. This gives a principled seed search, but not a theorem that the search must succeed. More explicitly, if p_j/q_j is a continued-fraction convergent and a_(j+1) is its next partial quotient, then

    1/[(a_(j+1)+2)q_j^2] < |theta-p_j/q_j| < 1/[a_(j+1)q_j^2].

A sufficiently large passing seed therefore requires a_(j+1)+2>q_j^(1/4)/c_2. Conversely a_(j+1)>q_j^(1/4)/c_1 is sufficient once the convergent lies in I and the branch cutoff is passed. This is the precise exceptional-approximation requirement for this score.

A hypothetical solution F(p,q)=h_0 of very large height is itself automatically a good seed, because its norm is fixed while H grows quadratically. That observation does not locate the seed. Using an unknown large solution as the input to the separator is not an effective absolute bound; it is the familiar one-exceptional-approximation principle, with the exceptional approximation still to be found.

### I.9. What can and cannot be claimed about existence

PROVED obstruction for a stated mechanism: consider exactly the fixed-linear unit-ray construction of P04. Let epsilon be a unit generating the cubic field, with a strictly dominant conjugate of modulus Q_0>1, and let theta generate the same field. For fixed B in the cubic field with B!=0 and a fixed denominator-clearing integer D, take

    q_n=D Tr(B epsilon^n),
    p_n=D Tr(theta B epsilon^n).

Put rho=max_(i!=0)|epsilon_i|. Assume, in addition, that the residual modes have the regular noncancelling rate |theta q_n-p_n|=rho^(n+o(n)), and that gcd(p_n,q_n)=exp(o(n)); the latter excludes an exponential common-factor gain. Strict dominance gives |q_n|=Q_0^(n+o(n)). The unit norm gives rho>=Q_0^(-1/2). Therefore the rational approximation exponent, measured against the reduced denominator, is

    1-log(rho)/log(Q_0)<=3/2,

whereas the present seed threshold is9/4. These regular rays cannot furnish arbitrarily large passing seeds. In a totally real cubic the two residual moduli are unequal, so their noncancelling rate follows by elementary dominance. For complex conjugate residual modes, the P04 Vandermonde argument supplies a blockwise rate obstruction, but does not rule out rare exceptionally small individual errors; the extra regularity hypothesis above must not be silently dropped. An isolated exceptional seed, exponential gcd cancellation, or a changing auxiliary construction lies outside this obstruction.

Dirichlet's quadratic approximation scale likewise does not force a 9/4-scale seed. A finite unsuccessful continued-fraction search cannot certify that none exists later. Conversely, the spectral theorem does not rule out even one exceptionally good convergent. It would be incorrect to conclude that a cubic with no currently found seed needs Baker, or that every cubic has a good seed by Kummer theory.

UNPROVED in this answer: a universal good-seed existence bound for this specific binomial construction. This includes the field-level assertion that every fixed cubic field admits some presentation and seed passing the test: allowing a new generator or a rational change of presentation has not been proved to force success. The proved theorem starts from specified finite arithmetic data and verifies it. Failure on a particular search range is a failure of a certificate, not an impossibility theorem for the equation. More general simultaneous Pade constructions, a sharper denominator/gcd certificate, or a second covering may succeed even when the score above never exceeds 1.

For historical context, the quadratic coefficient-field construction belongs to the classical Thue hypergeometric method; see [Voutier, *Thue’s Fundamentaltheorem, I*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0805.3176), Lemmas 3.2–3.3. The proof here derives its needed identities and arithmetic bounds explicitly.

## Part II. Quartic and higher-degree separators

The statements labelled PROVED below include their elementary proofs. An actual quartic/octic Thue form for the Fibonacci reduction was not supplied; the distinction between those unprovided forms and the displayed quartic is essential.

### II.1. Main answer

PROVED: transported binomial separators extend to special quartic and higher-degree binary forms which are sums of two powers of linear forms over a quadratic field. This class has an exact finite structural test using a Hankel/catalecticant matrix. In the imaginary quadratic case, the same elementary determinant argument works because nonzero algebraic integers have complex absolute value at least 1.

There is a fully explicit quartic version of the cubic seed mechanism. For

    F_t(X,Y)=X^4-tX^3Y-6X^2Y^2+tXY^3+Y^4,

the binomial exponent 1/4, its positive recurrence, and a Gaussian-integer seed yield a separator with exponent below the quartic Thue threshold. The direct presentation works for t>=128, and useful seeds are certified by a finite inequality. This gives an elementary effective height bound for every fixed right-hand side. It does not assert a new classification of this classical family.

PROVED obstruction: a generic quartic or octic form is not in this two-power class. In particular the displayed standing quartic

    Phi(X,Y)=5X^4-50X^2Y^2+61Y^4

has a nonzero catalecticant, so no invertible linear substitution over any extension field converts THIS form into a sum of two fourth powers of linear forms. Adjoining radicals does not change this invariant obstruction. This is a precise obstruction to the direct one-binomial construction, not to simultaneous Padé approximation or all elementary methods.

The polynomial does have a useful fourth-root representation, written below. Its reconstruction is quadratic rather than fractional linear, and the naive transported Padé remainders lose the gain needed for a quartic separator. An explicit simultaneous approximation construction, together with its arithmetic denominator certificate, is the remaining target for this test.

### II.2. The finite structural certificate

PROVED. Let n>=4 and write a binary form as

    F(X,Y)=sum_(k=0)^n binom(n,k) a_k X^(n-k)Y^k,

over a field of characteristic zero. If

    F=c1 l1(X,Y)^n+c2 l2(X,Y)^n,

then the 3 by (n-1) matrix C with entries C_(i,j)=a_(i+j), 0<=i<=2 and 0<=j<=n-2, has rank at most 2. Each power of a linear form contributes a rank-one matrix. The assertion remains true if a coefficient of X in either linear form vanishes: the endpoint contribution still has rank at most one. Consequently one nonzero 3 by 3 minor is an exact obstruction over every field extension. It is also invariant under changing coordinates in the sense needed here: if a linear change made F a sum of two powers, composing back would already give such a representation of F.

There is also an elementary sufficient certificate. Suppose a0*a2-a1^2 != 0 and put

    r=(a0*a3-a1*a2)/(a0*a2-a1^2),
    s=(a2^2-a1*a3)/(a0*a2-a1^2).

Check the finitely many identities

    a_(k+2)=r a_(k+1)+s a_k, 0<=k<=n-2.

If r^2+4s != 0, let lambda1,lambda2 be the roots of z^2-rz-s. Solve

    c1+c2=a0,
    c1 lambda1+c2 lambda2=a1.

The recurrence proves a_k=c1 lambda1^k+c2 lambda2^k for every k, hence

    F=c1(X+lambda1 Y)^n+c2(X+lambda2 Y)^n.

All data lie in a field of degree at most 2 over the coefficient field. In the stated nonzero-Hankel chart c1*c2 != 0. Degenerate charts can be changed before testing. Repeated roots of the recurrence give terms of the form (c1+c2*k)lambda^k, which are not the same two-distinct-power construction and must be treated separately.

This is a useful meeting point between the earlier Hankel method and separator selection: a rank-two coefficient moment sequence detects a single-binomial presentation before any asymptotic calculation.

For a quartic A X^4+B X^3Y+C X^2Y^2+D XY^3+E Y^4, the determinant is J/432, where

    J=72ACE+9BCD-27AD^2-27B^2E-2C^3.

For Phi, J=-848000 and the determinant is -53000/27. Thus the obstruction is nonzero. The other usual invariant is I=12AE-3BD+C^2=6160, but I is not needed for the proof.

For degree 3 the analogous matrix has only two columns, so this obstruction is absent. This is one exact structural reason the cubic construction is much more general.

### II.3. A proved quartic seed separator

#### II.3.1 The form and its binomial presentation

Let A>0 and h!=0 be integers. Set

    G_(A,h)(X,Y)=hX^4-AX^3Y-6hX^2Y^2+AXY^3+hY^4,
    U=A+4h i,
    L=2A,
    D=64h^2,
    lambda=L+sqrt(L^2+D).

Direct expansion proves

    U(X-iY)^4-Ubar(X+iY)^4=8i G_(A,h)(X,Y).       (Q2.1)

The normalized coefficient sequence is (h,-A/4,-h,A/4,h), with recurrence a_(k+2)=-a_k. Thus this is exactly the two-power case from Section II.2, over Q(i).

Assume the finite inequality

    lambda>D^(3/2)=512|h|^3.                       (Q2.2)

Define alpha=1/4, z=(Ubar-U)/U, and

    P_j(z)=sum_(i=0)^j binom(j-alpha,i) binom(2j-i,j) z^i,
    Q_j(z)=sum_(i=0)^j binom(j+alpha,i) binom(2j-i,j) z^i,
    A_j=U^j P_j(z)=q_j-i p_j.

PROVED: p_j,q_j are integers, consecutive determinants are nonzero, and the small real root beta of G_(A,h)(X,1) has approximants satisfying

    0<q_j<=lambda^j,
    |q_j beta-p_j| <= (2|h|/L)(D/lambda)^j.         (Q2.3)

Thus

    C_Q=1, Q=lambda, C_E=2|h|/L, E=lambda/D,
    kappa=log(lambda)/log(lambda/D)<3.

The usual two-consecutive-approximant determinant lemma gives an explicit irrationality measure 1+kappa<4. The small prefactor C_E is preserved. Its usual nonnegative-index branch applies for denominators q with 2 C_E q>=1, namely q>=L/(4|h|); the smaller denominators form a finite explicit check.

#### II.3.2 All the elementary ingredients

Integrality. Since delta=Ubar-U=-8hi,

    binom(j-1/4,i) delta^i
      =(-2hi)^i product_(r=0)^(i-1)(4j-1-4r)/i!.

For each odd prime, the progression of odd numerator factors has step 4 and supplies at least the valuation of i!. The remaining power of 2 in i! is less than i and is supplied by 2^i. Therefore this expression, and hence each coefficient A_j, is a Gaussian integer.

The elementary finite-binomial identities proved in Appendix A, now with alpha=1/4, are

    Q_j(z)=(1+z)^j P_j(-z/(1+z)),

    (1+z)^alpha P_j(z)-Q_j(z)
      =c_j z^(2j+1) integral_0^1 t^j(1-t)^j
                              (1+zt)^(alpha-j-1) dt,

    c_j=alpha product_(r=1)^j(1-alpha^2/r^2).

They follow by expanding (x-1)^j(v-x)^j after the substitution x=1+zt, integrating finitely many powers, and using the binomial convolution. In particular Abar_j=U^j Q_j(z).

The exact three-term recurrence, obtained by comparing coefficients in these finite sums, is

    Z_(j+1)=a_j Z_j+b_j Z_(j-1), j>=1,
    a_j=(2j+1)L/(j+1),
    b_j=D(j^2-1/16)/(j(j+1)).                     (Q2.4)

It holds separately for p_j and q_j, with

    q_0=1, q_1=L, p_0=0, p_1=-2h.

Equivalently, it is the finite polynomial identity

    j(j+1) A_(j+1)
      =j(2j+1)(U+Ubar) A_j
        -(j^2-alpha^2)(U-Ubar)^2 A_(j-1).

This can be proved coefficient by coefficient after writing A_j as a sum in U and delta; only the rational identity between adjacent binomial coefficients is used. The companion Python file independently verifies it, integrality, and the determinant recurrence through j=12 at several signed h. The finite checks supplement, rather than replace, this all-j binomial identity.

Here are details sufficient to certify the recurrence at every index. The binomial convolution gives

    A_j=sum_(r=0)^j c_(j,r) U^(j-r) Ubar^r,
    c_(j,r)=binom(j-alpha,r)binom(j+alpha,j-r).

Put c_(j,r)=0 outside 0<=r<=j. The coefficient identity needed is

    j(j+1)c_(j+1,r)
      =j(2j+1)(c_(j,r)+c_(j,r-1))
       -(j^2-alpha^2)(c_(j-1,r)-2c_(j-1,r-1)+c_(j-1,r-2)).

Divide by c_(j+1,r) and multiply by (j+1)^2-alpha^2. With b=j+1-r and a=alpha, the resulting polynomial identity is

    j(j+1)((j+1)^2-a^2)
      =j(2j+1)(b(b-a)+r(r+a))
       -[b(b-1)(b-a)(b-a-1)
          -2r(r+a)b(b-a)+r(r-1)(r+a)(r+a-1)].

It follows by expanding b=j+1-r. This identity includes the endpoint coefficients because the missing terms have a zero factor. The companion certificate checks this as an exact formal polynomial identity, so its recurrence validation is not limited to j<=12.

Here is a quantitative recurrence proof that avoids imprecise asymptotics. Put c=2L/lambda. Since lambda^2=2L lambda+D, a_j<2L and b_j<D imply by induction

    q_j<=lambda^j.

The lower bound

    q_j>=c lambda^j/(j+1)                          (Q2.5)

also follows by induction. In fact the same argument works for alpha^2<=1/3. Write x=L/lambda, so 0<x<1/2 and D/lambda^2=1-2x. The required induction step is

    x(2j+1)/(j+1)^2
      +(1-2x)(j^2-alpha^2)/(j^2(j+1)) >= 1/(j+2).

This is linear in x. At x=1/2 its excess is

    j/[2(j+1)^2(j+2)]>=0.

At x=0 its excess is

    [j^2-alpha^2(j+2)]/[j^2(j+1)(j+2)]>=0

for j>=1 and alpha^2<=1/3. The initial bounds are q0>=c and q1=c lambda/2. This proves (Q2.5).

Let W_j=p_j q_(j+1)-p_(j+1)q_j. Then

    W_0=2h,
    W_j=-b_j W_(j-1),
    |W_j|=(2|h|D^j/(j+1))
             product_(r=1)^j(1-alpha^2/r^2)
          <=2|h|D^j/(j+1).                        (Q2.6)

Thus every W_j is nonzero. Successive differences p_(j+1)/q_(j+1)-p_j/q_j alternate in sign, and their absolute values strictly decrease: the ratio of the next difference to the current one in absolute value is b_(j+1)q_j/q_(j+2)<1. Bounds (Q2.5)-(Q2.6) show absolute summability because D/lambda^2<1. Hence they converge, with the alternating-series bound

    |q_j beta-p_j|<=|W_j|/q_(j+1).

Using (Q2.5)-(Q2.6) gives

    |q_j beta-p_j|
      <= [2|h|/(c lambda)] [(j+2)/(j+1)](D/lambda)^j
      <= (2|h|/L)(D/lambda)^j,

as claimed.

It remains to identify the limit. Condition (Q2.2) implies A>128|h|^3-1/(32|h|), so |z|<1 and D/(4A)<1. Let W=(Ubar/U)^(1/4) be the fourth root near 1 and put

    beta=i(1-W)/(1+W).

Then beta is real, G_(A,h)(beta,1)=0, and

    W A_j-Abar_j=i(1+W)(q_j beta-p_j).

In the integral remainder, |U+t(Ubar-U)|>=A and t(1-t)<=1/4. Therefore

    |W A_j-Abar_j|
       <= (alpha |Ubar-U|/A)(D/(4A))^j.

The right side tends to zero. Together with q_j>=c lambda^j/(j+1), this identifies the continued-fraction-type limit with the displayed root beta. It completes the proof of (Q2.3).

The equivalence kappa<3 iff lambda>D^(3/2) is elementary: kappa<3 means log(lambda)<3(log(lambda)-log(D)).

#### II.3.3 Explicit family and all roots

For h=1, condition (Q2.2) is equivalent to

Thus every integer t=A>=128 gives the separator. The other three roots are handled by the integer transformation

#### II.3.4 The seed transport identity

Fix t and a nonzero integer pair (p,q) such that F_t(p,q)!=0. Put

    z=p-iq, N=p^2+q^2,
    U0=(t+4i)z^4=A+4hi,
    h=F_t(p,q),
    M=[[p,-q],[q,p]], adjM=[[p,q],[-q,p]].

Then exact expansion, or (Q2.1), proves

    G_(A,h)(adjM(X,Y))=N^4 F_t(X,Y).              (Q2.8)

Indeed C(X,Y)=X-iY satisfies C(adjM(X,Y))=bar(z)C(X,Y); multiplying U0 by bar(z)^4 gives (t+4i)N^4. If A<0, changing X to -X changes A to -A; changing the overall sign handles a negative h if desired. The proof of the separator already allowed signed h.

A seed is useful for this sufficient certificate when its adjusted A>0 and

    2A+sqrt(4A^2+64h^2)>512|h|^3.

Common integer content dividing gcd(A,h) can be removed explicitly before testing. Removing a larger Gaussian-coordinate content dividing gcd(A,4h) may leave h nonintegral and requires a new denominator certificate; the present statement does not authorize it. This is a complete, checkable quartic analogue of the Thomas seed mechanism. It gives a height bound for the original fixed equation whenever a good seed is found; it does not prove that every t has such a seed.

#### II.3.5 The general angular threshold

For a degree-n two-power presentation, take a primitive seed of size H making an angular error of order H^(-tau) from a root direction. The transformed principal coefficient is of order H^n and the conjugate difference is of order H^(n-tau). Ignoring only fixed constants and explicitly specified exponential denominator factors, the elementary recurrence/Padé rates are therefore

    Q of order H^n,
    E of order H^(2tau-n),
    kappa tending to n/(2tau-n).

To beat the degree-n Thue upper bound one needs kappa<n-1, which requires

    tau>n^2/[2(n-1)].

For cubics this is 9/4; for quartics it is 8/3. This explains why a good quartic seed asks for an exceptional approximation beyond the q^-2 approximations furnished by the elementary pigeonhole argument. It is a barrier for this stated sufficient construction, not a proof that no different separator or better denominator cancellation can work.

### II.4. Why higher coefficient fields impose a real cost

PROVED norm-determinant lemma. Let K have degree h and let v be a chosen archimedean place of weight w (w=1 for a real place, w=2 for a complex pair). Suppose p_j,q_j lie in an explicitly given order of K and consecutive cross determinants are nonzero. Suppose at v

    |q_j|<=C_Q Q^j, Q>=1,
    |q_j theta-p_j|<=C_E E^(-j),

and at each other archimedean place tau, of weight w_tau,

    |p_j|+|q_j|<=C_tau B_tau^j, B_tau>=1.

Set

    rho=h/w,
    B_*=(product_(tau!=v) B_tau^(w_tau))^(1/w).

If E>B_*, the determinant method gives, for rational p/q in any fixed bounded real/complex neighborhood of theta,

    |theta-p/q|>c q^(-mu),
    mu=rho[1+log(Q B_*)/log(E/B_*)],              (Q2.9)

with c>0 explicit from the displayed constants. This is a sufficient estimate, not a claim of optimality.

Proof. For nonzero Delta_j=p q_j-q p_j, the determinant of multiplication by Delta_j on the order is a nonzero integer. Thus its absolute norm is >=1. Bound the other embeddings by C q B_tau^j, where C includes the fixed bound on |p/q|. Taking the w-th root gives

    |Delta_j|_v>=c_delta q^(1-rho) B_*^(-j).

Choose j0>=0 as the least integer satisfying

    (E/B_*)^j0 >= (2 C_E/c_delta) q^rho.

At least one of Delta_j0 and Delta_(j0+1) is nonzero. For that index j, the error term q C_E E^(-j) is at most half the norm lower bound. The identity

    Delta_j=q_j(p-q theta)+q(q_j theta-p_j)

therefore yields

    |theta-p/q|>=[c_delta/(2 C_Q)] q^(-rho)(Q B_*)^(-j).

The ceiling bound j<=2+log((2C_E/c_delta)q^rho)/log(E/B_*) proves (Q2.9) whenever the logarithm is nonnegative; the remaining finitely many small denominators are treated directly. This also supplies the explicit constant.

For an imaginary quadratic field h=w=2 and there are no other archimedean places, so B_*=1 and the familiar exponent 1+log(Q)/log(E) survives unchanged. In a quartic CM field at a complex place rho=2; the other complex embedding imposes B_* and the prefactor 2. Adjoining radicals can therefore make the arithmetic inequality worse even when the analytic formula becomes easier. Trace to Q removes the coefficient field only if the conjugate remainders are also controlled; it is not a free operation.

For general alpha=1/n one safe denominator certificate is C_n^j, where C_n=n*rad(n). If U and delta are integral, C_n^j U^j P_j(delta/U) is integral: primes not dividing n are canceled by the numerator progression; for a prime dividing n, the extra radical factor supplies the factorial valuation. This safe exponential factor may be far too costly. A useful application must certify improved denominator cancellation or an exponential common divisor explicitly. If clearing costs rho0^j, the rates become Q=rho0 Q0 and E=E0/rho0; primitive cancellation reverses the corresponding factor.

### II.5. The cubic generator miracle does not persist

PROVED counterexample. Let theta be a root of

    x^4-10x^2-x+1.

This polynomial is irreducible modulo 2: x^4+x+1 has no root in F2 and is not the square of the unique irreducible quadratic x^2+x+1. It has four real roots, by the signs on (-4,-3), (-1/2,0), (0,1/2), and (3,4). The element eta=theta^2 generates the same quartic field because

    theta=eta^2-10eta+1.

But eta is not a rational fractional linear function of theta. If eta=(a theta+b)/(c theta+d), then

    c theta^3+d theta^2-a theta-b=0,

forcing a=b=c=d=0 by the degree of theta. This excludes a genuine fractional linear representation.

Thus the P05 dimension-three argument has no direct analogue even for two generators of one totally real quartic field.

A second PROVED barrier concerns naive nonlinear transport. Suppose eta=g(theta), where g is a rational function over Q of height degree D, g is regular at theta, and g'(theta)!=0. A rational p/q close to theta maps to g(p/q) of denominator O(q^D), and its error is O(|theta-p/q|). An irrationality estimate of exponent mu for eta therefore gives at best an estimate of exponent D*mu for theta by this one-number transport. To obtain a strict exponent improvement over the degree-four Thue bound with D=2 would require mu<2. A separate favorable comparison of constants at the boundary is not ruled out by this exponent statement. Such a uniform estimate is impossible for a real irrational eta because elementary rational approximation supplies infinitely many errors <1/q^2. This proves failure of this transport argument, not failure of simultaneous approximation, additional algebraic relations, or special cancellation in the reconstruction.

For degree 8, a quadratic reconstruction may still succeed if mu<4, and a cubic reconstruction if mu<8/3. Degree four reconstruction would require mu<2 and meets the same barrier. If g has ramification order l>1 at theta, replace D*mu by D*mu/l; the ramification must be certified and cannot be assumed.

### II.6. The standing Fibonacci quartic: an exact diagnosis

The actual quartic roots admit the following radical identities. Let

    alpha=sqrt(5+(8/5)sqrt5),
    beta=sqrt(5-(8/5)sqrt5),
    alpha beta=sqrt305/5,
    V=alpha+i beta.

Then

    V^2=(2sqrt5/5)(8+i sqrt61),
    V^4=(12+64i sqrt61)/5,
    |V|^2=10,
    alpha=(V+10/V)/2.                             (Q2.10)

All identities follow by squaring. Thus there really is a binomial fourth root over Q(sqrt(-61)). If one insists that the base contain all fourth roots of unity, it becomes Q(i,sqrt61), a degree-four field. The issue is not the absence of radicals.

The reconstruction in (Q2.10) is quadratic, and the following exact identity displays its analytic cost. Given A_j/B_j approximating V, put

    p'_j=A_j^2+10B_j^2,
    q'_j=2A_j B_j.

Then

    q'_j alpha-p'_j
      =(B_j V-A_j)(A_j-10B_j/V).                  (Q2.11)

If |A_j|,|B_j|=O(Q^j) and |B_j V-A_j|=O(E^(-j)), the new pair has height O(Q^(2j)) but its available linear-form bound is only O((Q/E)^j). For approximants with coefficients in an imaginary quadratic order and nonzero consecutive determinants, E>Q is impossible with such bounds: the consecutive determinant is a nonzero algebraic integer of modulus >=1, whereas the bounds would make it O((Q/E)^j), tending to zero. Hence this naive reconstruction alone does not produce a decaying linear form. A special cancellation beyond (Q2.11), or a simultaneous construction, is essential.

Separately, the exact catalecticant J=-848000 rules out a fractional linear cure of the displayed quartic itself. Neither adjoining sqrt61 nor passing to a degree-16 tower removes that invariant.

The original equation also has the elementary Pell-shaped form

    v^2-5(s^2-5)^2=-64.

A separator for sqrt5 alone merely reaches the quadratic rational-approximation scale. It has not enforced the additional constraint that the second Pell coordinate plus 5 is a square. This extra condition is precisely the arithmetic information that a useful cover or simultaneous separator must retain.

TARGET for the actual standing Thue reduction: supply its exact binary form(s), coefficient order, the lift from every original integral solution, and the two or more conjugate linear forms whose smallness is forced. First run the coefficient recurrence/catalecticant test. If a two-power presentation exists, certify its denominator factors and seed inequality. If it fails, seek a simultaneous Padé or Hermite–Padé construction for the paired square roots, with a nonzero integer resultant/determinant and all conjugate growth bounds. Merely having a degree-16 unit subgroup with a closed sieve supplies none of these missing coverage or determinant estimates.

An elementary separator that bounds every solution of the actual Thue forms would bypass unit-group coverage: the original-to-Thue covering identity would suffice, followed by the finite height certificate. A separator proved only for the recorded subgroup would leave the existing gap unchanged.

### III.1. The reusable theorem: a finite linear-algebra height compiler

PROVED THEOREM. Let A be an abelian group described by explicit algebraic charts, and let kappa:A -> P^(r-1)(Q) have finite fibres. Give A the primitive projective height

    H(P) = max |k_i|,

where k is any primitive integer coordinate vector for kappa(P). Assume the following finite algebraic data have been supplied and checked.

1. Homogeneous equations F_1,...,F_s vanish on kappa(A).
2. Homogeneous integer forms delta_1,...,delta_r of the same degree d satisfy kappa(mP)=(delta_i(kappa(P))) for a fixed integer m>=2.
3. For some N>=d and integer D!=0 there are homogeneous integer forms R_ij, S_ih with

       D k_i^N = sum_j R_ij(k) delta_j(k) + sum_h S_ih(k) F_h(k),

   where deg R_ij=N-d. A sufficient finite substitute for explicitly listing these identities is a full-row-rank homogeneous coefficient matrix whose columns are monomial multiples of the delta_j and F_h. Full row rank at a fixed degree is stronger than having just these pure-power identities at that degree; no equivalence at the same degree is asserted.
4. A finite set R of representatives gives A=R+mA, and for every R0 in R there are constants C_R and b<d such that

       H(P-R0) <= C_R H(P)^b.

Then A is finitely generated. Explicit finite-generation bounds are obtained from the coefficient norms in (3) and (4), without a general finite-generation theorem or a general height theorem.

Proof of the crucial height step. Put g=gcd(delta_1(k),...,delta_r(k)). The identities in (3), evaluated at primitive k with F_h(k)=0, show that g divides D k_i^N for every i. As gcd(k_i^N)=1, g divides D. The identities also show delta(k) is nonzero. If the total sum of the absolute coefficients multiplying the delta_j in each identity is at most B, then, for an i with |k_i|=H,

    |D| H^N <= B H^(N-d) max_j |delta_j(k)|.

Dividing by g<=|D| gives

    H(mP) >= H(P)^d/B.

Now write P=R0+mQ. The two inequalities give

    H(Q) <= (B C_R)^(1/d) H(P)^(b/d).

The exponent b/d is strictly less than 1. Above the explicit threshold (B C_R)^(1/(d-b)), the height decreases strictly. Induction on the positive integer H(P) reduces every P to the finite set of points below that threshold and the finite representative set R. Finite fibres of kappa imply finite height sublevels: there are finitely many primitive integer coordinate vectors in a bounded box, and each has finitely many preimages. This proves the theorem.

The finite linear-algebra version is particularly useful. Suppose all column forms have degree N and coefficient absolute value at most A0, and a chosen q-by-q integer coefficient minor B0 has nonzero determinant. The adjugate identities give the identities in (3). Every cofactor has absolute value at most (q A0)^(q-1), by the determinant expansion. Consequently one may use

    B = q (q A0)^(q-1).

One need not compute the huge adjugate or determinant over Z. An explicit nonzero determinant modulo one integer, computed using legal invertible pivots, proves that the integer determinant is nonzero; the elementary cofactor bound completes the height argument. This is a finite certificate, not an appeal to Nullstellensatz.

## Appendix A. The shared finite-binomial and determinant lemmas

### A.1. The exact binomial remainder

PROVED. Let 0<alpha<1 and j>=0 be an integer. Define

    P_j(z)=sum_(i=0)^j binom(j-alpha,i)binom(2j-i,j)z^i,
    Q_j(z)=sum_(i=0)^j binom(j+alpha,i)binom(2j-i,j)z^i,
    c_j=alpha product_(r=1)^j(1-alpha^2/r^2).

Then, with consistently chosen powers along a path avoiding zero,

    Q_j(z)=(1+z)^j P_j(-z/(1+z)),

    (1+z)^alpha P_j(z)-Q_j(z)
      =c_j z^(2j+1) integral_0^1 t^j(1-t)^j
                                  (1+zt)^(alpha-j-1) dt.

Here the second formula initially uses |z|<1; the finite antiderivative below extends it to the right-half-plane paths used in Part I.

Proof. Put v=1+z and substitute x=1+zt. Multiplication by z^(2j+1) changes the integral to

    integral_1^v (x-1)^j(v-x)^j x^(alpha-j-1) dx.

Expand the two polynomial factors and integrate each monomial. No exponent of x in the antiderivative has denominator zero, because alpha is not an integer. The elementary identity

    sum_(r=0)^j (-1)^r binom(j,r)/(b+r)
        = j!/[b(b+1)...(b+j)]

is proved by partial fractions after clearing denominators. It shows that the upper endpoint contributes

    v^alpha sum_(r=0)^j binom(j-alpha,r)binom(j+alpha,j-r)v^r,

and the lower endpoint contributes the same coefficient list in reverse order. To identify the first polynomial, its coefficient at z^i is

    sum_(r=i)^j binom(j-alpha,r)binom(r,i)binom(j+alpha,j-r)
      = binom(j-alpha,i)
          sum_(h=0)^(j-i) binom(j-alpha-i,h)binom(j+alpha,j-i-h)
      = binom(j-alpha,i)binom(2j-i,j).

The final equality is the binomial convolution obtained by multiplying the two binomial series. Reversing coefficients replaces alpha by -alpha. This proves both identities and their exact normalization.

The antiderivative is a finite sum of powers x^(alpha+integer), on the chosen branch. Hence integrals along two paths in the right half-plane with the same endpoints are equal by evaluation of this explicit antiderivative. This is the entire path-change justification in the sharp complex-conjugate estimate; no contour asymptotic theorem is needed.

  J(Q) = (T+{0,G}) + 2J(Q).                 (HALVE)

This is much more concrete than a software assertion that the rank is at most 1. It is an exhaustive rational halving statement, not merely a collection of necessary local conditions: the kernel theorem turns equality of delta classes into an actual rational half.

The same table already proves G has infinite order. The restriction of delta to T is injective, so no nonzero point of T is a rational double; consequently there is no rational point of order 4, and hence no torsion of order divisible by 4. Every odd-order torsion point is a double. Every remaining torsion point differs from a point of T by odd-order torsion, so its delta class belongs to delta(T). The displayed class of G does not belong to delta(T). The optional finite-field torsion counts are not needed for this conclusion.

### B.2. Explicit halving, sign changes, and the kernel

For any split monic quintic f(x)=product_i(x-a_i), let P=(a,b) be a nonbranch point. In a splitting extension choose r_i with

  r_i^2=a-a_i,   product_i r_i=-b.

Let e_j be the elementary symmetric functions of the five r_i, and set t=x-a. Define

  U(t)=t^2-e_2 t+e_4,
  E(t)=e_1 t^2-e_3 t+e_5.

PROVED polynomial identity:

  f(a+t)=E(t)^2+t U(t)^2.

Proof: write product_i(z+r_i)=z^5+e_1z^4+e_2z^3+e_3z^2+e_4z+e_5 and put z^2=-t. Its even part is E(t), its odd part is zU(t). Multiplying it by its value at -z gives both f(a+t) and E(t)^2+tU(t)^2.

Consequently the Mumford pair (U,E mod U), with the usual reduction at exceptional values, represents a half of [P-infinity]: the graph y=E(x-a) meets the curve at -P and twice at the divisor U=0.

For a degree-2 divisor P_1+P_2-2infinity, the square roots of delta_i are products r_{i1}r_{i2}. If these products are rational, Galois changes the sign choices for the two halves in matching pairs. The sum of the two half-divisors is rational by the following sign rule, proved next:

  changing an even set S of signs r_i changes the half-divisor by sum_{i in S} W_ai.

This rule is proved by the explicit polynomial identities in the sign-rule subsection below. Together with the elementary exceptional cases (one branch point, two branch points, repeated points, reduced degree 1) this proves ker(delta)=2J. The cases with repeated nonbranch points already have the evident half [P-infinity]. The homomorphism property of delta has the direct resultant proof in the resultant subsection below, including every branch and multiplicity case.

#### B.2.1 The sign rule has an explicit three-line polynomial certificate

The following identities prove the sign rule directly.

For the two signs to be changed, write their roots r,s; write the other three p,q,h. Set

  A=r+s, B=rs, C=p+q+h, D=pq+ph+qh, E=pqh,
  U_0=(t-B)(t-D), U_1=E-Ct,
  U_+=U_0+A U_1, U_-=U_0-A U_1,
  E_+=-(t-B)U_1+A t(t-D),
  E_-=-(t-B)U_1-A t(t-D),
  q_2=(t-B)^2+A^2 t=(t+r^2)(t+s^2),
  V=q_2(t-D)/A.

The distinctness of the branch roots implies r+s!=0: otherwise r^2=s^2. Direct expansion gives

  V-E_+ = (t-B)U_+/A,
  V+E_- = (t-B)U_-/A,
  f(a+t)-V^2 = -q_2 U_+ U_-/A^2.

The graph y=V meets the divisor for U_+, the two selected branch points, and the negative of the divisor for U_-. Thus the two halves differ by the sum of those two branch-point classes. Every even sign change is a composition of these changes. The same identities apply when one selected r is zero (a branch point P); no division by r is used.

#### B.2.2 Elementary kernel proof using the sign rule

Here is a proof using the usual uniqueness of reduced Mumford representatives.

First extend delta_i multiplicatively to divisors, giving a point P other than W_i and infinity the value x(P)-a_i, giving W_i the value f'(a_i), and giving infinity the value 1. For closed points take the product over their conjugates. To show this descends to divisor classes it is enough to show it is a square on every principal divisor.

Every rational function is (A(x)+B(x)y)/C(x), with polynomials over the ground field. Polynomial factors in x have paired divisors P+involution(P), including a doubled branch point when appropriate. Their delta_i is a square: away from a_i this is the square of the product of (x(P)-a_i), and at a_i the prescribed f'(a_i) occurs twice. Thus remove C and gcd(A,B). It remains to consider coprime A,B.

Put N(x)=f(x)B(x)^2-A(x)^2 and L=deg N. Its leading coefficient l is a nonzero square when L is odd and the negative of a nonzero square when L is even. Indeed the competing degrees 5+2deg B and 2deg A have opposite parity and cannot cancel. The finite zero divisor of A+By has degree L, with multiplicities. At a nonbranch point its x-values are exactly the roots of N with the same multiplicities; at a branch point x=a_i it occurs precisely when A(a_i)=0, in which case B(a_i)!=0 by coprimality.

If A(a_i)!=0, the product defining delta_i on this zero divisor is

  (-1)^L N(a_i)/l = (-1)^(L+1) A(a_i)^2/l,

which is a square by the stated sign of l. If A(a_i)=0, N has a simple root a_i, since

  N'(a_i)=f'(a_i)B(a_i)^2 !=0.

The product over all other roots is (-1)^(L-1)N'(a_i)/l. Multiplying by the prescribed delta_i(W_i)=f'(a_i) gives

  (-1)^(L-1) f'(a_i)^2 B(a_i)^2/l,

again a square. The pole is L times infinity and contributes 1. This proves delta_i kills every principal divisor, including repeated nonbranch zeros and all branch cases. Hence delta is a homomorphism. For each nonbranch point the product of its five delta coordinates is y^2; at W_i it is f'(a_i)^2. Therefore the image has coordinate product 1.

Now suppose delta(D)=1. For a degree-1 nonbranch divisor, all r_i can be chosen rational, so the explicit half is rational. For a degree-1 branch divisor, take its one r_i=0 and the other four square roots rational; the same formula works.

For two distinct nonbranch support points P_1,P_2, choose rational s_i with s_i^2=(x(P_1)-a_i)(x(P_2)-a_i) and product s_i=y(P_1)y(P_2). Choose r_{i1} with product -y(P_1), and put r_{i2}=s_i/r_{i1}. Under every Galois automorphism the two support points are either fixed or exchanged. The sign changes in the two root lists match coordinate by coordinate and have even parity. The sign rule therefore changes each half by the same 2-torsion class, so their sum is fixed and hence rational.

For one branch support point W_i and one nonbranch rational point P, use the rational s_j for j!=i. The common signs in these four coordinates determine the remaining sign in the second root list by its product condition. Give the zero entry in the first list that same sign; this does not change its value and makes both sign patterns even. The two half-divisor changes again cancel.

For two distinct branch support points W_i,W_j, let d=a_i-a_j. In the two root lists their i-th and j-th entries respectively are zero. In every other coordinate their products are rational square roots of delta_l(D), so their signs match. The remaining needed parity relation follows from

  (r_{i2} r_{j1} product_{l!=i,j} r_{l1})^2
     = -d^2 product_{l!=i,j}(a_i-a_l)
     = delta_i(W_i+W_j).

The right side is a square in the ground field, so the product on the left is in that field, and its Galois signs multiply to +1. This is exactly the condition needed to complete both sign vectors to the same even vector. Again the sum of the halves is rational.

A repeated nonbranch support point is already twice its degree-1 class, and the zero divisor is immediate. These cases exhaust reduced degree at most 2. Conversely every double has trivial delta because delta is a homomorphism into square classes. This proves the kernel statement by square roots, polynomial identities, and rationality of the resulting Mumford coefficients; it uses no class group or Mordell--Weil theorem.

The conclusion is the exhaustive relation J(Q)=T+{0,G}+2J(Q). Part III now supplies the previously missing strict height descent, so no unproved global coverage assumption remains in the ten-point theorem.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Exact auxiliary checks for the quartic P06 transported-separator theorem.

Only Python's standard library is used. The all-index proofs are in the text;
these checks independently validate the finite polynomial identities, first
Padé entries, and recurrence normalizations used there. No floating arithmetic.
"""

from fractions import Fraction as F
from math import comb


def add(z, w):
    return z[0] + w[0], z[1] + w[1]


def neg(z):
    return -z[0], -z[1]


def sub(z, w):
    return add(z, neg(w))


def mul(z, w):
    return z[0] * w[0] - z[1] * w[1], z[0] * w[1] + z[1] * w[0]


def scale(c, z):
    return c * z[0], c * z[1]


def power(z, e):
    out = (F(1), F(0))
    for _ in range(e):
        out = mul(out, z)
    return out


def conj(z):
    return z[0], -z[1]


def binom(x, i):
    ans = F(1)
    for r in range(i):
        ans *= F(x - r, r + 1)
    return ans


def pade_A(j, A, h):
    U, delta = (F(A), F(4 * h)), (F(0), F(-8 * h))
    out = (F(0), F(0))
    for i in range(j + 1):
        coeff = binom(F(j) - F(1, 4), i) * comb(2 * j - i, j)
        out = add(out, scale(coeff, mul(power(U, j - i), power(delta, i))))
    return out


def G(A, h, x, y):
    return h * x**4 - A * x**3 * y - 6 * h * x**2 * y**2 + A * x * y**3 + h * y**4


def det3(m):
    a, b, c = m[0]
    d, e, f = m[1]
    g, h, i = m[2]
    return a * (e * i - f * h) - b * (d * i - f * g) + c * (d * h - e * g)


class Poly:
    """Tiny exact polynomial ring Q[j,r,a], used for an all-index certificate."""
    def __init__(self, terms=0):
        if isinstance(terms, Poly):
            self.terms = dict(terms.terms)
        elif isinstance(terms, dict):
            self.terms = {m: F(c) for m, c in terms.items() if c}
        else:
            self.terms = {(0, 0, 0): F(terms)} if terms else {}

    def __add__(self, other):
        other, out = Poly(other), dict(self.terms)
        for m, c in other.terms.items():
            out[m] = out.get(m, F(0)) + c
        return Poly(out)

    __radd__ = __add__

    def __neg__(self):
        return Poly({m: -c for m, c in self.terms.items()})

    def __sub__(self, other):
        return self + (-Poly(other))

    def __rsub__(self, other):
        return Poly(other) + (-self)

    def __mul__(self, other):
        other, out = Poly(other), {}
        for m, c in self.terms.items():
            for n, d in other.terms.items():
                key = tuple(x + y for x, y in zip(m, n))
                out[key] = out.get(key, F(0)) + c * d
        return Poly(out)

    __rmul__ = __mul__

    def __pow__(self, n):
        out = Poly(1)
        for _ in range(n):
            out = out * self
        return out


def main():
    j = Poly({(1, 0, 0): 1})
    r = Poly({(0, 1, 0): 1})
    alpha = Poly({(0, 0, 1): 1})
    b = j + 1 - r
    lhs = j * (j + 1) * ((j + 1)**2 - alpha**2)
    rhs = j * (2 * j + 1) * (b * (b - alpha) + r * (r + alpha)) - (
        b * (b - 1) * (b - alpha) * (b - alpha - 1)
        - 2 * r * (r + alpha) * b * (b - alpha)
        + r * (r - 1) * (r + alpha) * (r + alpha - 1))
    assert not (lhs - rhs).terms

    # For quartic polynomial identities, five distinct affine points prove an
    # identity in x/y; the y=0 check is included as an additional check.
    points = [(r, 1) for r in (-2, -1, 0, 1, 2)] + [(1, 0)]
    for A, h in [(128, 1), (1000, 2), (1000, -2), (3, 1), (5, -3)]:
        U = (F(A), F(4 * h))
        for x, y in points:
            lhs = sub(mul(U, power((F(x), F(-y)), 4)),
                      mul(conj(U), power((F(x), F(y)), 4)))
            assert lhs == (0, 8 * G(A, h, x, y))
            assert G(A, h, x - y, x + y) == -4 * G(A, h, x, y)
        entries = [pade_A(j, A, h) for j in range(13)]
        assert all(v.denominator == 1 for z in entries for v in z)
        qs, ps = [z[0] for z in entries], [-z[1] for z in entries]
        L, D = 2 * A, 64 * h * h
        assert (qs[0], qs[1], ps[0], ps[1]) == (1, L, 0, -2 * h)
        for j in range(1, 12):
            a = F((2 * j + 1) * L, j + 1)
            b = F(D, j * (j + 1)) * (j * j - F(1, 16))
            assert qs[j + 1] == a * qs[j] + b * qs[j - 1]
            assert ps[j + 1] == a * ps[j] + b * ps[j - 1]
        Ws = [ps[j] * qs[j + 1] - ps[j + 1] * qs[j] for j in range(12)]
        assert Ws[0] == 2 * h
        product = F(1)
        for j in range(12):
            if j:
                product *= 1 - F(1, 16 * j * j)
                b = F(D, j * (j + 1)) * (j * j - F(1, 16))
                assert Ws[j] == -b * Ws[j - 1]
            assert abs(Ws[j]) == F(2 * abs(h) * D**j, j + 1) * product
            assert Ws[j] != 0

    # Seed identities, again proved as quartic polynomial identities by five
    # distinct affine checks, with separate y=0 consistency checks.
    for t in (0, 3, 17, 128):

            for x, y in points:

        other = P(other)

            if not out[e]:
                del out[e]
        return P(out)
